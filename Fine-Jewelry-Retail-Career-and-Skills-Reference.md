# Fine Jewelry Retail Career and Skills Reference

This is a general, employer-agnostic reference for anyone building or planning a career in fine jewelry retail sales, sales management, and store or regional leadership — spanning luxury maisons, mid-market and national chains, department-store fine jewelry counters, cruise-line and travel retail, e-commerce jewelry brands, auction houses, and independent jewelers. It compiles job-title terminology across regions, a detailed comparison of the most commonly posted roles (responsibilities, KPIs, qualifications, reporting lines), the operational workflows employers actually run stores on, a curated library of genuinely free learning resources mapped to each skill, and a synthesized career progression map. All factual claims are drawn from job postings, employer career pages, trade associations, and industry publications fetched in September 2026, with direct source links inline; foundational standards from GIA, AGS, and JSA are flagged as authoritative even where the underlying document predates that window. Where a data point (e.g., specific commission percentages, some cadence details) is not publicly disclosed, this is stated explicitly rather than estimated.

### Narrative: terminology patterns and regional differences

**1. Four distinct naming "dialects" exist, and they map to employer type more strongly than to geography.**
- *Maison / high-jewelry dialect*: **Client Advisor**, **Senior Client Advisor**, **Brand Ambassador**, **Sales Executive**, **Boutique Manager**, **Store Director**, **Commercial Director**. Cartier and Tiffany both standardize on Client Advisor globally — the same title appears in Amsterdam, Kuala Lumpur, Tokyo, Mexico City and Chicago ([Richemont](https://careers.richemont.com/en/jobs/jr129165/client-advisor/), [Tiffany](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/view/client-advisor-full-time-chicago-michigan-ave-at-tiffany-co-4400128648)). Van Cleef & Arpels and David Yurman instead use **Brand Ambassador**; Harry Winston uses **Sales Executive** even in the US, which is unusual for the American market ([Harry Winston](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/view/sales-executive-at-harry-winston-4434490531)).
- *Mid-market chain dialect*: **Sales Associate / Jewelry Consultant** (Signet's compound title), **Jewelry Consultant**, **Assistant General Manager**, **General Manager**, **District Manager**, **Luxury Bridal Manager** ([Signet Jared posting](https://www.simplyhired.com/job/TxvN_JYeKGbVXVTIheU07fpT2ZV188ELPg1xasBc3y93IAvwQKRXLg)).
- *E-commerce/DTC dialect*: **Jewelry Consultant**, **Luxury Jewelry Consultant**, **Customer Experience Assistant Manager**, **Store Director** ([Brilliant Earth](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/brilliantearth/jobs/4356257009), [Blue Nile](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/view/luxury-jewelry-consultant-full-time-rockingham-park-at-blue-nile-4415131157), [Mejuri Store Director](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/view/director-of-sales-%E2%80%93-fine-jewelry-at-the-bowerman-group-4434700428)). Note Brilliant Earth renames the assistant manager role "Customer Experience Assistant Manager" — a DTC-specific tell.
- *Auction dialect*: **Specialist**, **Jewelry Specialist**, **Cataloguer / Associate Cataloguer**, **Department Assistant**, **Head of Department** ([Bonhams](https://www.indeed.com/cmp/Bonhams/jobs), [Christie's](https://www.linkedin.com/company/christies/jobs), [Sotheby's via Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/q-estate-jewelry-jobs.html), [Heritage](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/view/jewelry-specialist-at-heritage-auctions-3977068926)). "Specialist" in auction = subject-matter expert/valuer, NOT a salesperson — a critical disambiguation from retail's "Fine Jewelry Specialist."

**2. Spelling is the loudest regional signal.** "Jewelry" (US, and used by US-headquartered brands worldwide) vs. "Jewellery" (UK, Canada, India, UAE, Qatar, Australia/NZ). Canada is mixed: Signet posts "Sales Associate / Jewelry Consultant – Peoples Jewellers" — American spelling in the title, British in the banner ([Indeed CA](https://ca.indeed.com/q-jewellery-sales-consultant-jobs.html)).

**3. "Executive" means IC in India and the Gulf, but senior management in the US/UK.** Malabar, Tanishq, Kalyan, Joyalukkas, Damas and Alfardan all post **Sales Executive** / **Retail Sales Executive** / **Retail Sales Officer** for entry shop-floor roles ([Malabar](https://careers.malabargroup.com/JobSearch/jobList/1), [Indeed India](https://in.indeed.com/q-tanishq-sales-jobs.html), [Indeed UAE](https://ae.indeed.com/q-jewellery-sales-jobs.html)). An American reader would misread these as senior. India also layers in **Customer Relation(s) Executive**, **Relationship Manager / Customer Relationship Manager**, **Floor Manager**, **Showroom Manager**, **Retail Team Manager**, **Area Business Manager** and even **Greeter** ([Indeed India CRM](https://in.indeed.com/q-customer-relationship-manager-jewellery-jobs.html), [Indeed India showroom](https://in.indeed.com/q-jewellery-showroom-jobs.html), [Tanishq](https://in.indeed.com/q-tanishq-sales-jobs.html)). "Showroom" replaces "store"/"boutique" almost entirely in Indian postings.

**4. The UK favors "Sales Assistant," "Sales Advisor," "Sales Consultant" and "Sales Professional," plus "Showroom Manager" and "Manager Designate."** "Manager Designate" (a manager-in-waiting) is essentially UK-only ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/q-jewellery-manager-jobs.html)). The NAJ's own careers board (Faceted Careers) skews toward craft/valuation titles — **Registered Valuer**, **Valuer**, **Jewellery Expert**, **Senior Sales Specialist**, **Apprentice Goldsmith** — showing that the UK trade association frames the industry around qualification-led roles rather than retail sales roles ([Faceted Careers / NAJ](https://faceted.careers/)).

**5. Gulf postings use "Sales Executive," "Sales Advisor," "Boutique Manager," "Store Manager (Showroom)" and gendered titles.** Live UAE/Qatar/Saudi boards still carry **Sales Lady**, **Salesgirl**, **Female Sales Representative** and **Female Senior Sales Consultant** — a legal impossibility in US/UK/CA postings and a strong regional marker ([Bayt UAE](https://www.bayt.com/en/uae/jobs/jewellery-jobs/), [Indeed UAE](https://ae.indeed.com/q-jewellery-sales-jobs.html), [Indeed Qatar](https://qa.indeed.com/q-jewellery-jobs.html)). Arabic-language capability is titled into roles ("Sales Advisor (Arabic Speaker)", Mouawad). Distributor groups (Chalhoub, Al Tayer) use **Fashion Consultant** / **Fashion Advisor** even for jewelry brands like Swarovski ([Indeed Qatar](https://qa.indeed.com/q-jewellery-jobs.html)).

**6. Canada's distinguishing markers are the "-ery" spelling in the job title itself and the Government-of-Canada-style lower-case NOC titles.** Indeed Canada returns literal postings titled **jewellery store manager** and **retail sales manager** (lower case, NOC-derived), alongside **Jewellery Sales Consultant**, **Sales Professional** (Michael Hill, Maison Birks) and **Client Specialist Jewellery** ([Indeed CA](https://ca.indeed.com/q-jewellery-sales-consultant-jobs.html), [Indeed CA ambassador search](https://ca.indeed.com/q-fine-jewellery-ambassador-jobs.html)).

**7. Cruise / travel retail has its own vocabulary layered on top of retail.** Starboard uses **Fine Jewelry Specialist**, **Fine Jewelry expert**, **Swiss Timepiece expert** and **Prestige Advisor**; Harding+ uses **Fine Jewellery Sales Specialist**; onboard concessions post **Shop Manager**, **Assistant Shop Manager**, **Retail Salesperson**, **Watch Specialist**, **Brand Curator** (Explora Journeys) and **Art Auctioneer**; Caribbean port-of-call operators (Diamonds International) post **Fine Jewelry and High-end Watch Sales Associate** with the port in the title ([Starboard careers](https://www.starboardretailgroup.com/careers/), [allcruisejobs retail board](https://www.allcruisejobs.com/retail-jobs/), [Harding+](https://careers.hardingretail.com/job/812669), [Diamonds International Cozumel](https://www.allcruisejobs.com/i35571/fine-jewelry-and-high-end-watch-sales-associate-mexico-cozumel/)). Airport travel retail (DFS) uses **Sales Associate, [Brand]** and **Team Lead, Sales** ([DFS](https://careers.dfs.com/go/View-All-Jobs_CN/4749410/)).

**8. Senior/leadership titles converge globally.** Above store level, everyone uses roughly the same words — **District Manager**, **Regional Sales Manager**, **Area Manager**, **Retail Director**, **Sales Director**, **VP of Retail Sales**, **Vice President of Regional Operations** — with only minor local flavor (India's **Area Business Manager**, UK's **Retail Regional Manager**, the Gulf's **Product Sales Manager**).

**Titles: Senior Sales Specialist / Luxury Sales Ambassador · Estate Jewelry Specialist · Gemologist–Sales Hybrid**

- **Experience thresholds escalate sharply:** 3 years (Tiffany Client Advisor, [LVMH](https://www.lvmh.com/en/join-us/our-job-offers/58927)) → 7–10 years (Leo Schachter Senior Diamond Jewelry Sales Specialist, [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/view/senior-diamond-jewelry-sales-specialist-at-leo-schachter-diamonds-4095531108)) → "Minimum 10 years" for a Bulgari flagship Senior Client Advisor ([LVMH](https://www.lvmh.com/en/join-us/our-job-offers/BULG10439)).

## Section 3: Workflows & Operating Systems Employers Expect

#### What employers actually expect from Sales Associates and Store Managers — grounded in job postings, trade publications, insurer/association guidance, and POS vendor documentation

**Research date:** September 3, 2026
**Sourcing rule applied:** every workflow claim below is tied to at least one fetched source URL. Where a source does *not* state a cadence, that is noted rather than invented.

---

### 0. Why the "operating system" framing is real in this category

Jewelry is a high-severity, high-shrink category, which is why employers codify routines rather than leaving them to judgment. The Jewelers' Security Alliance (JSA) logged 1,233 crimes against U.S. jewelry firms in 2025 — down 13% from 1,420 in 2024 — but total dollar losses rose to roughly $145 million, driven by professional crews cutting through walls and roofs to reach safes ([National Jeweler](https://nationaljeweler.com/articles/14966-jsa-s-2025-crime-report-shows-concerning-rise-in-violence), [JCK](https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/report-2025-jewelry-crimes/)).

Timing data explains why opening/closing and floor-coverage routines are so prescriptive: in JSA's 2024 report, robberies peaked between 5:00–6:00 p.m. (26 events) with a secondary peak at 12:00–1:00 p.m. (21), and "there was no 60-minute period during a normal workday when robberies did not occur"; grab-and-run thefts clustered 1:00–6:00 p.m. ([JSA 2024 Annual Crime Report](https://jewelerssecurity.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/2024-JSA-Annual-Crime-Report.pdf)).

JSA's operational recommendation is itself a recurring cadence: require **all employees to read JSA's weekly crime bulletin**, with a **training log employees sign** to confirm they read it ([National Jeweler](https://nationaljeweler.com/articles/14966-jsa-s-2025-crime-report-shows-concerning-rise-in-violence)).

Internal loss is treated as equally structural: "Most successful embezzlement schemes would fail if inventory and accounting records were organized and up-to-date," and "No one except the owner should have sole authority for any function in a store" ([JCK, Winning the Battle Against Internal Theft](https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/winning-the-battle-against-internal-theft/)).

### 1. Store opening and closing procedures

#### 1.1 The two-person rule and the all-clear signal

Insurer guidance treats open/close as the single riskiest routine of the day. Jewelers Mutual's 24/7 security guidance prescribes: "Use two or more people during opening procedures"; "Inspect the surrounding area for suspicious activity or signs of a break-in"; "Have one person access your facility while the other watches them enter safely"; "Lock the door and inspect the interior for signs of a break-in"; and only "After giving an all-clear signal" do other employees enter and "immediately lock the door" ([Jewelers Mutual guide, reproduced by The Plumb Club](https://plumbclub.com/a-jewelers-guide-to-24-7-security/)).

JSA's retailer security guide states the same rule and adds the reason: "Opening and closing are the most dangerous times for a jewelry store or business. Open and close with at least two people so one can open while the other watches from a safe distance with cell phone ready to call police" ([JSA/Jewelers Mutual retailer security guide, PDF](https://www.jacksondieken.com/media/1366/security-guide-for-retailers-us.pdf)).

JCK's security primer adds identity control at the door: "Do not open or close the store alone"; "One person should lock or unlock the doors, while another observes from a safe distance"; "Never let a stranger into your store before or after business hours"; "Carefully check the identification of all delivery personnel and other workers"; "If in doubt, keep them out!" ([JCK, Security: A Life and Death Issue for Jewelers](https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/security-a-life-and-death-issue-for-jewelers/)).

UK jewellers' block insurance guidelines formalize a signal protocol and key segregation: the procedure "should not be undertaken alone"; two colleagues act "in tandem"; the holder of safe/strong-room keys ideally waits outside while a colleague searches the premises (because criminals may hide overnight to ambush keyholders); the searcher then gives "a suitable visual signal or make[s] a call" to confirm entry is safe; staff carry portable panic alarms; and a pre-agreed code lets the person inside signal duress ([M&N Jewellers Block Guidelines, PDF](https://www.mninsure.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Jewellers-Block-Guidelines-as-at-11th-August-2021.pdf)).

#### 1.2 Opening sequence (merchandise out of safe, doors still locked)

- "When opening, keep the doors locked, remove merchandise from safes and vaults, and arrange displays. Unlock your doors only when you are ready for business." "Do not open your doors to anyone who is not an employee while you are preparing to open." "Have at least two employees on the show floor at all times." ([JSA retailer security guide](https://www.jacksondieken.com/media/1366/security-guide-for-retailers-us.pdf))
- "Remove merchandise and displays from the safe or vault and set out the inventory in showcases and show windows"; "Do not allow anyone (even a customer or delivery person) inside during the setup process"; "Make sure all display cases are locked before officially opening." ([Jewelers Mutual guide via Plumb Club](https://plumbclub.com/a-jewelers-guide-to-24-7-security/))
- Window/display work is a closed-store task: "Change or dress window displays only when the shop is closed"; "Never let anyone into the premises before or after business hours, including while dressing or undressing windows." ([M&N Jewellers Block Guidelines](https://www.mninsure.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Jewellers-Block-Guidelines-as-at-11th-August-2021.pdf))

#### 1.3 Closing sequence (customers out, then merchandise away)

- "At the close of the day, clear the store of all customers and lock the door before you remove merchandise." "Store as much merchandise as possible in safes or vaults." "Never reopen your doors after regular business hours." "Keep showcases locked and remove the keys unless you are showing jewelry from that case." "Be sure to comply with the in-safe warranty on your insurance policy." "Don't leave even low-valued jewelry in showcases that are visible from outside your store." ([JSA retailer security guide](https://www.jacksondieken.com/media/1366/security-guide-for-retailers-us.pdf))
- "Return all displays to the safe or vault, and do not allow anyone else in after you've locked up." ([Jewelers Mutual guide via Plumb Club](https://plumbclub.com/a-jewelers-guide-to-24-7-security/))
- Key segregation and post-departure control: one person leaves with the safe/strong-room keys and travels "a suitable distance away before giving a pre-arranged signal" to the colleague who then secures the premises; "At night, remove all keys to the safes and lock display-unit keys inside a safe"; "Premises keys must be strictly controlled and must not be held by the same people who hold safe keys"; and when staff leave the business, "change the intruder-alarm codes and safe combinations." ([M&N Jewellers Block Guidelines](https://www.mninsure.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Jewellers-Block-Guidelines-as-at-11th-August-2021.pdf))
- Nightly put-away volume is an insurance question, not a preference: each jeweler must put away enough merchandise "based on the nature and value of the merchandise" to meet insurance requirements; the article cites jewelers who keep roughly "70 percent of inventory in-safe," and recommends display trays, baker's carts wheeled "right into the vault," and descending showcase inserts to cut setup/take-down time ([JCK, The Most Preventable Crime](https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/the-most-preventable-crime/)).
- Do **not** substitute covers for put-away: showcases left loaded overnight "should not [be covered] with a cloth or blanket because that can attract burglars" ([National Jeweler](https://nationaljeweler.com/articles/14966-jsa-s-2025-crime-report-shows-concerning-rise-in-violence)); JCK quotes the burglar's logic: "Why's that covered? Because something is in it" ([JCK](https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/the-most-preventable-crime/)).
- Daily physical check: "Inspect window frames daily to ensure that they are secure and have not been tampered with," and never leave merchandise visible overnight "regardless of value" — "when closed, all goods belong in your safe" ([JCK, Security: A Life and Death Issue](https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/security-a-life-and-death-issue-for-jewelers/)).

#### 1.4 Case counts as part of open/close

- "Keep every slot in display trays filled with merchandise or markers so you know if an item is missing," and "Inspect showcases whenever you clean the glass, looking for evidence of tampering." Suspicious persons/vehicles are logged in a "Suspicious Incidents Book," used as an employee training tool and shared with police ([JSA retailer security guide](https://www.jacksondieken.com/media/1366/security-guide-for-retailers-us.pdf)).
- Employers put this in the SA job spec: "Maintain showcase presentation, merchandising standards, and **daily merchandise counts**"; "Follow all security, loss prevention, and cash-handling procedures with care and precision"; "**Support opening and closing procedures, including secure merchandise handling**" ([Vallera Jewelers, Sales Associate posting](https://www.simplyhired.com/job/32MtinpnYFs8Q0_v0FDtsuUbAaM784b1LClTqKxypXxPPC1rEZ36ZQ)). Shreve & Co. lists it as "Assist in opening and closing of store as scheduled" ([Shreve & Co. Sales Associate job description](https://shreve.com/pages/sales-associate-job-description)).
- Manager-level ownership: "**Oversee opening and closing procedures, cash reconciliation, and bank deposits**" ([Vallera Jewelers manager posting](https://www.simplyhired.com/job/32MtinpnYFs8Q0_v0FDtsuUbAaM784b1LClTqKxypXxPPC1rEZ36ZQ)); "Manage day-to-day store operations, including opening and closing procedures, cash handling, and security measures" ([NAJ-listed Jewellery Store Manager vacancy](https://www.naj.co.uk/current-vacancies/jewellery-store-manager/64564)).

#### 1.5 Alarm response is a defined procedure, not an ad-hoc call-out

- "Jewelers must respond to all alarm signals promptly, including notice of communication errors or power interruption"; "Jewelry personnel should never enter the jewelry premises alone in response to an alarm signal but should wait to be accompanied by police"; investigate "the roof, sidewalls, and all possible points of entry — including through neighboring premises"; keep the alarm call list current for vacations, holidays and weekends because "most burglaries happen over the weekend"; "periodically test" the system ([AGTA / JSA security series](https://agta.org/security-series-jewelers-security-alliance-tips-for-retailers-to-thwart-in-store-crime/)).
- Verify inbound alarm calls by calling back on the *published* number, "not necessarily the number given by the caller," and "Never enter the premises without the police or alarm company guard present" ([JCK](https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/security-a-life-and-death-issue-for-jewelers/)).
- Safe specification is part of the operating system: JSA states "Safes that are rated TL 15×6 and TL 30×6 are not adequate protection from today's burglars" and that burglars find a **TRTL 30×6** safe harder to penetrate; safes should not sit on an outside wall or a shared wall with a neighbor; rear safe/workroom gates stay "closed and locked" ([AGTA/JSA](https://agta.org/security-series-jewelers-security-alliance-tips-for-retailers-to-thwart-in-store-crime/), [JCK](https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/security-a-life-and-death-issue-for-jewelers/)).

### 2. Stock audits, perpetual inventory, cycle counts, and shrink control

#### 2.1 The record baseline insurers expect

Jewelers Mutual's inventory record-keeping guidance sets the floor: an **annual detailed/itemized inventory**, purchase invoices, component cost records and traceable sales receipts, retained **7+ years** and stored **securely off-premises**; build a **perpetual inventory system** with **unique inventory numbers**, category codes (e.g., CH for chains, D for diamonds) and sales tags; and use documented forms for receiving, selling, returns and inter-store transfers. The guide carries separate special-handling sections for pawned items, estate merchandise, memo/consignment goods, scrap gold, customer repairs, non-invoiced goods, custom-made jewelry, gold sold by the gram, bulk loose stones and giftware ([Jewelers Mutual, Inventory record keeping guide](https://www.jewelersmutual.com/resources/business/risk-management/inventory-record-keeping-guide)).

#### 2.2 Counting cadence: cycle counts beat the annual "big bang"

- Trade guidance is explicit that annual-only counting is inadequate: "Every store should do regular inventory control — **weekly, monthly, or bimonthly** — rather than just at year's end." Practices cited include "We inventory a different department each month," "I personally do a monthly count of diamonds," frequent **case counts**, **random counts of SKUs in back stock**, unscheduled checks, and **unannounced surprise inventories** on specific categories — because an employee who knows the schedule can prepare for it ([JCK, Winning the Battle Against Internal Theft](https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/winning-the-battle-against-internal-theft/)).
- A concrete published cycle-count model: "**Count one category each week**" so the whole store is counted by year-end and errors are caught "as they happen rather than all at once"; and "Anything above 5000 euro gets a **physical check every month**," using a four-step high-value count: "Two people count independently," "The count sheets are compared afterwards," "A third person recounts any differences," "Only then is the system adjusted" ([Gem Logic, How to do inventory for a jewelry store](https://www.gem-logic.com/articles/how-to-do-inventory-for-a-jewelry-store/)).
- Discrepancy protocol before declaring loss: "Recount first"; "Check whether the piece is in a repair box"; "Check whether it is out on approval with a customer"; "Check recent sales around that item number, in case of a wrong scan"; "Only as a last resort, write it off as a loss or suspected theft" ([Gem Logic](https://www.gem-logic.com/articles/how-to-do-inventory-for-a-jewelry-store/)). JCK makes the same point culturally: don't automatically attribute disappearances to miscounts — "try to determine the cause of every disappearance," and investigate clusters "quickly and thoroughly" ([JCK](https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/winning-the-battle-against-internal-theft/)).

#### 2.3 What the POS actually supports (so job specs can require it)

The Edge — a dominant jewelry POS — supports **three physical-inventory modes**: full physical inventory ("the entire store is scanned at one time"), **case count** ("allows you to scan specific locations as needed"), and partial scan (list-based, reconciled manually). The documented workflow is: scan tags location by location via `Inventory ► Physical Inventory ► Start Inventory`, "Start and stop as often as necessary," run the reconciliation/Finish step to compare Edge records against the count, then report missing items via Physical Inventory Reports; locations can be made mandatory via the "Require Physical Location for Inventory" system option ([The Edge, Physical Inventory v6.1, PDF](https://docs.edgeuser.com/TIP-2014-055_Physical_Inventory_v.6.1.pdf), [reconciliation step](https://docs.edgeuser.com/nethelp/Documents/physicalinventoryreconciliationfinish.htm)).

Memo and consignment goods are tracked as a separate stock type because "They are liabilities while in your possession" and "generate a payment due after being sold or consumed" — memo items are owned by a **vendor**, consignment items by a **customer** ([The Edge knowledge base](https://edgeuser.com/Knowledge/Knowledge-Base/inventory-consignment-and-memo-items)). Every piece should carry a serial number and a stock status such as "in stock," "on display," "on memo," "in repair," "reserved," and goods out on approval need "a signed receipt with a photo and an estimated value" ([Gem Logic](https://www.gem-logic.com/articles/how-to-do-inventory-for-a-jewelry-store/)).

#### 2.4 Segregation of duties and internal-theft controls

From JCK's internal-theft guidance ([link](https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/winning-the-battle-against-internal-theft/)):
- Dual control on "opening and closing out cash drawers," "signing off on bank deposits," and "signing for incoming merchandise" — "Two people should sign for incoming merchandise."
- "The person handling incoming shipments should not also conduct inventory."
- Rotate jobs regularly to add a check on records, prevent concentration of authority, and "frustrate possible collusion."
- Digital keys/locks programmed per employee and per schedule, recording "who went into a case or location and when."
- Unscheduled inspections of records and inventory by a top manager **and** by an outside auditor; owners are encouraged to come in unexpectedly on days off to count.

#### 2.5 How this shows up in real inventory/ops job specs

- Inventory Control Manager, King Jewelers (Nashville): "Maintain complete inventory accuracy across ~5,000 high-value jewelry, watch, and loose diamond items"; "Perform and oversee **cycle counts, physical inventories, reconciliations, and routine audits**"; "Investigate and resolve inventory discrepancies quickly and accurately"; "Monitor inventory movement, including receiving, transfers, sales, repairs, **memo items**, and returns"; "Produce inventory reports and performance metrics using Microsoft Excel" ([posting](https://bebee.com/us/jobs/inventory-control-manager-king-jewelers-nashville-tn--t7xk-768504163)).
- Store Manager, Mejuri: "Partner with Operations teams to hold successful **inventory & cycle counts on a monthly basis**"; "Responsible for mitigating risk within store … including internal & external theft, inventory accuracy & shrink" ([posting](https://www.indeed.com/viewjob?jk=f482e760d048e3b6)).
- Office Manager, Days Jewelers (multi-store US jeweler) — an unusually explicit calendar: incoming inventory and non-inventory invoices "reviewed, approved, and scanned … within **5 days**"; "**Reconcile transfers to ship and transfers to receive by the 25th of each month**"; "Oversee transfer of all defective merchandise and trade-ins to the corporate location **at least twice a week**"; "Send Pre-owned jewelry purchases to HEM **once a week**"; "Layaway report graded and submitted **by the 25th of each month**"; "Reconcile return/trade tile with the goal of nothing open over **two weeks**"; "Responsible for **physical inventory preparation** and in-store duties during physical inventory"; "Perform **quarterly bench sweeps**"; "ensures the **daily bank deposits** are delivered and deposited to the bank by the next business day"; "**Weekly** Time Card maintenance and approval"; "Create the store's **monthly** employee schedule and ensure that payroll meets budget requirements" ([posting](https://recruiting.paylocity.com/recruiting/jobs/Details/4385462/Days-Jewelers/Office-Manager)).
- Manager-side loss-prevention framing at store level: "Manage inventory control, receiving, transfers, and physical counts with strict loss prevention standards" ([Vallera Jewelers](https://www.simplyhired.com/job/32MtinpnYFs8Q0_v0FDtsuUbAaM784b1LClTqKxypXxPPC1rEZ36ZQ)); "Assist the stock and finance teams with conducting **regular stock audits**, implementing stock replenishment strategies, and monitor stock turnover" ([NAJ store manager vacancy](https://www.naj.co.uk/current-vacancies/jewellery-store-manager/64564)); "Assist in preparing for and taking store inventories" and "Safeguard and check-in all incoming and outgoing shipments" ([Shreve & Co. SA](https://shreve.com/pages/sales-associate-job-description)).
- Insurance-linked reporting cadence: insurers typically want an up-to-date inventory list "each quarter or half year," with an annual January export plus photo archive to the broker ([Gem Logic](https://www.gem-logic.com/articles/how-to-do-inventory-for-a-jewelry-store/)); JSA adds that jewelers should "update their inventory list with their insurance company to reflect the current price of gold" ([National Jeweler](https://nationaljeweler.com/articles/14966-jsa-s-2025-crime-report-shows-concerning-rise-in-violence)).

### 3. High-value transaction protocols

#### 3.1 Floor discipline while showing high-value goods

Jewelers Mutual's selling protocol is a step sequence: "Lock showcases immediately after removing merchandise to show a customer"; "**Loupe items before and after showing them to a customer**"; "**Show only one item at a time**"; "**Require a photo ID when showing high-value merchandise**"; "Use a private showroom to show items of exceptionally high value"; "Never leave customers alone with merchandise"; "Always keep showcase keys with you using a wrist key holder" ([Jewelers Mutual guide via Plumb Club](https://plumbclub.com/a-jewelers-guide-to-24-7-security/)). JSA adds: "Examine and quickly polish each item when the customer hands it to you and replace it in the showcase. This gives you time to determine whether it's the authentic item" — the anti-switch control ([JSA retailer security guide](https://www.jacksondieken.com/media/1366/security-guide-for-retailers-us.pdf)), and "After showing an item, make sure you return it to the showcase," with high-end watches and loose diamonds distributed across several cases rather than concentrated ([AGTA/JSA](https://agta.org/security-series-jewelers-security-alliance-tips-for-retailers-to-thwart-in-store-crime/)).

Cash display is itself a red flag: "Nobody comes into your jewelry store and flashes $10,000 in cash … If they do, [it's] game on; you're probably going to be the victim of a distraction theft" ([National Jeweler quoting JSA](https://nationaljeweler.com/articles/14966-jsa-s-2025-crime-report-shows-concerning-rise-in-violence)).

#### 3.2 Large cash transactions: the Form 8300 workflow

Any trade or business receiving **more than $10,000 in cash** in one transaction or in related transactions must file **IRS/FinCEN Form 8300**; since January 1, 2024 electronic filing is required for businesses that must e-file certain other information returns. The filer must also furnish a **written statement to each person named on the form on or before January 31 of the following year**, containing the business name and address, a contact name and phone number, the total reportable cash received in the 12-month period, and language stating the information is being furnished to the IRS. An invoice may serve as the statement if it contains all required elements; sending a copy of the actual Form 8300 is "not advisable due to the sensitive information." If the "suspicious transaction" box is checked, "do not send form as the statement" and "Under no circumstances should the parties on the form be made aware of a suspicious Form 8300 filing." Businesses are also "encouraged to document their attempts in trying to obtain required information from their customers" ([IRS Form 8300 reference guide](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/irs-form-8300-reference-guide); see also [IRS: understand how to report large cash transactions](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/understand-how-to-report-large-cash-transactions)).

#### 3.3 Cards, checks, wires and remote orders

- **Card-present discipline:** for a stolen card used in store, the card company generally covers the jeweler only if the jeweler "Obtained authorization," "Got an imprint," "Obtained the customer's signature," and "Followed proper card procedures." "If you don't get an imprint of the credit card and the customer's signature, you are at risk" ([JCK/JSA: Protect Yourself From Credit Card Fraud and Bad Checks](https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/jsa-protect-yourself-from-credit-card-fraud-and-bad-checks/)).
- **Remote/phone/email orders** are flagged as "one of the riskiest types of transactions": unknown customer, shipment to a remote location, next-day delivery demand, pressure to complete immediately, professional-sounding cover story, and a callback to divert the shipment away from the cardholder's address. The test question: "why a person in a distant city is buying jewelry from you when he can buy it from a dozen places locally" ([JCK/JSA](https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/jsa-protect-yourself-from-credit-card-fraud-and-bad-checks/)).
- **Checks and cleared funds:** do not rely on calling the bank or confirming funds — a confederate can remove the money right after verification. "The only surefire solution" is to wait until the money has actually transferred into the jeweler's account before releasing merchandise. Warning signs: out-of-state personal checks, no bank-printed return address, very low check numbers ([JCK/JSA](https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/jsa-protect-yourself-from-credit-card-fraud-and-bad-checks/)). Jewelers Mutual's red-flag list overlaps: out-of-state/post-dated checks or checks lacking a pre-printed address, rushed in-store customers, phone/email purchase requests, unusually high phone-order values, a customer supplying their own "bank" phone number for verification, demands for rushed shipment, and second IDs that don't match the card or check ([Plumb Club/Jewelers Mutual](https://plumbclub.com/a-jewelers-guide-to-24-7-security/)).
- **Layered verification stack** used by large jewelers: AVS (Address Verification Service), CVV, "scrutiny of first-time client transactions with an internal fraud review," tokenization of stored card data, point-to-point encryption and chip cards — with the caveat that chip technology "does not protect online transactions" and no single control is a "silver bullet" ([JCK, How the Jewelry Industry Is Battling Credit Card Fraud](https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/how-the-jewelry-industry-is-battling-credit-card-fraudand-what-you-can-do/)).
- **UK association guidance (NAJ SecurityWise)** frames it as a documented sales-order process: "Establish your sales order process to address risk concerns"; "Highlight required transaction fields"; "Verify card and cardholder data"; "Use cookies to recognise customers and identify high-risk international addresses"; maintain "an internal database of fraudulent transactions" and set "goals for reducing fraud as a percentage of sales"; deploy AVS, card security codes, Verified by Visa/Mastercard SecureCode; "Screen online card transactions to minimise fraud for large-ticket items and high-risk transactions"; "Require email order confirmations for approved transactions"; and act promptly on chargebacks ([NAJ, SecurityWise: preventing ecommerce fraud](https://naj.co.uk/news-and-blog/securitywise/securitywise-preventing-and-protecting-your-jewellery-store-from-ecommerce-fraud)).
- **EMV liability:** "If a bank issues an EMV card and you process a fraudulent transaction in a non-EMV terminal, the liability shifts to your business," and that loss type is not covered by the Jewelers Mutual policy ([Plumb Club/Jewelers Mutual](https://plumbclub.com/a-jewelers-guide-to-24-7-security/)).
- **Wire transfers** are positioned by retailers as the preferred instrument for the largest tickets, processed through the bank with routing details supplied after an appointment ([Dana Gold & Jewelry payment policy](https://shopdanagold.com/payment)) — consistent with the "wait for cleared funds" principle above.

#### 3.4 Financing and credit approval workflow

Consumer financing is a defined POS sub-workflow. Synchrony's retail orientation guide describes the flow — "You offer financing. Consumer completes financing application" — with multiple application channels including the Synchrony Transact online platform, an Online Consumer Application link on the retailer's site/social, and paperless in-store "Direct to Device" options; the credit card agreement must be in the consumer's hands before the sale proceeds ([Synchrony Retail Orientation Guide, PDF](https://www.synchrony.com/synchronyb/resources/Retail.pdf); [Synchrony jewelry financing page](https://www.synchrony.com/business/b2b/industries/jewelry)). Two compliance rules matter at the register: "You can only charge for products and services that are being received by the customer at the time of sale," and "Future delivery must be transacted using the Authorization Only and Forced Purchase processes" — the mechanism jewelers use for custom orders and specials ([Synchrony guide](https://www.synchrony.com/synchronyb/resources/Retail.pdf)). Retailer-facing reporting includes an Application Summary, Business Dashboard ("Summarizes application and credit limit approvals"), Authorizations Report, Credit Card Transaction Report (used "for reconciliation"), **Daily Funding Report**, **Monthly Statement**, and "My Customer List" showing existing customers' available credit ([Synchrony guide](https://www.synchrony.com/synchronyb/resources/Retail.pdf)).

Job specs mirror this: "Process sales, returns, layaways, and **financing applications** accurately at the point of sale" ([Vallera Jewelers](https://www.simplyhired.com/job/32MtinpnYFs8Q0_v0FDtsuUbAaM784b1LClTqKxypXxPPC1rEZ36ZQ)); "**Verify credit information for prospective customers**" and "Operate POS and process cash, credit card, store account charges and checks" ([Shreve & Co.](https://shreve.com/pages/sales-associate-job-description)).

#### 3.5 Appraisal / insurance documentation at point of sale

Each purchase should leave with a written appraisal that "describes all characteristics of the piece, including weights, grades, and measurements"; states any non-standard treatments (or that the gem is untreated); "states whether the gem is natural or synthetic"; "gives the manufacturer, workmanship, metal and karatage of the mounting"; "gives the value of the jewelry"; and "includes a photograph of the gem." Insurers recommend industry-standard **JISO/ACORD** forms: **JISO 805** Jewelry Sales Receipt for Insurance Purposes (completed by the selling retailer), **JISO 806** Jewelry Document for Insurance Purposes (second appraisal by a trained jeweler/appraiser), and **JISO 78 / 79** insurance appraisals for single/multiple items, which must be completed by a Certified Insurance Appraiser ([JCRS: what a jewelry appraisal should include](https://jcrs.com/JCRS_for_consumers/appraising_jewelry/jewelry_appraisal.htm)). Selling an appraisal so the piece can be insured is also treated as a standard attachment at repair/sale intake ([Stuller](https://www.stuller.com/benchjeweler/resources/bencharticles/view/boost-your-profitability-with-repair/)).

### 4. Custom design consultation workflow

Published consultation workflows converge on discovery → documented notes in CRM → sketch/CAD → approval → production tracking → presentation ([Jewel360, How to run a custom jewelry consultation](https://jewel360.com/blog/custom-jewelry-consultation)):

1. **Discovery interview** with a fixed question set: type of piece; occasion; preferred jewel; shape and cut; metal pairing; engraving (initials, date, message).
2. **Expectation setting** — explain "different jewels, costs, and production timelines to set realistic expectations," and show comparable examples.
3. **Documentation** — "Take detailed notes about the customer's thoughts," reuse those notes across multi-appointment projects, and "Record customer information in CRM software, including past purchases, maintenance, customer service interactions, consultations."
4. **Design iteration** — pencil sketches or CAD; produce "a few different mockups"; show sketches, images or 3D models; collect feedback throughout.
5. **Gate: written/explicit approval before manufacturing** — "Begin manufacturing after the customer approves the final design."
6. **Production tracking** — use the POS **work order module** to track "Progress," "Team members involved," and "the materials required," and "continue to send updates throughout the production process."
7. **Presentation and closeout** — present the finished piece, accept minor feasible adjustments, obtain final approval, complete the purchase.

Two supporting points from the trade press: custom/"Customization" is described as the "fastest growing segment in the jewelry industry," with "less than 2 out of 10 customers buy[ing] from the case" — the commercial reason employers expect associates to run consultations rather than only sell from inventory ([Stuller blog](https://blog.stuller.com/jewelry-repair-intake-strategy-plus-tips-on-marketing/)). And for special orders generally: "Whenever you place a special order, set reminders to check on its progress and keep the customer up to date on its status" ([INSTORE, 100 Things Every Jewelry Salesperson Should Know](https://instoremag.com/100-things-every-jewelry-salesperson-should-know/)); Shreve requires associates to "Accurately complete special orders and maintain record in CRM" ([Shreve & Co.](https://shreve.com/pages/sales-associate-job-description)).

### 5. Repair / service intake and bench coordination

#### 5.1 Who takes it in, and the standardized sequence

"Your sales staff should handle all take-ins" because intake creates sell-up opportunities (remounts, replacement vs. repair); "Have a standardized step-by-step take-in procedure that everyone follows"; and inexperienced staff must "feel comfortable calling on senior staff members for assistance" ([Stuller, How to improve your jewelry repair take-in process](https://www.stuller.com/benchjeweler/resources/bencharticles/view/how-to-improve-your-jewelry-repair-take-in-process/)).

The documented intake sequence ([Stuller, Taking a look at your jewelry repair take-in process](https://www.stuller.com/benchjeweler/resources/bencharticles/view/taking-a-look-at-your-jewelry-repair-take-in-process/)):
1. **Inspect thoroughly** — describe the piece in as much detail as possible; "Under camera, if possible, evaluate the prongs, shank, and stone"; "**Screen all diamonds and test metal in front of the customer** to set expectations."
2. **Document requested and recommended work** — "Document all repairs requested by the customer and additional repairs that are recommended," "Include if a repair was recommended and not acted upon by the customer."
3. **Clean and re-inspect** with ultrasonic/steam to reveal hidden damage.
4. **Price from a preset price list** and estimated labor time; maintain and "regularly update" the list (Geller's Blue Book cited as the pricing reference); "not to discount labor."
5. **Distribute copies of documentation** — "provide a copy of all documentation to both the customer **and the bench jeweler** to ensure complete understanding of the job."
6. **At pickup, discuss guarantees** — duration and restrictions.
7. **Recommend future checkups/maintenance.**

Physical description standards at intake: "Measure the stones," "Describe their color in detail," "Notate any damage to the piece and the condition of the stones," "Weigh the item," "Take pictures from all angles," and staple the photos to the repair envelope so the customer is confident they receive back exactly what they left ([Stuller, Boost your profitability with repair](https://www.stuller.com/benchjeweler/resources/bencharticles/view/boost-your-profitability-with-repair/)). Gem Logic states the same rule operationally: take a photo "the moment a piece crosses the counter" ([Gem Logic](https://www.gem-logic.com/articles/how-to-do-inventory-for-a-jewelry-store/)).

#### 5.2 The envelope/form as the legal record

The repair envelope is "your written record of the customer's jewelry and the work they authorized you to perform"; it "needs to be complete and accurate," with instructions "easily understood"; "Incomplete and inaccurate information must be eliminated if an efficient shop is to be maintained"; "**Never re-use an envelope** when a customer picks up a repair and leaves a new repair"; and "Proper identification, descriptions, and documentation of the jewelry reduce your store's potential liability" — "very important particularly with diamonds, gems, and estate pieces" ([Stuller](https://www.stuller.com/benchjeweler/resources/bencharticles/view/how-to-improve-your-jewelry-repair-take-in-process/)). Standard practice is a **3-part form**: "One for records, one to the customer, and one to the jeweler" ([Stuller blog](https://blog.stuller.com/jewelry-repair-intake-strategy-plus-tips-on-marketing/)). Jewelers of America supplies member repair envelopes with "a JA-approved disclaimer [that] protects both you and your customer" ([Jewelers of America membership benefits](https://www.jewelers.org/about/join-ja)).

#### 5.3 Bench handoff, estimate refinement, and communication cadence

Stuller's 10-step intake checklist sets the customer-communication rhythm ([Stuller blog](https://blog.stuller.com/jewelry-repair-intake-strategy-plus-tips-on-marketing/)):
- Greet, listen, clarify; visual inspection with written observations; complete the intake form (contact info, description of repair needed, other relevant details).
- Give a **preliminary** cost/time estimate at the counter, explicitly labeled preliminary, priced from Geller's Blue Book.
- **Bench jeweler performs a more detailed examination** under magnification to identify additional issues or risks.
- Discuss options and alternatives with the customer.
- Thank the customer; tour the showroom (future upsell).
- "**Keep the customer informed of the repair progress and notify them quickly of any changes or additional repairs.**"
- "**Always follow up to make sure your customer is happy with their repaired jewelry**" — via handwritten note, phone call, or an invitation for complimentary future cleaning/polishing.

At pickup, INSTORE's associate standards apply: "Check all prongs and settings before returning a piece of jewelry to the customer, even if all you did was clean it"; "Examine every repair with the customer before handing it off to them"; "When a customer picks up a repair, again, check to see if there's anything coming up they might need a gift for" ([INSTORE](https://instoremag.com/100-things-every-jewelry-salesperson-should-know/)).

Turnaround management is treated as a daily and weekly control:
- **Daily huddle item:** "Check all rush bags in the studio and be prepared to call ASAP when finished"; daily check-ins cover "repair status," "appointments," "sourcing followup" ([INSTORE, How to structure your sales team meetings](https://instoremag.com/how-to-structure-your-sales-team-meetings/amp/)).
- **Weekly meeting item:** "repair take-ins" and "tomorrow jobs that are due" ([INSTORE](https://instoremag.com/how-to-structure-your-sales-team-meetings/amp/)).
- **Weekly management report:** how many repair jobs were completed and ready for pickup, how many are open and within deadline, and how many are overdue — "Flag overdue repairs for immediate follow-up" ([Gem Logic, weekly reporting](https://www.gem-logic.com/articles/jewelry-business-reporting-weekly-numbers/)).
- Quick-turn expectations are also merchandising decisions: a prong re-tip quoted at "about 30 minutes," a "fifteen-minute turn-around time" giving customers time to browse, and premium pricing for rush jobs ([Stuller](https://www.stuller.com/benchjeweler/resources/bencharticles/view/boost-your-profitability-with-repair/)).
- Manager-level service ownership at a multi-brand watch retailer: "Supervise Service Take-In and Pick-Up processes" and "Partner with Service Coordinator/Sales Support to meet service KPIs (Auto-Accept, Immediate Estimates)" ([O.C. Tanner, Jewelry Store Manager – Timepieces](https://www.monster.com/job-openings/jewelry-store-manager-timepieces-ut--cb964cb5-7b70-42fa-9707-b6c4858a506b)). Days Jewelers assigns "quarterly bench sweeps" to store administration ([Days Jewelers](https://recruiting.paylocity.com/recruiting/jobs/Details/4385462/Days-Jewelers/Office-Manager)).

### 6. Clienteling and follow-up cadences

#### 6.1 The published post-sale cadence: 2 days / 2 weeks / 2 months

A widely marketed jewelry-specific clienteling cadence is the **2-2-2 strategy** ([Clientbook](https://www.clientbook.com/blog/mastering-clienteling-with-the-2-2-2-outreach-strategy)):
- **2 days after purchase:** personal thank-you message — "Show gratitude and reinforce their decision."
- **2 weeks after:** value-add check-in — how they're enjoying the piece, plus a cleaning appointment, trunk show, promotion or exclusive event.
- **2 months after:** next-purchase prompt — new arrivals "tailored to their style" (engagement ring → wedding bands; necklace → complementary bracelet), with pieces set aside for them. Messages are sent through the clienteling app "helping ensure consistency across your team," with past purchases logged rather than kept "manual spreadsheets or sticky notes."

#### 6.2 Gratitude as a daily habit with weekly accountability

Trade guidance is specific: "After a sale, especially to a first-time buyer, saying 'thank you' is non-negotiable"; salespeople should ask each guest for their **preferred contact method** and record it in the CRM (creating a custom field if none exists), then use that channel — phone, text, email, mail, or handwritten card; "When in doubt, a handwritten thank-you card is a sure bet"; "I encourage teams to send at least a few cards per week to keep the habit." The recommended management rhythm: "using **weekly team meetings** to check in on accountability and ensure team members are honoring guests' preferences and **sending thank-you notes daily**," with a set deadline for sending, and the rule that "the next time they hear from you should[n't] be a sales pitch" ([Southern Jewelry News, The most powerful words in business](https://southernjewelrynews.com/featured-article/retail-jewelry-business-featured/the-most-powerful-words-in-business/)).

#### 6.3 What employers write into the SA job spec

- Shreve & Co. (fine jewelry, San Francisco/Palo Alto): "Develop and maintain relationships with customers using our **client development program** including, but not limited [to], building rapport, **gathering customer information (birthdays, anniversaries, email addresses, wishlists, etc.)**, sending **personal thank you notes**, making **follow up calls and texts**"; "Stay sales focused throughout the day by promptly helping walk-in traffic, **working client book daily** and assisting other associates"; "Enthusiastically promote **events** in store and set up appointments"; "Accurately complete special orders and maintain record in CRM"; "Take in and process customers' repairs, **follow-up in a timely manner** and collect proper dollar amount" ([Shreve & Co.](https://shreve.com/pages/sales-associate-job-description)).
- Vallera Jewelers: "Build lasting client relationships through **follow-ups, wish lists**, and personalized service"; "Recommend add-ons such as protection plans, jewelry care products, and complementary pieces"; "Assist with repair intake, ring sizing, cleaning services, and special orders, setting clear expectations for clients" ([posting](https://www.simplyhired.com/job/32MtinpnYFs8Q0_v0FDtsuUbAaM784b1LClTqKxypXxPPC1rEZ36ZQ)).
- Tiffany & Co. Client Advisor: "**Capture client data. Cultivate new and existing client relationships**"; "Foster a clienteling culture, encourage an entrepreneurial spirit, reinforce client development to **grow outreach business**"; "**Deliver a significant portion of sales through repeat client business**"; "Deepen the relationship with our clients to drive lifetime loyalty and spend"; "Convert clients through curiosity and discovery daily"; "Consistently achieve or exceed **monthly, quarterly, and annual** store commercial targets"; "Demonstrate Client Experience Behaviors identified within the **NPS program**" ([Tiffany & Co. Client Advisor posting](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/view/client-advisor-the-landmark-nyc-at-tiffany-co-4364165983)).
- Mejuri Store Manager (manager-side ownership of clienteling): "Lead and validate client strategy to build lasting relationships with existing customers and drive new customer acquisition"; "Identify opportunities for marketing initiatives to drive new & repeat customers and community engagement at the store level"; "Provide in-store coaching … demonstrat[ing] best practices in hosting, customer service and product knowledge" ([posting](https://www.indeed.com/viewjob?jk=f482e760d048e3b6)).

#### 6.4 Daily communication hygiene and VIP tiering

Associate-level communication standards: "Check your email and voicemail **at least twice a day**"; respond to customers before leaving for the day; if a full answer isn't ready, say you'll follow up with more information soon; use text where the client prefers it; use email for "receipts, order forms, approvals, and other documentation"; follow up **discreetly** because the purchaser may not be the wearer; send handwritten thank-you notes after sales; respond to all online reviews, waiting and having someone review a response to a negative review ([INSTORE, 100 Things](https://instoremag.com/100-things-every-jewelry-salesperson-should-know/)).

On VIP structure, luxury clienteling practitioners describe tiering clients "based on purchase frequency, total spend, or tenure" and matching privileges — private events, early access to limited editions, personal shopping — to tier, rather than treating all loyal clients the same ([BSPK on luxury VIP clienteling](https://www.bspk.com/post/how-luxury-brands-deliver-vip-service-clienteling-personalization-and-repeat-customer-strategy); see also [Endear on clienteling in luxury retail](https://endearhq.com/blog/clienteling-in-luxury-retail), which defines clienteling as using "purchase history, preferences, life milestones, and communication style"). Note: these are vendor/practitioner sources, not association standards — treat as directional rather than authoritative.

### 7. CRM and MIS reporting cycles

#### 7.1 Daily

- Cash/deposit close: the manager "Oversee[s] opening and closing procedures, **cash reconciliation, and bank deposits**" ([Vallera Jewelers](https://www.simplyhired.com/job/32MtinpnYFs8Q0_v0FDtsuUbAaM784b1LClTqKxypXxPPC1rEZ36ZQ)); administratively, "ensures the **daily bank deposits** are delivered and deposited to the bank **by the next business day**" ([Days Jewelers](https://recruiting.paylocity.com/recruiting/jobs/Details/4385462/Days-Jewelers/Office-Manager)). Dual control applies to "opening and closing out cash drawers" and "signing off on bank deposits" ([JCK](https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/winning-the-battle-against-internal-theft/)).
- Daily check-in/huddle covering "that day's new products, appointments, promotions, repair status, clienteling efforts and other daily business," typically held "every[day]" in many businesses; sample agenda items include "Who needs help today on what appointment, situation or sourcing followup?", "ALWAYS highlight the big sales the day before," and "Heads up if that customer comes back in for the add-on" ([INSTORE, How to structure your sales team meetings](https://instoremag.com/how-to-structure-your-sales-team-meetings/amp/)).
- Financing/funding: Synchrony provides a **Daily Funding Report** and "Robust daily" reporting through Business Center ([Synchrony guide](https://www.synchrony.com/synchronyb/resources/Retail.pdf)).
- Sales/stock data is expected to be live: in-store POS stock "should drop immediately" when a piece is sold, with real-time sync between store and webshop, and gold spot price monitored daily ([Gem Logic](https://www.gem-logic.com/articles/how-to-do-inventory-for-a-jewelry-store/)).

#### 7.2 Weekly

- A published five-number weekly review, in "a fixed time slot, same day every week, with the same five reports open in sequence," ideally **Monday morning**, completed in "under fifteen minutes" when nothing unusual surfaces: (1) weekly turnover split between product sales and gift-card redemptions, compared to the same week last year and the previous four weeks; (2) net profit margin for the same period; (3) repairs completed/open-within-deadline/overdue; (4) customer purchases and old-gold acquisitions, identifying customers worth contacting; (5) top sellers and slow movers (top-ten/bottom-ten by product, category, seller, date range) ([Gem Logic, weekly reporting](https://www.gem-logic.com/articles/jewelry-business-reporting-weekly-numbers/)).
- Weekly team meeting used for clienteling accountability and skills training; a five-day-a-week training calendar can include "repair/shop one day" ([INSTORE](https://instoremag.com/how-to-structure-your-sales-team-meetings/amp/); [Southern Jewelry News](https://southernjewelrynews.com/featured-article/retail-jewelry-business-featured/the-most-powerful-words-in-business/)).
- Weekly compliance and admin: timecard maintenance and approval; defective merchandise/trade-in transfers to corporate "at least twice a week"; pre-owned purchases sent "once a week" ([Days Jewelers](https://recruiting.paylocity.com/recruiting/jobs/Details/4385462/Days-Jewelers/Office-Manager)). Security training cadence: all employees read JSA's weekly crime bulletin and sign a training log ([National Jeweler](https://nationaljeweler.com/articles/14966-jsa-s-2025-crime-report-shows-concerning-rise-in-violence)).
- Weekly stock control: "Count one category each week" ([Gem Logic](https://www.gem-logic.com/articles/how-to-do-inventory-for-a-jewelry-store/)); the broader principle being weekly-to-bimonthly inventory control rather than annual-only ([JCK](https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/winning-the-battle-against-internal-theft/)).

#### 7.3 Monthly / quarterly / annual

- "Review and monitor **daily, weekly, monthly business and people results** and create strategic plans **in partnership with the District Manager** to achieve ideal targets"; "Support store team in setting and achieving KPI goals"; "Partner with Operations teams to hold successful **inventory & cycle counts on a monthly basis**" ([Mejuri Store Manager](https://www.indeed.com/viewjob?jk=f482e760d048e3b6)).
- Monthly financial ownership at store level: the office/administrative manager's "primary objective is to ensure that the store achieves its **monthly budgeted contribution**," with transfers reconciled and the layaway report graded and submitted **by the 25th of each month**, and the monthly schedule built to payroll budget ([Days Jewelers](https://recruiting.paylocity.com/recruiting/jobs/Details/4385462/Days-Jewelers/Office-Manager)).
- Monthly high-value verification: items above the store's high-value threshold get a physical check every month using the independent double-count/third-person-recount protocol ([Gem Logic](https://www.gem-logic.com/articles/how-to-do-inventory-for-a-jewelry-store/)); a different department counted each month, plus a personal monthly diamond count ([JCK](https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/winning-the-battle-against-internal-theft/)).
- Monthly statements from the finance partner detail "monthly transactions of sales, fees and deposits" ([Synchrony](https://www.synchrony.com/synchronyb/resources/Retail.pdf)).
- Quarterly/annual: brand KPI scorecards and mystery-shop performance ("Assist in achieving **quarterly and annual** brand KPIs (Scorecards, FEO, MPDS, service goals)"; "Supervise Mystery Shopping Program performance") ([O.C. Tanner](https://www.monster.com/job-openings/jewelry-store-manager-timepieces-ut--cb964cb5-7b70-42fa-9707-b6c4858a506b)); quarterly bench sweeps ([Days Jewelers](https://recruiting.paylocity.com/recruiting/jobs/Details/4385462/Days-Jewelers/Office-Manager)); insurer inventory list refresh "each quarter or half year" plus a January export of the full list and photo archive to the broker ([Gem Logic](https://www.gem-logic.com/articles/how-to-do-inventory-for-a-jewelry-store/)); annual itemized inventory documented and retained 7+ years off-premises ([Jewelers Mutual](https://www.jewelersmutual.com/resources/business/risk-management/inventory-record-keeping-guide)); Form 8300 payer statements furnished by January 31 ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/irs-form-8300-reference-guide)).
- People cadence: "Set performance expectations, conduct **regular performance reviews**, and address any performance issues in a timely manner"; "Monitor and analyse sales reports, identify trends, and implement appropriate action plans" ([NAJ store manager vacancy](https://www.naj.co.uk/current-vacancies/jewellery-store-manager/64564)). Associates are expected to "Participate in and prepare for store meetings and work with manager on a **daily, weekly and monthly basis** to improve selling skills through practice," measured against "sales per hour and other KPIs" ([Shreve & Co.](https://shreve.com/pages/sales-associate-job-description)).

## Section 5: Career Path Map — From Entry-Level to Regional Leadership

This section synthesizes Sections 1–4 into a general, stage-by-stage progression map. It is designed as a self-assessment reference for anyone in the fine jewelry retail industry — not tied to any specific employer, region, or individual — showing what to master at each stage and which free resource (from Section 4) addresses each checklist item. Titles shown are illustrative anchors from the Tier 1–3 tables in Section 1; regional title variants (e.g., "Jewelry Executive" in India/Gulf for an entry-tier role, "Manager Designate" in the UK) map onto the same stage.

### Stage 1: Entry / Individual Contributor

**Illustrative titles:** Sales Associate, Jewelry Sales Consultant, Client Advisor (maison), Jewelry Executive (India/Gulf regional label), Fine Jewelry Specialist.

**What "ready to advance" looks like:** consistently meets individual sales targets, builds an organic client book through follow-up and referrals, operates the POS/CRM without supervision, and can explain the 4Cs and basic financing/appraisal concepts to a customer confidently.

### Notes on using this map

Progression timelines, title names, and even the number of distinct stages vary widely by employer type and region (see Section 1's regional quick-reference and Section 2's tier notes) — a maison client advisor and a department-store sales associate sit at the same career stage despite very different title prestige, and a bridal DTC brand may compress Stages 1–2 into a single generalist role. Treat the checklist items as a skills inventory to self-assess against, not a mandatory sequence, and cross-reference Section 2's KPI vocabulary and Section 3's operating cadences when preparing for a promotion conversation or a lateral move between employer types.
