# Role Profiles and Sourcing

## Why This Module Matters

Module 1 built the trait cluster that defines a great jewelry seller. This module turns that cluster into two concrete, practical documents a store leader actually needs before posting a single job ad: a role profile that names what the job is actually for, and a sourcing plan that gets the right candidates seeing that role in the first place. Skipping straight to posting a generic job ad without either of these steps is the single most common reason a hiring process produces a pile of resumes that don't match what the store actually needs.

## The Five-Block Role Profile

A well-documented job-profile structure breaks into five specific blocks, built in a deliberate order: role purpose (one sentence explaining why this role exists and who it serves), key outcomes (three to five specific results this hire owns, each with a measure attached, written before the day-to-day responsibilities are listed), must-have competencies (four to six items, each with a named piece of evidence that verifies it — a specific assessment, work sample, structured interview question, or reference-check question), nice-to-have competencies (two or three items explicitly not screened on), and success measures at 30, 60, and 90 days. The specific discipline behind writing outcomes before responsibilities: ask what has to be true twelve months in for this hire to be obviously worth it, write three to five answers, and those become the outcomes — only then work backward to the specific competencies a person needs to produce them.

## Why "Must-Have" Needs Evidence Attached

A specific, easy-to-skip discipline that makes the difference between a real hiring tool and a wish list: every must-have competency needs a named piece of evidence that actually verifies it during the hiring process — a specific interview question, a work-sample exercise, or a reference-check question. If a competency has no attached evidence source, it's not actually a must-have; it's a preference, and should be demoted to the nice-to-have list rather than silently inflating the requirements. This discipline connects directly to Module 3 of this course, where structured interview questions get built specifically to generate the evidence each must-have competency in the role profile requires — the role profile written in this module becomes the direct blueprint for the interview guide built next.

## Applying This to a Jewelry Sales Role

Using Module 1's trait cluster as the input, a jewelry sales associate role profile's must-have competencies might include: genuine product enthusiasm and storytelling ability (evidenced by asking a candidate to describe a piece of jewelry they personally love, listening for authentic detail versus generic scripted language), listening skill and restraint (evidenced by a role-play where the candidate responds to a customer who says they're "just looking"), and persistence without pushiness (evidenced by asking how the candidate has handled a customer who wanted to think it over, listening specifically for whether the follow-up they describe respects the customer's space). Each of these traces directly back to a specific finding from Module 1, and each has a specific evidence source attached — exactly the discipline the five-block structure requires.

## Pressure-Testing the Profile Before It Goes Live

A specific, low-cost check worth doing before posting any role: spend fifteen minutes with someone currently doing the job (an existing top-performing associate, ideally) and walk through the draft profile together. This catches invented responsibilities — items that sound reasonable on paper but don't actually reflect how the job is really done — before they end up misleading candidates and wasting everyone's time in the interview process.

## Writing the Job Posting Itself

A well-corroborated set of principles for the actual job posting, distinct from the internal role profile: a straightforward, keyword-driven title and description; clearly defined responsibilities pulled directly from the role profile's key outcomes; transparent compensation and benefits information; a clearly stated growth opportunity; and specific practical details a jewelry-retail candidate needs to evaluate fit (store format, product category, shift timing including weekend availability, and the store's incentive/commission structure). Jewelry-specific hiring guidance adds that the posting should also state grooming and communication expectations plainly, since these are legitimate, real considerations in a luxury retail environment that shouldn't be left for a candidate to discover only after applying.

## Sourcing: Matching Channel to Candidate Type

A specific, well-corroborated principle: match sourcing channel to the type of candidate being sought, rather than posting everywhere identically. Employee referrals are consistently identified as a particularly strong channel for retail hiring specifically, since a referred candidate arrives with an implicit, informal vouching from someone who already understands the job and the culture. LinkedIn works well for experienced, credentialed candidates (a Graduate Gemologist or an experienced bridal specialist, for instance). Local, in-person channels — walk-ins, local hiring events, and community partnerships — tend to work well for urgent, entry-level local roles. And social media, particularly Instagram, is specifically identified as an increasingly important channel for retail hiring broadly, reaching candidates who aren't actively browsing job boards but might respond to a well-designed, culture-forward post.

## Putting the Vocabulary to Work

The complete sequence this module establishes: write the five-block role profile first, using Module 1's trait cluster as the source material for must-have competencies and attaching real evidence to each one; pressure-test the draft with a current top performer; write the external job posting using the role profile's outcomes as its responsibilities section; and choose sourcing channels deliberately based on the specific candidate profile being sought, rather than defaulting to whatever channel was used last time regardless of fit.

## A Worked Scenario: The Role Profile That Fixed a Bad Hiring Streak

A store that had churned through three sales-associate hires in eighteen months discovers, when finally building a formal role profile, that its previous job postings emphasized "sales-driven, target-focused, competitive" language almost exclusively — exactly the general-sales-drive traits Module 1 identified as necessary but insufficient on their own. The rebuilt role profile explicitly adds must-have competencies for listening skill and genuine product passion, each with a specific evidenced interview question attached, and the resulting job posting's tone shifts from "closer wanted" to "trusted advisor wanted." The next hire, screened against this rebuilt profile, receives noticeably more consistent client feedback in their first ninety days than the three prior hires combined.

## Practice Exercise

Draft a five-block role profile for your store's next sales-associate opening (or your current role, if no opening exists), using Module 1's trait cluster as your source material for must-have competencies. For each must-have competency, name the specific piece of evidence (interview question, work sample, or reference-check question) that will verify it. Then pressure-test the draft with a current top-performing associate and revise based on what they flag as missing or unrealistic.

## Key Takeaways

- A well-structured role profile has five blocks, built in order: role purpose, key outcomes (written before responsibilities), must-have competencies, nice-to-have competencies, and 30/60/90-day success measures.
- Every must-have competency needs a named piece of evidence that verifies it during hiring; a competency with no attached evidence source is actually a preference, not a must-have, and should be demoted accordingly.
- Module 1's trait cluster (genuine product passion, listening skill, persistence without pushiness) becomes the direct source material for a jewelry sales role profile's must-have competencies, each paired with a specific evidenced interview question or work sample.
- Pressure-testing a draft role profile with a current top performer, in just fifteen minutes, catches invented responsibilities before they mislead candidates and waste time in the interview process.
- Sourcing channels should be matched deliberately to candidate type: employee referrals for reliable, culturally-aligned hires; LinkedIn for experienced, credentialed candidates; local walk-ins and community channels for urgent entry-level roles; and social media for reaching passive candidates who aren't actively browsing job boards.
