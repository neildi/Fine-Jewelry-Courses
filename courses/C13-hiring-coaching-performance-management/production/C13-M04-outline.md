# C13-M04 Outline: The 30-Day Onboarding Plan

## Module Metadata
- Course: C13 - Hiring, Coaching & Performance Management in Jewelry Retail
- Module: 4 of 10
- Persona: P7 (First-Time Store Leader), P8 (Multi-Store Leader), per official course architecture
- Working title: The 30-Day Onboarding Plan
- Learning objective: Store leaders can build a one-page 30-60-90 day onboarding plan using the Learn-Contribute-Own structure, populate it with jewelry-specific milestones, implement a peer buddy system alongside formal compliance training, and check a plan against the four C's (compliance, clarification, culture, connection) framework.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: Directly fulfills the C13 terminal outcome "onboard a new associate in 30 days" per this project's official course architecture, and directly supports the course's stated KPI link (ramp time, turnover).

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames onboarding as the payoff of the hiring process built in Modules 2-3, directly targeting ramp time and turnover.
2. **The Three-Phase Structure: Learn, Contribute, Own** — The general 30-60-90 framework and the shifting manager role across phases.
3. **What This Looks Like for a Jewelry Sales Associate, Specifically** — Jewelry-specific milestones mapped to each phase, including the partial (not full) ramp-quota target.
4. **The Buddy System: A Specific, Well-Corroborated Structural Choice** — Peer buddy pairing, its distinct function from manager coaching, and the jewelry-specific caveat about compliance training running alongside it.
5. **The Four C's: What Onboarding Actually Needs to Cover** — Compliance, clarification, culture, connection as a completeness check.
6. **One Page, Shared Before Day One** — Format and timing recommendations.
7. **Putting the Vocabulary to Work** — The complete onboarding-plan-building sequence.
8. **A Worked Scenario: The Hire Who Almost Left in Week Three** — Illustrative synthesis showing a culture/connection gap despite strong technical onboarding.
9. **Practice Exercise** — Building and four-C's-checking a real onboarding plan.
10. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The three-phase Learn/Contribute/Own onboarding structure, including the specific day ranges (1-30, 31-60, 61-90) and the shifting manager role (pairing with a buddy and weekly 1:1s in phase one; handing off real work in phase two; stepping back and running a full performance check-in in phase three) is sourced directly and specifically from rock.so's "How to Build a 30-60-90 Day Plan for New Hires" article, which names this exact three-phase structure and manager-role progression, including a specific sales-rep-role example (shadow 5 calls, learn product/ICP, complete CRM training in phase one; run 5 discovery calls solo, close one deal in phase two; hit 50% of full ramped quota in phase three).
- This module's jewelry-specific translation of the general sales-rep milestones (product-knowledge training with a pass threshold, shadowing live interactions, POS/CRM training, solo customer interactions, objection-handling role-play practice, a partial ramp-quota target, and a process-improvement suggestion by day 90) is this module's own original synthesis, adapting the confirmed general sales-onboarding milestone pattern (independently corroborated across Zendesk's "30-60-90 day sales plan," LeadSquared's "Sales Onboarding" guide, and leadbeam.ai's "30-60-90 Day Sales Plan" article, all describing a closely comparable Learn/Execute-or-Contribute/Own-or-Close structure with specific milestones like product training, shadowing, solo customer engagement, and a ramp-quota target) to this course's own previously-established jewelry-specific content (Modules 2 and 3).
- The peer buddy system recommendation (pairing every new hire with a peer, not their manager, from day one) is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sources: rock.so's onboarding article ("pair the hire with a buddy" as the first phase-one manager action) and BSPK's "Retail Store Training Strategies to Increase Efficiency" article, a retail-specific source explicitly naming "a well-implemented mentorship and buddy system" as supporting new hires through their initial weeks.
- The jewelry-relevant caveat that buddy/mentorship pairing must run alongside, not instead of, formal compliance and policy training is sourced directly from the same BSPK article, which explicitly states this pairing must occur "while ensuring compliance and policy training meet organizational standards."
- The "four C's" framework (Compliance, Clarification, Culture, Connection) is sourced directly and specifically from gopazo.com's "Retail Onboarding: A Step-by-Step Guide for New Hires" article, which explicitly names and defines these exact four categories.
- The one-page format and pre-day-one sharing recommendation, along with the specific starting content (name, role, start date, manager's name, key people to meet, day-one tools/access checklist) is sourced directly from joinhomebase.com's "30-60-90 Day Plan: Templates, Examples, And Free Guide" article, which explicitly recommends this exact format and timing.
- The worked scenario (the hire who almost left in week three due to a culture/connection gap) and the practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses created for this module, not externally sourced cases.
