# C13-M07 Outline: Performance Reviews With Evidence

## Module Metadata
- Course: C13 - Hiring, Coaching & Performance Management in Jewelry Retail
- Module: 7 of 10
- Persona: P7 (First-Time Store Leader), P8 (Multi-Store Leader), per official course architecture
- Working title: Performance Reviews With Evidence
- Learning objective: Store leaders can maintain a running evidence log fed by Modules 5-6, apply a three-step bias-neutralizing preparation discipline, write review language using behavior-not-personality framing and the SBI model, structure a complete review with a focused strength/growth-area/forward-plan format, and apply the simpler-rating-scale bias-reduction principle.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: Directly fulfills the C13 terminal outcome "run performance reviews with documented evidence" per this project's official course architecture.

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames formal reviews as the payoff (or squandering) of the coaching discipline built in Modules 5-6.
2. **The Running Evidence Log: Solving Recency Bias Before It Starts** — What to log, and why this directly counters recency bias.
3. **A Three-Step Discipline for Neutralizing Bias Before Writing** — Evidence collection, bias-check (recency/halo/leniency), outcome framing.
4. **Behavior, Not Personality: The Single Most Important Writing Discipline** — The most consistently repeated principle across performance-review sources.
5. **The SBI Model: Situation, Behavior, Impact** — Direct continuity with Module 5's observation-impact-alternative and Module 6's feedback structure.
6. **Structuring the Full Review: Strength, Growth Area, Forward Plan** — The focused two-to-four-strengths/one-to-two-growth-areas structure.
7. **Simpler Rating Scales Reduce Bias** — The Nature/Botelho et al. research finding, with appropriate context caveats.
8. **Putting the Vocabulary to Work** — The complete evidence-based review sequence.
9. **A Worked Scenario: The Review That Held Up Under Scrutiny** — Illustrative synthesis showing a log-based review correcting for recency drift.
10. **Practice Exercise** — Starting a real evidence log and backfilling it from existing Module 5/6 notes.
11. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The running evidence log (dated examples of strong work, dated examples of misses, feedback already given, goals progress, relevant third-party comments) and its direct function of countering recency bias are sourced directly and specifically from resources.rework.com's "Your First Performance Review as a Manager" article, which explicitly names this exact log structure and states "You should be adding to this document throughout the year" specifically to fight recency bias.
- The three-step bias-neutralizing preparation discipline (evidence collection spanning the full period, an explicit bias-check against recency/halo/leniency bias, outcome framing using a "[person] did [behavior] in [context], which [effect]" structure) is sourced directly and specifically from the same resources.rework.com article, which names this exact "Evidence-Based Review Prep" three-step sequence.
- The "behavior, not personality" writing principle is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across an unusually large number of independent sources: hr.wisc.edu's "Manager Writing Guide for Performance Review Comments," Culture Amp's "60 useful performance review phrases," Venngage's "200+ Performance Review Examples," hibob's "150+ performance review examples," AIHR's "192 Performance Review Phrases," PerformYard's "Negative Performance Review Examples," evalflow.com's "How to Write Performance Review Examples," Indeed's "45 Examples of Effective Performance Evaluation Phrases," LinkedIn's "How to Write Performance Review Comments," and status.net's "300 Examples for Performance Reviews" — an unusually large, independent convergence across nearly every source consulted for this module, giving very strong confidence in this specific principle.
- The SBI model (Situation, Behavior, Impact) is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sources: hr.wisc.edu, Culture Amp, and AIHR's "192 Performance Review Phrases" article, all describing this identical three-part structure with matching definitions.
- This module's explicit connection of the SBI model to Module 5's observation-impact-alternative structure and Module 6's feedback structure is this module's own original synthesis, connecting the confirmed general SBI framework to this course's own previously-established, independently-verified content; not itself an externally sourced claim, though the underlying structural parallel is genuine and direct.
- The focused review structure (2-4 strengths, 1-2 growth areas, each anchored to a specific dated example, closing with a forward plan and follow-up commitment) is sourced from Venngage's and status.net's articles, both independently recommending this same focused range and structure.
- The simpler-rating-scale bias-reduction finding is CONFIRMED, sourced directly from a Nature News & Views article (d41586-025-00304-y) discussing research by Botelho et al., which states that switching from a five-star scale to a simpler binary (thumbs up/down) scale eliminated racial bias in performance ratings in an online-marketplace context. This module explicitly notes the context difference (online-marketplace ratings vs. in-store performance reviews) rather than overstating the finding's direct applicability, while still presenting the underlying mechanism (finer granularity gives more room for bias to operate) as a reasonable, evidence-informed argument for simpler scales in any context.
- The worked scenario (the review that held up under scrutiny due to a full evidence log) and the practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses created for this module, not externally sourced cases.
