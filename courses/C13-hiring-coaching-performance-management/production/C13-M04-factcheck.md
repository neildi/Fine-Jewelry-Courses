# C13-M04 Fact-Check: The 30-Day Onboarding Plan

## Verification Status: The core three-phase structure, buddy-system, and four-C's claims are all CONFIRMED across multiple independent, credible sources, with no MUST VERIFY items identified in this module's research; the jewelry-specific milestone translations are original synthesis built on confirmed general sales-onboarding patterns

## Claims Reviewed

**1. The three-phase Learn/Contribute/Own onboarding structure (days 1-30, 31-60, 61-90) and the shifting manager role across phases.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly and specifically from rock.so's "How to Build a 30-60-90 Day Plan for New Hires" article, which names this exact three-phase structure ("First 30 days: Learn... Days 31-60: Contribute... Days 61-90: Own") with matching manager-role descriptions (pair with a buddy and schedule weekly 1:1s in phase one; hand off real work in phase two; step back from daily review and run a full performance check-in in phase three) and a specific sales-rep-role example.

**2. This module's jewelry-specific translation of general sales-onboarding milestones (product-knowledge training with a pass threshold, shadowing, POS/CRM training, solo customer interactions, objection-handling role-play, partial ramp-quota target, process-improvement suggestion).**
Status: ORIGINAL synthesis created for this module, adapting a general sales-onboarding milestone pattern that is independently corroborated across multiple sources: Zendesk's "30-60-90 day sales plan" (product/company knowledge in phase one, shadowing a top performer and role-playing in phase two, testing new approaches in phase three), LeadSquared's "Sales Onboarding" guide (product knowledge sessions, role-plays, mentor shadowing, measurable milestones), and leadbeam.ai's "30-60-90 Day Sales Plan" article (product training with a 90%+ assessment pass threshold, ride-alongs/shadowing, and a specific ramped-quota percentage target by day 90). This module's application of this well-corroborated general pattern specifically to a jewelry sales context is original synthesis, not itself a directly sourced claim, but built on confirmed underlying patterns.

**3. The peer buddy system recommendation (pairing every new hire with a peer, not their manager, from day one).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sources: rock.so's onboarding article (naming "pair the hire with a buddy" as the first phase-one manager action) and BSPK's "Retail Store Training Strategies to Increase Efficiency" article, a retail-specific source explicitly naming "a well-implemented mentorship and buddy system" as supporting new hires through their initial weeks.

**4. The jewelry-relevant caveat that buddy/mentorship pairing must run alongside, not instead of, formal compliance and policy training.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from the same BSPK article, which explicitly states this pairing must occur "while ensuring compliance and policy training meet organizational standards."

**5. The "four C's" framework (Compliance, Clarification, Culture, Connection).**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly and specifically from gopazo.com's "Retail Onboarding: A Step-by-Step Guide for New Hires" article, which explicitly names and defines these exact four categories: "Compliance: Covering rules, regulations, and policies. Clarification: Explaining roles, responsibilities, and expectations. Culture: Introducing company values, traditions, and customer service ethos. Connection: Building relationships with peers, managers, and the brand itself."

**6. The one-page format and pre-day-one sharing recommendation, with specific starting content (name, role, start date, manager, key people to meet, day-one tools/access checklist).**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from joinhomebase.com's "30-60-90 Day Plan: Templates, Examples, And Free Guide" article, which explicitly recommends this exact format ("Copy it into a doc, keep it to one page, and share it before their first day... Start with the basics: their name, role, start date, your name as their manager, the key people they need to meet and why, and a short checklist of tools and access they need on day one").

**7. Worked scenario (the hire who almost left in week three due to a culture/connection gap) and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not externally sourced cases.
