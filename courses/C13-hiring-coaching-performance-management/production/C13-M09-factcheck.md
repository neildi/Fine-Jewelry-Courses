# C13-M09 Fact-Check: Recognition, Incentives and Commission Fairness

## Verification Status: Recognition and incentive-design-failure principles are confirmed across multiple independent sources; the jewelry-specific commission and split-sale content is sourced from named jewelry-industry compensation experts and practitioner sources, with real variation across sources explicitly acknowledged rather than presented as a single settled answer

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Frequency-over-ceremony and specificity-over-generality recognition principles.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from goactify.com's "Retail Employee Recognition Program Examples" article, which explicitly names these as the top two design principles across successful retail-recognition programs, with matching example phrasing.

**2. Retail-specific retention/engagement statistics linked to integrated recognition.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from O.C. Tanner's "5 Recognition Best Practices for Retail Organizations in 2026" white paper, and independently corroborated in direction (though with different specific figures) by nectarhr.com's "10 Strategies To Build A Successful Retail Employee Recognition Program" article. Gallup's "Is Your Industry Delivering on Employee Recognition?" independently corroborates the general principle that authentic, equitable, personalized recognition combats burnout and turnover.

**3. Incentive-plan design-failure list (vague/complex criteria, inconsistent application, lack of transparency, gamed systems).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sources: BambooHR's "Why Incentive Programs Fail," Extu's "Avoid These 7 Common Sales Incentive Mistakes," and Forma.ai's "The Ultimate Guide to Sales Incentives for 2026," all naming a closely overlapping set of common failure modes.

**4. The pure-sales-volume-encourages-unsustainable-discounting risk.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced from hrdailyadvisor.hci.org's "Unintended Consequences When Setting Sales Incentive Programs" article, which explicitly warns of this exact risk.

**5. The gross-profit-based commission recommendation and the ~15-16% of gross profit jewelry-industry benchmark.**
Status: CONFIRMED as stated by a named jewelry-industry compensation consultant, sourced directly from JCK's "The Many Ways Retailers Pay and Reward Employees" article. IMPORTANT ACKNOWLEDGED VARIATION: this module explicitly notes that other credible jewelry-industry sources describe different, also-viable models: JCK's "The Savvy Jeweler's Guide to Compensation Packages" cites a jewelry retailer using straight sales-percentage commissions of 1-5%, and INSTORE's "Pros and Cons for 5 Popular Jewelry Store Commission Systems" (citing consultant David Geller) describes multiple sales-percentage models running roughly 6-10% of sales depending on discount level. This module presents the gross-profit approach as the more theoretically sound recommendation per the named consultant's stated reasoning, while explicitly acknowledging that a range of models are used successfully across the industry rather than asserting a single correct answer.

**6. The split-sale rules (50/50 for turnover-and-close; 75/25 favoring the associate who did original groundwork).**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from jewelerprofit.com's "Pay and Commission Structure for Sales Personnel" document, a jewelry-industry-specific compensation-policy template stating these exact percentages and scenarios, and independently corroborated by INSTORE's commission-systems article (citing David Geller's recommendation to split commission when two people help the same customer) and a Ganoksin Orchid jewelry-industry forum post describing a comparable practice from a practicing jeweler.

**7. The "shark environment" warning and the team-goal counterweight recommendation, including the vendor-spiff-to-team-reward conversion practice.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly and specifically from JCK's "Don't Swim With Sharks" article, a jewelry-industry-specific source explicitly naming this exact dynamic and fix.

**8. The proportionate team-bonus distribution practice (tied to hours worked or sales percentage, not an equal split).**
Status: CONFIRMED, independently corroborated by JCK's "Many Ways Retailers Pay" article (citing a named jewelry-store general manager's proportionate-distribution practice) and the Ganoksin Orchid forum discussion.

**9. The note recommending wage-and-hour legal review before rolling out any incentive plan.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced from worldatwork.org's "Incentive Plan Driving Unintended Behaviors?" article, which explicitly recommends legal, compliance, and governance review before rollout.

**10. This module's explicit connection to C10's negotiation/discounting content.**
Status: ORIGINAL synthesis created for this module, connecting an externally-sourced general sales-incentive risk to this project's own prior, independently-verified C10 course content. Not itself an externally sourced claim.

**11. Worked scenario (the split that almost cost a sale) and practice exercise.**
