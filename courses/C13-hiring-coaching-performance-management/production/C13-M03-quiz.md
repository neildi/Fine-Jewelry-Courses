**1. What is the specific build sequence for a structured interview process?**

B) Comprehensive job analysis identifying critical KSAOs, targeted question sets, standardized scoring rubrics, and thorough interviewer training

D) Structured interviews are identical to unstructured interviews in every respect

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The job analysis step is exactly the must-have competency list Module 2 already produced.

**2. What does the STAR method stand for, and what should an interviewer listen for specifically?**
A) Skills, Talent, Ability, Readiness — with no specific listening focus
B) Situation, Task, Action, Result — interviewers should listen specifically for Action and Result, since candidates often go vague exactly where the real evidence lives
C) STAR has no defined meaning in interview contexts
D) Story, Timing, Attitude, Reaction

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A useful extension, STAR-plus, adds a fifth element asking the candidate to name a lesson learned and tie the story back to the role.

**4. What should a manager watch for during a sales role-play audition?**
A) Only whether the candidate speaks confidently, regardless of content
B) Whether the candidate asks open-ended discovery questions and genuinely listens before pitching, whether the pitch feels tailored to what the "customer" said, and whether objections are handled with genuine empathy
C) Only whether the candidate can recite product specifications from memory
D) Role-plays provide no useful information beyond a standard verbal interview

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This maps directly onto this course's own established trait vocabulary from Module 1.

**5. What is a behaviorally anchored rating scale (BARS), and what problem does it solve?**
A) A scale with no defined criteria, left entirely to each rater's personal judgment
B) A scale pairing a numeric score (typically 1-5) with one concrete, observable behavior written out for each point, which solves the problem of different interviewers scoring the same answer very differently on a vague scale
C) BARS only applies to written exams, never to live interviews or role-plays
D) BARS eliminates the need for any interviewer training

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A BARS anchor removes most of the scoring drift caused by "good communication" meaning something different to each rater.

---

**6. What is the recommended rater-calibration process before using a new rubric on real candidates?**
A) Skip calibration entirely and begin live interviews immediately
B) Have every interviewer independently score recorded or scripted exemplar answers (weak, middling, strong), then hold a norming meeting to resolve any scores differing by more than a point by referencing the specific anchor language
C) Only the most senior interviewer's opinion should ever be used, with no discussion
D) Calibration is only relevant for written tests, not for interviews or role-plays

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This calibration step is what actually makes a shared rubric produce comparable scores across different interviewers.

**7. In the worked scenario, why did the role-play's BARS-scored result differ from the initial positive impression both interviewers had after the verbal interview?**
A) The candidate performed identically in both settings, with no meaningful difference
B) The candidate's role-play pitch barely referenced anything the "customer" said during discovery, running instead through a memorized, generic pitch — a gap the anchored rubric caught but the warm verbal interview alone likely would have missed
C) The role-play was scored purely on the candidate's confidence and tone of voice
D) Both interviewers disagreed sharply on the role-play score, revealing the rubric didn't work

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Because the rubric anchored "tailored pitching" to a specific observable behavior, both independent raters landed on the same lower score.

**8. Why does this module recommend using at least two independent raters for a candidate reaching a role-play or final-round stage?**
A) Multiple raters have no effect on scoring accuracy or bias
B) Multiple independent raters reduce individual bias and produce more reliable, comparable scores, especially when combined with a calibrated BARS rubric
C) Only one rater should ever be used, regardless of the stakes of the decision
D) Additional raters are only useful for entry-level roles, never for higher-stakes hiring decisions

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This panel-based, multi-rater practice is independently corroborated across multiple sources as a bias-reduction technique.
