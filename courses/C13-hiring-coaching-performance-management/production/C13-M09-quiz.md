# C13-M09 Quiz: Recognition, Incentives and Commission Fairness

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. What does research on retail employee recognition find regarding frequency and specificity?**
A) Annual banquets and generic praise are the most effective forms of recognition
B) Frequent recognition (weekly or same-day) built into the daily flow of work, combined with specific, named behavior, meaningfully outperforms occasional, generic recognition
C) Recognition has no measurable effect on retention or engagement
D) Specificity in recognition makes employees feel micromanaged and should be avoided

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: "Great job this week" registers as noise, while naming a specific behavior is what actually lands.

---

**2. What are the most common reasons incentive plans fail, according to this module?**
A) Incentive plans never fail if the reward amount is large enough
B) Vague or overly complex criteria, inconsistent application across managers, and a lack of transparency in how payouts are calculated
C) Incentive plans only fail due to insufficient marketing of the program
D) Incentive plans always succeed regardless of design

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This list is confirmed consistently across multiple independent incentive-design sources.

---

**3. What specific risk does basing commission purely on sales volume, with no reference to profit, create?**
A) No risk exists; sales-volume commission is always the ideal structure
B) It can quietly motivate salespeople to offer unsustainable discounts, since a sale is rewarded regardless of the margin actually protected
C) Sales-volume commission always produces higher margins than gross-profit commission
D) This risk only applies to industries outside of retail

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This connects directly to this project's own C10 course content on preserving margin in negotiation.

**4. What do jewelry-industry compensation experts recommend regarding the basis for commission, and what benchmark is cited?**
A) Commission should always be based on the number of hours worked, with no relationship to sales
B) Basing commission on gross profit rather than gross sales, with one cited jewelry-industry benchmark placing typical variable pay around 15-16% of the gross profit an associate produces
C) There is no variation across the industry; every jewelry store uses an identical commission structure
D) Commission should never be tied to profit under any circumstances

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This module also acknowledges real variation exists across sources and store models, with the right choice depending on margin structure and store goals.

---

**5. What are the jewelry-industry default split-sale rules this module describes?**
A) Split sales should never be divided; only the closing associate receives any commission
B) A common default is 50/50 for a genuine turnover-and-close, and 75/25 favoring the associate who did the original groundwork (like ordering or presenting) when a different associate closes
C) Split sales should always be divided exactly evenly regardless of who did what work
D) There is no benefit to having a written split-sale rule in advance

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Having this rule written down and known in advance prevents the inconsistent application that breeds perceived unfairness.

---

**6. What is the "shark environment" this module warns against, and what is the recommended counterweight?**
A) A shark environment describes healthy competition with no downsides
B) A purely individual, commission-only structure can create associates who guard customers rather than share them and resist handing off sales to better-suited colleagues; the counterweight is pairing individual commission with a proportionate team-based bonus
C) Team bonuses should always replace individual commission entirely
D) The shark environment only occurs in stores with no commission structure at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A common structure ties each associate's share of a team bonus to their share of total hours worked or sales contributed, rather than splitting it identically.

**7. Why does this module recommend reviewing any new incentive or commission plan for wage-and-hour legal compliance before rollout?**
A) Legal review is unnecessary for compensation structures
B) Commission structures interact with overtime calculations, minimum-wage guarantees, and other legal requirements that vary by jurisdiction, and this module presents design principles as practical industry practice, not legal advice
C) Wage-and-hour law is identical in every jurisdiction and requires no specific review
D) Legal compliance only matters for salaried employees, never for commissioned sales staff

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This mirrors the same legal-jurisdiction caution this course applied in Module 8's underperformance-management content.

**8. In the worked scenario, why didn't the second associate have any incentive to rush the close or discourage the customer from mentioning the earlier conversation?**
A) The store had no commission structure at all, so incentives were irrelevant
B) Because the store had a written 75/25 split rule for exactly this scenario, both associates knew in advance how the commission would divide
C) The two associates had a personal friendship that made money irrelevant
D) The customer insisted on paying both associates separately

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Without a written rule, this exact situation is a common source of quiet resentment and can even lead an associate to steer a customer away from a colleague's prior work.
