# C13-M01 Quiz: What Great Jewelry Sellers Have in Common

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. According to Jewelers of America's own jewelry sales professional skill list, what specific distinction is deliberately drawn regarding persistence?**
A) Sellers should never be persistent under any circumstances
B) Sellers should be "determined, persistent and persuasive, but not pushy" — a deliberate distinction that recurs across multiple sources
C) Persistence and pushiness are treated as identical, interchangeable qualities
D) Only aggressive, high-pressure selling produces results in jewelry retail

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This distinction shows up again and again across the jewelry-specific and luxury-specific sources this module draws on.

---

**2. What trait is identified as the cornerstone among successful luxury sales advisors, ahead of product knowledge or raw sales skill?**
A) Aggressive closing technique
B) Emotional intelligence
C) Years of prior retail experience
D) Formal sales certification

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The fuller luxury-advisor personality profile also includes authentic product passion, refined discretion, natural storytelling, cultural sophistication, poised confidence, and adaptive communication.

---

**3. What is the specific, screenable "serve, don't sell" hiring principle this module describes?**
A) Associates should refuse to serve any customer who appears unable to afford an item
B) Watch for any tendency in a candidate to size up a customer's spending power and adjust their effort accordingly, since that instinct is corrosive in jewelry retail
C) Serving and selling are exactly the same behavior with no meaningful difference
D) Only wealthy-appearing customers deserve full sales attention

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The best client relationships in jewelry retail often start with someone who looked unassuming and turned out to be the store's best customer.

---

**4. What does current, more nuanced personnel-selection research show about cognitive ability as a predictor of job performance, compared to an earlier, more simplified claim?**
A) Cognitive ability has no relationship whatsoever to job performance
B) While cognitive ability remains an important predictor (validity around r=.51 in the original Schmidt & Hunter 1998 meta-analysis), a more recent re-analysis finds structured interviews emerging as the single strongest predictor, with cognitive ability settling closer to fifth place among methods studied
C) Years of job experience is now considered the single best predictor of job performance
D) Unstructured interviews are now considered more predictive than any other method

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This module explicitly presents both the original and revised findings rather than asserting a single, now-contested figure as settled fact.

---

**5. What personality traits does the sales-assessment research this module cites identify as complementing cognitive ability in predicting sales success?**
A) Passivity, indifference, and conflict-avoidance
B) Achievement orientation, competitiveness, initiative, assertiveness, self-confidence, and — notably — coachability and resilience rather than raw aggression
C) Only formal education level matters; personality traits are irrelevant
D) Introversion and social withdrawal

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This cluster is confirmed consistent across multiple independent sales-assessment sources.

---

**6. Why does this module argue that jewelry selling requires a specific balance between "the closer" and "the advisor" traditions?**
A) Only closer-style aggression matters; advisory restraint is irrelevant to jewelry sales
B) Jewelry selling requires enough drive and initiative to actually ask for the sale, combined with enough restraint and listening skill to never feel like a closer to the customer — a candidate strong in only one dimension is a real mismatch risk
C) Only advisory restraint matters; drive and initiative are irrelevant to jewelry sales
D) The two traditions are identical and require no reconciliation

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A candidate who's all drive and no restraint reads as pushy in a jewelry setting; a candidate who's all warmth and no initiative never actually closes.

---

**7. In the worked scenario, what specific gap explained why the impressive-interviewing new hire generated client feedback describing them as "a bit much"?**
A) The hire lacked basic product knowledge entirely
B) Strong general sales drive from a competitive electronics-retail background, without the jewelry-specific restraint and listening orientation this category's customers expect
C) The hire was simply lazy and unmotivated
D) The hire had no prior retail experience of any kind

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The fix wasn't necessarily termination — it was targeted coaching aimed at dialing back visible closing pressure while preserving the underlying drive.

**8. Why does this module's factcheck flag and correct an initially drafted claim about cognitive-aptitude predictive multipliers?**
A) No correction was needed; the original claim was entirely accurate
B) The initial claim used an imprecise "2x/3x/4x" multiplier that was a transcription imprecision of a single vendor source's more specific figures, and conflicted with more recent, credible re-analysis research showing a more nuanced picture
C) Cognitive ability research is entirely unreliable and should never be cited
D) The correction only applies to non-sales job roles, not sales positions

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This module explicitly documents this correction as part of its own production process, modeling the kind of careful, honest fact-checking this project applies throughout.
