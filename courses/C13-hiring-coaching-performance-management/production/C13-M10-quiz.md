# C13-M10 Quiz: Building Bench Strength for Your Own Promotion

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. What three specific behaviors does this module identify as signaling leadership potential, distinct from sales performance?**
A) Highest sales numbers, most years of tenure, and best attendance record
B) Asking clarifying, forward-thinking questions (wanting to know why, not just what); helping without being asked; and making other people better, with newer associates naturally gravitating toward them
C) Willingness to work the most overtime hours
D) Leadership potential cannot be reliably identified through any observable behavior

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These signals are explicitly distinct from raw sales rank — a manager who only looks at the sales leaderboard will miss real future leaders.

---

**2. Why does this module emphasize "saying it out loud" once high-potential associates are identified?**
A) Naming leadership potential directly has no effect on an associate's own self-perception
B) Potential that's never named out loud often goes unrecognized by the person who has it; an associate may never have considered management as a real possibility until a manager says so explicitly
C) Managers should never discuss leadership potential directly with associates
D) Only associates who ask directly should ever be told they have leadership potential

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is an easy-to-skip step with a real, direct impact on whether an associate ever pursues a leadership path.

---

**3. What are the four readiness tiers this module describes for sorting a bench of potential leaders?**
A) Excellent, good, fair, poor
B) Ready now, ready within one year, ready within two to three years, and longer-term potential
C) Only two tiers exist: ready and not ready
D) Readiness tiers have no practical value in development planning

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This distinction changes what kind of development actually makes sense for each person, rather than treating every high-potential associate identically.

---

**4. What is the structured delegation discipline this module describes?**
A) Simply hand off any unwanted task to whoever is available, with no further structure
B) Define the desired outcome, select the person deliberately, explain the reasoning, define the authority level, agree on a check-in cadence, and close the loop with real feedback
C) Delegation requires no advance planning or communication
D) Only tasks the manager personally dislikes should ever be delegated

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Delegation done well is a deliberate development choice matched to a specific person's specific growth need, not simply unloading a task.

---

**5. What specific responsibilities does this module identify as ones that should never be delegated, regardless of a successor's readiness?**
A) Any task at all can always be delegated with no exceptions
B) Performance evaluations, disciplinary conversations and other confidential personnel matters, and situations requiring the manager's own specific authority or expertise
C) Only physically demanding tasks should never be delegated
D) Scheduling and inventory counts should never be delegated

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This isn't a contradiction of the delegation principle — genuine development depends on appropriately-scoped responsibility, not handing off everything difficult.

**6. In the worked scenario, why had the associate never pursued a management path despite showing strong leadership behaviors?**
A) The associate had no interest in management under any circumstances

C) The associate lacked the technical skills required for any leadership role
D) The store had no available leadership positions at any point

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This illustrates the direct, practical impact of the "say it out loud" practice this module describes.

**8. How does this module's closing section describe the relationship between all ten modules of this course?**
A) The ten modules are entirely unrelated to one another
B) They form a complete cycle: defining great-seller traits, building a role profile and sourcing plan, structured interviewing, onboarding, floor and one-on-one coaching, evidence-based reviews, managing underperformance, fair recognition and compensation, and finally developing the next generation of leadership from within the team
