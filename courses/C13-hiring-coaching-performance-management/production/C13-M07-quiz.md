# C13-M07 Quiz: Performance Reviews With Evidence

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. What is the running evidence log, and what problem does it directly solve?**
A) A document written entirely from memory in the final week before a review
B) A document maintained throughout the entire review period, containing dated examples of strong work, misses, feedback already given, and goals progress, which directly counters recency bias
C) A log that only records negative incidents, never positive ones
D) A document that has no relationship to recency bias or review quality

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A genuinely strong early period of performance fades from memory by the time a review gets written, unless dated evidence is captured as it happens.

---

**2. What is the three-step "Evidence-Based Review Prep" discipline this module describes?**
A) Guess, assume, and finalize the review with no preparation
B) Evidence collection spanning the full review period, an explicit bias-check against recency/halo/leniency bias, and outcome framing using a consistent "did X in context Y, which caused Z" structure
C) Only collect evidence from the final month of the review period
D) Skip any bias-checking step entirely

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This three-step sequence is designed specifically to neutralize bias before a review is drafted, not after.

---

**3. What is the single most consistently repeated principle across performance-review writing guidance, confirmed across an unusually large number of independent sources?**
A) Reviews should focus primarily on personality traits and general impressions
B) Describe the specific behavior and its impact, never the personality trait — a behavior gives an associate something they can actually act on, while a label like "disorganized" does not
C) Reviews should never include any specific examples
D) Personality-based labels are always more useful than behavioral descriptions

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This principle appears independently across nearly every source consulted for this module.

---

**4. What does the SBI model stand for, and how does it connect to earlier modules in this course?**
A) Sales, Bonus, Incentive — with no connection to any other module
B) Situation, Behavior, Impact — directly extending Module 5's observation-impact-alternative structure and Module 6's feedback structure into formal written review language
C) SBI has no standard meaning in performance-review contexts
D) Strategy, Budget, Implementation

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This represents genuine continuity across three modules of coaching and review practice in this course.

---

**5. What is the recommended focused structure for a complete performance review?**
A) An exhaustive list of every possible strength and weakness, with no limit
B) Two to four strengths and one to two growth areas, each anchored to a specific dated example, closing with a forward plan and a committed follow-up point
C) Only growth areas should ever be included; strengths should never be mentioned
D) A review should contain no specific examples at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This focused range, rather than an exhaustive list, keeps the associate able to actually act on the feedback given.

---

**6. What research finding does this module cite regarding rating-scale design, and what context limitation does it note?**
A) Rating-scale granularity has no relationship to bias in performance ratings
B) Research published in Nature found that switching from a five-star scale to a simpler scale eliminated racial bias in performance ratings in an online-marketplace context; this module explicitly notes this context differs from an in-store retail review rather than overstating direct applicability
C) Finer, more granular rating scales always reduce bias more effectively than simple scales
D) This research has been fully discredited and should never be cited

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The underlying mechanism (finer granularity gives more room for bias to operate) is presented as a reasonable, evidence-informed argument for simplicity, without overstating the original study's direct applicability to retail review contexts.

**7. In the worked scenario, what did the bias-check step reveal about the manager's initial draft?**
A) The draft was already perfectly balanced with no issues
B) The draft was starting to lean heavily on the two most recent log entries, prompting the manager to deliberately re-weight the review to reflect the full six-month period, including strong month-two performance that had started to fade from memory
C) The evidence log contained no useful entries at all
D) The manager decided to ignore the evidence log entirely and write from memory instead

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This illustrates exactly how the three-step bias-check discipline catches recency bias before it distorts a review.

**8. Why could the manager in the worked scenario respond specifically and credibly when the associate asked about a particular growth area?**
A) The manager had no specific information and had to guess
B) The manager could point to the exact dated log entry and the specific behavior and impact recorded at the time — a level of specificity a memory-based review could never have produced
C) The manager refused to discuss any growth areas with the associate
D) The review contained no growth areas at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is the direct practical payoff of maintaining a running evidence log throughout the review period.
