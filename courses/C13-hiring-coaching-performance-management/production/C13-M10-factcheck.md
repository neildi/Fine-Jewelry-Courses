## Verification Status: The core behavioral-signals, readiness-tier, and delegation-discipline claims are all confirmed across multiple independent, credible sources, with no MUST VERIFY items identified in this module's research

## Claims Reviewed

**1. The three specific leadership-potential behavioral signals (asks clarifying/forward-thinking questions; helps without being asked; makes other people better).**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly and specifically from retaildoc.com's "How to Develop Retail Managers From Within" article, which names these exact three signals with matching example language, and explicitly states the underlying claim this module relies on: "These are your future managers. Not because they sell the most. Because they demonstrate the behaviors you need in leadership."

**2. The "say it out loud" practice (identifying the top two or three high-potential people, having a direct conversation naming the observed potential).**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from the same retaildoc.com article ("Week 3: Identify and Tell Your High Potentials... Have a conversation with each one. 'I see leadership ability in you. Here's what the path looks like.'").

**3. The readiness-tier framework (ready now, ready within one year, ready within two to three years, longer-term potential).**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from retailreadyinsights.com's "Retail Talent Strategy Planning and Execution for Growth" article, which explicitly names this exact four-tier classification.

**4. General succession-planning principles (identifying high-potential employees, stretch assignments, preparing before a vacancy occurs).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sources: intoo.com's "Talent Management in the Retail Industry," LinkedIn's "How to Create a Succession Plan for Store Managers," retailreadyinsights.com's "Retail Leadership Bench Strength Development" article, and datacalculus.com's "Succession Planning for Retail HR Managers in Fashion."

**5. The structured delegation discipline (define the desired outcome, select the person deliberately, explain the reasoning, define the authority level, agree on check-ins, provide closed-loop feedback).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent, detailed sources: the University of Michigan's "Delegating Checklist," the University of Sussex's "Effective delegation" guide, and The Management Center's "Effective Delegation Process and Worksheet," all describing a closely matching multi-step delegation sequence with only minor variation in exact step count and ordering.

**6. The "delegation is a development choice, not just unloading a task" reframe.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced from the University of Michigan's "Delegating Checklist" ("Managers Must Delegate Effectively to Develop Employees") and independently corroborated by AMA's "Delegation Do's and Don'ts" article, which explicitly links effective delegation to "easier and more effective promotion and succession planning."

**7. The list of responsibilities that should never be delegated (performance evaluations; counseling/disciplining and other confidential personnel matters; tasks requiring the manager's own specific expertise, authority, or leadership).**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly and specifically from AMA's "Delegation Do's and Don'ts" article, which names these exact categories.

**8. This module's worked scenario reusing Module 4's onboarding framework as a delegated development responsibility.**
Status: ORIGINAL synthesis created for this module, connecting the confirmed general delegation and succession-planning frameworks above to this course's own previously-established, independently-verified Module 4 content. Not itself an externally sourced claim.

**9. The practice exercise and the "Closing the Course" synthesis.**
