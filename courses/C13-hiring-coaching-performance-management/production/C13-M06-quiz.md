# C13-M06 Quiz: Weekly One-on-Ones

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. What does Gallup research find regarding employees who have regular one-on-one meetings with their manager?**
A) Regular one-on-ones have no measurable effect on engagement
B) Employees with regular one-on-ones are nearly three times as likely to be engaged at work as those who don't
C) Regular one-on-ones actually decrease employee engagement
D) This relationship has never been studied

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This finding is cited consistently across multiple independent management-research sources.

---

**2. What is the more nuanced framing this module adds to the strong weekly-30-minute research consensus?**
A) There is no nuance; every employee needs an identical weekly 30-minute meeting with no exceptions
B) A more recent, careful synthesis cautions that the research doesn't support a universal rigid rule, and Gallup's own framework recommends a brief weekly "quick connect" paired with a more substantive check-in once or twice a month, adjusted for role complexity and coaching intensity
C) One-on-ones should never be held more than once per quarter
D) Cadence should be identical for every employee regardless of their tenure or role

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This module presents both the strong consensus and this more nuanced framing rather than oversimplifying to a single rigid figure.

---

**3. What is the employee-driven-agenda principle, and why does it matter?**
A) The manager should always set the entire agenda with no employee input
B) The employee prepares and sends the agenda in advance, ensuring the meeting covers the topics most important to them; a manager who always drives the agenda tends to hear about problems only once they're already serious
C) Agendas should never be prepared in advance for any one-on-one
D) Only managers should ever be allowed to raise topics in a one-on-one

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This structural choice directly determines whether the meeting actually surfaces what the associate most needs to discuss.

---

**4. What are the five recurring content areas that show up consistently across independent one-on-one frameworks?**
A) Weather, sports, gossip, complaints, and small talk
B) Current priorities, obstacles, recent evidence and feedback, recognition, and development
C) Only status updates; nothing else belongs in a one-on-one
D) Salary negotiations exclusively

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A one-on-one that only ever covers status updates and priorities, without recognition or development, is missing half of what this format is actually for.

---

**5. What is the recommended time-boxed structure for a standard 30-minute weekly one-on-one?**
A) An entirely unstructured, open-ended conversation with no time allocation
B) Catch-up and rapport, reviewing priorities and obstacles, feedback in both directions, and closing with specific next steps and named owners
C) The entire 30 minutes should be spent exclusively on manager-delivered criticism
D) One-on-ones should never have any time structure at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Ending with named actions and owners is what makes the next week's meeting able to start with a genuine follow-up rather than starting from scratch.

---

**6. Why does this module recommend recording commitments made in a one-on-one and reviewing them at the start of the next meeting?**
A) Recording commitments has no practical benefit
B) This is a direct extension of Module 5's follow-up principle to a longer time horizon; a one-on-one that never revisits prior commitments teaches an associate that what gets said doesn't actually matter
C) Commitments should only ever be recorded, never actually reviewed again
D) This practice is only relevant for new hires, never for experienced associates

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is the same failure mode Module 5 warned against for a single floor debrief, applied to the longer one-on-one format.

**7. In the worked scenario, how did the employee-driven agenda format help surface a real problem early?**
A) The manager discovered the problem through an anonymous survey
B) The associate's agenda included a recurring vague note about "scheduling stress" across three weeks, which the manager used the standing obstacles category to directly investigate, surfacing a genuine scheduling conflict
C) The problem was never actually identified or resolved
D) The associate never mentioned any concerns at all during the one-on-ones

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Because the agenda was associate-driven and obstacles was a standing category, the issue surfaced in week three rather than only becoming visible later as a performance problem.

**8. How does this module connect the "development" and "recent evidence and feedback" agenda areas to earlier course content?**
A) These areas have no connection to any other module in this course
B) "Recent evidence and feedback" draws directly on the floor-coaching observations Module 5 already generates, and "development" connects naturally to Module 5's GROW-model conversations for a specific in-progress skill
C) These areas are only relevant to Module 7, with no connection to Module 5
D) Development should never be discussed in a one-on-one setting

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This creates a coherent thread from floor coaching through the weekly one-on-one format rather than treating them as disconnected practices.
