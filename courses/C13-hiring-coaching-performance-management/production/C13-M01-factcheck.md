**2. The luxury-retail emotional-intelligence cornerstone finding and the six-part personality profile (authentic passion, refined discretion, natural storytelling, cultural sophistication, poised confidence, adaptive communication).**

**4. MAJOR CORRECTION DURING PRODUCTION: cognitive-ability predictive-validity research.**
Status: This module's research initially drafted a claim citing a "2x more predictive than interviews, 3x more than experience, 4x more than education" multiplier, sourced from a single hiring-assessment vendor page (Criteria Corp). Follow-up research specifically to verify this claim found: (a) Criteria Corp's own site copy actually states a more precise and different figure elsewhere ("1.6x as predictive as unstructured job interviews and four times as predictive as job experience"), meaning the initially drafted figures were an imprecise transcription/conflation, not an accurate quotation of even this single source; and (b) the broader, more authoritative personnel-selection literature presents a considerably more nuanced picture than any single multiplier suggests. The foundational Schmidt & Hunter (1998) meta-analysis (cited consistently across multiple detailed secondary summaries at cogn-iq.org) found general mental ability predicting job performance at r=.51, TIED with structured interviews at r=.51, with unstructured interviews at r=.38 and experience at r=.18. A more recent re-analysis (discussed in Cambridge University Press's "Industrial and Organizational Psychology" journal commentary and summarized in eSkill's "Best and Worst Predictors of Job Performance" article, both referencing I/O psychologist Paul Sackett's updated meta-analytic work) revises several validity figures downward and finds structured interviews — not cognitive ability alone — emerging as the strongest predictor when properly weighted, with cognitive ability settling to roughly fifth place among 19 methods studied. This module's final, published text was revised specifically to reflect this more accurate, nuanced, and current state of the research, explicitly presenting both the original and revised findings rather than asserting a single, now-contested figure as settled fact. This correction is documented here as a specific example of this project's fact-checking discipline catching and fixing a real, substantive drafting error before publication.

**5. The personality-cluster claims (achievement, competitiveness, initiative, assertiveness, self-confidence, coachability, resilience) correlating with sales success.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sales-assessment sources: Criteria Corp, Wonderlic's "Uncover Sales Superstars with Assessments" (citing TrueMatch's competency rankings: stress tolerance, integrity, persistence, dependability, achievement/effort, self-control, adaptability), and Testlify's "Sales Assessment Tests for Hiring" article.
