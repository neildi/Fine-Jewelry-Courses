# C13-M06 Fact-Check: Weekly One-on-Ones

## Verification Status: The Gallup engagement finding and weekly-30-minute cadence consensus are CONFIRMED across multiple independent sources; a more recent, careful re-analysis cautioning against a rigid universal cadence rule was identified and explicitly incorporated into this module's text rather than omitted

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Gallup finding that employees with regular one-on-ones are nearly three times as likely to be engaged.**
Status: CONFIRMED, cited consistently across multiple independent sources: Baylor University's "Manager Best Practices: One-on-One Meetings" and firsttimemanagers.com's "How Often Should You Have 1-on-1s?" both cite this exact Gallup statistic in matching language.

**2. Weekly, 30-minute cadence as the most consistently favored default, correlated with highest engagement.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sources: Baylor University's guide, physicianleaders.org's "Make the Most of Your One-on-One Meetings" (citing original survey research finding weekly 30-minute meetings rated most desirable and correlated with highest engagement levels among employees regardless of job level), Harvard Business Review's podcast interview with Steven Rogelberg (citing data showing a weekly cadence as most ideal, followed by biweekly), and firsttimemanagers.com's direct statement of this same conclusion.

**3. IMPORTANT NUANCE, explicitly incorporated into this module's text: a more recent, careful synthesis cautioning against a rigid universal weekly-30-minute rule.**
Status: CONFIRMED as an important, credible countervailing perspective, sourced directly from evalflow.com's "One-on-One Meeting Statistics and Research for 2026" article, which explicitly states "the research does not support a universal rule that every employee needs a 60-minute weekly one-on-one" and cites Gallup's own coaching framework recommending a brief 1-10 minute "quick connect" at least weekly paired with a more substantive 10-30 minute check-in once or twice a month, with cadence adjusted by role complexity, team needs, and coaching intensity. This module deliberately presents both the strong weekly-30-minute consensus and this more nuanced, flexible-by-need framing side by side, rather than omitting the nuance or asserting either framing as the sole settled answer — modeling the same fact-checking discipline this project applied when correcting the cognitive-ability claim in Module 1.

**4. The employee-driven-agenda principle.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from Baylor University's guide ("The employee prepares the agenda... and sends it to the manager in advance. Having the employee set the agenda ensures you are covering the topics and information most important to them") and independently corroborated by Lattice's "7 Free One-on-One Meeting Templates" article.

**5. The five recurring content areas (priorities, obstacles, recent evidence/feedback, recognition, development).**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from evalflow.com's article, and independently corroborated in substance (with somewhat different specific wording) by O.C. Tanner's "2020 Global Culture Report" one-to-ones section and AIHR's "12 Free One-on-One Meeting Templates" article.

**6. The time-boxed 30-minute structure (catch-up, priorities/obstacles, feedback, next steps with named owners).**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from HubSpot's "11 Questions to Ask Your Employees... in One on Ones" article, which provides this exact minute-by-minute breakdown, and independently corroborated in overall structure by PerformYard's "1:1 Meeting Agenda Templates" article.

**7. The commitment-recording and next-meeting-review discipline.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from evalflow.com's article and independently corroborated by PerformYard's template ("Recap of Previous Actions & Follow-Up — Start by reviewing tasks from your last 1:1 meeting agenda").

**8. This module's explicit connections between one-on-one agenda areas and Module 5's coaching content (recent evidence/feedback and development).**
Status: ORIGINAL synthesis created for this module, connecting confirmed general one-on-one frameworks to this course's own previously-established, independently-verified Module 5 content. Not itself an externally sourced claim.

**9. Worked scenario (the one-on-one that surfaced scheduling stress in week three) and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not externally sourced cases.
