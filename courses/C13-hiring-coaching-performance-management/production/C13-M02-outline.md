# C13-M02 Outline: Role Profiles and Sourcing

## Module Metadata
- Course: C13 - Hiring, Coaching & Performance Management in Jewelry Retail
- Module: 2 of 10
- Persona: P7 (First-Time Store Leader), P8 (Multi-Store Leader), per official course architecture
- Working title: Role Profiles and Sourcing
- Learning objective: Store leaders can write a five-block role profile with evidence-attached must-have competencies, apply Module 1's trait cluster as source material for those competencies, pressure-test a draft profile before posting, write an effective job posting, and match sourcing channels to candidate type.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: Directly fulfills the C13 terminal outcome "write a role profile and interview guide" (the role-profile half), per this project's official course architecture, and directly sets up Module 3's interview-guide content.

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames the role profile and sourcing plan as the prerequisite steps most hiring processes skip.
2. **The Five-Block Role Profile** — Role purpose, key outcomes (written before responsibilities), must-have competencies, nice-to-have competencies, 30/60/90-day success measures.
3. **Why "Must-Have" Needs Evidence Attached** — The evidence-or-demote discipline, explicitly tied forward to Module 3.
4. **Applying This to a Jewelry Sales Role** — Worked example translating Module 1's trait cluster into specific must-have competencies with evidence sources.
5. **Pressure-Testing the Profile Before It Goes Live** — The fifteen-minute check with a current job-holder.
6. **Writing the Job Posting Itself** — Posting-specific principles distinct from the internal role profile.
7. **Sourcing: Matching Channel to Candidate Type** — Referrals, LinkedIn, local/walk-in, and social media, matched to candidate profile.
8. **Putting the Vocabulary to Work** — The complete sequence: profile, pressure-test, posting, sourcing.
9. **A Worked Scenario: The Role Profile That Fixed a Bad Hiring Streak** — Illustrative synthesis contrasting "closer wanted" vs. "trusted advisor wanted" postings.
10. **Practice Exercise** — Drafting and pressure-testing a real role profile.
11. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The five-block role-profile structure (role purpose, key outcomes, must-have competencies, nice-to-have competencies, 30/60/90-day success measures) and the "write outcomes before responsibilities" discipline are sourced directly and specifically from Testlify's "Job profile examples for 10 roles, with template" article, which explicitly names this exact five-block structure and provides a detailed, step-by-step methodology (name the outcomes, work backwards to competencies, split must-have from nice-to-have, attach evidence to every must-have, write the context, set 30/60/90-day measures, pressure-test with someone doing the job) matching this module's structure closely.
- The "a must-have with no evidence source is a preference, so demote it" discipline is sourced directly, nearly verbatim, from the same Testlify article.
- The fifteen-minute pressure-test recommendation ("Pressure-test with someone doing the job... fifteen minutes with a current holder of the role catches the invented responsibilities") is sourced directly from the same Testlify article.
- This module's worked application of Module 1's trait cluster to specific must-have competencies and evidence sources (product enthusiasm/storytelling, listening/restraint, persistence without pushiness) is this module's own original synthesis, connecting the confirmed general role-profile methodology above to this course's own previously-established, independently-verified Module 1 content; not itself an externally sourced claim.
- The job-posting-writing principles (keyword-driven description, clear responsibilities, transparent compensation, growth opportunity, mobile-friendly format) are CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sources: LinkedIn's "Creating job postings that attract stronger" candidates guide and Employment Hero's "How to write retail job ads" guide.
- The jewelry-specific posting guidance (grooming/communication expectations, store format, product category, shift timing, incentive structure) is sourced directly from trymintly.com's "Retail Hiring Checklist for Jewellery Brands" article, a jewelry-specific hiring-guide source.
- The sourcing-channel-matching principles (referrals for retail reliability; LinkedIn for experienced/credentialed roles; local/walk-in for urgent entry-level roles; social media, particularly Instagram, for passive candidates) are CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sources: trymintly.com's jewelry-specific checklist ("LinkedIn for experienced profiles, referrals for faster trusted hiring, walk-ins for urgent local roles"), Oracle's "Five Recruiting and Onboarding Ideas to Win Retail Talent" (citing that 52% of retail candidates check Instagram regularly), ZipRecruiter's "How to Hire a Great Retail Sales" guide (citing internal referrals as a powerful channel), and sapia.ai's "Recruiting Ideas for Retail" article (citing rehires, referrals, local colleges, high-traffic pop-ups, and social media as core retail-recruiting channels).
- The worked scenario (the role profile that fixed a bad hiring streak) and the practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses created for this module, not externally sourced cases.
