# C13-M08 Quiz: Managing Underperformance

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. What is the standard progressive-discipline sequence this module describes?**
A) Termination is always the first and only step, regardless of the situation
B) Verbal warning, written warning, performance improvement plan (PIP)/final warning, and termination — worked through in order rather than jumping straight to termination
C) There is no standard sequence; discipline should always be entirely improvised
D) Only written warnings exist; verbal warnings and PIPs are never used

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This structured sequence is confirmed across an unusually large number of independent HR and employment-law sources.

---

**2. What is the "act early" principle in progressive discipline?**
A) Managers should wait as long as possible before addressing any performance issue
B) Address poor performance before it becomes a habit, giving the associate a genuine, fair opportunity to improve before the process escalates further
C) The first step should always be termination, with no earlier intervention
D) Acting early means skipping documentation entirely

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This principle is cited consistently across multiple progressive-discipline sources.

---

**3. What are the five components a performance improvement plan (PIP) typically contains?**
A) Salary details, vacation policy, dress code, parking assignment, and locker number
B) A clear expectations statement, a factual performance overview, SMART goals, a defined follow-up schedule, and stated consequences — typically over a 60-90 day timeframe
C) Only a list of the employee's past achievements, with no mention of the current issue
D) A PIP requires no specific components; any format is acceptable

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These five components are confirmed consistently across multiple independent HR sources.

---

**4. What specific documentation language does this module identify as failing to hold up, and what standard should replace it?**
A) Specific dates and behaviors are considered weak documentation; vague general impressions are preferred
B) Vague phrases like "bad attitude," "not a culture fit," or "needs to improve" with no detail fail to hold up; documentation should be detailed enough that a third party with no prior knowledge could understand exactly what happened
C) All documentation should focus exclusively on the employee's personality and character
D) Documentation should never include any specific examples

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is the direct, high-stakes extension of Module 7's behavior-not-personality principle.

---

**5. What must accompany every documented performance criticism, according to this module?**
A) Nothing; a criticism alone is sufficient documentation
B) Specific coaching on how to improve and a clear deadline by which the employer expects that improvement
C) Only a deadline, with no coaching guidance of any kind
D) A criticism should never include any timeline for improvement

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A document that only states what went wrong, without a path forward or a deadline, leaves the process looking punitive rather than genuinely corrective.

---

**6. Why does this module recommend genuine two-way communication and signed, dated acknowledgment at each formal disciplinary stage?**
A) These practices have no legal or practical value
B) Two-way communication confirms the associate had a real opportunity to respond, and signed acknowledgment confirms the conversation happened (not necessarily agreement) — both protect the associate and the store with a clear, fair record
C) Signatures should never be obtained on any disciplinary document
D) Only the manager's perspective should ever be documented, with no employee input

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A record showing only the manager's side of an exchange is weaker evidence than a record showing the associate had a genuine chance to respond.

---

**7. Why does this module include an explicit note on legal guidance rather than presenting its content as definitive legal advice?**
A) Employment law is identical everywhere and requires no jurisdictional consideration
B) Employment law varies significantly by jurisdiction, and specific legal requirements should be confirmed with qualified employment counsel or an HR professional before finalizing store-specific policy or acting on a termination decision
C) Legal considerations are entirely irrelevant to performance management
D) This module is intended as a substitute for professional legal counsel

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This module is explicitly framed as practical operations guidance, not legal advice.

---

**8. In the worked scenario, what specifically made the store's documentation protect both the associate and the store when a difficult final decision eventually arose?**
A) The documentation consisted entirely of vague character judgments
B) The documentation was specific, dated, behavior-focused (pulled from the evidence log), showed genuine coaching support offered throughout, and included the associate's signed acknowledgment of the conversation
C) No documentation existed at all; the decision was made entirely from memory
D) The associate was never given any opportunity to discuss the issue

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This gives both the associate and the store a clear, fair record of exactly what happened and what chances were given.
