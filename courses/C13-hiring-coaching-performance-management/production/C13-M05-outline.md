## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames ongoing floor coaching as the skill that turns onboarding's baseline competence into real strength, and the direct source of this course's assessment.
2. **The Core Coaching Loop: Observe, Debrief, Practice** — The three-part cycle and why the debrief must happen within roughly five minutes.
3. **What Makes a Debrief Actually Land: Observation, Impact, Alternative** — The three-part debrief structure and why the ordering matters.
4. **Self-Assessment First: Let Them Diagnose Their Own Interaction** — The "ask before telling" opening move and why it improves receptivity.
5. **The GROW Model: A Structure for the Bigger Conversation** — Goal, Reality, Options, Way Forward, distinct from the quick single-interaction debrief.
6. **A Five-Step Debrief Sequence for Structured Coaching Visits** — Self-assess, affirm, name one focus, commit, follow up.
7. **Watch for the Same Trait Cluster This Course Has Already Built** — Explicit continuity with Module 1's trait cluster and Module 3's interview screening.
8. **Putting the Vocabulary to Work** — The complete floor-coaching sequence.
9. **A Worked Scenario: The Debrief That Actually Changed Behavior** — Illustrative synthesis applying observation-impact-alternative to a real interaction.
10. **Practice Exercise** — Running a full five-minute debrief using the module's structure.
11. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The observation-impact-alternative three-part debrief structure is sourced directly and specifically from HubSpot's "Sales coaching: Examples, models, techniques & best practices" article, which explicitly names this exact structure with matching example language ("Observation: What specifically happened?... Impact: What did that behavior lead to?... " followed by an alternative), and explicitly recommends applying the same structure to role-play debriefs.
- The "ask the rep to self-assess before weighing in" opening move is sourced directly from the same HubSpot article.
- The GROW model (Goal, Reality, Options, Way Forward/Will) is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across numerous independent sources: HubSpot, mtdsalestraining.com's "5 Sales Coaching Models," Salesloft's "The Guide to Sales Coaching," johnnygrow.com's "The #1 Sales Coaching Model," mysalescoach.com's "Sales Coaching Techniques," investra.io's "Building A Coaching Culture," socoselling.com's "7 Popular Coaching Models," and Klozers' "How to use the GROW Sales Coaching Model" — an unusually large number of independent sources converging on the identical four-part structure, with only minor wording variation (Options vs. Obstacles/Options; Will vs. Way Forward).
- The specific "let the rep generate options before offering your own" and "one thing, agreed at the start" (for Goal) principles are sourced directly from HubSpot's and mysalescoach.com's articles, both stating this exact discipline.
- The five-step structured debrief sequence (self-assessment, affirm one specific strength, name one development focus, get a commitment, follow up at the next visit) is sourced directly and specifically from a detailed field-sales-coaching source (resources.rework.com's "Sales Capability and Coaching in FMCG"), which names this exact five-step sequence with a specific five-minute-maximum debrief timing, applied there to FMCG field sales visits; this module adapts this well-documented structured-coaching sequence to a jewelry retail floor-coaching context. The general practice of pairing a specific strength affirmation with exactly one development focus is independently corroborated by xenia.team's "Retail Sales Training" article ("Compliment something they did well... Then, offer one suggestion for improvement").
- This module's explicit tie connecting floor-coaching observation criteria back to Module 1's trait cluster and Module 3's interview screening is this module's own original synthesis, connecting the confirmed general coaching frameworks above to this course's own previously-established, independently-verified content; not itself an externally sourced claim.
- The worked scenario (the debrief that changed behavior around leading with discovery questions rather than specs) and the practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses created for this module, not externally sourced cases.
