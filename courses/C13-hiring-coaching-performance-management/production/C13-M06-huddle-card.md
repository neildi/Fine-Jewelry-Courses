# C13-M06 Huddle Card: Weekly One-on-Ones

## 5-Minute Pre-Shift Discussion

**The one idea to remember:** Let the associate set the agenda. A manager who always drives the conversation only hears about problems once they're already serious.

## The Cadence Question
Weekly, 30 minutes is the strong default — highest engagement across research. But adjust: new hires and anyone in active coaching benefit from weekly; a steady, experienced performer might do fine biweekly with a quick informal check-in between.

## They Set the Agenda
Ask them to send it in advance. Add your own topics separately. This alone surfaces problems weeks earlier than a manager-only agenda.

## Five Things a Good One-on-One Covers
1. **Priorities** — what actually matters this week
2. **Obstacles** — what's really blocking them (named specifically)
3. **Evidence/feedback** — pulling from recent floor observations
4. **Recognition** — specific, genuine, not generic
5. **Development** — the skill they're actively working on

## The 30-Minute Shape
5-10 min catch-up → 5-7 min priorities/obstacles → 5-7 min feedback both directions → 5-10 min next steps with NAMED owners

## Always Open With Last Week's Commitments
Did they follow through? Did you? A one-on-one that never revisits what was said teaches people it doesn't matter.

## Today's Discussion Prompt
Think about our last one-on-one with someone. Who actually set the agenda — us or them?
