# C13-M08 Fact-Check: Managing Underperformance

## Verification Status: The four-step progressive-discipline sequence and the documentation-quality principles are both confirmed across an unusually large number of independent HR and employment-law sources; this module explicitly notes it is operational guidance, not a substitute for qualified legal counsel, given genuine jurisdictional variation in employment law

## Claims Reviewed

**1. The four-step progressive-discipline sequence (verbal warning, written warning, PIP/final warning, termination).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across an unusually large number of independent HR and employment-law sources: Washoe County's "Performance Improvement Progressive Discipline" guide, Virginia Department of Health's "Progressive Discipline" guide, the Government of Canada's progressive-discipline guidance, RFSUNY's "Implementing the Progressive Discipline Policy" guide, MRSC's "A 4-Step Approach to Progressive Discipline," SHRM's "PIPs: Write, Implement and Time Them Precisely," HR Acuity's "Progressive Discipline Policy: A Guide for HR," and citationcanada.com's "Performance Improvement Plans vs. Progressive Discipline." SHRM notes some organizations use a five-step variant separating coaching/expectations-resetting as its own first step; this module's four-step framing (folding the coaching/expectations step into the verbal-warning stage) is a reasonable, common simplification consistent with most of the sources consulted.

**2. The "act early, before poor performance becomes a habit" principle.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from the Government of Canada's progressive-discipline guidance.

**3. The five PIP components (expectations statement, factual performance overview, SMART goals, follow-up schedule, stated consequences) and the 60-90 day typical timeframe.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sources: Washoe County's guide (explicitly citing a 60-90 day timeframe), Asana's "Performance improvement plan template" (naming these same five core components), Sage's "How to deliver a Performance Improvement Plan," and Atlassian's Confluence PIP template.

**4. The "specific behavior, never character" documentation principle, the named examples of failing documentation ("bad attitude," "not a culture fit," "needs to improve" with no detail), and the third-party-readability standard.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly and specifically from amundsendavislaw.com's "Documenting Employee Performance Problems" article (published by a labor and employment law firm), which names these exact examples and this exact readability standard, and independently corroborated by TechClass's "The Legal Side of Performance Reviews & Compliance" article, which describes a very similar standard.

**5. The "every criticism needs a coaching path and a deadline" pairing.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from the same amundsendavislaw.com article.

**6. The two-way-communication and signed-acknowledgment disciplines.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced from amundsendavislaw.com (two-way communication) and independently corroborated by TechClass's article, brightmine.com's "Performance improvement plan template" guidance, Atlassian's Confluence template, and Sage's PIP guide, all recommending employee sign-off on formal disciplinary documents.

**7. This module's explicit connections to Modules 5, 6, and 7 (coaching path, evidence log, SBI structure).**
Status: ORIGINAL synthesis created for this module, connecting confirmed general progressive-discipline and documentation frameworks to this course's own previously-established, independently-verified content. Not itself an externally sourced claim.

**8. The explicit legal-guidance disclaimer.**
Status: This module's own addition, included specifically because employment law varies significantly by jurisdiction (at-will employment rules, required notice periods, protected-class considerations) and this module's content should not be treated as a substitute for qualified legal counsel. This is standard, appropriate practice for content touching on employment-law-adjacent topics, consistent with this project's stated policy of never presenting legal, tax, credit, or appraisal guidance as certain.

**9. Worked scenario (the documentation that protected everyone at the written-warning stage) and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not externally sourced cases.
