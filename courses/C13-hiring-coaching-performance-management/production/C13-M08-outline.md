# C13-M08 Outline: Managing Underperformance

## Module Metadata
- Course: C13 - Hiring, Coaching & Performance Management in Jewelry Retail
- Module: 8 of 10
- Persona: P7 (First-Time Store Leader), P8 (Multi-Store Leader), per official course architecture
- Working title: Managing Underperformance
- Learning objective: Store leaders can apply the progressive-discipline sequence in order, build a performance improvement plan with its five required components, document underperformance using behavior-specific, legally-defensible language, pair every criticism with coaching and a deadline, and ensure genuine two-way communication and signed acknowledgment at each formal stage.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: Directly fulfills the C13 terminal outcome "manage underperformance within policy" per this project's official course architecture.

## Production Notes
- The four-step progressive-discipline sequence (verbal warning, written warning, PIP/final warning, termination) is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across an unusually large number of independent HR and employment-law sources: Washoe County's "Performance Improvement Progressive Discipline" guide, Virginia Department of Health's "Progressive Discipline" guide, the Government of Canada's progressive-discipline guidance, RFSUNY's "Implementing the Progressive Discipline Policy" guide, MRSC's "A 4-Step Approach to Progressive Discipline," SHRM's "PIPs: Write, Implement and Time Them Precisely," HR Acuity's "Progressive Discipline Policy: A Guide for HR," and citationcanada.com's "Performance Improvement Plans vs. Progressive Discipline" — all independently describing a closely matching four-step sequence (with SHRM noting some organizations use a five-step variant that separates coaching/expectations-resetting as its own first step before the verbal warning).
- The "act early, before poor performance becomes a habit" principle is sourced directly from the Government of Canada's progressive-discipline guidance.
- The five specific PIP components (expectations statement, performance overview, SMART goals, follow-up schedule, consequences) and the 60-90 day typical timeframe are CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sources: Washoe County's guide (explicitly citing 60-90 days), Asana's "Performance improvement plan template" (naming these same five core components), Sage's "How to deliver a Performance Improvement Plan," and Atlassian's Confluence PIP template.
- The "specific behavior, never character" documentation principle, the specific named examples of failing documentation ("bad attitude," "not a culture fit," "needs to improve" with no detail), and the third-party-readability standard are sourced directly and specifically from amundsendavislaw.com's "Documenting Employee Performance Problems" article (a labor and employment law firm's guidance), which names these exact examples and this exact readability standard, and independently corroborated by TechClass's "The Legal Side of Performance Reviews & Compliance" article, which describes a very similar standard ("avoid vague phrases like 'needs to improve' without context, detail which metrics weren't met or which behaviors are problematic").
- The "every criticism needs a coaching path and a deadline" pairing is sourced directly from the same amundsendavislaw.com article.
- The two-way-communication and signed-acknowledgment disciplines are sourced from amundsendavislaw.com (two-way communication) and independently corroborated by TechClass's article and brightmine.com's "Performance improvement plan template" guidance (signed and dated acknowledgment), Atlassian's Confluence template, and Sage's PIP guide, all recommending employee sign-off on formal disciplinary documents.
- This module's explicit connections to Module 5 (coaching path), Module 6, and Module 7 (evidence log, SBI structure) are this module's own original synthesis, connecting confirmed general progressive-discipline and documentation frameworks to this course's own previously-established content; not itself an externally sourced claim.
- The explicit legal-guidance disclaimer is this module's own addition, added specifically because employment law varies significantly by jurisdiction and this module's content should not be read as a substitute for qualified legal counsel; this is standard practice for content touching on employment-law-adjacent topics and is not itself a claim requiring source verification.
- The worked scenario (the documentation that protected everyone at the written-warning stage) and the practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses created for this module, not externally sourced cases.
