# C13-M03 Outline: Structured Interviews and Auditions

## Module Metadata
- Course: C13 - Hiring, Coaching & Performance Management in Jewelry Retail
- Module: 3 of 10
- Persona: P7 (First-Time Store Leader), P8 (Multi-Store Leader), per official course architecture
- Working title: Structured Interviews and Auditions
- Learning objective: Store leaders can build a structured interview guide directly from a Module 2 role profile, apply the STAR method to behavioral questions, design and score a sales role-play audition, build and apply a behaviorally anchored rating scale (BARS), and calibrate multiple interviewers before live interviews begin.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: Directly fulfills the "interview guide" half of the C13 terminal outcome "write a role profile and interview guide" and the separate terminal outcome "run structured interviews," per this project's official course architecture, and directly feeds the course's stated assessment (an interview guide).

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames this module as the direct payoff of Module 2's evidence-attached must-have competencies and Module 1's structured-interview research finding.
2. **What Makes an Interview "Structured"** — The build sequence: job analysis, targeted questions, standardized rubrics, interviewer training.
3. **The STAR Method for Behavioral Questions** — Situation, Task, Action, Result, plus the STAR-plus extension (lesson learned, tie-back to role).
4. **Building Questions From Module 2's Must-Have List** — Worked example mapping specific must-haves to specific STAR-format questions.
5. **The Sales Role-Play: A Live Audition, Not a Trick Question** — Structure, advance briefing, and what a manager watches for.
6. **Behaviorally Anchored Rating Scales: Making Scoring Actually Consistent** — The BARS technique and why it solves cross-rater scoring drift.
7. **Calibrating Raters Before Live Interviews Begin** — The exemplar-scoring and norming-meeting practice.
8. **Putting the Vocabulary to Work** — The complete interview-guide-building sequence.
9. **A Worked Scenario: The Rubric That Caught What the Conversation Missed** — Illustrative synthesis showing a BARS-scored role-play catching a gap a warm verbal interview missed.
10. **Practice Exercise** — Writing STAR questions and a BARS rubric from a real role profile.
11. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The structured-interview build sequence (job analysis identifying KSAOs, targeted question sets mixing behavioral/situational/competency-based questions, standardized scoring rubrics, thorough interviewer training, consistent multi-location protocols) is sourced directly and specifically from questsearch.co.uk's "How can structured interviews improve hiring outcomes in retail?" article, which names this exact five-step sequence.
- The STAR method definition and its application to retail/sales behavioral interview questions are CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across numerous independent sources: loopcv.pro's "Retail Interview Questions & Example Answers," careershifu.ai's "Retail Interview Tips," Apollo.io's "What Is a Sales Interview?," inspireambitions.com's "What Questions Are Asked in a Retail Job Interview," and LinkedIn's "Ace Retail Sales Interviews with Behavioral Question Tips" — all independently describing the same Situation/Task/Action/Result structure.
- The STAR-plus extension (adding a "lesson learned" and "tie back to role" element) is sourced directly from rockstarcv.com's "Retail Associate Interview: Ace Behavioral Questions" article, which explicitly names and demonstrates this extended framework with worked examples.
- This module's specific worked mapping of Module 2's must-have competencies (product passion, listening/restraint, persistence without pushiness) to specific STAR-format questions is this module's own original synthesis, connecting the confirmed general STAR framework to this course's own previously-established, independently-verified Module 1 and Module 2 content; not itself an externally sourced claim.
- The sales role-play/audition structure (advance briefing, specific goal and time limit, dedicated rater scoring against a rubric, watching for discovery questions, tailored pitching, and objection handling) is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sources: bluselection.com's "How to Succeed The Sales Role-Play Exercise In A Job Interview" (naming the discovery-pitching-objection-handling-closing structure and the "listen more than you speak" principle) and beefed.ai's "High-Impact Sales Role-Play Scenarios" (detailed operational guidance on briefing timing, rater setup, and scoring).
- The behaviorally anchored rating scale (BARS) technique, including the specific "6-8 core competencies," "1-5 scale," and "one observable behavior per anchor" guidance, is sourced directly from beefed.ai's "High-Impact Sales Role-Play Scenarios" article, a detailed, operationally specific source on this exact topic.
- The rater-calibration practice (training raters on recorded exemplars representing low/medium/high performance, holding a norming meeting to resolve scoring differences greater than one point by referencing anchor language, using at least two independent raters for higher-stakes stages) is sourced directly from the same beefed.ai article, which provides this exact operational sequence.
- The panel-interview/multiple-rater bias-reduction practice, and the specific 5-point/3-point/1-point (superior/satisfactory/unsatisfactory) benchmark-scoring approach, are independently corroborated by trisearch.com's "Retail Recruiting Best Practices" article, which cites a similar rating-scale and panel-interview practice from a separate source lineage than the beefed.ai article, strengthening confidence in these general practices beyond a single source.
- The worked scenario (the BARS rubric catching a gap the warm verbal interview missed) and the practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses created for this module, not externally sourced cases.
