# C9-M05 Fact-Check: Running the Room: Trunk Show and Pop-Up Event Logistics

## Verification Status: Security practices unusually well-corroborated across industry and law-enforcement sources; staffing benchmark from a single directly-on-point trunk-show planning source; vendor-terms content drawn from a mix of jewelry-specific informal and non-jewelry formal sources

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Trunk shows represent genuine, elevated security risk, explicitly warned about by the Jewelers' Security Alliance (JSA).**
Status: Sourced directly from JCK Online's "Hosting a Trunk Show? Here's How to Stay Safe" article, which explicitly quotes JSA's warning that trunk show season is "a magnet for criminals," and includes specific, named security-expert guidance (attributed to "Guginsky") on staff vigilance, designer-inventory tracking devices (e.g., Apple AirTags), and advance police notification. This is a strong, directly-on-point, named-source trade-press article specifically focused on trunk show security.

**2. Core event security practices (single-item display with rest secured in locked cases; two-person minimum specifically during setup/teardown as the most vulnerable windows; documented multi-point inventory counts; secure/armored transport for unsold merchandise).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent, directly-on-point sources: Jewelers Mutual's "Jewelry trade show security" guide (explicit sections on staff preparedness, booth security — "assign at least two staff members during setup, teardown and show hours" — and inventory management with multi-point counts), Jewelers Mutual's "6 Tips for Hiring a Security Guard for Special Events" article, The Tradeshow Network's "Trade Show Safety Tips" (independently confirming single-item display, locked showcases, and the alert-phrase practice), and the Beverly Police Department's official "Robbery Prevention for Jewelry Stores" PDF (a law-enforcement source explicitly recommending off-duty police officers as security during trunk shows/special events, and the two-person-minimum principle generally). This is an unusually well-corroborated set of practices spanning industry-specific insurer guidance, trade press, and law enforcement.

**3. The predetermined staff alert-phrase practice.**
Status: Sourced directly from The Tradeshow Network's "Trade Show Safety Tips" article ("make sure all employees know a predetermined phrase that alerts them to a potential threat without alarming customers").

**4. Staffing benchmark (30-40 minutes per full client conversation; a single consultation station handling roughly 12-16 clients across a full event day).**
Status: Sourced directly from Sartorly's "The Complete Guide to Running a Profitable Trunk Show" article, a directly-on-point trunk-show planning guide ("Total time per client: 30-40 minutes. With two consultation stations, you can see 12-16 clients in a full day" — figure recalculated here for a single station). This is a single-source figure, presented in this module as a useful planning benchmark rather than a universal constant, since actual conversation length will vary by store, event type, and price point.

**5. Vendor-terms content (minimum purchase commitments vs. consignment-style splits, commonly structured so the store retains retail markup while the designer receives wholesale pricing on sold items).**
Status: Synthesized from a mix of sources with varying direct relevance: trunkshowconsignment.com's consignment policy and ByAnnie.com's trunk show terms and conditions (both from non-jewelry retail contexts — general consignment retail and quilting/craft trunk shows, respectively — used only to illustrate the general commercial-structure concepts of consignment splits and minimum-order commitments) and a Ganoksin trade forum discussion (jewelry-specific, but an informal practitioner forum post rather than a formal industry source, describing a typical arrangement where "you would get the wholesale price and they would get the retail portion"). IMPORTANT SOURCING NOTE: this is the weakest-sourced claim in this module, combining non-jewelry formal sources with a jewelry-specific but informal source, and should be verified against the store's own actual vendor agreements before being presented as definitive industry practice.

**6. Setup/day-of sequence guidance (arriving at least two hours before doors open; inventory verification against a vendor's manifest).**
Status: The two-hour arrival recommendation is sourced from Tashvi.ai's "Jewelry Trunk Shows Complete Planning Guide" ("arrive at least two hours before the event opens"). The specific team-briefing-contents recommendation (security plan, vendor terms, staffing assignments, inventory notes) is this module's own original synthesis, combining the security and vendor-terms content established earlier in this module rather than being drawn from a single external checklist.

**7. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from any external source.
