# C9-M07 Quiz: When It Doesn't Close On the Spot: Fast, Honest Follow-Up

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. According to this module, how should an unclosed compressed-time conversation, handled well through discovery and a proper close attempt, be understood?**
A) As proof that the associate's technique failed and should be entirely reworked
B) As a normal outcome that this module exists to handle, not necessarily a failure of the discovery, trust-building, or closing techniques from earlier modules
C) As a signal that the client was never a real prospect and should be forgotten entirely
D) As something that should never happen if earlier modules' techniques are applied correctly

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A client genuinely needing to think something over is a normal, expected outcome, and follow-up is the next real opportunity rather than evidence of a mistake.

**2. What does general sales-response-time research (developed primarily in B2B contexts, and treated in this module as a directional finding) suggest about the effect of follow-up delay on conversion odds?**
A) Follow-up timing has no meaningful effect on the odds of ever converting a lead
B) There is a steep drop-off in the likelihood of converting or even reaching a lead as follow-up time increases, with leads contacted within five minutes commonly found to be around 21 times more likely to qualify than those contacted after thirty minutes
C) Waiting longer to follow up always improves the odds of a successful outcome
D) Follow-up delay only matters for online leads, never for in-person compressed-time events

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: While the specific multipliers come from general sales research rather than jewelry-specific studies, the underlying pattern of rapid decay in conversion odds is presented as directly applicable to compressed-time events.

**3. What is the practical translation of the speed-to-lead finding for a compressed-time event specifically?**
A) Follow-up should be delayed by at least a week to avoid seeming too eager
B) A same-day or next-morning follow-up for anyone who showed real interest but didn't close is the single highest-leverage action available once the conversation has ended
C) Follow-up is unnecessary if a client didn't close on the spot
D) Follow-up should only happen if the client explicitly requests it

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is the module's central practical recommendation, directly derived from the speed-to-lead research pattern.

**4. What does this module recommend as a realistic follow-up cadence, rather than a single attempt?**
A) A single follow-up attempt immediately, with no further contact regardless of response
B) A multi-touch cadence: an initial personal touch within 24 hours, a value-add touch a few days later, and a final low-pressure check-in one to two weeks out
C) Following up as many times as possible within the first hour, then never again
D) Waiting exactly 30 days before any follow-up attempt

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This structured cadence reflects the persistence finding that most eventual sales require multiple honest touches, not one attempt.

**5. What distinguishes honest follow-up from follow-up that feels pushy or manufactured, according to this module?**
A) Honest follow-up always includes an artificial deadline to motivate a quick decision
B) Honest follow-up references the actual conversation, offers genuine value at each touch, states any real constraint plainly (rather than inventing one), and explicitly makes room for the client to say no
C) Honest follow-up should never mention the specific piece or conversation discussed
D) There is no meaningful difference between honest and pushy follow-up; all follow-up is equally likely to be perceived as pressure

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These specific features directly extend Module 1's genuine-urgency principle and Module 3's genuine-attention principle into the follow-up context.

**6. Why does this module argue that a client who already declined to close on the spot is specifically primed to notice pressure in a follow-up message?**
A) Clients who declined once are never interested in hearing from the associate again
B) Having already made one decision not to close, this client is likely to be especially alert to language that tries to push them toward reversing that decision through manufactured urgency
C) This claim has no real basis and follow-up pressure is never noticed by clients
D) Pressure is only ever a concern during the original conversation, never in follow-up

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is why honest, low-pressure follow-up matters even more in this context than in the original conversation.

**7. In the worked scenario, what specifically made the associate's next-morning follow-up message effective?**
A) The message included a fake deadline to pressure the client into deciding quickly
B) The message referenced the actual conversation, delivered real value (the answer to her specific question), and explicitly removed pressure rather than adding it
C) The message was sent exactly one month after the original conversation
D) The message was identical to a generic follow-up template with no personalization

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This combination — speed, genuine reference, real value, and explicit pressure removal — is exactly what this module recommends and what led to the eventual close.
