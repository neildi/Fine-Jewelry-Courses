2. **The Itinerary Is the Deadline, and It's a Real One** — The ship's schedule as the genuine constraint; the "sales pitches getting more desperate" pattern as the manufactured-pressure mistake layered on top of a real constraint.
3. **The Seminar-to-Showroom Model** — The core mechanic; done well vs. done poorly (the "free bracelet" hook contrasted with substantive gemological education); direct tie to Module 3's warmth-before-competence sequencing.
4. **What the Showroom Conversation Should Actually Look Like** — Direct application of Modules 2-4; genuine comfort with independent price-checking as this format's specific addition.
5. **Handling the Skepticism Honestly** — Addressing documented, often-rational passenger skepticism directly; independent certification and cost/duty transparency as credibility-building moves.
6. **A Worked Scenario: The Seminar That Didn't Oversell** — Illustrative synthesis showing a substantive seminar and a showroom follow-up that stays consistent under a real skepticism test (the phone price-check).
7. **Practice Exercise** — Drafting a substantive seminar opening and practicing the phone-price-check response.
8. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The claim that onboard/cruise jewelry retail has a well-documented reputation problem specifically involving high-pressure tactics is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent consumer travel-press sources: The Points Guy's "Cruise port scams and schemes" article (explicitly describing free-bracelet hooks designed to "subject you to a high-pressure sales routine"), Cruise Critic's "What to Expect on a Cruise: Shopping on Cruise Ships" article (explicitly noting sales pitches get "more desperate as the cruise comes to a close"), the International Gem Society's "Should You Buy Jewelry on a Cruise?" article (explicitly noting onboard/port jewelers "know you're a captive audience"), and dealradar.com's "Cruise Port Scams" article (describing the "free gift" jewelry-pull pattern and recommending independent, non-seller-affiliated certification). This is strong, multi-source, independently corroborated evidence of a real and specifically documented industry reputation problem, which this module uses as direct motivation for its ethical-practice recommendations.
- The onboard seminar-to-showroom conversion mechanic and its KPI status were already established and fact-checked in this course's Module 1 (sourced from the project's own primary research file, the Global Fine Jewelry Retail Career, Operations, and Competency Framework document); this module builds directly on that previously-verified foundation rather than re-establishing it as a new claim.
- The specific recommendation to recommend independent (non-seller-affiliated) certification and to research/compare prices independently is sourced from GIA's own 4Cs consumer-education blog ("Cruise Ship Shopping: Tips for Buying Gems and Jewelry" — a GIA-authored source specifically written to help consumers protect themselves, directly relevant since it represents the exact kind of consumer guidance an ethical onboard specialist should be comfortable with a client following) and the International Gem Society's article (recommending consulting the IGS Gem Price Guide and taking photos rather than buying under pressure).
- The observation that "duty-free" is a commonly misunderstood term (not an automatic discount, since local VAT avoidance doesn't guarantee a lower price than home-market pricing) is sourced from multiple independent consumer travel-press sources (Cruise Critic's "Duty-Free Shopping on Cruises" and "What to Expect on a Cruise" articles, Cruiseline.com's duty-free guide, and dealradar.com's port-scams article), all making this same point independently.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
