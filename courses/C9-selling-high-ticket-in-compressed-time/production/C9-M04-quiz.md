# C9-M04 Quiz: The Decision Moment: Closing Without Pressure

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. What is the key distinction between a trial close and an actual close?**
A) There is no real distinction; they are the same technique with different names
B) A trial close asks for an opinion (e.g., "how does that feel?"), while an actual close asks for a decision (e.g., "would you like to move forward?")
C) A trial close should only ever be used once, at the very end of a conversation
D) A trial close always involves offering a discount, while an actual close never does

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This distinction is central to using trial closes throughout a conversation without prematurely forcing a decision the client isn't ready for.

---

**2. Why does this module recommend using trial closes throughout a compressed conversation rather than saving all closing questions for one final moment?**
A) Trial closes should never be used before the very end of a conversation
B) Trial closes let an associate read where a client actually stands well before time runs out, so the final ask is informed by real signals rather than a guess
C) Using trial closes throughout a conversation always slows it down unnecessarily
D) Trial closes and final closes should never be used in the same conversation

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A series of honest, low-pressure check-ins throughout the conversation builds toward a final ask that's grounded in actual reads of the client's interest.

---

**3. What distinguishes a confident, direct close from manufactured pressure, according to this module?**
A) There is no real difference; any closing question is inherently pressure
B) A confident close states plainly that a decision point has arrived and asks calmly; pressure adds urgency that isn't real or repeats the ask in a way that makes the client feel cornered
C) A confident close should always include a fake deadline to motivate the client
D) Directness is always the same thing as pressure, regardless of how it's delivered

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Directness and pressure are not the same thing — clarity and calm confidence are what distinguish a legitimate close from a pressure tactic.

---

**4. What is the purpose of briefly stepping back after addressing a client's final questions or objections, as described in this module's luxury-retail-specific guidance?**
A) Stepping back signals disinterest and should always be avoided
B) It creates a brief moment of unobserved space that some clients genuinely need to process a high-value decision, even within a compressed time window
C) Stepping back should only be used to avoid answering a difficult question
D) This practice has no real effect on how a client experiences the decision moment

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This deliberate, brief pause respects how some clients process large decisions, and signals confidence rather than anxiety about the outcome.

---

**5. Why does setting a validated piece aside (at a register or designated spot) while continuing to show other options help a client under time pressure?**
A) It has no real effect on the client's decision-making process
B) It reduces the cognitive load of comparing multiple pieces, keeping the client's attention on what's still genuinely in play rather than mentally re-litigating everything already shown
C) It signals to the client that the piece has already been sold to someone else
D) This practice should only be used when there is no time pressure at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This small logistical habit measurably reduces the mental burden of a decision, which matters even more under compressed-time conditions.

---

**6. According to this module's price-conversation guidance, what is the recommended way to state a price?**
A) Apologize for the price before stating the number, to soften the impact
B) State the price in one clean, calm sentence, then ask a next-step question, rather than hedging or apologizing
C) Avoid stating the exact price for as long as possible
D) Always state the price with a nervous laugh to ease tension

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Apologizing, hedging, or rushing past the price all signal, even unintentionally, that the number is a problem, when in a genuine luxury context it's simply a fact to be discussed plainly.

---

**7. What should happen immediately after an associate asks a genuine, direct closing question?**
A) The associate should immediately continue talking to reinforce the value of the piece
B) The associate should stop talking and let the silence sit, giving the client room to respond rather than second-guess
C) The associate should repeat the question multiple times in quick succession
D) The associate should change the subject to reduce tension

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Continuing to talk after a direct closing question is one of the most common ways associates undercut their own close; silence signals calm confidence rather than nervousness.
