# C9-M01 Fact-Check: Why Compressed-Time Selling Is Different

## Verification Status: Urgency-vs-pressure distinction confirmed across two independent high-ticket-sales sources; format descriptions sourced from a mix of trade press and the project's own prior primary research file

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Compressed-time formats (trunk shows, onboard cruise seminars, pop-up/VIP events) as distinct from standard multi-touch clienteling.**
Status: This framing distinction is this module's own original conceptual synthesis, built to contrast against the project's already-established C3 (Clienteling & CRM) content. The individual formats themselves are real and independently documented (see items 2-3 below).

**2. Trunk show event-length norms (commonly single-day/few-hours; sometimes 2-3 days).**
Status: Sourced directly from JCK Online's "Trunk Show Savvy" trade-press article, which quotes named jewelers' actual practices (John Dockery of John Dockery Jewelers: "hold trunk shows for four hours on a single day"), and from Elizabeth Anne Bonanno's trunk-show guide via JA New York (recommending a 2-3 day format as an alternative approach). These are two independent, named-source, real-world descriptions of actual trunk show practice, showing genuine variation in event length across the industry.

**3. Onboard cruise retail seminar-to-showroom conversion as a named KPI.**
Status: Sourced directly from this project's own prior primary research file, the Global Fine Jewelry Retail Career, Operations, and Competency Framework document, which explicitly lists "Seminar-to-showroom conversion rate" as a primary KPI for the "Onboard Fine Jewelry Specialist" role profile. This is a previously-established, internally-sourced fact carried forward into this new course, not a new external claim requiring independent web verification in this pass.

**4. The distinction between genuine/authentic urgency (grounded in a real, specific constraint) and manufactured/fake pressure.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across two independent sources specifically discussing high-ticket sales: bestbuyincentives.com's "Urgency and Objection Handling in High-Ticket Sales" article (explicitly framing authentic urgency around "the cost of delay, decision dates, real constraints, and the buyer's next step") and Forbes' "5 Ways To Convert High-Ticket Sales By Playing Hard To Get" article (explicitly distinguishing "real scarcity that makes sense" from "fake deadlines or manufactured pressure"). Both sources independently make the same core distinction this module relies on, giving it solid cross-source support.

**5. The claim that sophisticated luxury buyers recognize and are put off by manufactured pressure tactics specifically.**
Status: This specific claim about luxury-context sensitivity to pressure tactics is a reasoned extension consistent with the general luxury-sales-psychology principles already established in this project's Fine-Jewelry-Career-Guide.md research (which emphasizes that "pressure-driven sales tactics undermine trust in luxury environments" for HNWI clients). It is not independently re-verified via new web research in this pass, since it draws on the project's own already-established research base.

**6. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from any external source, designed specifically to demonstrate the honest-constraint-vs-manufactured-pressure distinction established by items 4-5 above.
