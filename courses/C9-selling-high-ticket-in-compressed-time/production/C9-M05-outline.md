# C9-M05 Outline: Running the Room: Trunk Show and Pop-Up Event Logistics

## Module Metadata
- Course: C9 - Selling High-Ticket in Compressed Time
- Module: 5 of 8
- Persona: P2
- Working title: Running the Room: Trunk Show and Pop-Up Event Logistics
- Learning objective: Associates and managers can apply core event security practices (single-item display, two-person minimums during vulnerable windows, documented inventory counts, alert phrases), staff an event using a realistic per-conversation time benchmark, understand common vendor/consignment terms and their floor-level implications, and run an adequate pre-event setup and briefing sequence.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Manage the logistics and staffing specific to compressed-time selling events (trunk shows, pop-ups, private viewings)."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames this module as the operational layer underneath the individual sales conversations covered in Modules 2-4.
2. **Security: The Risk Is Real and Well-Documented** — JSA's explicit warning; core security practices (single-item display, two-person minimums, documented counts, designer-inventory handling, alert phrase, dedicated event security).
3. **Staffing: Matching People to the Actual Conversation Load** — The 30-40-minute-per-conversation benchmark and its staffing implications; the compounding cost of understaffing on Modules 2-3's techniques.
4. **Vendor Terms: Know What's Actually Being Agreed To** — Minimum purchase commitments vs. consignment splits; why every associate needs to understand the terms in effect.
5. **Setup and the Day-Of Sequence** — Arrival timing, inventory verification, team briefing contents.
6. **A Worked Scenario: The Morning Before a Trunk Show** — Illustrative synthesis combining security, staffing, and vendor-terms practices into a single pre-event morning.
7. **Practice Exercise** — Drafting a one-page day-of event plan.
8. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The claim that trunk shows represent genuine, elevated security risk (not hypothetical) is sourced directly from JCK Online's "Hosting a Trunk Show? Here's How to Stay Safe" article, which explicitly quotes the Jewelers' Security Alliance (JSA) warning that trunk show season is "a magnet for criminals," and includes specific, named security-expert guidance (Guginsky) on staff vigilance, designer-inventory tracking (e.g., Apple AirTags), and advance police notification. This is a strong, directly-on-point, named-source trade-press article specifically about trunk show security.
- The core event security practices (single-item display; locked-case storage for remaining inventory; two-person minimum during setup/teardown specifically as the most vulnerable windows; documented multi-point inventory counts; secure transport/armored services for unsold merchandise) are CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent, directly-on-point sources: Jewelers Mutual's "Jewelry trade show security" guide (explicit staff/booth/inventory-management sections) and "6 Tips for Hiring a Security Guard for Special Events" article, The Tradeshow Network's "Trade Show Safety Tips" (explicitly including the predetermined alert-phrase practice), and the Beverly Police Department's official "Robbery Prevention for Jewelry Stores" PDF guide (a law-enforcement source explicitly recommending off-duty police officers as security during trunk shows/special events). This is an unusually well-corroborated set of practices from both industry-specific (Jewelers Mutual, JCK) and law-enforcement sources.
- The predetermined staff alert-phrase practice is sourced directly from The Tradeshow Network's "Trade Show Safety Tips" article.
- The staffing benchmark (30-40 minutes per full client conversation; a single consultation station handling roughly 12-16 clients across a full event day) is sourced directly from Sartorly's "The Complete Guide to Running a Profitable Trunk Show" article, a directly-on-point trunk-show planning guide. This single-source figure is presented as a useful planning benchmark, not a universal constant, since actual conversation length will vary by store and event type.
- The vendor-terms content (minimum purchase commitments; consignment-style splits, commonly structured so the store retains retail markup while the designer receives wholesale pricing on sold items) is synthesized from general trunk-show/consignment-terms sources (trunkshowconsignment.com's consignment policy, ByAnnie.com's trunk show terms and conditions, and a Ganoksin trade forum discussion of typical wholesale/retail trunk-show splits between designers and stores). IMPORTANT NOTE: the specific consignment-policy and ByAnnie.com sources are from non-jewelry retail contexts (general consignment and quilting/craft trunk shows respectively); the underlying commercial-structure concepts (minimum purchase vs. consignment split) are presented here as a reasoned, generalized description of common trunk-show commercial structures, adapted to the jewelry-specific context via the Ganoksin trade-forum discussion, which is jewelry-specific but informal (a practitioner forum post, not a formal industry source).
- The setup/day-of-sequence guidance (arriving at least two hours before doors open; inventory verification against a vendor's manifest; team briefing contents) is sourced from Tashvi.ai's "Jewelry Trunk Shows Complete Planning Guide" (recommending arrival "at least two hours before the event") and synthesized with the security and vendor-terms content established earlier in this module to build the team-briefing-contents recommendation, which is this module's own original synthesis rather than a directly sourced checklist.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.

- Quiz items should test: correctly identifying the core event security practices and why setup/teardown are the most vulnerable windows, applying the staffing benchmark to a hypothetical attendance figure, distinguishing minimum purchase commitments from consignment splits and their floor-level implications, and correctly sequencing a day-of setup and briefing.

- Factcheck should note that security practices are unusually well-corroborated across industry and law-enforcement sources, while the vendor-terms content draws on a mix of jewelry-specific informal (trade forum) and non-jewelry formal sources, and should be flagged for confirmation against the store's actual vendor agreements.
