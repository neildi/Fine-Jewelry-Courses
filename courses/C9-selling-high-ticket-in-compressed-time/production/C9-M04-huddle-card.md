# C9-M04 Huddle Card: The Decision Moment

## 5-Minute Pre-Shift Discussion

**The one idea to remember:** Trial closes throughout, one clean direct close at the end, then STOP TALKING. Directness is not the same thing as pressure.

## Trial Close vs. Actual Close
- Trial close = asking for an OPINION: "How does that feel on your hand?"
- Actual close = asking for a DECISION: "Would you like to take this home today?"
- Use trial closes throughout the conversation — don't save everything for one moment at the end.

## Give Them Space
After you've answered the last question, step back for a moment (check something at the counter, step aside). Some clients need a beat of quiet to decide. Come back, ask calmly: "Would you like to go ahead?"

## Reduce the Clutter
Once a piece is a real contender, set it aside at the register while you keep showing others. Keeps their head clear — comparing what's still in play, not re-deciding on everything they've already seen.

## Price: Say It Plainly
"This one is $7,950 — how does that feel against what you had in mind?" No apology, no hedge, no nervous laugh. If budget comes up: validate it, ask what range feels right, show what changes at each step. Calm, collaborative, not defensive.

## After You Ask — STOP TALKING
Ask the direct closing question. Then silence. Let them fill it. This is the hardest habit to build and the easiest one to blow.

## Today's Discussion Prompt
Practice asking a direct closing question out loud, then count five full seconds of silence before saying anything else. Notice how long five seconds actually feels.
