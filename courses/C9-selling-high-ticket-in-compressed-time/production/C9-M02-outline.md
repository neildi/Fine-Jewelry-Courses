# C9-M02 Outline: Compressed Discovery: Surfacing Priorities in Minutes, Not Weeks

## Module Metadata
- Course: C9 - Selling High-Ticket in Compressed Time
- Module: 2 of 8
- Persona: P1
- Working title: Compressed Discovery: Surfacing Priorities in Minutes, Not Weeks
- Learning objective: Associates can run a layered discovery sequence (situation, gap, impact, vision) within a self-imposed time budget, read body-language/tone signals to know when to slow down on a layer, and confirm understanding via paraphrase before presenting pieces.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Run a compressed discovery conversation that surfaces a client's real priorities in minutes without becoming shallow."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames this module as the direct technique answer to Module 1's "discovery must compress without becoming shallow" preview.
2. **The Real Failure Mode: Rushing to a Pitch, Not Slow Discovery** — Jewelry-specific needs-assessment logic (assessment produces answers; demonstrate the answers, not the reverse).
3. **A Layered Structure That Fits in Minutes** — Situation/gap/impact/vision sequence, adapted from general consultative-selling frameworks to jewelry.
4. **The Time-Boxing Discipline** — Rough internal time budget per discovery phase within a compressed conversation.
5. **Reading the Room, Not Just Listening to the Answers** — Body language/tone signals as a compressed-format-specific discovery input.
6. **Confirm Before You Present** — The paraphrase-back discipline before moving to presentation.
7. **A Worked Scenario: Ninety Seconds That Changed the Presentation** — Illustrative synthesis showing how a brief but real discovery layer changes what gets shown.
8. **Practice Exercise** — Timed four-layer discovery roleplay with body-language debrief.
9. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The jewelry-specific needs-assessment logic ("needs assessment equals answers, so demonstrate the answers" — i.e., discovery must precede and inform presentation, not follow it) is sourced directly from Southern Jewelry News's "The Needs Assessment: Open-ended questions" trade-press article, a jewelry-sales-specific source directly on point for this module's core claim.
- The layered situation/gap/impact/vision discovery sequence is adapted from general (primarily B2B) consultative-selling research — prospeo.io's "Consultative Selling Questions" and "Open-Ended Questions for Sales" articles (describing a SPIN-style situation/problem/implication/need-payoff sequence and a staged rapport→situation→problem→impact→vision→commitment arc) and tomba.io's "Consultative Selling Questions" article (explicitly warning against skipping layers). IMPORTANT NOTE: these sources are written for B2B sales contexts, not jewelry retail specifically — this module adapts the underlying layered-questioning logic to a jewelry sales floor context, which is a reasoned domain adaptation, not a jewelry-specific finding.
- The time-boxing discipline for a short discovery conversation is adapted from Skipcall's "30 Cold Call Discovery Questions to Qualify in 5 Min" article, which provides a specific minute-by-minute phase breakdown for a 5-minute cold call (opening/permission, context, pain, impact/cost, timing, next step). This is also a B2B-sales-context source (cold calling specifically), explicitly adapted here to a compressed jewelry sales conversation rather than presented as jewelry-specific research.
- The body-language/tone-reading content (posture, pace of speech, restlessness vs. ease as signals of trust or guardedness) is sourced from themindreader.ai's "How Relationship Selling Drives High-Ticket Luxury Deals" article, which is specifically written about luxury sales contexts and buyer-cue reading, making it a more directly on-point source for this module's specific persona and context than the general B2B discovery sources above.
- The paraphrase-back-before-presenting discipline is sourced from tomba.io's consultative-selling article ("Summarize before you pitch") and LinkedIn's discovery-questions article (emphasizing ongoing re-validation of priorities); both are B2B-context sources, adapted here to the jewelry compressed-time context.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
