# C9-M07 Fact-Check: When It Doesn't Close On the Spot: Fast, Honest Follow-Up

## Verification Status: Nearly all quantitative claims (speed-to-lead statistics, follow-up cadence timing, persistence figures) drawn from general (non-jewelry) sales-response research and explicitly flagged as directional; core urgency/genuine-attention principles are direct, previously-verified extensions of this course's own earlier modules

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Speed-to-lead response-time statistics (leads contacted within 5 minutes commonly found to be roughly 21x more likely to qualify than those contacted after 30 minutes; sharp qualification-odds decline as delay increases to 24+ hours).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across an unusually large number of independent sources, most explicitly tracing back to the same foundational MIT/InsideSales.com Lead Response Management study and a Harvard Business Review 2011 study: Verse.ai's "25 Eye-Opening Speed to Lead Statistics," LeadAngel's speed-to-lead statistics article, ZoomInfo's "Sales Follow Up Statistics" (citing the HBR 2011 study directly), Amplemarket's speed-to-lead article, farias.co's post-event marketing strategy article, and resources.rework.com's detailed response-time drop-off curve. IMPORTANT SOURCING NOTE: virtually all of these sources are general B2B/marketing sales-response-time research, not jewelry- or event-retail-specific. This module explicitly and repeatedly flags this as a directional finding adapted to the jewelry compressed-time-event context rather than a jewelry-specific statistic, given the reasonable likelihood that exact multipliers vary meaningfully by industry, price point, and context.

**2. Multi-touch post-event follow-up cadence (24-hour initial touch; 3-5-day value-add touch; 1-2-week final low-pressure touch).**
Status: Adapted from momencio.com's "10 post-event follow-up emails to convert leads into sales" article (explicitly recommending a within-24-to-48-hour first touch, a 3-5-day second touch, and a 1-2-week final touch) and farias.co's post-event marketing strategy article (a similarly staged day-by-day cadence). Both sources are general event-marketing sources, not jewelry-specific; this module adapts their staged-cadence structure to a compressed-time jewelry sales-event context as a reasoned application.

**3. Persistence finding (a large share of eventual sales require 5+ contacts, while a substantial share of salespeople give up after one attempt).**
Status: Sourced from ZoomInfo's "Sales Follow Up Statistics" article ("80% of deals need 5+ contacts, yet 44% of reps quit after one attempt"). This is general sales research, not jewelry-specific, and is presented in this module as a directional finding on the value of persistence rather than a precise, industry-specific figure that should be quoted to clients or used as a rigid internal target.

**4. Application of Module 1's genuine-urgency-vs-manufactured-pressure distinction and Module 3's genuine-vs-generic-attention principle to the follow-up context.**
Status: This is this module's own direct extension of two already-established, previously fact-checked C9 principles (see C9-M01 and C9-M03 factchecks) into the specific follow-up context, rather than a new externally-sourced claim requiring independent verification in this pass.

**5. The claim that a client who already declined to close once is specifically primed to notice pressure in follow-up.**
Status: This is a reasoned inference drawn from the same luxury-sales-psychology principles already established in this project's research base (pressure-driven tactics undermining trust in luxury environments, per the Fine-Jewelry-Career-Guide.md research previously cited in C9-M01's factcheck), applied specifically to the follow-up context. It is not independently re-verified via new external research in this pass.

**6. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from any external source.
