# C9-M02 Quiz: Compressed Discovery: Surfacing Priorities in Minutes, Not Weeks

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. Why is "rushing straight to presenting pieces" considered the wrong instinct under time pressure, according to this module?**
A) Presenting pieces immediately is always the fastest and best approach regardless of time pressure
B) Discovery is what determines whether the presentation actually lands with a specific client's real priorities; skipping it produces a scattered, unconvincing presentation regardless of how much time is available
C) Discovery has no real effect on how well a presentation lands
D) Time pressure eliminates the need for any discovery at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The jewelry-specific needs-assessment logic this module draws on is explicit: the assessment produces the answers, which are then demonstrated — reversing that order undermines the presentation.

---

**2. What are the four layers of the discovery sequence this module recommends for a compressed jewelry conversation?**
A) Price, brand, size, and color, asked in that order
B) Situation, gap, impact/significance, and vision
C) Only closed-ended yes/no questions, asked as quickly as possible
D) A single generic question repeated at different points in the conversation

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This layered sequence surfaces real priorities by building on each answer, rather than skipping straight to product features.

---

**3. Why does this module recommend giving a rough internal time budget to each discovery phase, rather than letting discovery run open-ended?**
A) Discovery should be eliminated entirely in a time-constrained conversation
B) A compressed-time interaction has a genuinely finite window, and without a rough time budget, discovery can either eat the whole interaction or get rushed past entirely, both of which hurt the outcome
C) Time-boxing has no real effect on how a compressed conversation unfolds
D) Every discovery phase should always take exactly the same fixed amount of time regardless of context

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A rough internal budget protects enough time for the highest-value questions (like impact/significance) while still leaving real time for presentation.

---

**4. Why does this module emphasize reading body language and tone specifically in a compressed-time format?**
A) Body language and tone carry no useful information in any sales context
B) In a compressed format, there's less verbal history to draw on than in a long relationship, so tone, posture, and word choice carry more of the available signal about whether discovery has reached what actually matters
C) Body language should always be ignored in favor of the client's literal words
D) Reading body language is only relevant in written communication, never in person

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: With less conversational history available, nonverbal signals become a more important source of information about the client's actual state.

---

**5. If a client shows restlessness or gives minimal answers during a discovery layer, what does this module recommend doing?**
A) Immediately move on to the next discovery layer regardless of the signal
B) Slow down on the current layer, since the guardedness suggests discovery hasn't yet reached what actually matters to the client
C) End the conversation immediately, since restlessness always means the client isn't interested
D) Ignore the signal entirely and proceed directly to presenting pieces

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Restlessness or guardedness is a cue that the current layer needs more time, not a signal to abandon discovery and move forward.

---

**6. What is the purpose of briefly paraphrasing back what you heard before presenting pieces?**
A) Paraphrasing wastes valuable time and should always be skipped in a compressed conversation
B) It confirms accurate understanding before presenting (correcting course now costs far less than mid-presentation) and signals to the client that they were genuinely heard
C) Paraphrasing is only useful in long-term clienteling relationships, never in a single compressed conversation
D) Paraphrasing should replace discovery entirely rather than follow it

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This brief step does two things at once — confirms understanding and builds trust — at a very low time cost.

---

**7. In the worked scenario, why did the associate's second discovery question ("what wasn't quite right about the anniversary bands you've already seen?") matter so much?**
A) It didn't matter; the associate should have shown sapphire rings immediately after the first question
B) It surfaced the client's real reason for continued searching — that everything she'd seen felt too matched to her existing ring rather than its own statement — which changed what should actually be presented next
C) The question was irrelevant to the client's actual needs
D) Any generic follow-up question would have produced the same useful information

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This specific gap question surfaced the actual driver behind her search, which a jump straight to "here are our sapphire rings" would have missed entirely.
