# C9-M01 Quiz: Why Compressed-Time Selling Is Different

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. What is the core structural difference between a standard clienteling conversation and a compressed-time selling format?**
A) There is no real difference; compressed-time selling is just clienteling done faster
B) In standard clienteling, a deferred decision costs little because the relationship absorbs the delay; in compressed-time formats, a deferred decision usually costs the sale outright since there's no second visit to fall back on
C) Compressed-time formats always involve lower-priced merchandise than standard clienteling
D) Compressed-time formats never involve any real time constraint at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This structural difference — no safety net for deferred decisions — is the foundational reason compressed-time selling requires its own distinct approach.

---

**2. Which of the following are examples of compressed-time selling formats discussed in this module?**
A) A client's third follow-up appointment scheduled two months after their first visit
B) Trunk shows, onboard cruise retail seminars, and pop-up/VIP events
C) A routine walk-in customer browsing on an ordinary Tuesday afternoon
D) A wishlist review conducted during an annual clienteling check-in

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These formats share the same core time-boundary challenge even though their specific operational structures differ.

---

**3. What is "genuine urgency" in a high-ticket sales context, according to this module?**
A) Any statement designed to make a client feel they must decide immediately, regardless of whether it's true
B) Urgency grounded in a real, specific constraint — like an actual decision date, a real cost of delay, or a piece leaving the store permanently — stated honestly
C) Urgency created by inventing scarcity that doesn't actually exist
D) Genuine urgency has no place in luxury sales and should never be mentioned

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Real, honestly stated constraints respect the client's intelligence and are distinct from fabricated pressure tactics.

---

**4. Why does this module argue that manufactured pressure (fake scarcity, invented deadlines) tends to backfire in a luxury sales context specifically?**
A) Manufactured pressure always increases sales with no real downside
B) Sophisticated luxury buyers recognize pressure tactics quickly, and the resulting damage to trust outlasts any single sale
C) Manufactured pressure is illegal in all sales contexts
D) Luxury buyers never notice the difference between real and fake urgency

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The reputational and trust cost of being caught using manufactured pressure is specifically higher in a luxury context, where trust is central to the relationship.

---

**5. Why does discovery need to "compress without becoming shallow" in a compressed-time format?**
A) Discovery isn't actually necessary in compressed-time formats
B) The open-ended questions that normally build understanding over multiple conversations need to surface a client's real priorities in minutes rather than weeks, without skipping the substance of genuine discovery
C) Discovery should always take exactly the same amount of time regardless of format
D) Shallow discovery is always preferable in a time-constrained setting

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The time constraint changes the pace of discovery, not whether real discovery needs to happen — this tension is what later modules in the course address directly.

---

**6. In the worked scenario, why is "these particular pieces go back with the designer after today" considered honest urgency, while "someone else was just asking about it" is considered manufactured pressure?**
A) Both statements are equally honest and equally acceptable
B) The first states a real, specific, verifiable constraint about the event's actual structure, while the second is a vague, unverifiable scarcity claim designed to create pressure without being grounded in a real fact
C) The second statement is always true in every trunk show, making it acceptable
D) Neither statement has any real difference in effect on the client

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This contrast is exactly what distinguishes the honest-constraint approach this course teaches from the manufactured-pressure approach it warns against.

---

**7. What is the "practical discipline" this module asks associates to apply in any compressed-time interaction?**
A) Always avoid mentioning any time constraint to the client, regardless of whether one exists
B) Identify the actual constraint governing the specific interaction and communicate it plainly, rather than hiding or exaggerating it
C) Always exaggerate the time pressure to maximize urgency
D) Rely on generic "limited time" language rather than any specific constraint

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is the module's core practical takeaway, directly setting up how later modules teach compressed discovery, fast trust, and decision-moment handling.
