# C9-M06 Quiz: Onboard and Travel Retail: Selling in a Single Itinerary Window

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

**1. What is the defining time constraint specific to onboard/travel retail, distinct from a trunk show's designer-travel-schedule constraint?**
A) There is no real time constraint in onboard retail
B) The ship's own itinerary — a passenger who doesn't visit the showroom before the next port or the "all-aboard" time has genuinely lost the opportunity, on a fixed schedule neither party controls
C) Onboard retail has no fixed schedule at all, unlike other compressed-time formats
D) The time constraint in onboard retail is entirely invented by sales staff with no real basis

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is a genuine, honestly-statable constraint structurally identical to Module 1's core principle, just tied to a ship's schedule rather than a designer's travel plans.

**2. According to consumer travel-press sources cited in this module, what pattern specifically damages onboard jewelry retail's reputation?**
A) Onboard retail has an excellent, uniformly positive reputation with no documented concerns
B) Sales pitches getting "more desperate" as a cruise nears its end, and free-gift hooks designed to draw people in before a high-pressure sales routine
C) Onboard retail is criticized only for having too few sales events, not for any pressure-related concerns
D) The reputation problem exists only in academic literature, with no real consumer-facing documentation

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is a real, multi-source documented pattern in consumer travel guidance, which this module uses as direct motivation for teaching the format's techniques ethically.

**3. What is the seminar-to-showroom model, and what determines whether it's run well or poorly?**
A) The model itself is inherently exploitative regardless of how it's executed
B) A specialist presents educational content to convert attendance into showroom visits; run well, this means substantive, genuine content that builds trust, while run poorly, it becomes a giveaway hook leading into high-pressure selling
C) The seminar and showroom are always run by completely separate, unrelated staff with no connection between them
D) There is no meaningful difference between how the seminar-to-showroom model can be executed

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The format itself is not the problem — whether it honors this course's trust-building principles or exploits structural time pressure is what determines the outcome and the client experience.

**4. How does Module 3's warmth-before-competence sequencing apply to seminar structure specifically?**
A) Warmth and competence have no relevance to how a seminar should be structured
B) A seminar that opens with genuine, generous education builds warmth before any sales conversation begins, rather than opening with a hard sell that reads as competence-without-warmth to a room of strangers
C) Seminars should always open with a hard sales pitch to establish competence immediately
D) Warmth-before-competence only applies to one-on-one conversations, never to group settings like a seminar

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The same sequencing principle from Module 3 explains why substantive, generous seminar content works better than an immediate sales pitch or giveaway hook.

**5. How should an associate respond if a client pulls out their phone to check independent pricing mid-conversation in the showroom?**
A) React with defensiveness or urgency, since price-checking suggests distrust
B) React with genuine comfort, since travel-industry consumer guidance consistently and reasonably advises travelers to research prices independently before buying
C) Immediately end the conversation, since price-checking clients never make a purchase
D) Take the client's phone away to prevent price comparison

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Reacting with defensiveness confirms exactly the negative assumptions a skeptical passenger might already carry into the room, while genuine comfort demonstrates the interaction is different from the pattern consumer guides warn about.

**6. Why does this module recommend recommending independent (non-seller-affiliated) certification when relevant to a sale?**
A) Independent certification has no real value and should be discouraged
B) It does real credibility-building work, since it directly addresses the rational, often well-founded skepticism many passengers already carry about onboard jewelry sales
C) Certification should only ever come from the seller itself, never an independent source
D) This recommendation only applies to sales made outside of onboard retail, never within it

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Given the category's documented reputation problem, recommending independent verification is a concrete way to demonstrate the interaction is trustworthy, not just claim it is.

A) It didn't matter; the specialist should have discouraged the price check entirely
