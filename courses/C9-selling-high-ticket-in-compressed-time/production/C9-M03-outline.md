# C9-M03 Outline: Building Fast Trust With No Relationship History

## Module Metadata
- Course: C9 - Selling High-Ticket in Compressed Time
- Module: 3 of 8
- Persona: P2
- Working title: Building Fast Trust With No Relationship History
- Learning objective: Associates can apply the warmth-before-competence sequencing principle in a compressed-time interaction, correctly identify specific warmth and competence signals, and use concrete fast-trust techniques (genuine small talk, visible active listening, matched pace, undivided attention, small honest admissions) within a short conversation window.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Build fast, credible trust with a client who has no prior relationship history with the associate or store."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames fast trust-building as the substitute for accumulated relationship history that standard clienteling relies on.
2. **The Single Most Important Finding: Warmth Comes Before Competence** — The core social-psychology finding and its counterintuitive sequencing implication for sales.
3. **What Warmth Actually Looks Like, Specifically** — Concrete, learnable warmth signals; the congruence/mismatch-detection point.
4. **What Competence Actually Looks Like, Once Warmth Is Established** — Concrete competence signals; why kept promises within a single conversation carry outsized weight in a compressed format.
5. **Fast-Trust Techniques That Work Inside a Short Window** — Genuine small talk, active listening/paraphrase tie-back to Module 2, matched pace, undivided attention, honest small admissions.
6. **A Worked Scenario: The Wrong Way to Open, and the Right Way** — Contrasting a competence-first opening against a warmth-first opening at the same moment.
7. **Practice Exercise** — Comparative roleplay of both openings plus identifying a built-in honest admission.
8. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Ties to Other Modules

## Production Notes
- The warmth-before-competence sequencing finding (people judge warmth first, weigh it more heavily, and read competence offered before warmth as a threat rather than reassurance) is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT and strongly corroborated across an unusually large number of independent sources: Amy Cuddy's own published "Building Trust" material (Cuddy being one of the original researchers behind this warmth/competence framework), Kellogg School of Management's "Measuring Trust: Through Competence or Warmth?" (featuring social psychologist Adam Waytz discussing Susan Fisk's research), Retorio's "Warmth & Competence: The Edge in Sales Performance" article, reinaldogonzalez.com's "The Neuroscience of First Impressions in Sales," braintrustgrowth.com's "The Neuroscience of Trust," and minutesofsmart.com's "How to Build Trust Fast." This is an exceptionally well-corroborated finding across academic, sales-training, and neuroscience-adjacent sources, making it the strongest-sourced claim in this module.
- The specific warmth signals (genuine facial expression, open posture, eye contact, matched pace/energy) and competence signals (clear explanation, confident problem-solving, follow-through on promises) are sourced from Retorio's article (explicit warmth/competence signal lists) and reinaldogonzalez.com's neuroscience-of-first-impressions article (face, body, and congruence/mismatch-detection points).
- The congruence/mismatch-detection point (the brain notices when words and nonverbal signals don't match, and treats the mismatch itself as a reason for doubt) is sourced directly from reinaldogonzalez.com's article.
- The specific fast-trust techniques (genuine vs. generic small talk, active listening/paraphrasing, matching pace, undivided attention, honesty in small uncomfortable moments) are sourced from marketing-method.com's "How to Build Rapport Instantly in Face-to-Face Sales" and Skailit's sales training module (specific behavioral checklist covering phone/device attention, keeping promises, and telling the truth even when uncomfortable), and prospeo.io's "Building Rapport in Sales" article (the paraphrase-before-pitching tactic). IMPORTANT NOTE: these fast-trust technique sources are general sales-training content, not jewelry- or luxury-specific — the underlying techniques are domain-general interpersonal trust-building principles and are presented here as reasoned applications to the jewelry compressed-time context, not as jewelry-specific findings.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
