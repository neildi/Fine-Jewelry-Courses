# C9-M03 Fact-Check: Building Fast Trust With No Relationship History

## Verification Status: Core warmth-before-competence finding exceptionally well-corroborated across academic and neuroscience-adjacent sources; specific fast-trust techniques adapted from general (non-jewelry-specific) sales-training content

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Warmth-before-competence sequencing (people judge warmth first, weigh it more heavily, and competence offered before warmth reads as a threat rather than reassurance).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT and unusually strongly corroborated across an exceptional number of independent sources: Amy Cuddy's own published "Building Trust" material (Cuddy being one of the original social psychology researchers behind the warmth/competence model of person perception), Kellogg School of Management's "Measuring Trust: Through Competence or Warmth?" video series (featuring social psychologist Adam Waytz specifically discussing Susan Fisk's research on this exact point), Retorio's "Warmth & Competence: The Edge in Sales Performance" article, reinaldogonzalez.com's "The Neuroscience of First Impressions in Sales" article, braintrustgrowth.com's "The Neuroscience of Trust" article, and minutesofsmart.com's "How to Build Trust Fast" article. This is the most strongly corroborated single claim in this module, drawing on both academic social-psychology sourcing and multiple independent sales-training applications of that research.

**2. Specific warmth signals (genuine facial expression, open posture, eye contact, matched pace/energy) and competence signals (clear explanation, confident problem-solving, follow-through on promises).**
Status: Sourced from Retorio's article (explicit warmth/competence signal lists distinguishing the two dimensions) and reinaldogonzalez.com's neuroscience-of-first-impressions article (specific face/body/congruence framework). Both sources are general sales-psychology content, not jewelry-specific, though the underlying interpersonal-perception principles are domain-general and apply directly to any in-person sales interaction, including jewelry retail.

**3. The congruence/mismatch-detection point (the brain notices when words and nonverbal signals don't match, and treats the mismatch as a reason for doubt).**
Status: Sourced directly from reinaldogonzalez.com's "The Neuroscience of First Impressions in Sales" article, which explicitly states this point ("the brain trusts the nonverbal every time, and the mismatch itself registers as a reason to doubt you").

**4. Specific fast-trust techniques (genuine vs. generic small talk, active listening/paraphrasing, matching pace, undivided attention, honesty in small uncomfortable moments).**
Status: Sourced from marketing-method.com's "How to Build Rapport Instantly in Face-to-Face Sales" article and Skailit's sales training module (a detailed behavioral checklist explicitly covering phone/device attention, keeping small promises, and telling the truth even when uncomfortable), and prospeo.io's "Building Rapport in Sales" article (the paraphrase-before-pitching tactic, also referenced in Module 2's factcheck). IMPORTANT SOURCING NOTE: these are general sales-training sources, not specific to jewelry or luxury retail. This module presents the underlying interpersonal-trust-building techniques as reasoned applications to the jewelry compressed-time context, not as jewelry-specific research findings.

**5. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from any external source, designed specifically to demonstrate the warmth-before-competence sequencing principle established by items 1-3 above.
