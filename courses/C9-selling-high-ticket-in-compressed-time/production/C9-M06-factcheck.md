# C9-M06 Fact-Check: Onboard and Travel Retail: Selling in a Single Itinerary Window

## Verification Status: Central motivating claim (documented reputation problem) unusually well-corroborated across independent consumer travel-press sources; seminar-to-showroom mechanic previously established in Module 1; ethical-practice recommendations sourced from GIA and gemological consumer-education sources

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Onboard/cruise jewelry retail has a well-documented reputation problem specifically involving high-pressure sales tactics, giveaway hooks, and escalating pressure near the end of a cruise.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent consumer travel-press sources: The Points Guy's "Cruise port scams and schemes" article ("if you're not in the market to buy jewelry... do not accept any free bracelets... they want to subject you to a high-pressure sales routine"), Cruise Critic's "What to Expect on a Cruise: Shopping on Cruise Ships" article ("the shops are usually busiest on sea days, and you might find the sales pitches getting more desperate as the cruise comes to a close"), the International Gem Society's "Should You Buy Jewelry on a Cruise?" article ("onboard jewelers and port jewelry stores know you're a captive audience"), and dealradar.com's "Cruise Port Scams" article (describing the "free gift" jewelry-pull pattern in detail and recommending independent certification). This is strong, multi-source, independently corroborated evidence of a real, specifically documented industry reputation problem, used in this module as direct, constructive motivation for its ethical-practice recommendations rather than as an unsupported cautionary aside.

**2. The seminar-to-showroom conversion mechanic and its status as a named, tracked KPI for onboard fine jewelry specialist roles.**
Status: This claim was already established and fact-checked in this course's Module 1 (see C9-M01 factcheck), sourced from the project's own primary research file, the Global Fine Jewelry Retail Career, Operations, and Competency Framework document, which explicitly names "Seminar-to-showroom conversion rate" as a primary KPI for the "Onboard Fine Jewelry Specialist" role. This module builds directly on that previously-verified foundation.

**3. Recommendation to seek independent (non-seller-affiliated) certification and to research/compare prices independently before an onboard purchase.**
Status: Sourced from GIA's own 4Cs consumer-education blog, "Cruise Ship Shopping: Tips for Buying Gems and Jewelry" (a GIA-authored source specifically written to help consumers protect themselves during cruise jewelry purchases — directly relevant since it represents exactly the kind of consumer guidance an ethical onboard specialist should be genuinely comfortable with a client following), and the International Gem Society's article (recommending the IGS Gem Price Guide and taking photos rather than buying under pressure). Both sources are independent, credible gemological/consumer-protection organizations, not travel bloggers, giving this recommendation strong authority.

**4. "Duty-free" is a commonly misunderstood term; it does not guarantee a lower price than home-market pricing since it only reflects avoidance of local VAT, not necessarily net savings.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent consumer travel-press sources: Cruise Critic's "Duty-Free Shopping on Cruises" article ("duty-free usually is not a blanket bargain; true tax savings apply only to certain goods"), Cruise Critic's "What to Expect on a Cruise" article, Cruiseline.com's duty-free guide, and dealradar.com's port-scams article ("'Duty-free' means the seller doesn't pay import duty. It does not mean the price to you is lower"). This point is independently and consistently made across four sources.

**5. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from any external source, designed specifically to demonstrate consistency between a substantive seminar's promise and the showroom's actual behavior under a realistic skepticism test (the mid-conversation price check).
