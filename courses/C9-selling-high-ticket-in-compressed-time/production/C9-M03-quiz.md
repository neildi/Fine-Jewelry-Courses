# C9-M03 Quiz: Building Fast Trust With No Relationship History

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. According to this module, which of the two dimensions of first-impression judgment (warmth and competence) do people evaluate first, and weigh more heavily?**
A) Competence is always judged first and weighted more heavily
B) Warmth is judged first, often within a fraction of a second, and weighted more heavily than competence
C) Warmth and competence are always judged with exactly equal weight and timing
D) Neither warmth nor competence has any real effect on trust judgments

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is the module's core finding, confirmed across multiple independent sources on trust psychology.

---

**2. What happens when competence is demonstrated before warmth has been established?**
A) Competence demonstrated first always increases trust regardless of timing
B) It tends to read as a threat rather than a reassurance, because the brain hasn't yet decided whether the person's skill will be used for the client's benefit
C) Warmth and competence have no real relationship to each other
D) Demonstrating competence first always eliminates the need for warmth entirely

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is the specific, counterintuitive mechanism behind why leading with product expertise under time pressure often backfires.

---

**3. Which of the following is a specific, learnable warmth signal described in this module?**
A) Speaking as quickly as possible regardless of the client's own pace
B) A genuine, relaxed facial expression, open posture, comfortable eye contact, and matching the client's energy and pace
C) Avoiding eye contact to seem less intrusive
D) Using highly technical language immediately to establish authority

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These are concrete, observable signals that communicate good intent, distinct from vague general friendliness.

---

**4. Why does a mismatch between a client's words and an associate's nonverbal signals (tone, face, energy) create doubt, according to this module?**
A) Mismatches between words and nonverbal signals are never noticed by clients
B) The brain is specifically tuned to notice when words and nonverbal signals don't match, and treats that mismatch itself as a reason for doubt
C) Nonverbal signals are always irrelevant compared to spoken words
D) Mismatches always increase trust rather than decrease it

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Congruence between what's said and how it's delivered is itself a trust signal; incongruence registers as suspicious even if the words themselves are fine.

---

**5. Why do kept promises made within a single compressed-time conversation carry unusually high weight, compared to a standard long-term clienteling relationship?**
A) Promises made during a short conversation are always less important than in a long relationship
B) There's no accumulated track record of kept promises to draw on in a compressed format, so the promises made and kept within that single conversation carry outsized proportional weight
C) Promises should never be made during a compressed-time conversation
D) Kept promises have no real effect on trust in any context

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: With no history to point to, small in-conversation follow-throughs (like actually checking a detail and reporting back) become the primary evidence of reliability available to the client.

---

**6. What distinguishes "genuine small talk" from "generic small talk," according to this module?**
A) There is no real difference between the two
B) Genuine small talk makes a specific observation tied to something actually true about the moment (the piece the client is looking at, a detail about the event), while generic small talk (like a comment on the weather) signals nothing
C) Generic small talk is always more effective at building trust than genuine, specific small talk
D) Small talk of any kind should always be avoided entirely in a compressed-time interaction

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Specificity is what makes small talk function as a genuine attention signal rather than empty filler.

---

**7. In the worked scenario, why does the second opening ("That's a beautiful piece to be drawn to — is this for something specific...") work better than the first?**
A) The second opening avoids mentioning the product at all, which is always the correct approach
B) It opens with genuine curiosity and matches the client's energy, establishing warmth before any competence claim follows — which lets the later technical detail land as expertise rather than pressure
C) Both openings are equally effective regardless of sequencing
D) The first opening is actually more effective because it demonstrates more technical knowledge upfront

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This directly illustrates the warmth-before-competence sequencing principle in a concrete, realistic sales moment.
