# Capstone: Running a Full Compressed-Time Selling Event

## Why This Module Matters

Every module in this course covered one piece of compressed-time selling in isolation: the core mindset shift, discovery, trust, closing, event logistics, the onboard-retail variant, and recovery when a sale doesn't happen on the spot. A real trunk show, pop-up, or onboard seminar day asks for all seven at once, often with several clients moving through different stages simultaneously, on a schedule that won't wait for any one conversation to finish before the next one starts. This capstone brings the full course together into a single simulated event, so the skill being assessed isn't any one technique in isolation, but whether all of it holds up together under real, simultaneous pressure.

## What This Capstone Actually Assesses

Unlike the earlier modules' shorter practice exercises, this capstone is the course's formal assessment. A manager or trainer should evaluate the simulation against every prior module's specific standard, not just overall impression:

- **From Module 1**: does every stated deadline or scarcity claim in the simulation reflect something genuinely true about the event, rather than manufactured pressure?
- **From Module 2**: does the associate's discovery sequence (situation, gap, impact, vision) surface something real about each simulated client, rather than jumping straight to product?
- **From Module 3**: does the associate lead with warmth before competence, and does trust-building look different for a client with zero relationship history than it would for a returning client?
- **From Module 4**: are trial closes used throughout, is the actual close calm and direct rather than forceful, and does the associate hold silence after asking?
- **From Module 5**: does the simulation's setup reflect the two-person security minimum, correct staffing math against expected volume, and clear vendor-terms awareness?
- **From Module 6**: if the simulation includes an onboard/travel-retail scenario, does the seminar content stay substantive rather than relying on a giveaway hook, and does the associate stay calm if a simulated client checks prices independently?
- **From Module 7**: for any simulated client who doesn't close, does the associate draft an appropriate, honest, non-manufactured follow-up sequence?

## The Simulation Structure

A full capstone simulation should include, at minimum, three concurrent client scenarios running across a single simulated event day, each requiring a different combination of this course's skills:

1. **A client who closes on the spot** — requiring genuine discovery, fast trust-building, trial closes, and a clean decision-moment close followed by silence.
2. **A client who needs to think it over** — requiring the same discovery and trust-building work, a respectful handling of "I need some time," and a properly drafted fast-follow-up sequence per Module 7.
3. **A logistically complicated moment** — a security or staffing wrinkle (an unscheduled arrival during a already-tight staffing window, a question about vendor terms mid-conversation) that has to be handled without derailing either of the other two client conversations.

Running all three with realistic overlapping timing — the way an actual event day compresses multiple conversations into limited staff and limited hours — is what distinguishes this capstone from simply demonstrating each module's skill on its own, one at a time, with no competing demands.

## A Worked Scenario: A Ninety-Minute Simulated Trunk Show Window

A trainee is given a ninety-minute simulated trunk show window with three roleplay "clients" (played by colleagues or a trainer) staggered to arrive at different points, plus a mid-simulation logistical complication (a designer's inventory discrepancy that needs to be flagged and resolved without disrupting an active client conversation). The trainee runs full discovery and trust-building with the first client, reaches a genuine close, and holds silence after asking. While transitioning to the second client, the inventory discrepancy surfaces; the trainee flags it to a "manager" briefly and returns to the second client without losing the thread of that conversation. The second client, after real discovery and a fair closing attempt, says she needs to think it over; the trainee closes warmly, without pressure, and later drafts an honest three-touch follow-up sequence per Module 7. The third client arrives with limited remaining time; the trainee compresses discovery further without skipping it, and reaches a close within the time available. A manager reviewing this simulation checks each of the seven module standards listed above against what actually happened, not against a single idealized conversation.

## Practice Exercise (This Is the Capstone Assessment)

Working with your manager or trainer, schedule and run a full ninety-minute (or store-appropriate length) simulated compressed-time event with at least three overlapping client scenarios as described above, including at least one client who closes, one who needs to think it over, and one logistical complication. Your manager should score the simulation against each of the seven module-specific standards listed in this module, not just overall impression, and provide written feedback identifying which modules' skills held up well and which need further practice.

## Key Takeaways

- This capstone is the course's formal assessment: it evaluates whether all seven prior modules' skills hold up together, under simultaneous, overlapping pressure, not any single technique in isolation.
- A genuine capstone simulation should include a client who closes, a client who needs to think it over, and a logistical complication, running with realistic overlapping timing rather than one at a time with no competing demands.
- Every stated urgency or deadline in the simulation should reflect Module 1's genuine-constraint standard; every discovery sequence should reflect Module 2's real depth; every trust-building moment should reflect Module 3's warmth-before-competence sequencing; every close should reflect Module 4's calm directness and silence.
- Event-level logistics (Module 5's security and staffing standards, and Module 6's onboard-retail-specific practices where relevant) need to hold up even while an individual sales conversation is actively underway.
- Any simulated client who doesn't close should receive a properly drafted, honest follow-up sequence per Module 7, since recovering an unclosed opportunity is as much a tested skill in this capstone as closing one on the spot.
