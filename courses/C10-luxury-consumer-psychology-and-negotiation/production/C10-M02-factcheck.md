# C10-M02 Fact-Check: Reading the Buyer: Matching Your Approach to Personal vs. Social Motivation

## Verification Status: Self-gifting/landmark-event research well-corroborated across multiple academic sources including a 50-study systematic literature review; language-style-matching content flagged as general, non-jewelry-specific sales-technique research

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Self-gifting definition ("internally attributed, exclusively personal, pleasure orientated and independent of an immediate need") and the ordinary-vs-landmark-predecessor-event distinction.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple academic sources. The core research is sourced directly from a peer-reviewed study published in RAM (Revista de Administração Mackenzie), "Which purchase do I deserve? Understanding self-gift and..." (scielo.br), which explicitly states: "In ordinary predecessor events, individuals seek to reward themselves with ordinary purchases, while in landmark predecessor events, individuals seek to reward themselves with extraordinary purchases." This is independently corroborated by a QUT (Queensland University of Technology) eprints academic paper on self-gifting consumer behavior, which cites the same foundational definition (Atalay & Meloy 2011: self-gifting as "internally attributed, exclusively personal, pleasure orientated and independent of an immediate need"), and by a Springer-published systematic literature review examining 50 studies of self-gifting consumer behavior spanning 35 years, confirming this is an established, actively researched academic area rather than an isolated or fringe finding.

**2. The practical implication that a landmark-event self-gift purchase is measured against the significance of the preceding event rather than primarily against other product alternatives.**
Status: This is a direct, reasonable interpretation of the ordinary-vs-landmark research finding (item 1), translated into a practical sales-conversation implication. The underlying research supports this interpretation, though the specific framing ("measured against the event" as opposed to "measured against other products") is this module's own reasoned application rather than a verbatim finding stated in those exact terms in the source material.

**3. The three linguistic-signal categories (personal-oriented, social-oriented, self-completion/identity language).**
Status: This is this module's own direct, practical translation of Module 1's already-established and independently fact-checked motivational categories (see C10-M01 factcheck) into observable, real-time language patterns. It is not a new externally-sourced claim requiring independent verification in this pass, since it operationalizes previously-verified psychological categories rather than introducing new psychological claims.

**4. Language Style Matching (LSM) research (mirroring vocabulary, tone, sentence structure, and pace to build trust and rapport).**
Status: Sourced from a LinkedIn article specifically titled "Language Style Matching and Its Impact on Sales Success," cross-consistent with NLP-sales-technique sources (Sybill.ai's "NLP Sales Techniques" and inlpcenter.org's "5 Essential NLP Sales Techniques"), both independently describing matching/mirroring speech patterns, energy, and pace as trust-building techniques. IMPORTANT SOURCING NOTE: these are general, popular sales-training sources, not jewelry- or luxury-specific, and not rigorous academic psychology research (the NLP-labeled sources in particular represent popular sales-training content with contested scientific standing in the broader psychology literature, which this module does not independently evaluate). This module presents the underlying matching principle as a reasoned, domain-general communication technique, not a rigorously validated or jewelry-specific psychological finding.

**5. The specific technique of reflecting a client's own phrasing back rather than substituting a paraphrase.**
Status: This is this module's own practical extrapolation from the general LSM/vocabulary-matching research (item 4), applied specifically to the luxury jewelry sales context. It is a reasoned application rather than a claim independently verified in a jewelry-specific source.

**6. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from any external source.
