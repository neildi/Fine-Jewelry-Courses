**1. According to academic self-gifting research cited in this module, what distinguishes a self-gift purchase following a "landmark" predecessor event from one following an "ordinary" predecessor event?**
A) There is no real distinction; all self-gift purchases are identical regardless of what preceded them
B) Following an ordinary event, people tend toward ordinary, modest self-gift purchases, while following a landmark event, people specifically seek extraordinary purchases that match the scale of what they just went through
C) Landmark events always result in smaller purchases than ordinary events
D) Self-gifting research has found no relationship between preceding life events and purchase behavior

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This well-established academic finding has a direct practical implication: the purchase is measured against the significance of the preceding event, not primarily against other product options.

**2. Why does asking "what happened that made you want to do something like this for yourself?" speak directly to the self-gifting research finding?**
A) This question is irrelevant to self-gifting behavior and should be avoided
B) It surfaces the specific preceding event (ordinary or landmark) that the purchase is actually being measured against, which is exactly what the academic research shows drives this kind of purchase
C) This question only works for social-oriented purchases, never personal-oriented ones
D) Self-gifting purchases have no relationship to any preceding event at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This question is a direct, practical application of the ordinary-vs-landmark-event research finding.

**3. What is the key difference between personal-oriented language and social-oriented language, as described in this module?**
A) There is no real difference between the two; all client language sounds identical
B) Personal-oriented language centers the client's own internal state ("this is for me," "I deserve this"), while social-oriented language centers other people's perception ("people will notice," "it needs to make a statement")
C) Social-oriented language always involves a specific dollar amount
D) Personal-oriented language is only ever used by clients who are not genuinely interested in buying

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These are the specific, observable linguistic signals this module teaches associates to listen for in real time.

**4. What does this module recommend doing when a client uses a specific, distinctive phrase (e.g., "make a statement")?**
A) Ignore the specific phrase entirely and use only the associate's own preferred vocabulary
B) Reflect that same specific phrase back to the client, rather than substituting a paraphrase in different words
C) Correct the client's word choice to sound more professional
D) Repeat the phrase back sarcastically to signal disagreement

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This reflects the language-style-matching research finding that mirroring a client's own vocabulary is a distinct trust-building technique, separate from simply identifying the correct motivational category.

**5. Why does this module distinguish "reflecting a client's own phrasing back" from "paraphrasing"?**
A) These are identical techniques with no meaningful difference
B) Reflecting a client's own specific words signals that the client has been heard in their own terms, which does trust-building work beyond simply conveying the same general meaning in the associate's own words
C) Paraphrasing is always more effective than reflecting a client's own words
D) Neither technique has any real effect on trust or rapport

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The specificity of using the client's own words, not just capturing the same idea, is what gives this technique its particular trust-building value.

**6. What does this module recommend when a client's language shows a mix of personal, social, and identity signals, rather than falling neatly into one category?**
A) Force the conversation into a single category regardless of what the client actually says
B) Track which language recurs or the client returns to unprompted, and lead with that dominant thread while still acknowledging the other signals rather than ignoring them
C) Ignore all motivational signals entirely and default to a generic pitch
D) Ask the client to explicitly declare which single motivation applies

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Trying to serve all motivational threads equally, with no sense of which is primary, usually ends up serving none of them well; identifying the dominant thread is the practical skill.
