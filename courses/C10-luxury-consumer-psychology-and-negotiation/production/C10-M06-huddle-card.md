# C10-M06 Huddle Card: Serving High-Net-Worth Clients

## 5-Minute Pre-Shift Discussion

**The one idea to remember:** The quietest, plainest-dressed client in the room might be your biggest sale. Appearance-based judgment is real, documented, and often UNCONSCIOUS — good intentions alone don't stop it.

## The Research Is Clear
Salespeople measurably give more attention to clients with visible status signals — not because of anything the client said or did, just appearance. A large national retail study found staff genuinely BELIEVE they're treating everyone the same based on behavior, while shoppers experience the opposite. The gap is real.

## Why Genuinely Wealthy Clients Often Look Unremarkable
"Quiet luxury" / "stealth wealth" is real and well-documented:
- Security (visible wealth = a target, especially while traveling)
- Avoiding awkwardness with friends who have less
- Seeing themselves as CUSTODIANS of wealth, not people who display it
- Established ("old money") wealth signals LESS, not more, than new money

## Two Real Types — Read the Language, Not the Look
- Quiet-luxury: wants expertise, discretion, no commentary on their spending
- Loud-luxury: genuinely enjoys visible recognition and prestige
Neither is "better." Listen (Module 2 skills) to know which one is in front of you.

## Discretion, Every Time, No Exceptions
- No unsolicited comments on spending, appearance, or apparent means
- Complete confidentiality on identity, purchases, visits
- Careful with travel/schedule info — real security reasons, not just etiquette

## Today's Discussion Prompt
Think of a time appearance may have shaped how much attention a client got — even unintentionally. What behavior-based signal (not appearance) should have driven that decision instead?
