# C10-M01 Huddle Card: Why People Really Buy Luxury

## 5-Minute Pre-Shift Discussion

**The one idea to remember:** Emotion motivates the purchase. Reason justifies it afterward. Lead with the feeling, not the spec sheet.

## Two Motivations — Listen for Which One You're Hearing
- **Personal-oriented:** "This is just for me." Self-reward, personal meaning, internal identity. No audience but themselves.
- **Social-oriented:** "This needs to make a statement." Status, recognition, how it's perceived by others (Veblen's classic status-signaling).

Most clients lean toward one. Match your language to which one you're actually hearing.

## Identity Language = Self-Completion
"I've worked for this." "This is who I am now." "I finally feel like I've arrived."
This is a THIRD thing — not showing off, not simple pleasure. It's about making a new identity feel real. Lean into what the piece represents, not who sees it.

## Status-Driven Doesn't Always Mean Confident
Some research links status-seeking to LOW self-esteem, not high confidence — people compensating for insecurity about their standing. Never treat a status-driven client as simply "showing off." Handle it gently.

## Rational Detail Comes SECOND
Metal purity, certification, craftsmanship specs — all real and valuable, but they JUSTIFY a decision already being driven by something emotional. Don't open with the spec sheet.

## Today's Discussion Prompt
Think of your last real client conversation. What language did they actually use? Personal, social, or identity-driven? Did your approach match it?
