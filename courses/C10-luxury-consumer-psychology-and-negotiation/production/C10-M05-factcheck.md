# C10-M05 Fact-Check: Negotiating with Confidence: Technique for High-Ticket Luxury Sales

## Verification Status: First-offer anchoring effect unusually well-corroborated (anchored in a 119-article structured literature review plus independent empirical studies); diagnostic-sequence and concession-trading content flagged as general, non-jewelry-specific sales sources; "negotiate motivations" framing drawn from a luxury real estate (not jewelry) source

## Claims Reviewed

**1. First offers have a significant, well-established anchoring effect on negotiation outcomes, favoring the first-offer proposer.**
Status: Sourced from a structured literature review of 119 academic journal articles published since 1967, "Toward a Process Model of First Offers and Anchoring in Negotiations" (published via Negotiation and Conflict Management Research / ncmr.lps.library.cmu.edu), representing an unusually rigorous, comprehensive evidentiary base for this specific claim. Independently corroborated by a Springer-published empirical study, "Beyond the First Offer: Decoding Negotiation Openings and Their Impact on Economic and Subjective Outcomes," and a PMC-hosted academic study on BATNA and first-offer behavior, both describing the same core anchoring mechanism. This is one of the most strongly corroborated claims in this course's C10 modules to date.

**2. The "anchor zone" finding: a more aggressive counteroffer reduces subjective value felt by both negotiators, even with similar final economic outcomes.**
Status: Sourced directly from the Springer study "Beyond the First Offer," which explicitly reports this finding from two original empirical studies (a vignette study, n=190, and an online experiment, n=212): "a more aggressive counteroffer reduced the subjective value of both negotiators."

**3. The pause-acknowledge-diagnose sequence for price objections (brief silence, acknowledgment, clarifying diagnostic question before responding).**
Status: Sourced from roleplay.xl.net's "Price Objection Handling" article (explicitly recommending "three to five seconds of silence" followed by acknowledgment and an isolating question), cross-consistent with resources.rework.com's "Price Objections" playbook (diagnosing budget/value-perception/competitive-comparison/internal-justification before responding) and salesmate.io's "Price is too high" article. IMPORTANT SOURCING NOTE: these are general B2B/general sales sources, not jewelry-specific. This module adapts the underlying diagnostic sequence to the jewelry luxury-retail context; the explicit connection to C9's silence-after-close discipline (C9-M04) is this module's own reasoned extension of previously-established course content.

**4. "Negotiate motivations, not numbers" — the stated price number is usually a symptom, not the actual underlying cause.**
Status: Sourced from drumelia.com's "The Psychology of Luxury Real Estate Negotiation" article, which explicitly states "the number is a symptom; the motivation is the cause." This is the most directly luxury-context-relevant single source in this module, though it is drawn from a different luxury vertical (real estate) rather than jewelry specifically, and represents a single source for this specific framing within this research pass.

**5. Trade concessions rather than giving them away unconditionally (every concession should come with a reciprocal ask).**
Status: Sourced from Shapiro Negotiations' "Sales Negotiation Skills for Confidence and Better Deals" article. This is a general (non-jewelry-specific) sales-negotiation source.

**6. The connection of price-objection motivation back to Modules 1-2's personal-vs-social motivational framework.**
Status: This is this module's own direct application of previously-established and independently fact-checked C10 content (see C10-M01 and C10-M02 factchecks), not a new externally-sourced claim requiring independent verification in this pass.

**7. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from any external source.
