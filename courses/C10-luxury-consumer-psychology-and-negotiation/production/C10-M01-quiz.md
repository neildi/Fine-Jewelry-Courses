# C10-M01 Quiz: Why People Really Buy Luxury: Status, Identity, and Emotion Over Utility

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. According to the two-stage purchase pattern established in this module, what is the relationship between emotional and rational factors in a luxury purchase?**
A) Rational factors always come first and create the initial desire, while emotional factors justify the decision afterward
B) Emotional factors (status, pleasure, self-expression) provide the initial motivation, while rational factors (quality, durability, investment value) function afterward as justification
C) Luxury purchases are made entirely on rational grounds with no meaningful emotional component
D) Emotional and rational factors have no real relationship to each other in luxury purchasing

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This well-corroborated pattern means leading a sales conversation with rational features before connecting to the emotional driver is answering a question the client isn't asking yet.

---

**2. What is the practical implication of the emotion-motivates/reason-justifies pattern for how a sales conversation should be structured?**
A) Rational detail (metal purity, certification, craftsmanship) should never be mentioned in a luxury sales conversation
B) Rational detail works best as justification supporting a decision already being driven by something emotional, not as the opening argument meant to create the desire in the first place
C) The sequencing of emotional vs. rational content has no real effect on how a conversation lands
D) Emotional content should only be used with clients who explicitly ask for it

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Rational detail still matters, but its role is to support and justify, not to originate, the desire to purchase.

---

**3. What distinguishes personal-oriented motivation from social-oriented motivation in luxury purchasing?**
A) There is no meaningful distinction between the two; all luxury purchases are motivated identically
B) Personal-oriented motivation involves hedonic pleasure, self-reward, and internal identity, independent of anyone else seeing the purchase, while social-oriented motivation involves status signaling and social recognition within a reference group
C) Personal-oriented motivation only applies to inexpensive purchases, never to genuine luxury items
D) Social-oriented motivation is the only real driver of luxury consumption; personal-oriented motivation doesn't exist

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This distinction, rooted partly in Veblen's conspicuous consumption theory for the social-oriented category, calls for genuinely different conversational emphasis depending on which motivation is actually driving a specific client.

---

**4. What does self-completion theory propose, and how is it distinct from simple status-signaling?**
A) Self-completion theory is identical to status-signaling with no meaningful difference
B) Self-completion theory proposes that people acquire and display symbols associated with a desired identity to achieve a complete sense of self in a chosen area, and the "audience" for this can be entirely internal rather than aimed at impressing others
C) Self-completion theory only applies to professional achievements, never to personal milestones
D) Self-completion theory has no real connection to jewelry purchasing behavior

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is a distinct psychological driver from outward social signaling, since the purchase can be about making an identity feel real to oneself, not about being seen by others.

---

**5. What language should an associate listen for as a signal of self-completion / identity-driven motivation specifically?**
A) Any mention of price or budget
B) Phrases like "I've worked for this," "this is who I am now," or "I finally feel like I've arrived"
C) Questions about return policy or warranty coverage
D) Requests to see a specific gemstone certification

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This kind of language points toward identity/self-completion motivation specifically, distinct from either simple hedonic pleasure or outward social signaling.

**6. According to the research on social class, self-esteem, and conspicuous consumption cited in this module, what is a more complicated truth about status-oriented luxury buying?**
A) Status-oriented buying is always a simple, confident expression of established social position
B) Some research finds that people with high subjective social class but comparatively low social self-esteem are specifically more likely to engage in conspicuous consumption as a way of compensating for that insecurity
C) Status-seeking behavior has no relationship whatsoever to self-esteem
D) Only people with low social class ever engage in status-oriented luxury consumption

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This nuance means a status-driven client should never simply be assumed to be "showing off" out of confidence, since the underlying driver may be more vulnerable than that framing suggests.

**7. Why does this module caution against treating a status-driven client as simply "wanting to show off"?**
A) Status-driven clients should always be treated with suspicion or distrust
B) The actual psychological driver may be considerably more vulnerable (rooted in compensating for lower social self-esteem) than a simple "showing off" framing implies, and clumsy handling of that vulnerability can damage the relationship
C) Status-driven clients never make purchases and should be deprioritized
D) There is no real risk in treating any client dismissively regardless of their motivation

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Careful, non-judgmental handling matters specifically because the underlying psychological need may be more sensitive than it initially appears.

**8. In the worked scenario, why did the two clients admiring the same ring receive different conversational approaches?**
A) They should have received the identical script, since the product was the same
B) Their language pointed toward different motivations — one personal-oriented (self-completion tied to a graduate degree) and one social-oriented (recognition at a company gala) — and the associate's approach should reflect what each client actually communicated
C) The associate should have ignored what each client said and defaulted to discussing price
D) Only the second client's motivation was legitimate; the first client's reasons were less valid

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Matching the conversational approach to the actual motivation a client expresses, rather than using a generic script, is the core practical skill this module teaches.
