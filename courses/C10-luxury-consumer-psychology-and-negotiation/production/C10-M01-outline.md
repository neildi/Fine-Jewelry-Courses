# C10-M01 Outline: Why People Really Buy Luxury: Status, Identity, and Emotion Over Utility

## Module Metadata
- Course: C10 - Luxury Consumer Psychology & Negotiation
- Module: 1 of 8
- Persona: P1 (foundational for the course; establishes the shared psychological frame all later modules build on)
- Working title: Why People Really Buy Luxury: Status, Identity, and Emotion Over Utility
- Learning objective: Associates can explain the emotion-motivates/reason-justifies pattern in luxury purchasing, distinguish personal-oriented from social-oriented purchase motivations, recognize self-completion/identity-driven language, and handle status-driven clients with appropriate sensitivity to the insecurity that can underlie status-seeking.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: This module establishes the foundational psychological frame for the C10 terminal outcome on applying luxury consumer psychology accurately and ethically in real sales and negotiation conversations.

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames psychological accuracy as the foundation the rest of the course's negotiation and objection-handling content depends on.
2. **Emotion Motivates, Reason Justifies** — The two-stage purchase pattern; implications for sequencing rational vs. emotional content in a sales conversation.
3. **Two Different Motivations, Two Different Approaches** — Personal-oriented vs. social-oriented motivation categories; Veblen's conspicuous consumption theory.
4. **Identity: Buying to Complete a Picture of Who You Are** — Self-completion theory; listening for identity language as distinct from simple status-signaling or hedonic pleasure.
5. **A More Complicated Wrinkle: Status-Seeking Can Come From Insecurity, Not Just Confidence** — The social-class/self-esteem/conspicuous-consumption research nuance; implications for sensitive handling of status-driven clients.
6. **A Worked Scenario: Two Clients, Same Ring, Different Conversations** — Illustrative synthesis contrasting a personal-oriented and a social-oriented client response to the same product.
7. **Practice Exercise** — Reviewing real or recalled client conversations for motivation-category language.
8. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The emotion-motivates/reason-justifies two-stage purchase pattern is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sources: the International Journal For Multidisciplinary Research (IJFMR) paper "The Psychology Behind Purchasing Luxury Goods" (explicitly describing emotional factors as symbolizing "personal success, social status, and self-identity" while rational factors like "quality, durability, and potential investment value" are considered alongside), stylerave.com's "Premium Purchases Psychology" article (explicitly: "Emotional factors... provide the initial motivation... Rational factors... then help justify the decision"), and Psychology Today's "The Emotions of Luxury" (Kit Yarrow, a consumer psychologist, writing that "the decisions to purchase a luxury product are overwhelmingly emotional"). This is a well-corroborated pattern across academic, trade, and consumer-psychology-expert sources.
- The personal-oriented vs. social-oriented motivation distinction and Veblen's conspicuous consumption theory are sourced from multiple peer-reviewed and academic sources: the PMC article "Going (in)conspicuous: antecedents and moderators of luxury consumption" (explicitly citing Veblen 1899/1992 on status-signaling via visible expensive goods), the Wiley meta-analysis "Luxury value perceptions and consumer outcomes" (explicitly distinguishing status consumption per Eastman et al. 1999 from other luxury value dimensions), and the City University London open-access paper "Explaining Variation in Luxury Consumption" (which directly introduces self-completion theory alongside status-motivation research). This is strong, multi-source academic corroboration.
- Self-completion theory (acquiring/displaying symbols associated with a desired identity to achieve a complete sense of self in a chosen area) is sourced directly from the City University London open-access academic paper, which explicitly frames this as "a theory of self-completion" citing the foundational concept of achieving "a complete identity in a chosen area, by means of displaying material prestige symbols associated with the desired identity."
- The status-seeking-as-compensation-for-low-self-esteem nuance is sourced directly from the PMC peer-reviewed article "Social class, social self-esteem, and conspicuous consumption," which explicitly reports that "individuals in a high social class become conducive to conspicuous consumption when their social self-esteem is low" as a compensatory, self-presentational strategy rather than a simple expression of confidence. This is a single peer-reviewed academic source; while credible, this specific nuance has not been independently cross-corroborated by a second source within this research pass and should be treated as a genuine but singly-sourced finding.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
