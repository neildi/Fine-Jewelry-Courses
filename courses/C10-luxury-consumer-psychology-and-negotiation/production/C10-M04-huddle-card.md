# C10-M04 Huddle Card: Anchoring and the Decoy Effect

## 5-Minute Pre-Shift Discussion

**The one idea to remember:** Anchoring happens automatically — the first price sets the frame no matter what. The real question is whether that anchor is HONEST. And the "decoy effect" everyone talks about? It's shakier in the real world than most sales training admits.

## Anchoring: Use It Honestly
The first price a client sees becomes their reference point for everything after. That's just how it works. The ethical question: is that first price REAL (a genuinely available piece) or artificially inflated just to make something else look cheap by comparison?

## The Decoy Effect Has an Asterisk
Yes, it's a famous finding (Huber, Payne, Puto, 1982). But large 2014 replication studies found it mostly DISAPPEARED with realistic products and prices — it mainly showed up in abstract lab choices. Don't build a pricing strategy around a fake "decoy" option assuming it definitely works. It might not, and it's manipulative either way.

## Three Legit Reasons to Show Multiple Prices
1. Genuine range — letting the client find their real comfort zone
2. Real quality/design differences that justify a real price gap
3. Letting the client self-select, not funneling them toward a predetermined target

## The Test
Is every single option on the table something a client could ACTUALLY choose and be well-served by? If one option exists purely to make another look better — with nobody ever meant to buy it — that's a fake decoy, not honest range.

## Today's Discussion Prompt
Pick two real price points we carry. Practice explaining, specifically, what real difference justifies the price gap between them.
