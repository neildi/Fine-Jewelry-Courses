## Production Notes
- The self-gifting research (self-gifts defined as "internally attributed, exclusively personal, pleasure orientated and independent of an immediate need," per Atalay & Meloy 2011) and the ordinary-vs-landmark-predecessor-event distinction (ordinary events prompt ordinary self-gift purchases; landmark events prompt extraordinary self-gift purchases, measured against the significance of the event) are sourced directly from a peer-reviewed academic study published in RAM (Revista de Administração Mackenzie), "Which purchase do I deserve? Understanding self-gift and..." (scielo.br), which explicitly compares self-gifting behavior following ordinary vs. landmark predecessor events. This is corroborated by a QUT (Queensland University of Technology) eprints academic paper on self-gifting consumer behavior, independently citing the same Atalay & Meloy definition, and a Springer systematic literature review of 50 studies spanning 35 years of self-gifting research, confirming this is a well-established academic research area, not an isolated finding.
- The specific linguistic-signal categories (personal-oriented language centering internal state; social-oriented language centering external perception; self-completion language centering identity/transition) are this module's own direct, practical translation of Module 1's already-established and fact-checked motivational categories into observable language patterns; they are not themselves drawn from a new external source requiring independent verification, since they operationalize Module 1's previously-verified psychological categories.
- Language Style Matching (LSM) research (mirroring vocabulary, tone, sentence structure, and pace to build trust and rapport) is sourced from a LinkedIn article specifically titled "Language Style Matching and Its Impact on Sales Success," and is cross-consistent with NLP-sales-technique sources (Sybill.ai's "NLP Sales Techniques" and inlpcenter.org's "5 Essential NLP Sales Techniques," both independently describing matching/mirroring speech patterns, energy, and pace as trust-building techniques). IMPORTANT NOTE: these are general sales-technique sources, not jewelry- or luxury-specific, and the NLP-labeled sources in particular should be treated as popular sales-training content rather than rigorous academic psychology; this module presents the underlying matching principle as a reasoned, domain-general communication technique rather than a jewelry-specific or rigorously validated psychological finding.
- The specific technique of reflecting a client's own phrasing back rather than substituting a paraphrase is this module's own practical extrapolation from the general LSM research (vocabulary-matching specifically), applied to the luxury jewelry sales context.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
