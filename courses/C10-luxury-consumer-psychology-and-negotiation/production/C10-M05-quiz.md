# C10-M05 Quiz: Negotiating with Confidence: Technique for High-Ticket Luxury Sales

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

**1. According to the structured academic literature review cited in this module, what effect do first offers have on negotiation outcomes?**
A) First offers have no measurable effect on negotiation outcomes
B) First offers have a significant, well-established effect, anchoring both parties' perceptions and consistently influencing the final settlement in favor of whoever made that first offer
C) First offers only matter in negotiations involving more than two parties
D) Making the first offer always guarantees a worse outcome for the person who makes it

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This well-documented anchoring effect means a marked price functions as a real anchor in a retail negotiation, not an arbitrary number to be abandoned at the first sign of pushback.

---

**2. What did research on negotiation "anchor zones" find about the effect of a more aggressive counteroffer?**
A) A more aggressive counteroffer always improves the final economic outcome for both parties
B) A more aggressive counteroffer measurably reduces the subjective value both negotiators feel they got from the negotiation, even when the final economic outcome is similar
C) Counteroffers have no measurable relationship to how either party feels about a negotiation
D) The size of a counteroffer is completely irrelevant to negotiation outcomes

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: How firmly and calmly a price is held, and how the counteroffer is framed, affects both parties' satisfaction with the outcome, not just the final number.

---

**3. What is the recommended sequence for responding to a price objection, rather than immediately offering a discount?**
A) Immediately match any price objection with an equivalent discount
B) A brief pause, an acknowledgment of the concern, and a clarifying diagnostic question before responding
C) Ignore the objection entirely and continue presenting product features
D) Always end the conversation the moment a price objection is raised

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This sequence prevents a reactive discount offered purely to relieve the associate's discomfort, rather than a genuine, considered response to what the client actually needs.

**4. Why does this module argue that the number stated in a price objection is usually "a symptom, not the actual cause"?**
A) Price objections are never genuine and should always be dismissed
B) The real underlying motivation (budget concern, value uncertainty, need for comparison, need to justify the purchase to someone else) is what actually needs addressing, and a generic discount often fails to resolve the real underlying concern
C) All price objections have exactly the same underlying cause regardless of context
D) The stated number is always the complete and only relevant piece of information

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This directly connects to the module's broader point that different underlying motivations (tying back to Modules 1-2's personal-vs-social framework) call for genuinely different responses.

**5. How does this module connect a price objection's underlying motivation back to earlier course content?**
A) Price objections have no relationship to the personal-vs-social motivational framework covered in Modules 1-2
B) A client's hesitation rooted in social-oriented motivation calls for a different response than hesitation rooted in personal-oriented motivation, since offering the same generic discount to both often fails to resolve what's actually driving the hesitation in each case
C) Only social-oriented motivation is ever relevant to price objections
D) Motivational framework only applies during initial discovery, never during a negotiation

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is a direct, practical application of Modules 1-2's already-established motivational categories to the negotiation context.

**6. What does this module recommend when a genuine concession is actually warranted?**
A) Always give the concession away unconditionally with no reciprocal ask
B) Trade the concession for something in return (a commitment, a reciprocal ask) rather than handing it over unconditionally
C) Never offer any concession under any circumstances, regardless of the situation
D) Concessions should always take the form of a straight price reduction with no alternative structure

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Trading a concession preserves the honest anchor of the marked price while still giving the client something of real value, and it's experienced by both sides as a fair trade rather than pressure-driven capitulation.

**7. Why does an anxious or over-justified price presentation tend to invite more pushback, rather than less?**
A) Anxious presentation always makes clients trust the price more
B) A price stated nervously, or justified before any objection has even been raised, signals to the client that the associate themselves isn't fully confident the number is fair, which invites exactly the pushback the anxious presentation was trying to avoid
C) Price presentation style has no real effect on how a client responds
D) Clients always prefer an apologetic tone when discussing price

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Calm, confident presentation of a price as a considered fact, rather than a nervous opening bid, is what this module recommends instead.
