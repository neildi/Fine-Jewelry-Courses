# C10-M04 Quiz: Anchoring and the Decoy Effect: Presenting Price and Options Responsibly

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. According to Tversky and Kahneman's foundational research, what is the anchoring effect?**
A) People always ignore the first piece of information they receive and evaluate every option independently
B) People rely heavily on the first piece of information they receive (the "anchor") and evaluate subsequent judgments relative to that anchor, even when it is arbitrary or irrelevant
C) Anchoring only applies to numerical estimates and never to pricing decisions
D) The anchoring effect has been definitively disproven by all subsequent research

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This robust, well-replicated cognitive bias means the first price a client sees becomes their mental reference point for everything shown afterward.

---

**2. What is the ethical distinction this module draws regarding anchoring, given that anchoring happens regardless of intent?**
A) Anchoring should always be avoided entirely, which is impossible since it happens automatically
B) The question is whether the anchor itself reflects something real (a genuinely available, comparable piece) or something artificially inflated purely to create a contrast effect
C) There is no meaningful ethical distinction to be made about anchoring at all
D) Anchoring is always unethical regardless of what the anchor actually represents

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Since anchoring is unavoidable, the ethical question shifts to whether the specific anchor used is honest and real.

---

**3. What is the decoy effect (asymmetric dominance effect), as originally documented by Huber, Payne, and Puto?**
A) Adding a third, deliberately inferior option to a choice between two alternatives can shift preference toward one of the original two, because the decoy is worse than the target on every attribute but only worse than the competitor on some attributes
B) The decoy effect describes how removing options always increases sales
C) The decoy effect has never been studied or documented in any research
D) The decoy effect only applies to grocery store pricing, never to any other retail context

**Correct answer: A**
Explanation: This is the original, well-documented mechanism, though this module raises an important caveat about how reliably it applies outside the lab (see the next question).

---

**4. What important caveat does this module raise about the decoy effect's real-world reliability?**
A) The decoy effect has been proven to work even more strongly in real-world settings than in laboratory studies
B) Two large 2014 replication efforts found the effect largely disappeared once researchers moved from abstract laboratory choices to more realistic purchasing scenarios
C) There is no caveat; the decoy effect is universally accepted as fully reliable in every context
D) The decoy effect was never actually studied by any researchers before 2014

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is a genuinely important nuance often missing from popular sales-training content, and it directly affects how much weight should be placed on deliberately engineering a decoy option.

---

**5. What distinguishes a legitimate reason to show multiple price points from manipulative "decoy engineering"?**
A) There is no real distinction; showing any additional option is always manipulative
B) A legitimate range presentation includes only options a client could genuinely choose and be well-served by, while manipulative decoy engineering includes an option purely to make another look better, with no genuine intention anyone would choose it
C) Manipulative decoy engineering is always more effective and should be preferred regardless of ethics
D) Only single-option presentations (showing just one price) can ever be considered ethical

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The presence of a genuine option a client could actually choose is what separates honest range presentation from a manufactured decoy.

---

**6. What is the "genuine-option verifiability test" this module recommends applying to any pricing presentation?**
A) Whether the presentation includes at least three options regardless of their purpose
B) Whether every option shown is something a client could genuinely choose and be well-served by, rather than existing purely to manipulate perception of another option
C) Whether the highest-priced option is always presented first
D) Whether the presentation uses confident body language

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This test directly extends Module 3's verifiability framework to pricing presentation specifically.

**7. In the worked scenario, why was the second approach (adding a poorly-designed $7,900 piece) considered unethical, beyond simply being a questionable practice?**
A) It was unethical because it was more expensive than the other options
B) It manufactured a decoy specifically to manipulate perception of the $6,800 piece, with no real intention that any client would actually buy the $7,900 piece — and it relied on a psychological effect whose real-world robustness is genuinely contested
C) There was nothing wrong with the second approach; it was equally ethical to the first
D) The second approach is always more effective regardless of any ethical concerns

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This combines the ethical problem (a fake option with no genuine intended buyer) with the practical problem (relying on an effect that may not reliably work outside a lab setting).
