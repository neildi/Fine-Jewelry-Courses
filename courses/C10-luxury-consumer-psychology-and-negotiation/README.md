## Terminal outcomes

The learner can:
- Explain why luxury purchases are driven primarily by emotional and identity-based motivations rather than pure rational utility, and sequence a sales conversation accordingly
- Distinguish personal-oriented from social-oriented purchase motivations and adapt conversational approach to match what a specific client is actually communicating
- Recognize and ethically leverage genuine scarcity, exclusivity, and social proof principles without resorting to manufactured versions of any of them
- Apply anchoring and related pricing-psychology principles responsibly when presenting price and options
- Negotiate price and terms with confident, non-defensive technique appropriate to a luxury retail context
- Serve high-net-worth clients with appropriate discretion, privacy awareness, and sensitivity to the psychology of status-driven behavior
- Navigate cross-cultural differences in luxury buying and status signaling
- Apply this course's full framework in a capstone negotiation simulation

**Assessment:** A written analysis correctly classifying sample client language by motivation type; an observed negotiation roleplay scored against ethical-influence standards; a capstone negotiation simulation (delivered in Module 8, scored against a seven-point checklist covering every prior module's standard).
**KPI link:** average ticket size, close rate on high-consideration purchases, client retention/repeat-purchase rate among HNW clients, negotiated-discount rate and its effect on margin.
