# C10 Luxury Consumer Psychology & Negotiation

**Track:** Specialist
**Personas:** P1, P2, P3
**8 articles, 6-8 learner hours**
**Research anchor:** Academic and peer-reviewed luxury-consumption psychology research (Veblen, self-completion theory, PMC research, Wiley meta-analysis); self-gifting research; Cialdini's influence-principle research; the Wharton "exclusivity paradox"; Tversky/Kahneman anchoring research; the decoy effect with its 2014 replication caveat; negotiation first-offer/anchoring research; general price-objection/concession research; luxury real estate negotiation psychology; HNW/appearance-bias research (peer-reviewed French academic study; the Sephora Racial Bias in Retail Study); quiet-luxury/stealth-wealth psychology; private-client service practices; cross-cultural luxury consumption research (Wiley cross-cultural perspective; face-consumption research via NYU/Wong & Ahuvia; Hofstede-based cultural-dimension research via Concordia thesis); cross-cultural gifting etiquette (numbers, colors, hand etiquette, confirmed across four independent sources); this project's existing luxury-sales-psychology research base (Fine-Jewelry-Career-Guide.md) and C9's genuine-urgency and silence-after-close principles

## Terminal outcomes

The learner can:
- Explain why luxury purchases are driven primarily by emotional and identity-based motivations rather than pure rational utility, and sequence a sales conversation accordingly
- Distinguish personal-oriented from social-oriented purchase motivations and adapt conversational approach to match what a specific client is actually communicating
- Recognize and ethically leverage genuine scarcity, exclusivity, and social proof principles without resorting to manufactured versions of any of them
- Apply anchoring and related pricing-psychology principles responsibly when presenting price and options
- Negotiate price and terms with confident, non-defensive technique appropriate to a luxury retail context
- Serve high-net-worth clients with appropriate discretion, privacy awareness, and sensitivity to the psychology of status-driven behavior
- Navigate cross-cultural differences in luxury buying and status signaling
- Apply this course's full framework in a capstone negotiation simulation

**Assessment:** A written analysis correctly classifying sample client language by motivation type; an observed negotiation roleplay scored against ethical-influence standards; a capstone negotiation simulation.
**KPI link:** average ticket size, close rate on high-consideration purchases, client retention/repeat-purchase rate among HNW clients, negotiated-discount rate and its effect on margin.
