## Claims Reviewed

**1. The hammer-smashed-wedding-band anecdote and broader emotional-client-handling guidance (listen first, know when to stop selling, take clients to a private space, read body language).**
Status: Sourced directly from JCK's "Keep the Tissues Handy" feature article, which interviews multiple named, veteran jewelry-industry professionals (Cathy Calhoun, Linda Abell, and others) specifically on this exact topic. This is a credible, detailed, single-source trade-press account; the specific anecdote is presented in this module exactly as described in the source, and the broader behavioral guidance (listening, reading body language, moving to a private space) is drawn from the same article's multiple interviewed sources, giving it more than single-anecdote weight even though it is one overall article.

**2. Offering to document a piece's story (photographs, provenance, occasion) as a genuinely helpful practice for clients selling sentimental jewelry.**
Status: STRONGLY CROSS-CORROBORATED across at least seven independent consumer-facing sources (a dedicated "Selling Sentimental Gold Jewellery" site, Abercrombie Jewelry, Desirables.com, Golden Anvil, Gold Star Tampa, AnaLuisa, Laura Summers), all independently recommending this same specific practice (photographing the piece, writing down its story/provenance/occasion) with closely overlapping reasoning about its emotional value. This is one of the more heavily corroborated specific practices in this module.

**3. Applying Module 4's explain-the-structure valuation approach and Module 5's appraisal-type distinctions to the trade-in conversation.**
Status: This is this module's own direct, explicit cross-reference to already-established, previously fact-checked content from Modules 4 and 5 within this same course, rather than new externally-sourced claims requiring independent verification in this module.

**4. The business case for trade-in-to-upgrade programs (a modest trade-in toward a substantially higher-value upgrade producing a better outcome than a standalone sale of similar size) and enhanced trade-in credit for upgrades as a deliberate program structure.**
Status: Sourced directly from Jewel360's trade-in management guide, which states this exact comparison ("A customer trading a $500 engagement ring towards a $2,000 upgrade delivers far better business than selling a $300 standalone piece") and recommends dedicated upgrade-credit programs. Cross-corroborated by MRKT360's jewelry marketing case study, which describes a real, implemented in-store trade-in-to-upgrade program at a specific Toronto jewelry retailer.

**5. The assumptive, benefit-first upselling technique (stating confidence the customer would want to see a related item, rather than a tentative permission-seeking question).**
Status: Sourced directly from Jewelry Making Journal's upselling guide, which explicitly recommends this exact framing approach with a specific example phrasing ("I have a great ring that would go perfectly with that bracelet!" vs. "Would you like to see my collection of rings?"), directly informing this module's recommended language style.

**6. The reach-out-years-later trade-up-offer practice.**
Status: Sourced from Jewel360's customer-experience guide ("Reach out to customers five or more years after their purchase with trade-up offers"), corroborated by DealSpeak.ai's diamond-upgrade-appointment guide, which describes a similar respectful, opt-in outreach approach for returning clients considering an upgrade.
