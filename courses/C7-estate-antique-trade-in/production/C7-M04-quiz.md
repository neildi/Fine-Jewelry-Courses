# C7-M04 Quiz: Trade-In Evaluation

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly applying the valuation logic and communication approach, not memorizing exact percentages.

---

**1. Why does a trade-in evaluation start from wholesale and melt value rather than the client's original retail purchase price?**
A) Because retail price is always the most accurate value
B) Because new jewelry is typically marked up 100-300% over material/wholesale cost to cover overhead, design, and margin, and that markup isn't recoverable in a trade-in
C) Because wholesale value is always higher than retail value
D) Because trade-in evaluations have no relationship to how the piece was originally priced

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The retail markup covers costs and margin that exist at the point of the original sale but don't transfer into what a piece can realistically be resold for later, which is why trade-in evaluation works from a different starting point.

---

**2. What is the formula for calculating a piece's gold melt value?**
A) Weight in grams divided by purity
B) Weight (grams) × purity (as a decimal) × current spot price per gram
C) Purity alone, regardless of weight or spot price
D) The original retail purchase price minus a flat discount

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is a precise, objectively calculable formula — weight and purity are directly measurable, and spot price is public, real-time information, leaving no real guesswork in the metal-value component.

---

**3. Approximately what percentage of original retail price does a natural diamond typically recover through realistic resale channels?**
A) 100%, since diamonds never lose value
B) Roughly 20-40%, reflecting the structural retail-to-wholesale spread that exists from the point of original sale
C) Exactly 75%, a fixed industry-wide figure
D) 0%, since diamonds have no resale value whatsoever

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This range is consistently supported across multiple industry sources and reflects a structural feature of the retail/wholesale spread, not a flaw in any individual stone.

---

**4. How does lab-grown diamond resale recovery generally compare to natural diamond resale recovery?**
A) Lab-grown diamonds recover a much higher percentage than natural diamonds
B) Lab-grown diamonds typically recover far less, often close to zero, given their comparatively low and continuously falling wholesale cost
C) There is no meaningful difference between the two
D) Lab-grown diamonds cannot be resold under any circumstances

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Lab-grown diamond wholesale costs have been falling, which compounds the already-structural retail-to-wholesale resale gap, resulting in typically minimal resale recovery.

---

**5. Which of the following is NOT one of the four components of a complete trade-in offer, per this module?**
A) Metal melt value
B) Stone wholesale value
C) Condition adjustments
D) The client's emotional attachment to the piece

**Correct answer: D**
Explanation: While emotional attachment is a real factor in how a client experiences the conversation, it is not a component of the objective valuation itself, which is built from melt value, stone wholesale value, condition adjustments, and the store's margin requirement.

---

**6. How should condition affect a trade-in offer?**
A) Condition should never affect the offer at all
B) Significant damage or missing stones should reduce the offer to account for restoration cost, while exceptional condition or genuine design/brand value can support an offer above pure melt-plus-wholesale
C) Condition only matters for antique pieces, never for recently purchased items
D) A damaged piece should always receive a higher offer to compensate the client

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Condition adjustments can move an offer in either direction depending on what restoration would cost or what additional design/brand value the piece carries beyond its raw materials.

---

**7. What is the recommended approach when a trade-in offer comes in meaningfully lower than a client expects?**
A) Simply state the number with no further explanation
B) Explain the structure behind the number — the markup gap, the wholesale-not-retail basis, and how the specific offer was built — so the client can understand and evaluate it as fair
C) Refuse to explain anything and end the conversation
D) Increase the offer regardless of the actual valuation to avoid an uncomfortable conversation

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A client who understands the reasoning behind a number is far more likely to accept it as fair than one simply handed an unexplained figure, and this approach is also the more honest one.

---

**8. In the worked scenario, why does the associate explain the retail markup structure to the disappointed client rather than just repeating the $900 figure?**
A) Because explaining the structure has no real effect on how the client receives the offer
B) Because it turns a number that could feel arbitrary or insulating into one the client can understand and evaluate for themselves, framing the gap as normal industry structure rather than something wrong with the client's ring specifically
C) Because the associate is trying to avoid giving the client accurate information
D) Because the original purchase price is actually irrelevant to any part of the conversation

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Providing genuine context transforms the conversation from a bare number the client might reasonably distrust into an explanation they can actually follow and accept.

---
