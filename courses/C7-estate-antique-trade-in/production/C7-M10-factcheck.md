# C7-M10 Fact-Check: Capstone — Running an Estate and Trade-In Buying Event

## Verification Status: National Rarities-specific figures are primary-source company marketing claims, appropriately flagged as self-reported; general event-security sourcing explicitly flagged as adapted from non-jewelry-specific industry sources

## Claims Reviewed

**1. National Rarities partner-run buying event model (3-day events, ~10% commission on purchases, partner-handled marketing, specialized multi-category buyers, ~175 additional customer visits per event).**
Status: Sourced directly from National Rarities' OWN OFFICIAL MARKETING MATERIALS — their LinkedIn company page and a specific LinkedIn post explicitly stating the "10% commission on everything we buy during the event" and "average of 175 customers per event" figures, plus their own "Host an Estate Buying Event" website page ("We market the event. We bring in the customers. We buy. We pay you."). This module explicitly and correctly flags these as the partner company's OWN SELF-REPORTED marketing claims rather than independently audited statistics — an important distinction, since a company's own promotional figures, while directly sourced, are not the same evidentiary tier as independently verified data. That said, the underlying partnership model itself (not just the specific numbers) is independently corroborated as genuinely and currently implemented in practice by Jewelry Creations Inc.'s own blog post describing hosting an actual National Rarities event at their store, and by Centurion Jewelry Show's directory listing describing the same partnership model.

**2. Mayflower Estate Buyers' marketing-support model (custom direct mail campaigns, provided social/email/website graphics, store-run digital channels).**
Status: Sourced directly from Mayflower Estate Buyers' own program page, which independently corroborates the general partner-marketing-support pattern described for National Rarities, using a highly similar division of marketing responsibility (partner handles direct mail and provides graphics; store runs its own digital channels). This independent corroboration from a second partner-firm source strengthens confidence in the general model description, even though the specific National Rarities numbers remain that company's own figures.

**3. General event marketing best practices (advance multi-channel promotion, event documentation/photography for extended social content, clear process signage/communication).**
Status: Synthesized from JewelryMarketer's gold-jewelry-marketing-ideas guide, Poket's jewelry-store-marketing-ideas guide, and PostcardMania's jewelry-marketing-guide, all independently recommending overlapping event-marketing practices (photo/video documentation for extended social content, in-store event hosting as a marketing strategy). These are general jewelry-marketing sources, not specifically buying-event-focused, but their recommendations are directly applicable and consistent with the buying-event context.

**4. General event-security and cash-handling planning principles (staffing ratios, controlled entry/exit during peak volume, secure monitored transaction areas, advance insurance-coverage confirmation).**
Status: IMPORTANT SOURCING NOTE — the specific sources consulted for general event-security-insurance principles (Security Insured, JustQuoteMe, Miller & Partner, BJP Insure, Envisage Promotions) are UK-focused, general event-security-insurance industry sources, NOT jewelry-specific and not U.S.-specific. These were used explicitly and only to inform the general security-planning principles applied here to a jewelry buying-event context (the need for adequate staffing, controlled access, and advance insurance verification), not presented as jewelry-trade-specific primary sources. The specific application to jewelry buying events (cash reserve planning with a bank, jewelers-block-policy coverage verification) is this module's own reasoned synthesis connecting these general principles to the jewelry-retail context established throughout this course.

**5. Module 8's compliance discipline applying without exception during high-volume events.**
Status: This is this module's own direct, explicit extension of Module 8's already-established, previously fact-checked content (federal AML thresholds, state/local secondhand-dealer holding periods and reporting, documentation requirements) into the high-volume event context, rather than a new externally-sourced claim requiring independent verification in this module.

**6. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
