# C7-M02 Fact-Check: Reading a Piece

## Verification Status: Strong cross-source agreement across all major claim categories, consistent with well-documented, widely-taught trade knowledge

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Period-style characteristics (Georgian, Victorian, Art Nouveau, Edwardian, Art Deco, Retro) and their approximate date ranges.**
Status: STRONGLY CROSS-CORROBORATED across five independent sources (Aladdins Cave Jewellery, Hemswell Antiques, Mozeris Fine Antiques, Antique Jewellery Company, AntiqueIdentifier.app), all describing matching or closely overlapping characteristics per era with consistent date ranges (Georgian 1714-1837, Victorian 1837-1901, Art Nouveau ~1890-1915, Edwardian 1901-1915, Art Deco 1920s-30s, Retro 1930s-40s).

**2. The "style, stones, and hallmarks must agree" cross-check heuristic.**
Status: Sourced directly and explicitly from JewelryCarats' dating guide, which states this exact principle with a matching example ("A piece marked 1875 should look Victorian, not Art Deco"). This is a single specific source for the exact framing, but consistent with the general cross-verification logic implicit across all other sources reviewed.

**3. Construction-based dating clues (clasp types with specific date ranges: C-clasp pre-1900, trombone clasp 1890-1940, rollover safety clasp post-1940; closed- vs. open-back settings; hand-fabrication vs. casting; wear/patina consistency).**
Status: Clasp-type date ranges sourced specifically from InstAppraisal's identification guide, which provides these exact ranges. Closed-/open-back setting information and hand-fabrication-vs-casting distinctions are corroborated across Aladdins Cave Jewellery (era-specific construction notes) and Mozeris Fine Antiques' Art Deco authentication guide (construction/wear-consistency checks, including the "no modern casting texture or laser welds" and "honest age-consistent wear" criteria used directly in this module).

**4. Diamond cut evolution (old mine cut ~1750s-1890s; old European cut ~1890s-1930s; specific outline/table/crown/culet proportions for each; modern round brilliant contrast).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across five independent sources, including GIA's own official 4Cs blog (a primary, highly credible source specifically confirming the old mine cut's association with Georgian and Victorian jewelry), Gem Society, Rare Carat, Savoir-Faire Jewellery, and CaratWire. CaratWire's side-by-side comparison table (outline, table, culet, crown, typical era) was used as the clearest single reference and is fully consistent with the other four sources.

**5. Hallmark types and examples (purity marks 925/750/585/375; GF vs. GP/HGE distinction; named maker's-mark formats for Tiffany, Cartier, David Yurman; British lion passant, date letters, duty marks 1784-1890).**
Status: Specific purity-mark values and named maker's-mark examples (Tiffany "T&CO." format, Cartier serial-number format, David Yurman "D.Y." format) are sourced directly from InstAppraisal's identification guide. British hallmark specifics (lion passant since the 1700s; duty marks used 1784-1890; date-letter system) are corroborated across AntiqueIdentifier.app and Antiques Know How, both independently describing matching British hallmark mechanics; general hallmark history and purpose is cross-consistent with DS Fine Antique Jewelry's broader hallmark guide.

**6. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from any external source.
