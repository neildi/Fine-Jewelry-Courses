# C7-M07 Huddle Card: The Consignment and Auction Pathway

## 5-Minute Pre-Shift Discussion

**The one idea to remember:** A verified signature (Cartier, Tiffany, VCA) or exceptional provenance often means AUCTION, not a straight trade-in. Recognizing this is good judgment, not a missed sale.

## The Three Pathways, Quick Reference
- **In-house purchase**: routine, verifiable value → immediate cash, full store margin control
- **Consignment**: uncertain-but-real demand → commission split (commonly 60/40 to seller, up to 80/20 for high-end jewelry), no upfront cash to client
- **Auction**: signed/exceptional pieces → biggest potential upside, longest timeline (8-12 weeks), most complex fees

## Auction Fees — Get This Right
- **Buyer's premium** (20-28% typical): paid by the WINNING BIDDER, on top of hammer price. Does NOT reduce seller proceeds.
- **Seller's commission** (10-25%, often negotiable, sometimes WAIVED for high-value pieces): paid by the CONSIGNOR, calculated on hammer price.
- Both are based on HAMMER PRICE, not the buyer's total including premium.

## The Honest Trade-Off Conversation
"Immediate purchase = certain but lowest ceiling. Consignment = higher potential, real wait. Auction = highest potential for the right piece, but 8-12 weeks and real fees — and no guarantee it sells at or above what you hope."

## Today's Discussion Prompt
Practice spotting when a piece's signature or provenance (not just its material value) should redirect the conversation toward consignment or auction instead of a straight offer.
