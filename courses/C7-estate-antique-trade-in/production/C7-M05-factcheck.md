# C7-M05 Fact-Check: Appraisals for Estate and Trade-In

## Verification Status: Strong cross-source agreement on value-type definitions and purpose-matching; one specific IRS-scrutiny figure flagged as single-source

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Retail replacement value, fair market value, and liquidation value definitions, and the RRV > FMV > liquidation relationship with percentage ranges (FMV ~30-60% of RRV; liquidation ~10-30%, one source up to 45%).**
Status: STRONGLY CROSS-CORROBORATED across seven independent sources (JewelTrendPro's two separate appraisal guides, Antwerp Diamond Store, Lemonade, Maverick Jewelry Appraisals, Nidiffer Gem Lab, 210.jewelry's USPAP glossary, Diamond Source Jewelers). JewelTrendPro's "Jewelry Appraisal vs. Selling Price" article specifically states FMV "typically 40-60% of RRV" and forced liquidation value "often 25-45% of RRV"; Antwerp Diamond Store states FMV as "30% to 60% of retail" and liquidation as "10% to 30% of retail." This module presents the fuller range (30-60% for FMV, 10-30% with a note up to 45% for liquidation) to reflect this genuine but modest cross-source variance.

**2. The IRS's specific legal definition of fair market value.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across three independent sources: JewelTrendPro (direct quote attributed to the IRS), Antwerp Diamond Store (citing "IRS Publication 561 and IRS Revenue Procedure 66-49" as the specific legal source), and Maverick Jewelry Appraisals (nearly identical language: "price at which property would change hands between a willing buyer and willing seller, neither being under any compulsion to buy or sell and both having reasonable knowledge of all relevant facts"). This is a well-documented, standard legal definition with no meaningful disagreement across sources.

**3. Purpose-to-appraisal-type matching (insurance→RRV; estate/probate/Form 706/donation→FMV; divorce→FMV or marketable cash value depending on jurisdiction; bankruptcy/urgent sale→liquidation value).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across Maverick Jewelry Appraisals (which explicitly lists appraisal types by purpose, including a specific "Marriage Dissolution" category), David Stern Jewelers' probate-specific guide, Huntington Gem Lab's estate-appraiser guide (which specifically notes appraisals for "divorcing spouses (equitable distribution)"), JA-World's USA appraisal-definitions glossary (identifying "Marketable Cash Value" as "the most commonly requested value" within resale-oriented appraisals, used in divorce calculations per Alara Jewelry), and Alara Jewelry's consumer guide, which independently lists Marketable Cash Value Appraisal as specifically "requested in divorce calculations."

**4. IRS Form 706 requirement for estate tax reporting, and the "appraisal older than 2 years can trigger IRS scrutiny" claim.**
Status: The general IRS Form 706 requirement for federal estate tax reporting is well-established, standard tax-practice knowledge, consistent with multiple sources reviewed. However, the SPECIFIC "2-year" appraisal-currency threshold is sourced from a single source (JewelTrendPro's "What Does Jewelry Appraisal Mean" article) and was not independently cross-confirmed against a primary IRS source or a second independent secondary source during this research pass. This module presents it as a general caution about appraisal currency rather than a verified, universal IRS rule.

**5. Trade-in offers (Module 4) as conceptually related to but distinct from formal liquidation-value appraisals.**
Status: This is this module's own reasoned synthesis connecting Module 4's trade-in content to this module's liquidation-value definition; both concepts share the same underlying "forced/rapid sale" logic per Lemonade's and Nidiffer Gem Lab's liquidation-value definitions, but the distinction between an informal internal buying calculation and a formal, credentialed written appraisal is this module's own clarifying framing, not a directly cited external claim.

**6. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from any external source.
