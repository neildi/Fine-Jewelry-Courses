# C7-M02 Quiz: Reading a Piece

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly applying dating evidence across style, construction, and hallmarks together, not memorizing every date range in isolation.

---

**1. What construction feature is genuinely diagnostic of Georgian and much of the Victorian era, and why?**
A) Laser-welded joints, because laser technology was available
B) Foil-backed gemstones and closed-back settings, because they boosted brilliance in candlelight before electric lighting existed
C) Modern round brilliant center stones, because cutting technology was already advanced
D) Perfectly uniform cast settings, because casting was the standard method

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Foil backing and closed-back settings compensated for the lack of electric lighting, making them genuinely period-consistent features rather than arbitrary stylistic choices.

---

**2. What is the key visual difference between an old mine cut and an old European cut diamond?**
A) There is no meaningful difference between the two
B) Old mine cut has a squarish, cushion-shaped outline; old European cut has a genuinely round outline with longer, more precise facets
C) Old European cut always has a smaller table than old mine cut
D) Old mine cut was only used after 1930

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The shift from a cushion-shaped to a round outline, along with more precise faceting, reflects the technological progression from roughly the 1750s-1890s (old mine) to the 1890s-1930s (old European).

---

**3. Why would a modern round brilliant-cut center stone be a red flag in a piece presented as genuine Art Deco?**
A) It wouldn't be a red flag; round brilliants were standard throughout the Art Deco period
B) The modern round brilliant cut, with its typically absent/minimal culet and precisely uniform faceting, wasn't yet the standard cut during the Art Deco period, making its presence inconsistent with an authentic period piece
C) Round brilliants are only found in Georgian jewelry
D) Diamond cut has no bearing on dating a piece

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Cut style corresponds to specific technological eras; a modern round brilliant appearing as the "original" stone in a claimed Art Deco piece is a genuine inconsistency worth investigating.

---

**4. What does a C-clasp (a simple hook with no safety catch) on a brooch generally indicate about its age?**
A) It indicates the piece was made after 1980
B) It generally dates a piece before 1900, since safety-catch mechanisms became standard later
C) Clasp type has no relationship to a piece's age
D) It indicates the piece is definitely a modern reproduction

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Specific clasp mechanisms (C-clasp, trombone clasp, rollover safety clasp) correspond to different manufacturing eras, making clasp type a useful, specific dating clue.

---

**5. What does a "925" stamp on a piece of jewelry indicate?**
A) 925-karat gold
B) Sterling silver (92.5% pure silver)
C) A specific maker's mark with no relation to metal purity
D) The piece was made in 1925

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: 925 is the millesimal purity mark for sterling silver, indicating 92.5% silver content, unrelated to a manufacturing year despite superficially resembling one.

---

**6. What is the difference between a "GF" mark and a "GP" or "HGE" mark on jewelry?**
A) They are identical terms with no real difference
B) "GF" (gold-filled) indicates a thick, mechanically bonded layer of gold over a base metal; "GP" or "HGE" indicates thin gold plating with minimal actual gold content and value
C) "GP" indicates solid 24-karat gold
D) "GF" indicates the piece contains no gold at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This distinction matters directly for valuation — gold-filled pieces carry meaningfully more actual gold content than thinly plated pieces, even though both may look similar at a glance.

---

**7. What specific dating information can the British hallmarking system provide that many other national systems don't offer as reliably?**
A) No dating information at all
B) Date letters (a specific letter assigned to a specific year) and duty marks (used specifically 1784-1890), which can pin down an exact year or a hard date range
C) Only the country of origin, never the year
D) British hallmarks only indicate the retailer, never the manufacturer

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The British system's date-letter cycling and historical duty-mark requirement make it unusually rich with precise dating information when the marks are legible.

---

**8. In the worked scenario, why does the associate have real, evidence-based confidence in the "Art Deco" attribution rather than simply trusting the family story?**
A) Because family stories are always accurate and require no verification
B) Because three independent lines of evidence (geometric style, old European cut diamond, platinum hallmark) all agree with each other and with the claimed era
C) Because Art Deco pieces never require any verification
D) Because the associate simply decided to trust the client without examining the piece

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Multiple independent lines of evidence agreeing with each other provides much stronger, more defensible confidence than any single piece of evidence (including a family story) considered alone.
