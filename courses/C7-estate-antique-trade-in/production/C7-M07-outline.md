# C7-M07 Outline: The Consignment and Auction Pathway: When to Sell In-House vs. Refer Out

## Module Metadata
- Course: C7 - Estate, Antique & Trade-In
- Module: 7 of 10
- Persona: P2
- Working title: The Consignment and Auction Pathway: When to Sell In-House vs. Refer Out
- Learning objective: Associates can distinguish which pieces suit in-house purchase, general consignment, or auction referral; explain typical consignment commission splits and auction fee structures (buyer's premium, seller's commission, hammer-price basis) accurately; and honestly communicate the speed/certainty/upside trade-off across all three pathways.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Decide when a piece belongs in-house (resale, trade-in inventory) versus when it should be referred to consignment or auction."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames pathway selection as serving the piece and client best, not defaulting to whichever option is easiest for the store.
2. **When a Piece Belongs In-House** — Routine, verifiable-value pieces suited to outright purchase; immediate certainty and full margin control.
3. **When a Piece Belongs on Consignment** — Uncertain-but-real-demand pieces; commission-split ranges (60/40 general, 60-70% to seller for high-end jewelry specifically, tiered online-platform structures); essential written-agreement terms.
4. **When a Piece Belongs at Auction** — Signed/provenanced/exceptional pieces; buyer's premium vs. seller's commission mechanics; hammer-price basis; negotiability and fee waivers at high value; 8-12 week timeline.
5. **The Honest Conversation About Trade-Offs** — Speed/certainty/upside comparison across all three pathways, including auction non-sale/reserve risk.
6. **A Worked Scenario: The Signed Piece** — Illustrative synthesis of recommending auction referral for a verified maker-signed piece over a straight trade-in.
7. **Practice Exercise** — Five-piece pathway-categorization exercise with commission/timeline explanation practice.
8. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- General consignment commission-split ranges (60/40 most common; 50/50 to 80/20 range; luxury/high-end goods trending toward 60-70% to consignor) are CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across six independent sources (Jewelry Making Journal, Puppet Vendors, Lion and Unicorn, Grailhuntr, Oreate AI's consignment-contract guide, WorthLens.ai), all describing matching or closely overlapping ranges and the specific pattern that higher-value/luxury categories favor the consignor more heavily than lower-priced general merchandise.
- Auction buyer's premium (20-28% typical range, sliding scale at major houses) and seller's commission (10-25% typical range, often privately negotiated, sometimes waived for high-value consignments) mechanics, and the hammer-price (not final buyer price) calculation basis, are STRONGLY CROSS-CORROBORATED across nine independent sources (Rapaport, Christie's own fee-explanation page, Antique Trader, Skinner, Manuscript.org, Signed Vintage Jewelry, Grailhuntr, Insta-Auction, Estimonia, WorthLens.ai). Rapaport's specific sliding-scale buyer's-premium figures (25% up to $100K, 20% to $2M, 12% above) and Christie's own confirmation that seller's commission is individually agreed are both primary/near-primary sourced.
- The 8-12 week auction-consignment timeline (appraisal through payout) is sourced from Insta-Auction's operator playbook, a source specifically written as a step-by-step estate-jewelry-auction guide; cross-consistent with the general multi-stage process (appraisal, condition report, cataloging, marketing, sale, payout) described across Grailhuntr and Antique Trader.
- The distinction of which piece types suit in-house vs. consignment vs. auction (routine pieces vs. uncertain-demand pieces vs. signed/provenanced/exceptional pieces) is this module's own synthesis, applying Module 3's and Module 6's already-established evidence/attribution frameworks to a commercial-pathway decision, rather than independently sourced from a single dedicated pathway-decision reference.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
