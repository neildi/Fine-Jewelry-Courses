# C7-M04 Fact-Check: Trade-In Evaluation

## Verification Status: Melt-value formula unanimous and mathematically precise; diamond resale-recovery range well-corroborated; one specific rule-of-thumb figure flagged as sourced from a non-authoritative community discussion

## Claims Reviewed

**1. New-jewelry retail markup range (100-300% over material/wholesale cost).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across six independent sources (OYDisplay: "typically 100-300%"; OM Jewels Inc: markup covering overhead/staff/marketing; CutGrade: "100-200% above wholesale cost"; Jackie Abraham: "around 250% to 300%" for new luxury jewelry; Diamonds and Gem: "100 to 200 percent... in some luxury boutiques even higher"; DiamondCheck-AI: "often 2-3x the wholesale price"). The specific percentage varies by source and product tier, which this module addresses by citing a range rather than a single figure.

**2. Gold melt value formula (weight × purity × spot price per gram; troy ounce = 31.1035 grams).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT AND UNANIMOUS across four independent sources (MeltValue.com's two separate pages, GoldValueHub, MetalsRadar) — this is a mathematically precise, objectively verifiable formula with the troy-ounce conversion figure identical across all sources. No meaningful disagreement exists or could exist, since this is arithmetic rather than a matter of professional judgment or opinion.

**3. Diamond resale recovery rate (~20-40% of retail for natural diamonds through realistic channels).**
Status: Sourced primarily from CaratWire's detailed resale-math analysis, which provides a specific channel-by-channel recovery-rate table and states this range explicitly ("roughly 20-40% of retail"). Cross-corroborated by DiamondCheck-AI ("typically resells for 25-50% of what was paid" — a closely overlapping but not identical range), CutGrade ("resale buyers pay based on wholesale or below-wholesale values"), TopVegasBuyer, and Diamonds and Gem, all describing a consistent general pattern (well below retail, tied to wholesale) with minor variance in the specific percentage range cited.

**4. Lab-grown diamonds recovering far less than natural diamonds on resale, often near zero.**
Status: Sourced from CaratWire's resale-math analysis, which explicitly states lab-grown diamonds recover "near zero" due to continuously falling wholesale costs. This is a single-source-type specific claim, though consistent with widely-reported industry commentary on lab-grown diamond price declines generally (also referenced in this course's C6 Module 4 content on natural vs. lab-grown advising).

**5. Rapaport as the standard wholesale diamond-pricing benchmark reference.**
Status: Mentioned specifically by DiamondCheck-AI; consistent with well-established, widely-known industry practice, and Rapaport's role as a standard diamond-industry pricing reference is independently corroborated by this course's own research anchor documents (Fine-Jewelry-Career-Guide.md references Rapaport's library/pricing resources).

**6. The offer-building framework's fourth component: a rule-of-thumb ceiling of roughly 50% of realistic pre-owned resale value.**
Status: IMPORTANT SOURCING NOTE — this specific "no more than 50% of realistic pre-owned resale" figure is sourced from a Reddit r/jewelry community discussion thread, which is explicitly a non-authoritative, community-sourced opinion rather than a verified industry standard or trade-association guideline. This module presents it as "a common rule of thumb cited across sources" rather than an authoritative benchmark, which is the appropriate level of confidence for this specific figure given its sourcing.
