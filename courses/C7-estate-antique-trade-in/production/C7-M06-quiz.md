# C7-M06 Quiz: Authentication

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly applying the testing protocols and disclosure discipline, not memorizing every device specification.

---

**1. Why is acid testing alone insufficient to confirm a piece is solid gold rather than plated?**
A) Acid testing is always completely reliable with no limitations
B) Acid testing only reacts with the metal surface, so even a very thin layer of real gold plating over a base-metal core will pass an acid test perfectly
C) Acid testing can only be used on silver, never on gold
D) Acid testing destroys the piece being tested

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Since acid never reaches the base metal underneath a plated surface, a plated piece can pass an acid test just as readily as a genuinely solid piece, making acid testing a surface-only check with a specific, well-documented blind spot.

---

**2. What does XRF (X-ray fluorescence) analysis provide that acid testing does not?**
A) XRF only works on gemstones, never on metal
B) XRF provides a quantitative elemental composition reading and can detect a base-metal core underneath plating, up to a real but finite penetration depth
C) XRF is less reliable than acid testing in every case
D) XRF and acid testing measure exactly the same thing

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: XRF answers "what is this material actually made of" rather than acid testing's binary "does the surface react like X karat," making it capable of detecting hidden cores that acid testing simply cannot see.

---

**3. Why is scanning multiple points on a single piece part of the correct XRF testing protocol?**
A) A single scan point is always sufficient and multiple points are unnecessary
B) A single scan point can miss a partial plating job or a soldered repair using a different metal, so multiple points (e.g., band, interior, and setting on a ring) give a more complete picture
C) Multiple scans are only needed for silver, never for gold
D) XRF cannot be used more than once on the same piece

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Since plating or repairs can be localized to specific areas of a piece, testing only one spot risks missing evidence that a different area would reveal.

---

**4. Why can't a thermal conductivity tester reliably distinguish diamond from moissanite?**
A) Thermal testers don't actually work on any gemstone
B) Moissanite's thermal conductivity is close enough to diamond's that it registers as "diamond" on a thermal-only device
C) Moissanite doesn't conduct heat at all
D) Thermal testers only work on colored stones, never colorless ones

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is the well-documented core limitation of thermal-only testing — it can rule out cubic zirconia and glass, but moissanite's thermal properties are close enough to diamond's to produce a false "diamond" reading.

---

**5. What property does electrical conductivity testing exploit to reliably separate diamond from moissanite?**
A) Diamond and moissanite have identical electrical properties
B) Diamond is an electrical insulator, while moissanite is a semiconductor that conducts a small applied electrical current
C) Electrical conductivity testing only works on metal, never on gemstones
D) Moissanite doesn't conduct electricity under any circumstances

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This fundamental difference in electrical behavior is what makes electrical conductivity testing the reliable follow-up check when a thermal test alone can't rule out moissanite.

---

**6. Can thermal or electrical conductivity testing distinguish a natural diamond from a lab-grown diamond?**
A) Yes, both tests reliably distinguish natural from lab-grown diamonds
B) No — both natural and lab-grown diamonds share the same carbon crystal structure and register identically on these tests; confirming origin requires laboratory-level testing
C) Only electrical conductivity testing can make this distinction
D) Only thermal conductivity testing can make this distinction

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is an important, honest limitation to communicate — neither handheld testing method can confirm natural versus lab-grown origin, regardless of how confident a reading might seem.

---

**7. What is a specific, testable warning sign that a stone may have been fraudulently substituted into a period setting?**
A) The stone perfectly matches the setting's prong depth and dimensions
B) The setting's prong depth or bezel width appears cut for a different stone shape or size than what's currently seated
C) The stone shows honest, age-consistent wear
D) The setting has a legible, period-appropriate hallmark

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A mismatch between a setting's actual construction and the stone currently seated in it suggests the original stone was removed and replaced at some point, which may or may not have a legitimate explanation (see Module 3's married-jewelry concept).

---

**8. What is the recommended approach when a fraud or misrepresentation finding is confirmed?**
A) Immediately accuse the client of fraud without further investigation
B) Document everything before taking action, communicate findings to the client factually and without accusation (since the client may be an innocent party), and escalate through proper channels
C) Ignore the finding entirely to avoid an uncomfortable conversation
D) Change the finding to whatever is most convenient for closing the sale

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A client may genuinely not know their piece isn't what they believed it to be; professional, documented, non-accusatory handling protects both the store and the client's dignity.
