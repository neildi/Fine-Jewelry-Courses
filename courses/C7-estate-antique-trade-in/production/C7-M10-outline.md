# C7-M10 Outline: Capstone: Running an Estate and Trade-In Buying Event

## Module Metadata
- Course: C7 - Estate, Antique & Trade-In
- Module: 10 of 10 (Capstone)
- Persona: P3
- Working title: Capstone: Running an Estate and Trade-In Buying Event
- Learning objective: Associates/managers can compare partner-run vs. in-house buying-event models, plan marketing/staffing/security/cash-handling for an event at scale, and apply every prior module's standards (dating/attribution, valuation, authentication, pathway judgment, compliance, conversational discipline) simultaneously and without shortcuts under real time pressure.

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames the buying event as this course's full skill set applied simultaneously, at volume, under time pressure.
2. **Two Models: Partner-Run vs. In-House Events** — National Rarities-style partner model (commission, marketing handled, foot-traffic benefit) vs. full in-house staffing/margin retention.
3. **Marketing and Staffing the Event** — Advance marketing channels, staffing-for-unpredictable-volume, documentation/photography, clear client-facing process communication.
4. **Security and Cash-Handling at Volume** — Cash-reserve planning with the bank, physical security scaling, insurance-coverage verification, and compliance discipline holding at volume.
5. **Bringing the Whole Course Together: Running the Floor** — Explicit synthesis connecting Modules 1-9 to the live, high-volume event context.
6. **A Worked Scenario: The Line Out the Door** — Illustrative synthesis of maintaining full procedural discipline under unexpected high-volume pressure.
7. **Practice Exercise** — Full event-planning exercise (marketing timeline, staffing plan, cash-reserve conversation, staff reminder).
8. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The partner-run buying event model (National Rarities specifically: 3-day events, ~10% commission on purchases, partner-handled marketing, specialized multi-category buyers spanning jewelry/watches/fine art/collectibles, and the specific ~175-additional-customer-visits-per-event figure) is sourced directly from National Rarities' own official marketing materials: their LinkedIn company page, their "Host an Estate Buying Event" page, and a LinkedIn post specifically citing the 10% commission and 175-visit figures. This is PRIMARY-SOURCE company marketing material — the specific foot-traffic and commission figures should be understood as the partner company's own self-reported claims rather than independently audited statistics, though they are consistent with the general, cross-corroborated pattern (Mayflower Estate Buyers describes a highly similar marketing-support model; Jewelry Creations Inc.'s own blog post describes hosting an actual National Rarities event, corroborating that this partnership model is genuinely implemented in practice, not merely advertised).
- Mayflower's buying-event marketing-support model (custom direct mail campaigns, provided social/email/website graphics, store-run digital channels) is sourced directly from Mayflower Estate Buyers' own program page, independently corroborating the general partner-marketing-support pattern described for National Rarities.
- General event marketing best practices (advance direct mail/digital/social promotion, event documentation and photography for extended social content, clear signage/process communication) synthesize guidance from JewelryMarketer's gold-jewelry-marketing-ideas guide, Poket's jewelry-store-marketing-ideas guide, and PostcardMania's jewelry-marketing-guide, all independently recommending overlapping event-marketing practices.
- General event-security and cash-handling planning principles (staffing ratios, controlled entry/exit during peak volume, secure monitored transaction areas, advance insurance-coverage confirmation) are adapted from general event-security-insurance industry sources (Security Insured, JustQuoteMe, Miller & Partner, BJP Insure, Envisage Promotions) — these are UK-focused general event-security-insurance sources, NOT jewelry-specific, and are used here only to inform the general security-planning principles applied to a jewelry buying-event context, not as jewelry-industry-specific primary sources. This module explicitly synthesizes these general principles rather than presenting them as jewelry-trade-specific guidance.
- The requirement that Module 8's compliance discipline (ID verification, documentation, reporting) applies without exception during a high-volume event is this module's own direct, explicit extension of that module's already-established, previously fact-checked content, not a new externally-sourced claim.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
