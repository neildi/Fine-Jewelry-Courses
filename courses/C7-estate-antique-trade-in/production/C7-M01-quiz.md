# C7-M01 Quiz: The Secondary Market

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly distinguishing the four terms, not memorizing exact market-size figures.

---

**1. What does the term "estate jewelry" actually describe?**
A) A piece that is at least 100 years old
B) Ownership history — any piece that has been previously owned, regardless of its age
C) A piece manufactured in a deliberately old-fashioned style
D) A piece currently being traded in for store credit

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Estate is about ownership history, not age — a piece purchased five years ago and resold is technically "estate" the moment it changes hands, just as much as a Victorian-era piece is.

---

**2. What is the age threshold for a piece to be correctly called "antique"?**
A) At least 10 years old
B) At least 100 years old
C) At least 500 years old
D) Antique has no age threshold at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is the most consistently agreed-upon of the four definitions across sources — 100 years is a hard, widely-used threshold.

---

**3. What does "vintage-style" mean, as distinct from "vintage"?**
A) They are identical terms with no meaningful difference
B) Vintage-style describes a newly manufactured piece designed to evoke an older era's aesthetic — it is not actually old or previously owned
C) Vintage-style is simply an older, less common term for "antique"
D) Vintage-style only applies to pieces made before 1950

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Calling a newly made piece simply "vintage" without the "-style" qualifier is a genuine, avoidable misrepresentation, since it implies actual age and prior ownership that don't exist.

---

**4. Can a single piece belong to more than one of these categories at once?**
A) No, the categories are always mutually exclusive
B) Yes — for example, a 100-year-old ring passed down through a family is simultaneously estate, vintage-adjacent, and antique
C) Only vintage and antique can overlap; estate is always separate
D) Overlap between categories is impossible by definition

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These terms describe different dimensions (ownership vs. age vs. manufacturing style), so a single piece can genuinely satisfy multiple definitions simultaneously.

---

**5. How does a typical jewelry trade-in program work, per this module?**
A) The store simply discards the old piece with no value given
B) A client's existing piece is assessed and its value applied as credit toward a new, typically higher-value purchase
C) Trade-in programs only accept pieces originally purchased at a completely different store
D) Trade-in is simply another word for "antique"

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This structure (full or assessed value as credit toward an upgrade) is consistent across major chain retailers' own program descriptions.

---

**6. Why is trade-in described as a "transaction structure" rather than a jewelry classification?**
A) Because trade-in pieces are always antique
B) Because trade-in describes how a sale is structured (credit toward a new purchase), not the age or ownership history of the piece itself
C) Because trade-in and estate mean exactly the same thing
D) Because trade-in only applies to vintage-style pieces

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A traded-in piece could be recently purchased, vintage, or antique — trade-in describes the transaction mechanism, independent of what category the piece itself falls into.

---

**7. What does current market research suggest about the secondary jewelry market, per this module?**
A) It is a shrinking, niche segment with no real growth
B) It represents a genuine, sizable, and growing segment of the industry, with multiple independent analyses projecting continued high-single-digit to low-double-digit annual growth
C) There is no meaningful market research available on this topic
D) The secondary market is declining rapidly due to lab-grown diamonds

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Multiple independent market-research sources, despite some variance in exact dollar figures, consistently point to meaningful, ongoing growth in this segment.

**8. In the worked scenario, how does the associate respond to a client who calls their inherited ring an "antique"?**
A) By immediately correcting the client and dismissing their claim
B) By respectfully explaining what "antique" specifically means and proposing to examine the piece together to determine its actual era
C) By simply agreeing with the client regardless of the ring's actual characteristics
D) By refusing to discuss the ring's age at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This respects the client's emotional connection to the piece while being precise about terminology, setting up an honest, collaborative evaluation rather than a flattering guess.
