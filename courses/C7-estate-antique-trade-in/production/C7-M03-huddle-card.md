# C7-M03 Huddle Card: Dating and Attribution

## 5-Minute Pre-Shift Discussion

**The one idea to remember:** Independent evidence agreeing with itself is meaningful. The SAME story repeated three times (family + tag + memory) is still just ONE piece of evidence. Don't confuse repetition with confirmation.

## The Examination Sequence
1. 10x triplet loupe (the standard — don't eyeball it)
2. Silhouette and proportions first
3. Stone-by-stone: table → crown → pavilion → girdle
4. Metalwork and joins
5. Hallmarks LAST (easy to miss if rushed)
6. Photograph everything BEFORE any cleaning or repair

## Say This, Not That
- ❌ "This is definitely from 1887."
- ✅ "This is consistent with 1880-1900 manufacture, based on the construction and stone cut."

## Married Jewelry — Not Automatically a Red Flag
A piece with parts from different eras (family re-set an heirloom stone decades later) is common and legitimate. The obligation is to DISCLOSE it honestly, not treat the whole piece as one uniform age.

## When Evidence Conflicts
Don't force an answer. Say: "The hallmark suggests X, the stone cut suggests Y — I can't reconcile that yet, and I want to look closer / bring in a second opinion before we commit to a story."

## Today's Discussion Prompt
Practice stating an attribution with a qualified date range and a stated confidence level, instead of a confident guess — even in casual conversation with a colleague.
