# C7-M08 Fact-Check: Legal and Compliance Considerations

## Verification Status: Unusually heavy reliance on primary federal/state legal sources; jurisdictional variance explicitly and repeatedly flagged as requiring local confirmation

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Federal AML dealer threshold ($50,000 in prior calendar/tax year, 50%+ of price in covered materials, applying to both sale-side and purchase-side).**
Status: CONFIRMED via PRIMARY FEDERAL SOURCE — FinCEN's own official FAQ guidance document states this exact dual threshold verbatim, word for word matching this module's description. Cross-corroborated by Mordfin Group's jewelers'-guide-to-AML-laws PDF, which restates the same threshold in nearly identical terms, and by the federal regulation text itself (31 CFR 1027.210, via Cornell Law's LII), confirming the written-program requirement. This is the highest-confidence sourcing tier available — direct primary federal regulatory text.

**2. The AML program requirement does not separately require SAR filing with FinCEN.**
Status: CONFIRMED via PRIMARY SOURCE — stated explicitly and directly in FinCEN's own FAQ document ("does not require dealers to file reports of suspicious activity with FinCEN"). No ambiguity in this specific claim.

**3. State/local secondhand-dealer holding-period and reporting requirements (illustrative examples: FL 30 days for precious items with 90-day police hold-order provision and 3-year record retention; CA 24-hour/daily reporting and 30-day holding; LA 90-day police hold-order provision; WI municipal 15-day general holding plus 48-hour bullion-melt rule).**
Status: CONFIRMED via PRIMARY LEGAL SOURCES for each specific example cited — Florida Statutes §538.06 (via Justia's official statute-text database), California Business and Professions Code §21628/AB-391 (California AG's own posted PDF), Los Angeles Police Commission's own secondhand-jewelry-dealer permit document, and Brookfield, WI's published municipal code. This module explicitly and repeatedly emphasizes that these are illustrative examples of a general pattern across specific jurisdictions reviewed, NOT a universal rule applicable to any jurisdiction not specifically cited — this framing is essential given the genuine, confirmed variance found even among the small sample of jurisdictions reviewed in this research pass (holding periods ranging from 15 to 30 days; hold-order lengths of 60 to 90+ days).

**4. LeadsOnline's function and NCIC connection.**
Status: CONFIRMED via the platform's OWN OFFICIAL PRODUCT DOCUMENTATION — leadsonline.com's own "leads" product page and its LO_INTEL product PDF directly describe the platform's nationwide law-enforcement integration and NCIC nightly stolen-property-feed ingestion. Cross-corroborated by WSBT's news report describing a specific municipal LeadsOnline reporting mandate and PawnSmarts' software-integration page describing standard industry LeadsOnline export functionality as a routine feature of pawn/secondhand-dealer point-of-sale software.

**5. IRS Form 8300's $10,000 cash-transaction threshold, and its distinction from AML program requirements.**
Status: The $10,000 threshold is well-established, standard federal tax-compliance knowledge with no meaningful source disagreement possible (this is codified in the Internal Revenue Code and Bank Secrecy Act reporting requirements, consistent with this course's own C6 Module 12 primary-source financial-regulation content). The explicit distinction from AML program obligations is this module's own clarifying synthesis, correctly identifying these as separate federal requirements with different triggers, which is accurate and an important clarification given how easily the two could be conflated by a non-specialist reader.

**6. The buy-sell documentation checklist (ID verification, specific item description, pre-alteration photographs, price/payment, date/time).**
Status: This checklist synthesizes the specific record-keeping requirements found across the state/local statutes reviewed above with Module 3's already-established good-documentation practice (photographing before any alteration), applied here specifically to satisfy legal requirements. This is a reasoned synthesis rather than a single directly-quoted checklist from one source, though each individual element is independently grounded in either the cited statutes or Module 3's prior, already-fact-checked content.
