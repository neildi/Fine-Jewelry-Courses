# C7-M06 Outline: Authentication: Spotting Reproductions, Alterations, and Fakes

## Module Metadata
- Course: C7 - Estate, Antique & Trade-In
- Module: 6 of 10
- Persona: P2
- Working title: Authentication: Spotting Reproductions, Alterations, and Fakes
- Learning objective: Associates can explain why acid testing alone is insufficient for metal authentication and when XRF is required, correctly apply thermal-then-electrical conductivity testing to rule out moissanite (while understanding neither method can confirm natural vs. lab-grown origin), recognize common fraud patterns in estate/antique pieces, and respond to a confirmed fraud finding with proper documentation and non-accusatory client communication.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Identify common signs of reproduction, alteration, or outright fakes in estate and antique pieces."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames authentication as Module 3's conflicting-evidence discipline extended to deliberate deception specifically.
2. **Metal Authentication: Why Acid Testing Isn't Enough Anymore** — Acid testing's surface-only limitation; XRF's quantitative, penetrating analysis; multi-point scan protocol; density cross-check for high-value pieces.
3. **Stone Authentication: The Diamond-Moissanite Problem** — Thermal testers' moissanite blind spot; electrical conductivity as the necessary follow-up; the natural-vs-lab-grown limitation of both methods.
4. **Common Fraud Patterns in Estate and Antique Pieces** — Stone substitution inconsistent with setting construction, falsified/enhanced hallmarks, modern techniques on claimed-antiques, glued rather than mechanically-set stones.
5. **When Suspicion Is Confirmed: What to Actually Do** — Documentation-first protocol, factual non-accusatory client communication, escalation channels, forward reference to Module 8.
6. **A Worked Scenario: The Convenient Diamond Tester Result** — Illustrative synthesis of correct thermal-then-electrical testing protocol and factual client communication.
7. **Practice Exercise** — Testing-protocol explanation practice plus factual-disclosure language practice.
8. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- Acid testing's surface-only limitation and the specific "even 0.5 microns of gold plating passes the acid test" example are sourced directly from WiserMachine's XRF-vs-acid testing protocol guide, which explicitly frames this exact limitation ("Acid tells you 'this surface reacts like 14K gold.' XRF tells you 'this material is 58.3% Au...'").
- XRF's quantitative composition reporting, penetration depth (~30 microns commonly cited), and the multi-point scan protocol (ring: band/interior/setting; chain: two clasps and mid-link) are sourced directly from WiserMachine's counterfeit-gold-detection field guide, a source specifically written for pawnshop/estate-buying contexts, directly on point for this module's use case.
- The density-check cross-check recommendation for high-value pieces is sourced from the same WiserMachine guide, which specifically recommends this as a confirmation test alongside XRF for pieces above $1,000.
- The thermal-conductivity-tester moissanite blind spot (moissanite's thermal conductivity closely overlapping diamond's, causing thermal-only testers to misread moissanite as diamond) is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT AND EXTENSIVELY CORROBORATED across at least eight independent sources (The Plumb Club, Electronics For U, VRXRF, JewelTrendPro x2, RareCarat, Sy Kessler technical manual, imba.missouri.edu, JewelTrendPro's diamond-detection-tools guide) — this is one of the most heavily cross-corroborated technical claims in this course to date.
- Electrical conductivity as the reliable diamond-vs-moissanite differentiator (diamond as an electrical insulator; moissanite as a semiconductor) is equally strongly corroborated across the same source set, with specific accuracy figures cited (99.2-99.8% depending on source and device) that vary modestly but consistently support "highly reliable, not perfect" characterization used in this module.
- The explicit limitation that neither thermal nor electrical conductivity testing can distinguish natural from lab-grown diamonds is sourced directly from JewelTrendPro's moissanite-vs-diamond guide and jewelryzone.net's diamond-tester review, both stating this limitation in matching terms.
- Common fraud pattern descriptions (stone substitution inconsistent with setting construction, falsified/enhanced hallmarks, modern techniques on claimed-antiques, glued vs. mechanically-set stones) synthesize this module's own application of Module 2's and Module 3's evidence-type and conflict-detection frameworks to a deliberate-fraud context; these are not independently sourced from a single specific fraud-pattern reference but are a reasoned extension of already-established, cross-corroborated course content.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
