# C7-M07 Quiz: The Consignment and Auction Pathway

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly categorizing pieces by pathway and correctly explaining fee mechanics, not memorizing every exact percentage.

---

**1. What type of piece is generally best suited to an outright in-house purchase, per this module?**
A) A piece with a verified, notable maker signature and exceptional provenance
B) A routine piece with straightforward, verifiable value that the store can resell within a reasonable holding period
C) Only pieces valued above $50,000
D) In-house purchase is never actually appropriate for any piece

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Routine gold and diamond pieces with clear, verifiable value are well-suited to an outright purchase, giving the client immediate certainty and the store full margin control.

---

**2. What is a commonly cited consignment commission-split range for general consignment shops and galleries?**
A) Always exactly 50/50, with no variation
B) Commonly around 60/40 favoring the consignor, though the range spans from 50/50 to 80/20 depending on the shop and item category
C) Always 90/10 favoring the shop
D) Consignment never involves any commission at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This range reflects genuine variation across venues and categories, with high-end jewelry and luxury goods specifically trending toward a higher percentage for the consignor given the sustainability challenge of large percentage splits at high price points.

---

**3. What is the difference between a buyer's premium and a seller's commission at an auction house?**
A) They are identical fees charged to the same party
B) The buyer's premium is paid by the winning bidder on top of the hammer price; the seller's commission is a separate fee charged to the consignor, both calculated relative to the hammer price
C) Only the seller's commission actually exists; buyer's premiums are not a real auction industry practice
D) The buyer's premium is deducted directly from the consignor's proceeds

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These are two separate, distinct fees paid by two different parties in the transaction, and understanding this distinction is essential for accurately explaining auction economics to a client.

---

**4. Is seller's commission at a major auction house typically a fixed, publicly disclosed rate?**
A) Yes, it is always fixed and identical for every consignor
B) No — it is frequently privately negotiated and can be reduced or even waived entirely for sufficiently high-value consignments
C) Seller's commission does not exist at any auction house
D) Seller's commission is always higher than the buyer's premium

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Unlike the buyer's premium, which is typically published, seller's commission is commonly negotiated on a case-by-case basis and major houses may waive it for high-value pieces to compete for strong consignments.

---

**5. What is the correct basis for calculating an auction seller's commission?**
A) The buyer's final price, including their premium
B) The hammer price (the winning bid amount), not the buyer's final price including their premium
C) The item's original retail purchase price
D) A completely arbitrary figure with no consistent basis

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Seller's commission is calculated on the hammer price specifically; the buyer's premium is an entirely separate amount added on top for the buyer and does not affect the consignor's commission calculation.

---

**6. Approximately how long does the full auction consignment process (appraisal through payout) typically take?**
A) Same-day
B) Roughly 8-12 weeks
C) Over five years
D) Auction consignment has no meaningful timeline at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This meaningfully longer timeline compared to an outright purchase or typical consignment should be communicated honestly to a client considering this pathway.

---

**7. What type of piece is generally best suited to auction referral rather than in-house purchase or general consignment?**
A) Any routine, unbranded piece regardless of value
B) A piece with a verified, notable maker signature or documented, exceptional provenance, where a major auction house's specialized global buyer network could produce a meaningfully better outcome
C) Auction referral is never actually appropriate for any jewelry piece
D) Only pieces under $100 in value

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Genuinely significant pieces benefit from a specialized buyer network and marketing reach that neither in-house sales nor general consignment can typically match.

---

**8. In the worked scenario, why does the associate recommend auction consignment rather than a straight trade-in for the Cartier-signed piece?**
A) Because auction consignment is always faster and simpler than a trade-in
B) Because the piece's verified signature gives it access to a specialized collector market that in-house pricing logic can't capture, even though the auction process takes longer and involves commission fees
C) Because the store never purchases signed pieces under any circumstance
D) Because the client specifically requested the slowest possible option

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This recommendation honestly weighs the auction pathway's longer timeline and fee complexity against its potential to meaningfully exceed what an immediate in-house purchase could offer for this specific type of piece.
