# C7-M02 Outline: Reading a Piece: Period Styles, Construction Techniques, and Hallmarks

## Module Metadata
- Course: C7 - Estate, Antique & Trade-In
- Module: 2 of 10
- Persona: P1
- Working title: Reading a Piece: Period Styles, Construction Techniques, and Hallmarks
- Learning objective: Associates can identify the major jewelry design periods (Georgian through Retro) by characteristic style, use construction details (clasp type, closed/open-back settings, hand-fabrication vs. casting) and diamond cutting style (old mine, old European, modern round brilliant) as dating evidence, and read common hallmark types (purity marks, maker's marks, British date letters/duty marks) to build or challenge a piece's attribution.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Date and attribute a piece using period style, construction technique, and hallmark/makers'-mark evidence."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames reading a piece as a three-source evidence discipline (style, construction, hallmarks) underlying every later module.
2. **The Major Period Styles, at a Glance** — Georgian, Victorian, Art Nouveau, Edwardian, Art Deco, Retro; the style-stones-hallmarks-must-agree cross-check principle.
3. **Construction Clues: Reading the Physical Evidence** — Clasp-type dating, closed- vs. open-back settings, hand-fabrication vs. casting, wear/patina consistency.
4. **Diamond Cuts as Dating Evidence** — Old mine cut, old European cut, modern round brilliant, and what each cut implies about a piece's plausible era.
5. **Hallmarks: The Most Direct Evidence, When Present** — Purity marks, maker's marks, British lion passant/date letters/duty marks.
6. **A Worked Scenario: The Three Clues in Agreement** — Illustrative synthesis of cross-checking style, stone cut, and hallmark evidence together.
7. **Practice Exercise** — Multi-piece identification exercise across all three evidence types.
8. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- Period-style characteristics (Georgian hand-fabrication/foil-backing; Victorian silver-topped gold/closed-back/rose-cut-or-old-mine; Art Nouveau organic motifs; Edwardian platinum filigree; Art Deco geometric motifs/calibré-baguette stones/platinum-white-gold; Retro chunky rose-gold forms) are STRONGLY CROSS-CORROBORATED across five independent sources (Aladdins Cave Jewellery, Hemswell Antiques, Mozeris Fine Antiques, Antique Jewellery Company, AntiqueIdentifier.app), all describing matching or closely overlapping characteristics per era with consistent date ranges.
- The "style, stones, and hallmarks must agree" cross-check principle is sourced directly from JewelryCarats' dating guide, which explicitly states this exact heuristic ("A piece marked 1875 should look Victorian, not Art Deco").
- Construction-based dating clues (clasp types with specific date ranges; closed- vs. open-back settings; hand-fabrication vs. casting; wear/patina consistency) are sourced from InstAppraisal's identification guide (specific clasp-type date ranges) and Mozeris Fine Antiques' Art Deco authentication guide (construction/wear-consistency checks), cross-consistent with Aladdins Cave Jewellery's era-by-era construction notes.
- Diamond cut evolution (old mine cut ~1750s-1890s; old European cut ~1890s-1930s; specific outline/table/crown/culet characteristics for each) is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across GIA's own 4Cs blog (a primary, highly credible source), Gem Society, Rare Carat, Savoir-Faire Jewellery, and CaratWire — all independently describing matching proportions and date ranges, with CaratWire's side-by-side comparison table used as the clearest single reference.
- Hallmark types and specific examples (purity marks 925/750/585/375; GF/GP/HGE plating terms; named maker's-mark formats for Tiffany, Cartier, David Yurman; British lion passant, date letters, duty marks 1784-1890) are sourced from InstAppraisal's identification guide (specific maker's-mark examples) and AntiqueIdentifier.app / Antiques Know How (British hallmark system specifics), cross-consistent with DS Fine Antique Jewelry's general hallmark history guide.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
