# C7-M08 Quiz: Legal and Compliance Considerations

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly applying the compliance framework and recognizing jurisdictional variance, not memorizing every state's exact figures.

---

**1. What is the federal dollar/percentage threshold that triggers the requirement for a jewelry business to maintain a written AML program?**
A) Any jewelry sale of any amount
B) More than $50,000 in jewels, precious metals, or precious stones in the prior calendar/tax year, where those materials comprise 50% or more of the selling or purchase price, on either the sale side or the purchase side
C) Only cash transactions over $10,000
D) AML programs are never actually required for jewelry businesses

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is a specific, dual-sided federal threshold (applying to both sales to and purchases from the general public) sourced directly from FinCEN's own guidance — both conditions matter for a store actively buying estate/trade-in pieces.

---

**2. Does the federal AML rule require a jewelry dealer to file Suspicious Activity Reports with FinCEN?**
A) Yes, this is a separate mandatory requirement under the same rule
B) No — the rule requires dealers to establish an AML program but does not separately require filing Suspicious Activity Reports with FinCEN
C) SAR filing is required only for cash transactions
D) The AML rule has no connection to FinCEN at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is a specific, important nuance directly stated in FinCEN's own guidance — the program requirement and SAR filing are distinct obligations, and the jewelry-dealer AML rule specifically does not impose the latter.

---

**3. What is the general pattern for secondhand-dealer holding periods before a purchased item can be resold or altered, and why does this module caution against assuming a single universal number?**
A) There is a single, universal 30-day holding period required in every jurisdiction with no exceptions
B) Holding periods are common (often 15-30 days) but vary meaningfully by state and municipality, so the specific figure must be confirmed for the store's own actual jurisdiction rather than assumed from an example
C) No jurisdiction actually requires a holding period
D) Holding periods only apply to firearms, never to jewelry

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This module cites specific examples (Florida, California, a Wisconsin municipality) precisely to illustrate the general pattern, while explicitly warning that these examples are not a substitute for confirming local law.

---

**4. What can happen even after a standard holding period has expired, if law enforcement has probable cause an item is stolen?**
A) Nothing; once the holding period ends, the dealer can freely dispose of the item regardless of any police concern
B) A law enforcement officer can place an additional hold order on the item (commonly up to 90 days, sometimes longer with court approval), during which the dealer must produce it for law enforcement and cannot dispose of it
C) Police hold orders only apply to firearms
D) Police hold orders eliminate the need for any standard holding period

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is a distinct, additional layer of protection beyond the standard holding period, specifically triggered by probable cause of theft.

---

**5. What is LeadsOnline, and what does it do?**
A) A private social media platform with no connection to law enforcement
B) The dominant electronic reporting platform used by pawn shops, secondhand dealers, and precious-metals buyers to transmit transaction records to law enforcement, which can search them against nationwide stolen-property databases including the NCIC feed
C) A tool used only by consumers to sell jewelry directly to buyers
D) A tool with no actual connection to stolen-property recovery

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: LeadsOnline is the standard industry mechanism connecting individual store transactions to law enforcement's ability to identify and recover stolen property, sometimes well after the original transaction.

---

**6. What should a complete internal buy-sell documentation record include?**
A) Only the item's purchase price, with no other details
B) Verified seller ID, a specific and detailed item description, photographs taken before any alteration, purchase price and payment method, and the transaction date/time
C) No documentation is actually required for jewelry purchases
D) Only a verbal description is sufficient; written records are unnecessary

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This complete record satisfies legal reporting/retention obligations and protects the store's own position if a piece's provenance is later questioned.

---

**7. What triggers the requirement to file IRS Form 8300, and how does this differ from the AML program requirement?**
A) Form 8300 and the AML program requirement are the same obligation with the same trigger
B) Form 8300 is triggered by receiving more than $10,000 in cash in a single or related transaction; this is a distinct federal requirement from the AML program obligation, which has its own separate $50,000/50%-of-price threshold and purpose
C) Form 8300 has no connection to jewelry businesses at all
D) Form 8300 only applies to businesses with fewer than 10 employees

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These are genuinely separate federal requirements with different triggers and purposes, and conflating them is a common but avoidable mistake.

**8. In the worked scenario, what is the correct response to a new client bringing in a high-value, unbranded piece for an urgent cash sale with no explanation of provenance?**
A) Skip standard procedures to complete the transaction quickly, since urgency from the client justifies shortcuts
B) Follow every procedural step precisely — ID verification, detailed photographs/description, required police reporting, and the full mandatory holding period — without shortcuts, and escalate to a manager if genuine concern arises
C) Refuse to interact with the client at all without any further consideration
D) Immediately accuse the client of possessing stolen property

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Following complete procedural discipline, especially in situations that warrant extra attention, protects the store, respects the client (who may be entirely legitimate), and preserves the actual function of these legal requirements.
