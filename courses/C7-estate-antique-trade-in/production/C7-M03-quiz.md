# C7-M03 Quiz: Dating and Attribution

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly applying the evidence-weighing and conflict-handling discipline, not memorizing every technique detail in isolation.

---

**1. What is the standard magnification for hand-held gemological examination, and why?**
A) 2x, because higher magnification distorts color
B) A 10x triplet loupe, which is the gemological industry standard, correcting for chromatic and spherical aberration
C) 50x, since more magnification is always better
D) Magnification standards don't exist in this field

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: 10x triplet loupes are the consistent, unanimous industry standard across gemological sources, with higher magnifications (14x, 20x) reserved as supplementary tools for resolving finer diagnostic detail.

---

**2. In the systematic examination sequence described in this module, why are hallmarks typically examined last?**
A) Because hallmarks are never actually useful evidence
B) Because they are often small and easy to miss if looked for too early or too casually, so working through silhouette, stones, and metalwork first builds context before the final, detailed hallmark search
C) Because hallmarks should only be examined by outside experts
D) Because hallmark examination requires no magnification at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A consistent, sequential examination order helps ensure nothing is missed, and saving the detailed hallmark search for last after other context has been gathered is part of that systematic approach.

---

**3. Why does this module distinguish between "independent" and "repeated" evidence when weighing an attribution claim?**
A) There is no meaningful distinction; all evidence should be weighted equally
B) Multiple claims that all derive from the same underlying source (like a family story repeated on a dealer's tag) count as one line of evidence, not several, while genuinely independent evidence types agreeing with each other is more meaningful
C) Repeated evidence is always more reliable than independent evidence
D) Independent evidence should always be disregarded in favor of family stories

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Counting derivative repetitions of the same original claim as if they were separate, independent confirmations creates false confidence; genuine independent agreement (e.g., hallmark plus construction plus stone cut) is what actually strengthens a conclusion.

---

**4. Why does this module recommend qualified language and date ranges over an exact, confidently stated year?**
A) Because exact years are always more accurate and preferred
B) Because stating a precise, unsupported year invents a false level of precision the evidence doesn't actually support, while a qualified range like "consistent with 1880-1900" honestly reflects what the evidence shows
C) Because clients always prefer vague answers
D) Because exact dates are illegal to state under any circumstance

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Honest, qualified language accurately represents the actual level of confidence the evidence supports, rather than sounding more impressive at the cost of accuracy.

---

**5. What is a "married jewel," and is it automatically evidence of fraud?**
A) A married jewel is always fraudulent and should never be sold
B) A married jewel is a piece combining a component from its original period with a later addition or alteration, either from the same or a different period; this has a long, legitimate history and is not automatically fraud
C) Married jewelry only refers to wedding rings specifically
D) Married jewelry is a term with no real meaning in the jewelry trade

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Family heirlooms are commonly re-set or updated legitimately over generations; the key professional obligation is to disclose the piece's mixed-component history honestly, not to treat the concept itself as inherently suspicious.

---

**6. What is the disciplined response when two pieces of evidence about a single piece point toward different conclusions?**
A) Immediately choose whichever conclusion makes the piece seem more valuable
B) Preserve the disagreement, test whether the conflict is real (e.g., the piece could be married), list plausible explanations, and document the reasoning rather than forcing false certainty
C) Ignore one piece of evidence entirely without investigation
D) Refuse to examine the piece any further

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Conflicting evidence is common and doesn't require an immediate, possibly false resolution — the professional response is to investigate carefully and be honest when a conclusion can't yet be reached.

**7. When does this module recommend referring a piece to outside expertise rather than resolving attribution in-house?**
A) Never; all attribution questions should always be resolved on the sales floor
B) For genuinely significant, high-value, or contested pieces where internal evidence conflicts can't be resolved with a loupe and reference knowledge, or where a claimed maker/origin requires lab-level testing
C) Only for pieces under $100 in value
D) Outside expertise is never useful or necessary in this field

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Recognizing the limits of in-house attribution and referring genuinely complex or high-stakes pieces to a credentialed gemologist, appraiser, or auction specialist is a mark of professionalism, not a failure.

**8. In the worked scenario, why does the associate describe the ring as a likely "married piece" rather than presenting it as uniformly from the 1890s?**
A) Because the band and the center stone show inconsistent evidence — the band's construction fits the claimed era, but the modern round brilliant center stone does not, suggesting the stone was replaced later
B) Because married pieces are always frauds and should be rejected outright
C) Because the associate simply guessed without examining the piece
D) Because the client's story should always be dismissed entirely

**Correct answer: A**
Explanation: Distinguishing the ring's components based on the actual evidence, rather than forcing a single uniform date onto the whole object, is the honest and professionally correct conclusion here.
