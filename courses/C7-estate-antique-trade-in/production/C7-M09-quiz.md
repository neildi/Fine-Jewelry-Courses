# C7-M09 Quiz: The Trade-In Sales Conversation

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly sequencing and applying the conversation skills, not memorizing the specific anecdote details.

---

**1. What is the first thing an associate should do in a trade-in conversation, before proceeding to valuation?**
A) Immediately state the trade-in offer amount
B) Read the emotional register of the conversation, since trade-in pieces are frequently tied to significant life transitions, and know when to listen rather than sell
C) Immediately pivot to discussing upgrade options
D) Ask the client to leave if they appear at all emotional

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Trade-in conversations carry more emotional weight than an average sales interaction, and reading this correctly before proceeding is the foundational skill this module opens with.

---

**2. What is a genuinely helpful, low-cost practice to offer a client navigating an emotionally significant trade-in?**
A) Refusing to discuss the piece's history at all
B) Offering to document the piece's story — photographing it and recording who gave it and what occasion it marked — before the transaction concludes
C) Rushing the client through the transaction as quickly as possible
D) Insisting the client not think about the piece's sentimental value

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This practice is independently and consistently recommended across multiple sources as something that genuinely helps clients process the decision to sell a sentimental piece.

---

**3. When delivering the valuation portion of a trade-in conversation, which prior module's approach should be applied directly?**
A) The valuation should simply be stated as a number with no further explanation
B) Module 4's approach of explaining the structure behind the offer (wholesale/retail markup gap, how the offer was built) rather than just stating a number
C) Valuation should never be discussed at all during a trade-in conversation
D) The client's original purchase receipt should always be the final word on value

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This module explicitly builds on Module 4's communication approach, since a client who understands the reasoning behind a trade-in figure is more likely to accept it as fair.

---

**4. If a piece appears to meet Module 7's criteria for consignment or auction referral rather than an in-house trade-in, what should the associate do?**
A) Ignore this and proceed with an in-house trade-in regardless, to avoid losing the immediate sale
B) Honestly raise the consignment or auction option, even though this may delay or forgo the immediate in-house sale, since it serves the client and the piece better
C) Never mention any alternative pathway under any circumstances
D) Pathway alternatives are irrelevant to a trade-in conversation

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is an explicit extension of Module 7's principle into the live client conversation — honesty about the best pathway for a piece builds trust, even at the cost of an immediate transaction.

---

**5. Why is a trade-in-to-upgrade transaction generally a stronger business outcome than a standalone sale of similar size?**
A) It isn't; trade-in transactions are always worse for the business than standalone sales
B) A customer trading a modest piece toward a substantially higher-value upgrade typically produces a larger overall transaction than a comparable standalone sale, which is why many stores build dedicated upgrade-credit programs around this moment
C) Trade-in transactions never lead to any additional sale
D) Upgrade programs have no real business rationale

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is the concrete business case for treating trade-in conversations as genuine sales opportunities, not just appraisal moments, and it's why enhanced trade-in credit for upgrades is a common, deliberate program structure.

---

**6. What is the recommended framing style for raising an upgrade opportunity during a trade-in conversation?**
A) A tentative, permission-seeking question like "would you like to see anything else?"
B) An assumptive, benefit-first statement made with genuine confidence that the client would want to see the relevant option, based on what's already been discussed
C) No framing at all; upgrades should never be mentioned
D) An aggressive, high-pressure demand that the client decide immediately

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Stating confidence that a client would want to see a relevant option, tied directly to what they've already expressed interest in, reads as natural and helpful rather than as an unwelcome pivot or a pressured pitch.

---

**7. In what order should the elements of a trade-in conversation generally be sequenced, per this module?**
A) Upgrade pitch first, emotional acknowledgment last
B) Emotional acknowledgment and any story-documentation first, honest valuation second, and the upgrade opportunity (if appropriate) raised only after those steps
C) All elements should happen simultaneously with no particular order
D) Valuation should never be discussed until after a sale is finalized

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This sequencing respects the client's emotional state and ensures the valuation conversation is handled honestly before any sales opportunity is introduced, rather than skipping ahead or treating the sale as more urgent than the client's own readiness.

---

**8. In the worked scenario, why does the associate wait until after the valuation conversation to mention the upgrade option?**
A) Because upgrade options should never be mentioned under any circumstances
B) Because the client's emotional readiness and an honest valuation conversation take priority, and raising the upgrade only after those are handled respects the client's own pace rather than rushing toward a sale
C) Because the associate forgot to mention it earlier
D) Because the client explicitly forbade any further discussion

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This sequencing is a deliberate, considered choice reflecting the module's core principle that emotional acknowledgment and honest valuation must come before any sales opportunity is raised.
