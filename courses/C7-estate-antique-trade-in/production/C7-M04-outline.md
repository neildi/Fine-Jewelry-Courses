# C7-M04 Outline: Trade-In Evaluation: Setting a Fair Offer Without Under- or Over-Paying

## Module Metadata
- Course: C7 - Estate, Antique & Trade-In
- Module: 4 of 10
- Persona: P2
- Working title: Trade-In Evaluation: Setting a Fair Offer Without Under- or Over-Paying
- Learning objective: Associates can calculate gold melt value directly from weight/purity/spot price, apply wholesale-not-retail logic to diamond and colored-stone valuation, build a complete trade-in offer incorporating condition adjustments and margin requirements, and explain a below-retail offer to a client in a way that builds understanding rather than defensiveness.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Evaluate a trade-in piece and set a fair offer that protects margin without alienating the client."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames trade-in evaluation as the point where estate expertise meets real financial and relational risk.
2. **The Core Reality: Retail, Wholesale, and Melt Are Three Different Numbers** — New-jewelry markup structure (100-300% over material cost); why trade-in evaluation starts from wholesale/melt, not retail.
3. **Valuing the Metal: The Melt Value Formula** — Weight × purity × spot price per gram; troy ounce conversion; melt as the objective floor.
4. **Valuing the Stones: Wholesale, Not Retail** — Natural diamond resale recovery (~20-40% of retail); lab-grown recovering far less; Rapaport benchmark reference.
5. **Building the Actual Offer** — Four components: melt value, stone wholesale value, condition adjustments, store margin requirement (~50% of realistic pre-owned resale as a common rule of thumb).
6. **Communicating the Offer Honestly and Confidently** — Explaining structure, not just stating a number; building client understanding rather than managing a reaction.
7. **A Worked Scenario: The Disappointed Client** — Illustrative synthesis of a full, structured explanation for a below-expectation offer.
8. **Practice Exercise** — Hand-calculating melt value; practicing the offer-explanation conversation.
9. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- New-jewelry retail markup range (100-300% over material/wholesale cost) is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across six independent sources (OYDisplay, OM Jewels Inc, CutGrade, Jackie Abraham, Diamonds and Gem, DiamondCheck-AI), with most sources clustering around 100-200% for diamonds specifically and up to 250-300% cited for luxury/branded jewelry broadly.
- The gold melt value formula (weight × purity × spot price per gram, with troy ounce = 31.1035 grams for per-gram conversion) is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT AND UNANIMOUS across four independent sources (MeltValue.com x2, GoldValueHub, MetalsRadar) — this is a mathematically precise, objectively verifiable formula with no meaningful source disagreement.
- Diamond resale recovery rate (~20-40% of retail for natural diamonds through realistic channels; lab-grown recovering far less, often near zero) is sourced primarily from CaratWire's detailed resale-math analysis (which provides a channel-by-channel recovery-rate table), cross-corroborated by DiamondCheck-AI ("resale... 25-50% of what was paid"), CutGrade, TopVegasBuyer, and Diamonds and Gem, all describing a consistent general range with some natural variance depending on specific channel and diamond quality.
- The Rapaport benchmark as the standard wholesale diamond-pricing reference is mentioned specifically by DiamondCheck-AI, consistent with well-established, widely-known industry practice (Rapaport price lists are referenced elsewhere in this course's research anchor documents as well).
- The offer-building framework (melt value + stone wholesale value + condition adjustments + store margin requirement, with a common rule-of-thumb ceiling of roughly 50% of realistic pre-owned resale value) synthesizes the general wholesale/retail-spread logic found across all sources above with a specific rule-of-thumb figure sourced from a Reddit r/jewelry community discussion (a secondary, non-authoritative source, appropriately flagged as such rather than presented as an industry standard).
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
