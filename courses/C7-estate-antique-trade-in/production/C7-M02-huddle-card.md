# C7-M02 Huddle Card: Reading a Piece

## 5-Minute Pre-Shift Discussion

**The one idea to remember:** Style, stones, and hallmarks all have to AGREE. A piece "from 1875" should look Victorian, have an old mine or old European cut stone, and carry period-consistent marks — not a modern round brilliant and a laser-cut setting.

## Period Cheat Sheet
- **Georgian (1714-1837):** Hand-made, foil-backed stones, closed-back settings
- **Victorian (1837-1901):** Silver-topped gold, closed-back, rose-cut/old mine diamonds
- **Art Nouveau (1890-1915):** Flowing, organic, nature motifs
- **Edwardian (1901-1915):** Delicate platinum filigree
- **Art Deco (1920s-30s):** Geometric, symmetrical, calibré/baguette stones, platinum/white gold
- **Retro (1930s-40s):** Chunky, bold, rose gold

## Diamond Cut Quick ID
- **Old mine cut** (Georgian/Victorian): squarish/cushion outline, small table, tall crown, big OPEN culet visible
- **Old European cut** (late Victorian/Edwardian/Deco): ROUND outline, similar tall proportions
- **Modern round brilliant:** minimal/no culet, bigger table, precise uniform facets — shouldn't be the "original" stone in a genuinely antique piece

## Clasp Dating (Brooches/Pins)
- C-clasp (simple hook, no safety) → before 1900
- Trombone clasp (sliding tube) → 1890-1940
- Rollover safety clasp (hinged lock) → after 1940

## Hallmark Basics
- 925 = sterling silver | 750 = 18k gold | 585 = 14k gold | 375 = 9k gold (UK)
- GF = gold-filled (real gold layer) | GP/HGE = thin plating (minimal value)
- British marks can carry exact date letters + duty marks (1784-1890)

## Today's Discussion Prompt
Practice the three-way cross-check out loud on the next estate piece that comes across the counter — style, stone cut, hallmark. Do they all agree?
