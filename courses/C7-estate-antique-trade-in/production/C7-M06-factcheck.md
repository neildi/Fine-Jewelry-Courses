# C7-M06 Fact-Check: Authentication

## Verification Status: Diamond-vs-moissanite testing limitation is among the most heavily cross-corroborated technical claims in this course; fraud-pattern section is a reasoned synthesis rather than independently sourced

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Acid testing's surface-only limitation; plating passing acid tests due to no contact with base metal.**
Status: Sourced directly and specifically from WiserMachine's "Gold-Filled vs Gold-Plated vs Solid Gold" XRF protocol guide, which explicitly states this exact mechanism ("A gold-plated piece with even 0.5 microns of pure gold on the surface passes the acid test perfectly"). This is a specialized, pawnshop/estate-buying-context source directly on point for this module's use case.

**2. XRF's quantitative composition reporting, ~30 micron penetration depth, and multi-point scan protocol.**
Status: Sourced directly from WiserMachine's counterfeit-gold-detection field guide, which specifies the exact scan protocol (ring: band/interior/setting; chain: two clasps and mid-link) and the "up to the 30 µm penetration limit" figure explicitly. This is a single specialized source but highly specific and directly applicable.

**3. Density-check cross-check recommendation for pieces valued above $1,000.**
Status: Sourced from the same WiserMachine guide, which specifically recommends density checking via precision scale and water displacement, plus plating-thickness mode on XRF, as confirmation tests for higher-value pieces.

**4. Thermal conductivity testers' inability to reliably distinguish diamond from moissanite.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT AND EXTENSIVELY CORROBORATED across at least eight independent sources: The Plumb Club, Electronics For U, VRXRF ("both materials conduct heat at similar rates and will trigger a 'diamond' reading"), JewelTrendPro's two separate articles, RareCarat, Sy Kessler's technical manual (a manufacturer specification document), imba.missouri.edu, and jewelryzone.net. This is among the most heavily cross-corroborated technical claims encountered in this course to date, with remarkable consistency in both the underlying mechanism described and the practical recommendation (always follow up with electrical testing).

**5. Electrical conductivity testing as the reliable diamond-vs-moissanite differentiator (diamond as insulator, moissanite as semiconductor).**
Status: Equally strongly corroborated across the same source set as item 4. Specific accuracy figures cited vary modestly (99.2% per one JewelTrendPro article citing a specific dual-mode device; 99.7% per another JewelTrendPro article; 99.8% cited elsewhere) but all sources agree on very high, though not absolute, reliability — this module's "highly reliable, not perfect" framing accurately reflects this range without overstating precision to a single decimal figure that varies by source.

**6. Neither thermal nor electrical conductivity testing can distinguish natural from lab-grown diamonds.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across JewelTrendPro's moissanite-vs-diamond guide ("Lab-grown diamonds register identically to natural diamonds on thermal tests") and jewelryzone.net's diamond-tester review ("no thermal conductivity tester alone can separate lab-grown diamonds from natural ones"). This is a well-documented, important limitation with no source disagreement found.

**7. Common fraud pattern descriptions (stone substitution inconsistent with setting construction, falsified/enhanced hallmarks, modern techniques on claimed-antiques, glued vs. mechanically-set stones).**
Status: IMPORTANT SOURCING NOTE — these specific fraud-pattern descriptions are this module's own reasoned synthesis, extending Module 2's construction/hallmark evidence categories and Module 3's conflicting-evidence framework into a deliberate-fraud context, rather than being independently sourced from a single dedicated fraud-detection reference during this research pass. The underlying logic (evidence-type violations as fraud indicators) is a direct, defensible extension of already-established, well-corroborated course content, but this specific section should be understood as applied synthesis rather than primary-sourced fraud-pattern documentation.

**8. Documentation-first, non-accusatory disclosure protocol for confirmed fraud findings.**
Status: This is a general professional-conduct principle consistent with the escalation and documentation practices referenced elsewhere in this course's research anchors (loss-prevention and compliance content in C4, referenced again in this course's own Module 8), rather than a single specific external citation for this module.

**9. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
