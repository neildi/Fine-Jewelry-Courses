# C7-M05 Outline: Appraisals for Estate and Trade-In: Fair Market Value, Retail Replacement, and Liquidation Value

## Module Metadata
- Course: C7 - Estate, Antique & Trade-In
- Module: 5 of 10
- Persona: P2
- Working title: Appraisals for Estate and Trade-In: Fair Market Value, Retail Replacement, and Liquidation Value
- Learning objective: Associates can precisely define retail replacement value, fair market value, and liquidation value; correctly match appraisal type to a client's actual purpose (insurance, estate/probate, donation, divorce, bankruptcy/urgent sale); and ask the necessary clarifying question before producing any appraisal.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Explain the differences between fair market value, retail replacement value, and liquidation value, and produce the correct appraisal type for a given purpose."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames correct appraisal-type matching as a real financial/legal issue, not a technicality, extending C6's RRV/FMV content with the estate-specific liquidation-value addition.
2. **The Three Value Types, Precisely Defined** — RRV, FMV (IRS legal definition), liquidation value; the RRV > FMV > liquidation relationship and percentage ranges.
3. **Matching Appraisal Type to Purpose** — Insurance → RRV; estate/probate/IRS Form 706/donation → FMV; divorce → FMV or marketable cash value (jurisdiction-dependent) or forced liquidation (lender/trustee context); bankruptcy/urgent sale → liquidation value; trade-in's conceptual overlap with liquidation logic.
4. **Why This Matters Beyond Compliance** — Real financial/legal harm from mismatched appraisal type during emotionally difficult client situations (estate settlement, divorce).
5. **A Worked Scenario: The Executor's Question** — Illustrative synthesis of proactively identifying the correct appraisal type for an estate-filing purpose.
6. **Practice Exercise** — Five-scenario appraisal-type matching exercise with a required clarifying question for each.
7. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- Retail replacement value, fair market value, and liquidation value definitions, and the RRV > FMV > liquidation relationship with specific percentage ranges (FMV ~30-60% of RRV; liquidation ~10-30%, with one source citing up to 45%), are STRONGLY CROSS-CORROBORATED across seven independent sources (JewelTrendPro's two separate appraisal guides, Antwerp Diamond Store, Lemonade, Maverick Jewelry Appraisals, Nidiffer Gem Lab, 210.jewelry's USPAP glossary, Diamond Source Jewelers), all describing matching or closely overlapping percentage ranges and value-type relationships.
- The IRS's specific legal definition of fair market value ("a willing buyer and a willing seller, neither being under any compulsion... both having reasonable knowledge") is sourced from JewelTrendPro's appraisal-vs-selling-price guide, which directly quotes this definition and attributes it to the IRS; independently corroborated by Antwerp Diamond Store, which cites the same standard as defined under "IRS Publication 561 and IRS Revenue Procedure 66-49" and by Maverick Jewelry Appraisals, which uses nearly identical language.
- Purpose-to-appraisal-type matching (insurance→RRV; estate/probate/Form 706/donation→FMV; divorce→FMV or marketable cash value depending on jurisdiction; bankruptcy/urgent sale→liquidation value) is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across Maverick Jewelry Appraisals (which explicitly lists each appraisal type by exact purpose), David Stern Jewelers' probate-specific guide, Huntington Gem Lab's estate-appraiser guide, JA-World's USA appraisal-definitions glossary (which specifically identifies "Marketable Cash Value" as "the most commonly requested value" within resale/divorce-oriented appraisals), and Alara Jewelry's consumer-facing appraisal-types guide.
- IRS Form 706 and the "appraisal older than 2 years can trigger IRS scrutiny" guidance is sourced from JewelTrendPro's "What Does Jewelry Appraisal Mean" article; this specific 2-year figure is a single-source claim and is treated in this module as a general caution about appraisal currency rather than a verified universal IRS rule, since it was not independently cross-confirmed against a primary IRS source in this research pass.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
