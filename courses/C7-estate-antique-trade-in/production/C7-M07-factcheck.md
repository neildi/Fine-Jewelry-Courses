# C7-M07 Fact-Check: The Consignment and Auction Pathway

## Verification Status: Strong cross-source agreement on both consignment-split ranges and auction fee mechanics; piece-to-pathway categorization framework flagged as reasoned synthesis

## Claims Reviewed

**1. General consignment commission-split ranges (60/40 most common; 50/50 to 80/20 range; luxury/high-end goods trending toward 60-70% to consignor).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across six independent sources: Jewelry Making Journal ("most common consignment percentage is 60/40... may be anywhere from 80/20... to 50/50"), Puppet Vendors ("Most common arrangement is a 60/40 split... Higher-value categories like furniture and luxury goods pay consignors more, often 70% to 90%"), Lion and Unicorn (60/40 as standard, some shops offering more), Grailhuntr, Oreate AI's consignment-contract guide (specifically noting 10-20% commission for luxury watches/jewelry given "the high unit price makes a large percentage split unsustainable"), and WorthLens.ai ("High-end jewelry consignment: 60-70% to seller"). This is strong, independent agreement across sources with different primary focuses (general consignment vs. jewelry-specific).

**2. Buyer's premium mechanics and typical range (20-28%, sliding scale at major houses).**
Status: CONFIRMED via NEAR-PRIMARY SOURCE — Rapaport's "Behind The Scenes" article provides specific historical sliding-scale figures (25% up to $100K, 20% to $2M, 12% above), and Christie's own official fee-explanation page confirms the buyer's-premium mechanism directly. Cross-corroborated by Antique Trader, Skinner, Signed Vintage Jewelry, Grailhuntr, Insta-Auction, Estimonia, and WorthLens.ai (citing Sotheby's current 27% for lots under $2M), all describing consistent mechanics with modestly varying specific percentages reflecting different houses and time periods.

**3. Seller's commission mechanics (10-25% typical range, often privately negotiated, sometimes waived for high-value consignments) and hammer-price calculation basis.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across the same source set as item 2, with particularly strong corroboration from Signed Vintage Jewelry ("The seller's premium is negotiated privately and rarely disclosed... Every seller's rate is negotiated"), Rapaport ("Sotheby's seller's premiums are stated to be 6 percent but may be negotiable"), Estimonia ("zero seller fees available for pieces estimated above $50,000"), and Grailhuntr's specific worked example confirming commission is "calculated on hammer price — not final price including buyer premium." This is one of the more heavily and consistently corroborated specific mechanics in this module.

**4. The 8-12 week auction consignment timeline (appraisal through payout).**
Status: Sourced specifically from Insta-Auction's operator playbook, a source written specifically as a step-by-step estate-jewelry-auction guide, directly on point. This is a single specific source for the exact figure, though the general multi-stage process it describes (appraisal, condition report, cataloging, marketing, sale, payout) is cross-consistent with the process described in less specifically time-boxed terms across Grailhuntr and Antique Trader.

**5. Piece-type-to-pathway categorization (routine pieces suit in-house; uncertain-demand pieces suit consignment; signed/provenanced/exceptional pieces suit auction).**
Status: IMPORTANT SOURCING NOTE — this categorization framework is this module's own reasoned synthesis, applying the evidence/attribution principles already established in Modules 3 and 6 (verified signatures, documented provenance, outside-expertise referral triggers) to a commercial pathway-decision context. It is not independently sourced from a single dedicated "which piece goes where" reference, though it is consistent with the general logic implicit across the consignment and auction sources reviewed (e.g., Estimonia's and MADLORDS' framing of auction houses as suited specifically to "an exceptional signed piece").

**6. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from any external source.
