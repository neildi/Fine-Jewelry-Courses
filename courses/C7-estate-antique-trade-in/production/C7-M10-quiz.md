# C7-M10 Quiz: Capstone: Running an Estate and Trade-In Buying Event

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This capstone quiz emphasizes correctly integrating concepts across the full course, not memorizing new isolated facts.

---

**1. What is the core trade-off between a partner-run and an in-house buying event model?**
A) There is no real difference between the two models
B) A partner-run event shares buying margin via commission but typically includes marketing support and specialized buyers, generating documented foot-traffic benefits; an in-house event retains full margin but requires the store's own marketing, staffing, and security planning
C) In-house events are always cheaper with no downsides
D) Partner-run events never actually increase foot traffic

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Neither model is universally better — the right choice depends on the store's existing estate expertise, staffing capacity, and whether the marketing/foot-traffic benefit of a partner-run event is worth the commission cost.

---

**2. What foot-traffic benefit does a partner-run buying event (e.g., the National Rarities model) commonly claim?**
A) No measurable foot-traffic benefit at all
B) A meaningfully increased number of store visits during the event window (one source cites an average of roughly 175 additional customer visits per event)
C) A guaranteed doubling of annual store revenue
D) Buying events never bring in any new customers

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This specific figure is the partner company's own self-reported marketing claim; it illustrates the real, commonly-cited rationale for this event model (increased foot traffic, with customers browsing the host store's cases and potentially spending their payout in-store).

---

**3. What should a store confirm with its bank in advance of a multi-day buying event with immediate cash payouts?**
A) Nothing; cash planning is unnecessary for buying events
B) Adequate on-hand cash reserves and same-day or next-day replenishment capability, since running out of cash mid-event stalls the operation and disappoints clients
C) Only the bank's opening hours
D) Cash reserves are only relevant for online transactions

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A buying event concentrates cash-flow demands into a short window, making advance planning with the store's bank a genuinely important operational step, not an afterthought.

---

**4. Why should a store verify its insurance coverage before running a buying event, rather than assuming existing coverage is sufficient?**
A) Insurance coverage is never actually relevant to a buying event
B) Elevated cash and inventory volumes during an event may exceed what a store's existing jewelers block policy or cash-on-premises coverage was designed for, so confirming adequate coverage in advance avoids discovering a gap only after the fact
C) All insurance policies automatically scale to cover any event size with no action needed
D) Insurance only matters for online retailers

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Proactively confirming coverage extends to the event's actual scale is a straightforward, avoidable-risk-reduction step that should happen well before the event, not during or after it.

**5. Does the volume and pace of a busy buying event justify skipping any of Module 6's authentication steps or Module 8's compliance/documentation steps?**
A) Yes, faster processing is always the priority during a high-volume event
B) No — these standards apply to every single transaction during a buying event exactly as they do on an ordinary day, regardless of how busy the event is
C) Only compliance steps can be skipped, never authentication steps
D) Only authentication steps can be skipped, never compliance steps

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A busy event is precisely when shortcuts feel most tempting and are most dangerous — the standards established throughout this course do not have a volume-based exception.

**6. Which prior module's conversational discipline (reading emotional register, sequencing valuation before any upgrade pitch) should be maintained during a high-volume buying event?**
A) This discipline only applies during quiet, low-traffic periods
B) Module 9's conversational discipline remains the standard for every single client during a buying event, not just the ones with time to spare
C) Conversational discipline is irrelevant during buying events
D) Only the upgrade-pitch portion of Module 9 matters during an event; emotional acknowledgment can be skipped

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Every client at a buying event deserves the same respectful, well-sequenced conversation as any other trade-in interaction, regardless of how many other clients are waiting.

**7. What is the recommended source of advance marketing support in a partner-run buying event model?**
A) The host store must always handle 100% of marketing itself with no outside support
B) The partner firm (e.g., National Rarities or Mayflower) typically handles direct mail, marketing materials, and advertising for the event, while the host store may still run its own digital/social channels
C) Marketing is never actually part of a buying event
D) Only word-of-mouth is used, with no planned marketing at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This division of marketing responsibility is a key part of the partner-run model's value proposition, distinguishing it from the fully self-managed in-house model.

**8. In the worked scenario, why does the estate lead insist on maintaining full procedural discipline despite a long line of waiting clients?**
A) Because a rushed mistake during a high-pressure event costs the store far more than a slower, careful process, even if it means some clients wait longer

C) Because procedural discipline only matters when the store isn't busy
D) Because the estate lead wants to discourage clients from waiting at all

Explanation: This directly applies the module's core principle — authentication and compliance discipline don't get relaxed under pressure, since the cost of a mistake during a high-volume event is genuinely higher, not lower.
