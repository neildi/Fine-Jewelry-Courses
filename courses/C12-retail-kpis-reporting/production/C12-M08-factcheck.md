# C12-M08 Fact-Check: Reporting Up: The One-Page Store Review

## Verification Status: Core reporting-structure claims are directly and specifically sourced from named retail-reporting-template sources; the cascading-goals framework is independently confirmed across multiple general HR sources; this module's specific application to jewelry-store target-setting is original synthesis clearly distinguished from the externally sourced general framework

## Claims Reviewed

**1. The context-insight-action three-part reporting structure, and the four-sentence verbal executive-summary script.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly and near-verbatim from retailbusinessacademy.com's "Reporting Scripts" article, which explicitly names this exact flow ("Every high-impact report follows this simple flow: 1. Context → 2. Insight → 3. Action") and provides the specific verbal script quoted in this module ("Overall, the business is performing __________. Sales are __________, driven by __________. The biggest opportunity right now is __________, and the biggest risk is __________. Our focus moving forward is __________.").

**2. The four-required-fields executive-summary structure (current-period result, comparison, driver, decision) and the "never end on an unexplained red or green KPI" discipline.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly and specifically from AnyGen's "Retail KPI Presentation Template" page, which explicitly names these exact four fields for "the executive-summary slide" ("Current-period result... Comparison... Driver... Decision: state one action, one accountable owner, and one due date rather than ending with an unexplained red or green KPI").

**3. The "not a dashboard dump" framing and the broader structure from which this module's one-page compression derives.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced from the same AnyGen source ("It is not a dashboard dump: every slide should answer one management question using actual-versus-target and period-over-period comparisons"), independently corroborated in structure (though not exact wording) by AnyGen's companion "Retail Sales Presentation Template" page, which describes a very similar executive-summary-plus-drivers-plus-decisions structure.

**4. The one-page/decision-ready principle for leadership-level reporting.**
Status: Independently corroborated by getamply.co's "Retail Store Visit Report" article, which describes a similar one-page structure in a related but distinct context (store-visit reporting rather than sales/KPI reporting): "For board-level reporting, a one-page format works best: overall network score, three key wins, three key risks, and recommended actions. Keep it decision-ready." This reinforces the general one-page/decision-ready principle across two independent sources covering related but not identical reporting contexts.

**5. The cascading-goals framework (top-level goals broken into department/team/individual targets, visible traceability, employee involvement in goal-shaping).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent HR/goal-setting sources: Lattice's "How to Use Cascading Goals" ("Each department, team, and employee then sets their own goals, which align with and support the level above them"), AIHR's "Cascading Goals: 5 Examples + How-To for HR Leaders" ("Involve staff in shaping these goals to build ownership"), and Leapsome's "Cascading Goals Framework" ("Picture a goal cascade like a tree with ambitious top-level goals branching out into department, team, and individual objectives"). These are general business/HR sources, not retail- or jewelry-specific.

**6. This module's application of the cascading-goals framework specifically to jewelry-store sales-target-to-associate-target setting (accounting for scheduled hours, role-specific targets like a bridal specialist vs. general associate).**
Status: ORIGINAL synthesis created for this module, applying the confirmed general cascading-goals framework above to a retail-specific context. This is not itself an externally sourced claim, though it is consistent with, and builds directly on, this project's own prior, independently-verified content in C11-M06 (Scheduling to Traffic and Peak Coverage), which already establishes the relevance of role- and hours-specific staffing considerations in this course's broader content.

**7. Worked scenario (the diagnosed, specific staffing request that gets approved vs. a vague ask) and practice exercise.**
