# C12-M05 Quiz: Retention and Client-Book KPIs

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. According to Bluecore's benchmark data (via stylograph.ai), what is the approximate repeat purchase rate for luxury goods, and how does it compare to apparel retail?**
A) Luxury goods have a higher repeat purchase rate than apparel
B) Luxury goods sit at roughly 9.9%, well below apparel's 20-26% and health and beauty's 21.5%
C) There is no meaningful difference between luxury and apparel repeat purchase rates
D) Luxury retail repeat purchase rates cannot be measured

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This means roughly nine out of every ten luxury customers who make a significant purchase never return to that brand, though brands with strong clienteling can push top-tier segments above 40%.

---

**2. Why hasn't heavy investment in automated CRM technology closed luxury retail's retention gap?**
A) CRM technology has fully solved this problem
B) Algorithmic personalization contradicts the scarcity, exclusivity, and personal attention that define the luxury purchase experience — a personalized email that's clearly automated can feel worse than a plainly generic one
C) CRM technology is too expensive for luxury brands to afford
D) Luxury customers do not use email or text messaging at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This reinforces why physical, personal contact from a named associate consistently outperforms scaled digital outreach for this specific customer segment.

---

**3. Why is the 30-day period following a major purchase identified as the single biggest leak in the retention funnel?**
A) Nothing significant happens during this window at most luxury brands, despite it being when the customer is most emotionally engaged with the purchase and the brand
B) This period is legally required to be silent under consumer protection law
C) Customers are unreachable during the first 30 days after a purchase
D) The 30-day window is actually the least important period for retention

**Correct answer: A**
Explanation: A $2-5 handwritten note during this window can produce a 4-9% response rate, compared to roughly 0.12% for email, making it a highly cost-effective retention intervention.

---

**4. What is the named-associate-ownership model that high-retention luxury and jewelry businesses use?**
A) Customer relationships are routed entirely through automated systems with no individual staff involvement
B) Ownership of each significant customer relationship is assigned to a specific, named associate who takes responsibility for periodic, primarily physical contact
C) Customer relationships are randomly reassigned to a different associate every visit
D) Only the store manager is allowed to contact any customer

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This deliberate lack of scalability is actually the point — it signals that the customer is worth the time of an actual person, not a database entry.

**5. What is a realistic daily personal-contact target per associate, according to this module's client-book KPI guidance?**
A) 50-100 contacts per day
B) 5 to 10 personal contacts per associate per day, a range that holds up over months without collapsing under floor pressure
C) Contacts should never be tracked as a daily target
D) 1 contact per associate per week

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: More aggressive targets tend to collapse the first time the floor gets busy, making this modest, sustainable range more effective over time.

**6. How should a store read its own repeat-purchase rate, according to this module?**
A) Only against a single fixed industry-wide number, with no other context
B) Quarterly, against the store's own prior-year performance, consistent with the trend-based benchmarking principle established elsewhere in this course
C) Repeat-purchase rate should never be tracked or reviewed
D) Daily, compared against every other store in the district with no adjustment for size

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This mirrors the same trend-based benchmarking principle this course established for conversion rate in Module 2.

**7. What is the CLV (customer lifetime value) formula, and why does it make the retention argument concrete?**
A) CLV = total sales divided by total inventory value
B) CLV = average purchase value × purchase frequency × average customer lifespan; it shows that a customer with several smaller purchases over years can generate more total value than a customer with one larger purchase who never returns
C) CLV has no relationship to purchase frequency or customer lifespan
D) CLV can only be calculated for online retail businesses

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Research cited from Bain & Company indicates a 5% retention increase can raise profits by 25-95% across industries, a disproportionately large effect for a high-margin category like jewelry.

**8. Why does this module flag a discrepancy between the stylograph.ai retention figures and a different figure (60-70%/80%) found in another source during research?**
A) There is no discrepancy; both sources report identical figures
B) The competing source uses a fictional example business, shows signs of templated/AI-generated content, and may be measuring a conceptually different metric ("customer retention rate" vs. "repeat purchase rate") without acknowledging the distinction
C) The 60-70%/80% figure should always be used instead of the 9.9% figure
D) Retention benchmarks are entirely unknowable and neither figure should ever be referenced

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This module treats the stylograph.ai figures as more credible given their specific, attributed sourcing, while flagging both the sourcing concern and the definitional distinction for human review.
