# C12-M07 Quiz: Diagnosing a Miss

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. What is the sales decomposition formula this module's diagnostic decision tree is built on?**
A) Sales = employee count × store hours
B) Sales = traffic × conversion rate × average transaction value
C) Sales = inventory value × markdown percentage
D) Sales cannot be meaningfully decomposed into component drivers

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A genuine diagnosis checks traffic and conversion against a prior baseline first, since the answer at that first branch determines an entirely different set of follow-up questions.

---

**2. If traffic is down but conversion is holding steady, what does this indicate, and what follow-up question separates two different problems within this branch?**
A) This indicates a problem entirely inside the store, unrelated to traffic
B) The store is performing fine on the people who did arrive; the problem sits outside the store, and the key follow-up question is whether passing traffic itself is down (a location/marketing question) or passing traffic is steady while capture rate has dropped (a storefront/window question)
C) This branch requires no further investigation
D) This always means the store's staff are underperforming

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These are genuinely different problems with different owners and different fixes.

---

**3. If traffic is holding steady but conversion has dropped, what is the recommended approach?**
A) Immediately cut staff hours to reduce costs
B) Work the internal funnel in order: check whether layout/category placement changed, whether in-store engagement dropped, whether checkout lines lengthened, or whether staffing coverage at peak hours fell short
C) Assume the problem is entirely due to external market conditions
D) There is no recommended approach for this specific branch

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This branch connects directly to Module 2's foot-traffic-vs-conversion measurement content.

---

**4. What is the specific trap this module warns about when both traffic and conversion are down together?**
A) There is no trap; cutting staff hours is always the correct response to falling traffic
B) The instinctive response of cutting scheduled hours to control labor cost actively worsens conversion further and accelerates the decline
C) Staffing levels have no relationship to conversion rate
D) This situation should always be blamed entirely on external weather conditions

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is a genuinely counterintuitive trap: the natural cost-control reaction to a traffic problem actively worsens the conversion half of the same problem.

---

**5. If neither traffic nor conversion has fallen, but total sales still missed plan, what does this indicate?**
A) The dashboard data must be incorrect, since sales cannot miss without traffic or conversion falling
B) The problem isn't whether people buy, but what they buy — pointing to pricing, product mix shifts, or attachment/add-on rates as the likely drivers
C) This situation is impossible and cannot occur in practice
D) This always indicates a staffing problem

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This points directly to Module 3's ATV/UPT/mix content and its mix-versus-behavior distinction as the exact tool needed to isolate which driver is at play.

---

**6. Beyond traffic, conversion, and basket size, what additional drivers does the full eight-driver framework identify as worth ruling out before concluding a miss is purely behavioral?**
A) Only weather and holidays matter; no other drivers exist
B) Availability (stock-outs on what customers wanted), promotion effects (an underperforming promotion), and returns (an unusual spike quietly erasing an otherwise strong week)
C) Employee birthdays and personal schedules
D) There are no additional drivers beyond traffic, conversion, and basket size

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A well-run floor can still miss plan badly if these specific factors are at play, so ruling them out prevents misattributing the miss to selling behavior alone.

---

**7. What is the general five-step diagnostic sequence this module describes as applicable to any KPI variance, not just sales?**
A) Guess, blame, discipline, hope, repeat
B) Define the variance clearly, locate where it occurred, decompose it into drivers, test the likely root causes against actual data, and define the corrective action, its owner, and its timing
C) Ignore the variance until it resolves itself
D) Immediately escalate every variance to senior leadership without any internal investigation

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This general sequence applies to a shrinkage-rate miss, a retention miss, or a GMROI miss just as readily as a sales miss.

---

**8. In the worked scenario, what was the actual cause of the sales miss initially blamed on rainy-week traffic?**
A) Traffic actually was down significantly due to the rain, confirming the initial assumption
B) Traffic held steady against the same week last year; the real cause was a conversion drop from a peak-hour staffing gap, where a single still-training associate was scheduled alone during the store's highest-traffic Saturday afternoon window
C) The miss was never actually explained or resolved
D) The cause was a sudden, unexplained drop in average transaction value with no identifiable driver

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The fix was adjusting next week's schedule to match peak-hour coverage to the traffic pattern the dashboard already tracked, not a traffic-generating marketing push.
