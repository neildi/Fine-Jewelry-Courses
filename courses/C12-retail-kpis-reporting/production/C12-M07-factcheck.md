## Verification Status: The core decision-tree logic and the eight-driver framework are both CONFIRMED as directly, specifically sourced from named, detailed retail-diagnostics sources; no MUST VERIFY items identified in this module's research

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Sales = traffic × conversion × average transaction value decomposition.**
Status: CONFIRMED, this is the same formula this course established across Modules 1-4, independently corroborated across multiple additional sources consulted for this module (Scribd's "MECE Framework" document, dmsretail.com's "Retail Cognition System in Action," both independently stating this same core decomposition).

**2. The four-branch decision tree (traffic down/conversion holding; traffic holding/conversion down; both down; neither down) with specific follow-up questions for each branch.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly and specifically from Ariadne's "Traffic Problem or Conversion Problem?" article, which explicitly lays out this exact branching logic with named branch labels ("Branch 1, visits down, conversion holding," etc.) and the specific follow-up questions cited in this module (capture rate vs. passing traffic in Branch One; layout, engagement, checkout, and staffing questions in Branch Two).

**3. The staffing-cut trap in Branch Three ("the standard response to falling traffic is cutting hours, which lowers conversion further and accelerates the decline").**
Status: CONFIRMED, stated explicitly and specifically in the same Ariadne source: "Treat traffic first but check staffing immediately, because the standard response to falling traffic is cutting hours, which lowers conversion further and accelerates the decline." This is treated as a genuinely important, well-sourced, counterintuitive warning central to this module.

**4. Branch Four's connection to pricing, mix, and attachment as the remaining drivers when neither traffic nor conversion has fallen.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced from the same Ariadne source ("Branch 4, neither down but sales down. Conversion and visits are healthy, so the problem is what people buy, not whether they buy: pricing, mix, or attachment"). This module's explicit tie to C12-M03's mix-vs-behavior distinction is this module's own original synthesis connecting two already-independently-sourced pieces of course content.

**5. The eight-driver "full driver tree" (traffic, conversion, basket, price, mix, availability, promotion, returns).**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from umbrex.com's "Diagnosing Performance and Finding Root Causes" article (part of the same Retail KPI Dashboard & Weekly Business Review Playbook series already used and confirmed reliable in C12-M06), which explicitly names this exact eight-driver decomposition for a "sales diagnosis sequence": "Start with total sales, isolate where the variance occurred, decompose into traffic, conversion, basket, price, mix, availability, promotion, and returns, then agree on the corrective action."

**6. The general five-step diagnostic sequence (define, locate, decompose, test, act).**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from the same umbrex.com article, which lays out a six-step sequence (this module's five steps, plus a sixth step assessing controllability, which this module folds into the "test" and "act" steps for simplicity and floor-readiness). The umbrex.com source's exact sequence: "First, define the variance clearly. Second, locate where the variance occurred. Third, decompose the variance into drivers. Fourth, test the likely root causes. Fifth, identify whether the cause is controllable... Sixth, define the action, owner, and timing."

**7. Independent corroboration of structured-diagnosis principles from a separately consulted source.**
Status: scoopanalytics.com's "The 10-Hypothesis Problem" article independently corroborates the general principle that retail underperformance diagnosis benefits from a defined, repeatable set of hypotheses tested systematically rather than ad hoc investigation, reinforcing this module's overall structured-diagnosis approach, though this specific source was not directly quoted in this module's text.
