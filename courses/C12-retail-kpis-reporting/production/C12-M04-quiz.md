# C12-M04 Quiz: Inventory KPIs: Sell-Through, Turn, GMROI

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. What is the typical jewelry-industry inventory turnover benchmark, and why is it structurally slow rather than a sign of poor management?**
A) 8-12 turns per year, similar to apparel retail
B) Typically 1-2 turns per year (boutique/luxury stores often 0.7-1.5), driven by high per-unit price, deliberate purchase decisions, and long customer research/browsing periods
C) Jewelry stores should always aim to match grocery-store turnover rates
D) Inventory turn is not a meaningful metric for jewelry retail

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A manager who benchmarks jewelry turn against general-retail apparel or grocery numbers will wrongly conclude their inventory is badly mismanaged when it's actually normal for the category.

---

**2. Why does jewelry's GMROI typically run lower than the general retail "healthy" benchmark of 2-3, even though jewelry margins are often high?**
A) Jewelry margins are actually low, not high
B) GMROI multiplies margin percentage by turn rate, and jewelry's high margins are partially offset by its structurally low turnover — mass-market jewelry GMROI often clusters near 1.0-1.1
C) GMROI has no relationship to either margin or turnover
D) Jewelry stores should never track GMROI since it's not applicable to the category

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A mass-market jewelry GMROI near 1.0 isn't automatically alarming given this category-specific context, though a GMROI meaningfully below even that modest benchmark is worth investigating.

---

**3. What discrepancy does this module flag regarding inventory turn benchmarks, and how does it recommend treating it?**
A) There is no discrepancy; all sources agree exactly on a single number
B) Most sources converge on 1-2 turns/year, but one source cites a notably higher 2.0-4.1x range; this module treats the 1-2x range as primary given broader corroboration, while flagging the discrepancy for human review
C) The higher 2.0-4.1x figure should always be used instead of the 1-2x range
D) Inventory turn benchmarks are entirely unknowable and should not be used at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The outlier source's own distinction between traditional store-based chains and faster-turning DTC/online players may help explain the difference.

**4. According to jewelry-specific aging guidance, what is considered a normal aging window for engagement rings and bridal jewelry?**
A) Bridal jewelry should always sell within 30 days or be considered a failure
B) 12 to 18 months is considered normal, given the category's deliberate, milestone-driven purchase cycle
C) Bridal jewelry has no meaningful aging tolerance and should never be tracked
D) Bridal jewelry should sell within the same 6-month window as fashion jewelry

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Treating all jewelry inventory against a single aging threshold, regardless of category, produces bad decisions in both directions.

---

**5. What is the tiered markdown ladder this module recommends for aged jewelry inventory?**
A) A single 50% discount applied uniformly the moment any piece turns 6 months old
B) A first markdown of 10-15% at 12-18 months, a second markdown of 20-30% at 18-24 months, and a final clearance markdown of 40%+ after 24 months
C) No markdowns should ever be applied to jewelry inventory under any circumstances
D) Markdowns should be applied randomly with no consistent schedule

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This ladder structure removes emotional decision-making from aged-inventory management by letting a schedule, not a manager's reluctance, decide when a markdown happens.

---

**6. What specific non-discount technique involves paying a bonus to whichever associate sells a particular aged piece?**
A) A markdown
B) A bounty or spiff, placed on items just entering the aging process, to incentivize staff to feature that piece in customer presentations before a markdown becomes necessary
C) A vendor consignment arrangement
D) A customer loyalty discount

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This gives staff a direct financial incentive to actively work an aging piece into sales conversations rather than letting it sit passively in a case.

---

**7. What is bundling, as a technique for moving aged inventory?**
A) Combining multiple discount codes into a single larger discount
B) Pairing an aging, slow-moving piece with a strong seller (e.g., a slow-moving wedding band with a popular engagement-ring style) to move the older item without a direct price cut on either piece
C) Storing aged inventory in a bundled group in the back room, out of customer view
D) A method that only applies to online retail, not in-store jewelry sales

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Bundling is one of several specific, non-discount techniques for moving aged stock alongside or instead of a markdown.

---

**8. Why does this module recommend pairing an aging report's raw age data with category-specific aging tolerances before deciding whether a piece needs attention?**
A) All jewelry categories should be treated identically regardless of type
B) A ring that's 14 months old is a non-event in bridal, but the same 14-month age on a fashion-jewelry piece is well past its practical window and belongs in the markdown ladder immediately
C) Category-specific tolerances are irrelevant to markdown decisions
D) Aging reports should never be used to make markdown decisions

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Applying a single aging threshold across all categories produces incorrect markdown decisions in both directions — too aggressive for bridal, too lenient for fashion.
