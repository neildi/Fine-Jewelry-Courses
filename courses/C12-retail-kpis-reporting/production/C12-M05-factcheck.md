# C12-M05 Fact-Check: Retention and Client-Book KPIs

## Verification Status: The core luxury repeat-purchase-rate figure is sourced at one remove from Bluecore's benchmark data and should be independently confirmed against the original report; client-book activity KPIs are sourced from a detailed, jewelry-specific vendor source with real, named client examples; a meaningful discrepancy exists against a lower-quality, likely templated source using different retention terminology

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Luxury goods repeat purchase rate (~9.9%), apparel (20-26%), health and beauty (21.5%).**
Status: Sourced from stylograph.ai's "Luxury Retail Repeat Purchase Rate: 9.9% vs Beauty's 21.5%" article, which attributes the core 9.9% figure to "Bluecore's benchmark data" as reported via Retail Brew, and the apparel figure to Mobiloud. This module's research accessed this figure at one remove (a secondary blog citing the primary benchmark firm) rather than the original Bluecore report directly. MUST VERIFY: locate and confirm the original Bluecore/Retail Brew source directly before using this specific figure in any external-facing or high-stakes context (e.g., formal target-setting presented to corporate leadership).

**2. Target repeat purchase rate for luxury (25-35%) and top-tier achievable rate (40%+).**
Status: Sourced from the same stylograph.ai article, labeled there only as "industry benchmark" without a more specific named primary source. This is a secondary MUST VERIFY item given the less specific sourcing compared to the Bluecore-attributed figure above.

**3. MUST VERIFY / discrepancy: businessplankit.com's competing retention figures (60-70% "robust" for luxury retail, ~80% general jewelry benchmark).**
Status: FLAGGED and NOT used as a primary source in this module. This source uses a fictional example business ("Lumiere Luxe") throughout its KPI discussion, exhibits characteristics consistent with templated or AI-generated business-plan content (near-identical repeated sentence structures across its KPI sections), and provides no identifiable primary source or named benchmark study for its figures. This module's own text notes a plausible explanation for the discrepancy: "customer retention rate" and "repeat purchase rate" are conceptually distinct metrics that can be defined and measured differently, and the two sources may not be measuring precisely the same underlying customer behavior. This distinction is this module's own original observation, offered as a plausible reconciliation rather than a confirmed resolution of the discrepancy.

**4. The technology/personalization-paradox explanation for why automated CRM hasn't improved luxury retention.**
Status: Sourced from stylograph.ai's article. This is a reasoned, internally consistent explanation rather than an independently, quantitatively verified causal claim; it should be read as a credible analytical framework rather than a proven fact.

**5. The 30-day post-purchase window as the primary retention leak point, and the physical-mail-vs-email response-rate comparison (4-9% vs. ~0.12%).**
Status: The response-rate figures are attributed by stylograph.ai to the ANA (Association of National Advertisers, formerly the DMA), a real, well-established advertising trade association that has historically published exactly this kind of response-rate benchmark data. This module's research did not independently locate and verify the specific original ANA publication, so this remains a secondary-source citation, though the ANA is a credible, real organization for this type of data, unlike the templated source flagged in item 3.

**6. The named-associate-ownership model as the pattern among high-retention luxury brands (e.g., Cartier/Richemont client-dossier practices).**
Status: Sourced from stylograph.ai's article, which names specific real companies (Richemont, Cartier, LVMH) in its discussion. This module's research did not independently verify the specific internal practices attributed to these named companies, so these specific company-practice claims should be treated as reported characterizations from a secondary source rather than independently confirmed facts.

**7. Bain & Company/Harvard Business Review: a 5% retention increase can raise profits by 25-95% across industries.**
Status: This is a widely cited, long-standing statistic in customer-retention literature, traceable to well-known Bain & Company/Frederick Reichheld research from the 1990s that has been repeatedly cited across the retention-marketing field for decades. This module's research accessed this figure via stylograph.ai's citation of Harvard Business Review rather than the original Bain publication directly; given its extremely wide and long-standing citation history across independent, credible sources, this figure is treated as reliable, though a fully rigorous citation chain would trace it to the original Bain/Reichheld research directly.

**8. Client-book activity KPIs (contacts/associate/day target 5-10, response rate floor ~1-in-10, appointments booked/kept, repeat-purchase rate read quarterly) and named client examples (Herzog Jewelers, Schmitt Jewelers, Wilson Diamonds, H.L. Gross and Bro., Adorn).**
Status: CONFIRMED as sourced directly and specifically from Clientbook's "The 30/60/90 Day Plan to Grow Jewelry Store Revenue From the Customers You Already Have" article, a detailed, jewelry-specific source citing real, named jewelry retailers and specific attributed figures (e.g., "Herzog Jewelers saw a 10.8 percent response rate," "Wilson Diamonds sees a 98 percent read rate... over 71 percent response rate"). This is a vendor source — Clientbook sells a clienteling CRM product — and should be read with that commercial context in mind, since the source has an incentive to present its own platform favorably. However, the specific performance figures cited are attributed to named, real, checkable jewelry retailers rather than presented as anonymous or generic industry averages, which increases their credibility relative to a fully generic, unattributed claim.

**9. CLV formula (average purchase value × purchase frequency × average customer lifespan).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across numerous independent general-business sources: Shopify, IBM, Twilio, Ramp, CMSWire, and Zoho all state functionally identical formulas for this core CLV calculation.
