# C12-M04 Outline: Inventory KPIs: Sell-Through, Turn, GMROI

## Module Metadata
- Course: C12 - Retail KPIs & Reporting for Jewelry Store Leaders
- Module: 4 of 8
- Persona: P7 (First-Time Store Leader), P8 (Multi-Store Leader), per official course architecture
- Working title: Inventory KPIs: Sell-Through, Turn, GMROI
- Learning objective: Store leaders can apply jewelry-specific benchmarks for inventory turn and GMROI (rather than general-retail benchmarks), apply category-specific aging tolerances, and apply a tiered markdown ladder plus non-discount techniques to manage aged inventory systematically.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: Directly deepens the C12 terminal outcome "define and calculate...sell-through, GMROI" from Module 1, adding jewelry-specific benchmark depth and practical markdown discipline.

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames aged inventory as the single biggest inventory-management risk in a slow-turn category.
2. **Jewelry's Inventory Turn Is Genuinely, Structurally Slow** — 1-2 turns/year benchmark (boutique 0.7-1.5), explicit contrast with general retail, and why this is structural, not a management failure.
3. **GMROI in Jewelry Runs Lower Than General Retail, and That's Expected Too** — Jewelry GMROI ~1.0-1.1 (mass) to 3.0-3.4 (luxury boutique) vs. general 2-3 "healthy" benchmark, explained via the margin-times-turn relationship.
4. **A Meaningful Discrepancy Worth Flagging** — One outlier source (2.0-4.1x) vs. the more corroborated 1-2x range; explicit MUST VERIFY treatment.
5. **What "Normal" Aging Looks Like, By Category** — Bridal (12-18mo), fashion (~6mo), classic fine jewelry (12-24mo), seasonal (must clear within season).
6. **A Tiered Markdown Ladder That Removes the Emotion** — 10-15% at 12-18mo, 20-30% at 18-24mo, 40%+ after 24mo.
7. **Beyond Discounting: Bounties, Bundling, and Vendor Returns** — Non-discount techniques for moving aged stock.
8. **Putting the Vocabulary to Work** — Aging-report habit combined with category-specific tolerance before acting.
9. **Practice Exercise** — Running an aging report, bucketing by category, and applying the markdown ladder.
10. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The jewelry-specific inventory turn benchmark (1-2 turns/year, boutique/luxury stores often 0.7-1.5) is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sources: jewel360.com's "What Is a Good Inventory Turnover Rate for Your Jewelry Store?" (1-2x/year generally, 0.8-1.5x for boutiques), jewelrybuydirect.com's "How to Improve Inventory Turnover for Jewelry Businesses" (1-2 turns/year), Luxare's "Jewelry Store Reporting and Analytics Tracking" (1.0-1.5x/year), and Retail Operations Toolkit's benchmarks page (1-3x/year for Luxury & Jewelry). instoremag.com's Brain Squad trade-press piece independently cites an even lower range (0.7-1.2x/year) specifically for American retail jewelry stores, further corroborating the low end of this range.
- MUST VERIFY: one source, aislestock.com's "2026 Benchmarks (Signet Jewelers 10-K)," cites a notably higher range of 2.0x to 4.1x per year for jewelry retailers. This is flagged as an outlier against the otherwise well-corroborated 1-2x consensus and may reflect a difference in scope (e.g., this source may be measuring across a broader mix of jewelry-adjacent retail formats, including faster-turning DTC/online players, which the source itself distinguishes from "traditional store-based chains at the low end"). This module treats the 1-2x range as primary given its broader corroboration, but flags the discrepancy for human review, particularly regarding how DTC/online jewelry retailers may differ meaningfully from a single-location brick-and-mortar store.
- The jewelry-specific GMROI benchmark (mass-market ~1.0-1.1, luxury boutique 3.0-3.4) is sourced from Toolio's "The Complete Guide to GMROI for Retail Brands" (Luxury & Jewelry "$1.10 for mass jewelry to $3.40 for luxury boutiques") and independently corroborated in direction (though not exact figures) by Apshan's "GMROI: Formula, Benchmarks and Fashion Guide" ("Jewelry: lower, often near 1, where high margins are offset by very slow turnover") and Luxare's reporting-analytics page ("Above 1.0 is profitable; jewelry benchmarks sit around $1.00"). Three independent sources converge on a GMROI near 1.0 for mass-market jewelry specifically, which is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT.
- The general-retail GMROI "healthy" benchmark (2-3, with 3.5+ excellent) is sourced from Toolio's guide, used here for explicit contrast with the jewelry-specific figure.
- Category-specific aging tolerances (bridal 12-18mo, fashion ~6mo, classic fine jewelry 12-24mo, seasonal must-clear-in-season) are sourced directly from jewel360.com's "Inventory Aging Reports: When To Mark Down Expensive..." article, a jewelry-specific operations source.
- The tiered markdown ladder (10-15% at 12-18mo, 20-30% at 18-24mo, 40%+ after 24mo) is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across two independent, closely aligned jewelry-specific sources: jewel360.com's aging-reports article and a jewel360-affiliated LinkedIn post describing the same three-tier structure with matching percentages and timeframes. Older jewelry-trade sources (JCK's "Out With the Old, In With the New Inventory" from 2016, citing a similar ~30%-then-50% two-step ladder at the 12-18 month mark) independently corroborate the same general tiered-discipline pattern, though with somewhat different specific percentages, suggesting this general approach has been stable industry practice for at least a decade.
- The bounty/spiff, bundling, and vendor consignment/return techniques are sourced from multiple independent jewelry-trade sources: Abbott Jewelry Systems' "Aged Inventory Management" blog (bounty/spiff technique, tiered markdown starting at 30%), jewel360.com (bundling), and southernjewelrynews.com's "7 Steps to Manage Aging Inventory at full price" (staff training to feature aging pieces, vendor exchange arrangements).
- The practice exercise is an ORIGINAL synthesis designed for this module, not an externally sourced case.
