# C12-M07 Outline: Diagnosing a Miss

## Module Metadata
- Course: C12 - Retail KPIs & Reporting for Jewelry Store Leaders
- Module: 7 of 8
- Persona: P7 (First-Time Store Leader), P8 (Multi-Store Leader), per official course architecture
- Working title: Diagnosing a Miss
- Learning objective: Store leaders can apply the sales = traffic × conversion × ATV decomposition as a sequential diagnostic decision tree, correctly identify which of four branches applies to a given sales miss, avoid the staffing-cut trap when both traffic and conversion are down, rule out availability/promotion/returns as drivers before concluding a miss is purely behavioral, and apply the general five-step diagnostic sequence to any KPI variance.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: Directly fulfills the C12 terminal outcome "diagnose a sales miss by KPI" per this project's official course architecture.

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames this module as where the KPI vocabulary and dashboard from Modules 1-6 actually earn their keep.
2. **The Sales Equation, and Why Order Matters** — Sales = traffic × conversion × ATV, and the sequential-check-first discipline.
3. **Branch One: Traffic Down, Conversion Holding** — Catchment/marketing vs. storefront-capture distinction.
4. **Branch Two: Traffic Holding, Conversion Down** — Working the internal funnel in order (layout, engagement, checkout, peak-hour staffing), tied to Module 2.
5. **Branch Three: Both Down** — The staffing-cut trap that worsens the exact problem it's meant to fix.
6. **Branch Four: Neither Down, But Sales Down** — Points to Module 3's ATV/UPT/mix content and mix-vs-behavior distinction.
7. **The Full Driver Tree: Beyond Traffic and Conversion** — Availability, promotion, and returns as additional drivers to rule out.
8. **A Structured Sequence for Any Diagnosis** — The general five-step sequence (define, locate, decompose, test, act) applicable beyond sales misses.
9. **Putting the Vocabulary to Work** — The practical habit: check traffic/conversion before discussing a miss in the weekly meeting.
10. **A Worked Scenario: The Miss That Looked Like a Traffic Problem** — Illustrative synthesis showing a peak-hour staffing gap misdiagnosed initially as weather-driven traffic.
11. **Practice Exercise** — Running a real or hypothetical miss through the four-branch decision tree.
12. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The sales = traffic × conversion × ATV decomposition and the four-branch decision tree (traffic down/conversion holding; traffic holding/conversion down; both down; neither down) are sourced directly and specifically from Ariadne's "Traffic Problem or Conversion Problem?" article, which explicitly lays out this exact branching logic with named branch labels and specific follow-up questions for each branch (including the capture-rate vs. passing-traffic distinction in Branch One, the funnel-in-order questions in Branch Two, and the explicit staffing-cut warning in Branch Three).
- The staffing-cut trap ("the standard response to falling traffic is cutting hours, which lowers conversion further and accelerates the decline") is CONFIRMED as stated explicitly and specifically in the Ariadne source; this is a genuinely important, counterintuitive point that this module treats as one of its central warnings.
- The eight-driver "full driver tree" (traffic, conversion, basket, price, mix, availability, promotion, returns) is sourced directly from umbrex.com's "Diagnosing Performance and Finding Root Causes" article (part of the same Retail KPI Dashboard & Weekly Business Review Playbook series used in C12-M06), which explicitly names this exact eight-driver decomposition for a "sales diagnosis sequence."
- The general five-step diagnostic sequence (define the variance, locate where it occurred, decompose into drivers, test root causes, define action/owner/timing) is sourced directly from the same umbrex.com article, which explicitly lays out this exact sequence (with a sixth step, assessing controllability, which this module folds into the "test" and "act" steps for simplicity).
- A separately consulted source (scoopanalytics.com's "The 10-Hypothesis Problem") independently corroborates the general principle that retail underperformance diagnosis benefits from a defined, repeatable set of hypotheses tested systematically rather than ad hoc, reinforcing (though not directly quoted in) this module's overall structured-diagnosis approach.
- The worked scenario (peak-hour staffing gap misdiagnosed as a weather-driven traffic problem) is an ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not an externally sourced case, though it is constructed to be directly consistent with the confirmed decision-tree logic above.
- The practice exercise is an ORIGINAL synthesis designed for this module, not an externally sourced case.
