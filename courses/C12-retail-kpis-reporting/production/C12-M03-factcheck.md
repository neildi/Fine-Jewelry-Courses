# C12-M03 Fact-Check: Average Ticket, UPT and Mix

## Verification Status: Core definitional distinctions and jewelry-specific UPT benchmarks are CONFIRMED across multiple independent, credible sources; specific cross-selling/upselling percentage-effect claims are flagged MUST VERIFY due to reliance on a single, non-primary source

## Claims Reviewed

**1. The footfall-vs-basket-size conceptual distinction ("footfall counts how many people entered; basket size measures how much each buyer bought once inside... both describe the buyer, not the visitor").**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly and near-verbatim from Ariadne Maps' "Basket Size vs Footfall: Does More Traffic Help?" article, a specialized retail-analytics source explicitly contrasting these two metric families with this exact framing.

**2. ATV formula (total sales ÷ number of transactions) and UPT formula (total items sold ÷ number of transactions).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sources, including Ariadne Maps, Northbeam's "UPT (Units per Transaction) Explained," loveinstore.com's "The Power of Basket Size," and editorialge.com's basket-size guide. All state functionally identical formulas.

**3. The worked two-associate UPT/ATV comparison (Employee 1: 3 items, $50 avg sale, $5,000 total on 100 transactions; Employee 2: 6 items, $90 avg sale, $9,000 total on 100 transactions).**
Status: CONFIRMED as sourced from a LinkedIn retail-management post by a named author (Shubham Batham). The underlying arithmetic was independently re-verified during this module's production: 100 transactions × $50 average sale = $5,000 (correct); 100 transactions × $90 average sale = $9,000 (correct); the $4,000 difference cited in this module is correct. This is a single social-media source rather than a peer-reviewed or trade-association source, but the underlying arithmetic is independently verifiable and correct regardless of source credibility.

**4. Jewelry-specific ATV/UPT benchmarks: Retail Operations Toolkit (ATV $800-$3,000, UPT 1.2-1.6) and dojobusiness.com (ATV $500-$5,000, UPT target 1.3-1.5).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT on UPT specifically — two independent sources converge closely (1.2-1.6 vs. 1.3-1.5), giving reasonable confidence in this specific range. The ATV ranges are wider and less precisely aligned across the two sources ($800-$3,000 vs. $500-$5,000), which this module explicitly attributes to genuine variation by market positioning rather than treating either range as a single fixed, universally applicable number.

**5. Cross-selling associated with a 25-50% increase in average transaction value; upselling associated with a 10-20% increase in average sale value; add-on conversion rates above 15%.**
Status: FLAGGED MUST VERIFY. These specific percentage-effect figures are sourced from a single source, startupfinancialprojection.com's "Top 5 Strategies to Maximize Profitability for Jewelry" article — a financial-projection/business-planning content site, not a primary trade research source or peer-reviewed study. This module's research could not locate a second independent source corroborating these specific percentage ranges. Given the pattern of quality concerns already identified in similar business-planning content sites during this course's production (see C12-M02's factcheck regarding financialmodelslab.com), these figures should be treated as directional and illustrative only, not as reliable, guaranteed outcomes, and should be verified against a primary source (a jewelry trade association study, a POS vendor's aggregated customer data, or a peer-reviewed retail study) before being used in any formal training or target-setting context.

**6. Point-of-sale add-ons, cross-selling, upselling, and complementary-item merchandising as general basket-growth techniques.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT as a general technique set across multiple independent general-retail sources: Northbeam's UPT guide, Novelli's basket-size guide, and loveinstore.com's basket-size article all independently recommend materially the same technique set (product adjacency/placement, staff training on complementary recommendations, bundling/promotions). None of these sources are jewelry-specific, but the underlying techniques are general retail-merchandising principles that apply directly to a jewelry sales-floor context.

**7. The product-mix-vs-behavior distinction and its explicit tie-forward to Module 7 (Diagnosing a Miss).**
Status: ORIGINAL synthesis created for this module, built directly on the already-confirmed ATV/UPT formulas from Module 1 and the mix-and-margin distinction implicit in standard retail-math practice; not itself an externally sourced claim, but logically follows from confirmed underlying formulas.

**8. Worked scenario (bridal-season mix shift) and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from any external source.
