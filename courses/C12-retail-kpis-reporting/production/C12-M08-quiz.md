# C12-M08 Quiz: Reporting Up: The One-Page Store Review

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. What is the three-part flow that every high-impact upward report follows?**
A) Data, more data, even more data
B) Context (what happened), insight (why it happened), and action (what happens next)
C) Blame, excuse, and promise
D) There is no consistent structure to effective reporting

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A report that stops at context alone forces the reader to ask the follow-up questions themselves, while a report including all three answers them before they're asked.

---

**2. What are the four required fields of a genuinely useful leadership-facing executive summary?**
A) Store address, manager name, phone number, and email
B) Current-period result, comparison (to plan and prior year), driver (the two or three measures explaining the movement), and decision (one action, one owner, one due date)
C) Weather conditions, competitor activity, holiday calendar, and staff schedule
D) There are no specific required fields; any format works equally well

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A report should never end on an unexplained red or green KPI — a red number without a stated action creates anxiety without direction, while a green number without a next step wastes an opportunity to lock in what's working.

---

**3. Why does this module describe a genuinely useful one-page report as deliberately NOT a "dashboard dump"?**
A) One-page reports should include every single number a store's systems can produce
B) Every element on the page should answer a specific question a district manager or owner actually has, rather than simply displaying everything the store's systems are capable of producing
C) Dashboards and one-page reports are exactly the same thing
D) One-page reports should never include any actual numbers

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This selectivity is what makes a one-page report genuinely useful rather than overwhelming.

---

**4. What is the four-sentence verbal script structure this module recommends for live or phone reporting?**
A) A completely unscripted, improvised response with no preparation
B) "Overall, the business is performing [above/at/below] plan. Sales are [up/down] [X], driven by [driver]. The biggest opportunity is [X], and the biggest risk is [Y]. Our focus moving forward is [action]."
C) A script consisting only of the word "fine" repeated multiple times
D) A ten-minute unstructured monologue covering every possible topic

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Having this rehearsed means a manager doesn't scramble for words when a district manager calls unexpectedly asking how the week went.

---

**5. What is cascading-goals logic, and how should it be applied to setting individual associate targets from a store target?**
A) Individual targets should simply be the store target divided evenly by headcount, with no other adjustment
B) The store's top-level target should be broken down accounting for each associate's actual scheduled hours and specific role, with the target visibly and traceably tied to the store-level goal it supports
C) Individual targets have no relationship to store-level targets and should be set completely independently
D) Only the store manager should have any target; individual associates should not have targets at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A bridal specialist's target looks different from a general sales associate's target, and building targets with associate input generates more genuine ownership than an arbitrarily imposed number.

---

**6. Why does this module recommend that individual targets reconcile visibly back to the store-level target?**
A) Visible reconciliation has no effect on associate trust or motivation
B) An associate who suspects their individual number was set arbitrarily has much less reason to commit to hitting it than an associate who can trace their specific target back to the store number
C) Targets should be kept secret from associates and never explained
D) Reconciliation is only relevant for store managers, not for individual associates

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This transparency directly supports morale and trust in the target-setting process.

---

**7. In the worked scenario, why did the district manager approve the store's staffing request in the same conversation?**
A) The request was vague and open-ended, giving the district manager maximum flexibility
B) The report named a specific, diagnosed driver (a peak-hour staffing gap identified through Module 7's decision tree) and asked for a specific, bounded resource with a named owner and a review date attached
C) The district manager approves all requests regardless of how they are presented
D) The store manager threatened to quit if the request was denied

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A report built on guesswork or a vague "we need more staff" ask would likely have been deferred pending further review.

A) The three modules are completely unrelated to each other
B) Module 6's dashboard produces the numbers, Module 7's decision tree diagnoses what's driving them, and Module 8's context-insight-action structure turns that diagnosis into a report leadership trusts and acts on
C) Only Module 8 matters; Modules 6 and 7 are optional background material
D) Modules 6, 7, and 8 all cover exactly the same content with no distinct contribution

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A store leader who runs this full cycle consistently is doing exactly what this course's terminal outcomes describe.
