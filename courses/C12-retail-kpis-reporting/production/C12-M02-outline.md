# C12-M02 Outline: Conversion and Traffic: Measuring What You Can't See

## Module Metadata
- Course: C12 - Retail KPIs & Reporting for Jewelry Store Leaders
- Module: 2 of 8
- Persona: P7 (First-Time Store Leader), P8 (Multi-Store Leader), per official course architecture
- Working title: Conversion and Traffic: Measuring What You Can't See
- Learning objective: Store leaders can select an appropriate foot-traffic counting method for their store's size and budget, correctly interpret jewelry-specific conversion-rate benchmarks rather than general-retail benchmarks, correct for staff/vendor inflation of the traffic count, and read conversion trends against a store's own rolling baseline rather than only against industry averages.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: Directly deepens the C12 terminal outcome "define and calculate conversion" from Module 1, focusing specifically on the traffic-measurement half of the formula that Module 1 left unexplored.

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames traffic measurement as the genuinely hard half of the conversion-rate formula.
2. **Five Ways to Count Foot Traffic, and What Each One Actually Gets Right** — Manual counting, infrared, thermal, stereo-video, Wi-Fi/mobile, and AI video analytics, with accuracy and cost ranges for each.
3. **The Free Option: Google's Popular Times** — A no-cost baseline for stores without hardware budget.
4. **What Conversion Rate Should Actually Look Like in a Jewelry Store** — Jewelry/luxury-specific 8-15% benchmark vs. general retail (grocery 20-40%, apparel 15-30%, etc.), and why category-mismatched benchmarking misleads.
5. **Staff-Exclusion: A Small Detail That Changes the Number** — How uncorrected staff/vendor traffic inflates the denominator and understates true conversion.
6. **Reading Conversion Against Your Own Trend, Not Just an Industry Number** — Trend-based benchmarking as the primary practice, tying back to C11-M10's weekly scorecard discipline.
7. **Putting It Together: What a Manager Actually Does With This** — Practical selection guidance for a single-location jewelry store.
8. **Practice Exercise** — A one-week staff-exclusion traffic-tracking exercise comparing raw vs. adjusted conversion rate.
9. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Ties to Other Modules
- Directly extends C12-M01's conversion-rate formula (transactions ÷ foot traffic × 100) by addressing the traffic-measurement side in depth.
- Explicitly ties trend-reading practice back to C11-M10 (The Manager's Own Weekly Scorecard), applying that module's "read trends, not single numbers" principle specifically to conversion rate.
- Sets up later C12 modules: Module 6 (Building the Weekly Dashboard) will incorporate conversion rate as a tracked line item, and Module 7 (Diagnosing a Miss) will use the jewelry-specific benchmark range established here to correctly diagnose whether a conversion number is actually a problem.

## Production Notes
- The five foot-traffic counting methods and their accuracy/cost ranges are sourced directly from GrowthFactor.ai's "How to Measure Foot Traffic In Store: 5 Methods Compared" article, a detailed, methodologically transparent industry source. The specific accuracy figures cited (manual 85-95%, infrared 75-85%, thermal ~85%, stereo-video 98%+, LiDAR 95%+, Wi-Fi/mobile 60-80%, AI video 95-99%) are CONFIRMED as stated in that source, including its explicit note that Wi-Fi/mobile tracking accuracy has declined since 2020 due to MAC address randomization in iOS and Android.
- Google's "Popular Times" and Business Profile Insights as a free baseline option are CONFIRMED, sourced from the same GrowthFactor.ai article, which explicitly describes these as free tools providing "useful baseline data" for relative comparison, not absolute counts.
- The jewelry/luxury-specific conversion-rate benchmark (8-15%) along with average transaction value ($800-$3,000) and units per transaction (1.2-1.6) is sourced directly from Retail Operations Toolkit's "Luxury & Jewelry Retail KPI Benchmark" page — this is the exact "Retail Ops Toolkit KPI dashboard and scorecards" resource named in this project's official Course Architecture for C12's resource pairing. The general-retail category conversion comparisons (grocery 20-40%, apparel 15-30%, specialty retail 15-30%, etc.) are cross-checked and CONFIRMED CONSISTENT between Retail Operations Toolkit's benchmark page and GrowthFactor.ai's conversion-rate-by-category table, which independently lists closely comparable ranges for these same general categories.
- A second, independent jewelry-specific source (financialmodelslab.com, "7 Critical KPIs to Track for Your Jewelry Store") cites a notably higher range (established luxury retailers 15-30%, aggressive boutique goal 25-35%) than the Retail Operations Toolkit range (8-15%). This is flagged as a MUST VERIFY discrepancy in the factcheck: this module deliberately presents the Retail Operations Toolkit range as primary, since it is the source explicitly named in this project's own official Course Architecture, but the discrepancy with a second source should be resolved by human review before treating either range as definitive for coaching or target-setting purposes.
- The staff-exclusion accuracy point is sourced from GrowthFactor.ai's description of AI video analytics' ability to "distinguish between employees and customers for accurate conversion rate calculation," generalized in this module into a broader staff-adjustment principle applicable even to manual counting methods; this generalization is this module's own original synthesis built on that sourced capability description.
- The trend-based benchmarking recommendation ("benchmark against your own rolling 13-week average by daypart rather than relying solely on industry averages") is directly sourced from GrowthFactor.ai's article, and this module's explicit tie to C11-M10's weekly-scorecard trend-reading principle is this module's own original synthesis connecting two already-independently-sourced pieces of content.
- The practice exercise is an ORIGINAL synthesis designed for this module, not an externally sourced case.

## Assessment Alignment
- Quiz items should test: correctly matching each foot-traffic counting method to its approximate accuracy range, correctly identifying the jewelry/luxury-specific conversion benchmark (8-15%) versus general-retail benchmarks, correctly explaining why staff/vendor traffic inflates the denominator and understates conversion, and correctly applying trend-based rather than only benchmark-based conversion-rate interpretation.
- Huddle card should focus on the staff-exclusion practice and the jewelry-specific benchmark range as the two most immediately actionable takeaways.
- Factcheck should flag the discrepancy between the two jewelry-specific conversion-rate benchmark sources (8-15% vs. 15-35%) as a MUST VERIFY item requiring human resolution before either range is used for formal target-setting.
