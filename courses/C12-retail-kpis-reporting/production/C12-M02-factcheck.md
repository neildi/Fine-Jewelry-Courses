# C12-M02 Fact-Check: Conversion and Traffic: Measuring What You Can't See

## Verification Status: Foot-traffic counting methods and accuracy ranges are CONFIRMED, sourced from a detailed, transparent industry source; the jewelry/luxury conversion-rate benchmark is sourced from the exact resource named in this project's official Course Architecture, but a MUST VERIFY discrepancy exists against a second, notably different jewelry-specific benchmark source

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Five foot-traffic counting methods with specific accuracy and cost ranges (manual 85-95% at $10-50; infrared beam 75-85% at $50-300/sensor; thermal ~85% at $300-700/unit; stereo-video 98%+ at $300-700/unit; LiDAR 95%+ at $800-2,000/unit; Wi-Fi/mobile 60-80% at $100-500/month; AI video analytics 95-99% at $500-2,000/month).**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly and in detail from GrowthFactor.ai's "How to Measure Foot Traffic In Store: 5 Methods Compared" article, which presents these figures in an explicit comparison table with stated methodology. This source is written by a named author (GrowthFactor's founder) with disclosed MIT Sloan MBA and family retail-business background, and presents accuracy figures with appropriate caveats (e.g., noting infrared sensors "can be triggered by shopping carts, strollers, or groups").

**2. Wi-Fi/mobile tracking accuracy decline since 2020 due to MAC address randomization in iOS and Android.**
Status: CONFIRMED, explicitly stated in the same GrowthFactor.ai source: "MAC address randomization in iOS and Android has significantly reduced the reliability of Wi-Fi-based counting since 2020." This is a well-known, independently verifiable mobile-OS privacy feature change, consistent with general industry knowledge of smartphone privacy developments in this period.

**3. Google's "Popular Times" and Google Business Profile Insights as free relative-baseline tools.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced from the same GrowthFactor.ai article, which explicitly describes these as free tools useful "for relative comparisons" that "should not be treated as absolute counts."

**4. Jewelry/luxury retail conversion-rate benchmark (8-15%), alongside ATV ($800-$3,000) and UPT (1.2-1.6).**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from Retail Operations Toolkit's "Luxury & Jewelry Retail KPI Benchmark" page — this is the exact resource named in this project's official Course Architecture for C12 ("Retail Ops Toolkit KPI dashboard and scorecards"). The source itself includes an explicit methodology disclaimer: "Ranges reflect commonly cited industry benchmarks aggregated across public retail-finance sources... Treat as directional, not authoritative for your specific business."

**5. General-retail conversion-rate benchmarks by category (grocery 20-40%, apparel 15-30%, specialty retail 15-30%, electronics 10-20%, big-box 10-20%).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT between two independent sources: Retail Operations Toolkit's benchmark page and GrowthFactor.ai's conversion-rate-by-category table, which lists closely comparable ranges for grocery (20-40%), apparel (15-30%), electronics (10-20%), and specialty retail (15-30%).

**6. MUST VERIFY: Discrepancy between jewelry-specific conversion-rate benchmark sources.**
Status: FLAGGED FOR HUMAN REVIEW. A second source consulted during this module's research (financialmodelslab.com, "7 Critical KPIs to Track for Your Jewelry Store") cites a notably different and higher range: "established luxury retailers often see rates between 15% and 30%," with an "aggressive starting goal" of 25% for a boutique reaching toward 35%. This is a meaningfully different range from the 8-15% cited by Retail Operations Toolkit. Additionally, this financialmodelslab.com source was found during research to contain clear internal errors and likely AI-generation artifacts elsewhere in the same article (e.g., a stated gross margin target of ">878%," a COGS figure of "122% of sales" that is mathematically impossible alongside an 87% margin target, and repeated use of the non-standard word "defintely" throughout the text). Given these quality concerns, this module deliberately treats the Retail Operations Toolkit range (8-15%) as primary, since it is both the source explicitly named in this project's official Course Architecture and free of the internal inconsistencies found in the competing source. However, the discrepancy itself, and the general reliability question around any specific jewelry-industry conversion benchmark, should be resolved by human review — ideally against a primary, credentialed jewelry-trade-association source — before either range is used for formal target-setting with store staff.

**7. Staff-exclusion capability of AI video analytics, generalized into a broader staff-adjustment principle.**
Status: The specific capability claim (AI video analytics can "distinguish between employees and customers for accurate conversion rate calculation") is CONFIRMED, sourced from GrowthFactor.ai. This module's broader generalization — that even manual counting methods should build in a rough staff-adjustment factor — is this module's own original synthesis extending that sourced capability description, not itself an independently sourced claim, though it follows directly and logically from the underlying accuracy concern.

**8. Trend-based benchmarking recommendation ("benchmark against your own rolling 13-week average by daypart").**
Status: CONFIRMED, directly sourced from GrowthFactor.ai's article. This module's explicit tie to C11-M10's weekly-scorecard trend-reading principle is this module's own original synthesis connecting two already-independently-sourced pieces of content from different points in this project.
