# C12-M03 Outline: Average Ticket, UPT and Mix

## Module Metadata
- Course: C12 - Retail KPIs & Reporting for Jewelry Store Leaders
- Module: 3 of 8
- Persona: P7 (First-Time Store Leader), P8 (Multi-Store Leader), per official course architecture
- Working title: Average Ticket, UPT and Mix
- Learning objective: Store leaders can read ATV and UPT together rather than in isolation, apply jewelry-specific benchmarks for both, apply four specific techniques (POS add-ons, cross-selling, upselling, complementary merchandising) to grow ATV/UPT, and correctly distinguish a genuine behavioral improvement from a product-mix shift when either number moves.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: Directly deepens the C12 terminal outcome "define and calculate...ATV, UPT" from Module 1, and directly feeds Module 7 (Diagnosing a Miss), where the mix-vs-behavior distinction becomes essential.

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames ATV/UPT as the "buyer" side of the sales equation, distinct from the "visitor" side covered in Module 2.
2. **ATV and UPT Describe the Buyer, Not the Visitor** — Formulas restated, and the distinct-lever framing (traffic/conversion get more buyers; ATV/UPT get bigger baskets).
3. **Reading the Two Numbers Together, Not Separately** — Worked numeric comparison of two associates with identical transaction counts but different UPT/ATV combinations.
4. **Jewelry-Specific Benchmarks for ATV and UPT** — Two independently converging benchmark ranges (Retail Operations Toolkit and BusinessDojo/dojobusiness.com).
5. **Three Specific Techniques That Move ATV and UPT** — POS add-ons, cross-selling/upselling (with reported percentage effects), and complementary-item merchandising.
6. **Product Mix: What's Actually Driving ATV Changes** — The mix-vs-behavior distinction, explicitly tied forward to Module 7.
7. **Putting the Vocabulary to Work** — The practical weekly sequence: track together, check mix first, then attribute to technique.
8. **A Worked Scenario: The Rising ATV That Wasn't a Coaching Win** — Illustrative synthesis showing a seasonal bridal-mix shift masquerading as a training win.
9. **Practice Exercise** — Pulling ATV/UPT plus category mix for four weeks and picking one technique to practice.
10. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The core definitional distinction ("footfall counts how many people entered; basket size measures how much each buyer bought once inside... both describe the buyer, not the visitor") is sourced directly and near-verbatim from Ariadne Maps' "Basket Size vs Footfall: Does More Traffic Help?" article, a specialized retail-analytics source explicitly contrasting these two metric families.
- The worked two-associate UPT/ATV comparison (3 items/$50 vs. 6 items/$90 average sale, $5,000 vs. $9,000 total revenue on 100 transactions) is sourced directly from a LinkedIn retail-management post by a named author (Shubham Batham), used here as an illustrative comparison; the underlying arithmetic was independently re-verified during this module's production and confirmed correct.
- Jewelry-specific ATV/UPT benchmarks are drawn from two independent sources that converge closely on UPT: Retail Operations Toolkit's "Luxury & Jewelry Retail KPI Benchmark" (ATV $800-$3,000, UPT 1.2-1.6, already used in C12-M02) and dojobusiness.com's "Jewelry Store: Weekly Sales Requirements" (ATV $500-$5,000 depending on market positioning, UPT target 1.3-1.5). The UPT ranges from these two independent sources overlap closely (1.2-1.6 vs. 1.3-1.5), which is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT and gives reasonable confidence in this specific number; the ATV ranges are wider and less precisely aligned, reflecting genuine variation by market positioning that this module explicitly notes rather than treating as a single fixed number.
- The specific percentage-effect claims for cross-selling (25-50% ATV increase) and upselling (10-20% average sale value increase, 15%+ add-on conversion) are sourced from startupfinancialprojection.com's "Top 5 Strategies to Maximize Profitability for Jewelry" article. This source is flagged as a MUST VERIFY item: it is a financial-projection/business-planning content site rather than a primary trade research source, and the specific percentage ranges cited could not be cross-verified against a second independent source during this module's research. These figures should be treated as directional and illustrative rather than as guaranteed, precisely reproducible outcomes for any individual store.
- The point-of-sale add-on and complementary-item-merchandising techniques are CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple general-retail sources (Northbeam's UPT guide, Novelli's basket-size guide, loveinstore.com's basket-size article), all independently recommending materially the same set of techniques (product placement/adjacency, staff training on complementary recommendations, bundling).
- The product-mix-vs-behavior distinction and its explicit tie-forward to Module 7 is this module's own original synthesis, built on the confirmed ATV/UPT formulas from Module 1, not itself an externally sourced claim.
- The worked scenario (bridal-season mix shift) and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses created for this module.

- Huddle card should focus on the mix-vs-behavior distinction and the specific POS add-on/cross-sell techniques as the two most actionable takeaways.
- Factcheck should flag the cross-selling/upselling percentage-effect claims (25-50%, 10-20%, 15%+) as MUST VERIFY, since they could not be cross-verified against a second independent source and come from a business-planning content site rather than a primary trade research source.
