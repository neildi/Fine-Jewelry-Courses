# C12-M02 Quiz: Conversion and Traffic: Measuring What You Can't See

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. What is the approximate accuracy range for manual foot-traffic counting (clicker or tally sheet)?**
A) 30-50%
B) 85-95% for a single entrance when someone is consistently counting
C) 99.9%, always exact
D) Manual counting cannot be assigned an accuracy range

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Manual counting is the cheapest option but depends entirely on someone being present and consistent.

---

**2. Why has Wi-Fi and mobile-device tracking become less reliable for foot-traffic counting since 2020?**
A) Wi-Fi tracking has always been the most accurate method available
B) MAC address randomization, now standard on modern iOS and Android devices, has undercut the sample these systems depend on, dropping accuracy to roughly 60-80%
C) Wi-Fi tracking became more accurate after 2020, not less
D) Wi-Fi tracking is illegal and therefore no longer used

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This privacy feature reduces the pool of detectable devices, which directly undermines Wi-Fi-based counting accuracy.

**3. What is a genuinely free option for getting a baseline sense of relative store busyness by hour and day?**
A) There is no free option; all traffic measurement requires paid hardware or software
B) Google's "Popular Times" feature on Google Maps and Google Business Profile Insights
C) A paid enterprise stereo-video sensor system
D) Third-party mobile-data platforms designed for site selection

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These tools show relative patterns, not an absolute count, but provide useful free baseline data for comparison.

**4. Why is a jewelry store's conversion rate typically lower than a grocery store's, and why does this matter for benchmarking?**
A) Jewelry stores are simply worse at selling than grocery stores
B) Jewelry purchases involve far more browsing-without-buying, price research, and multi-visit decision journeys than grocery purchases, where most visitors already intend to buy — comparing a jewelry store's conversion against a general-retail average produces a misleading conclusion
C) There is no meaningful difference between jewelry and grocery conversion rates
D) Grocery stores actually have lower conversion rates than jewelry stores

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: One jewelry/luxury-specific benchmark source places typical conversion in the 8-15% range, reflecting the category's low-velocity, high-consideration purchase pattern.

**5. Why does uncorrected staff and vendor traffic distort a store's conversion-rate calculation?**
A) Staff and vendor traffic has no effect on conversion-rate calculations
B) It inflates the traffic denominator without contributing to the transaction numerator, which understates the true conversion rate — this matters most at a small store where a few employees passing through the same entrance repeatedly can meaningfully distort the count
C) Staff traffic always increases the conversion rate, never decreases it
D) Only vendor traffic matters; staff traffic is irrelevant

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: AI video analytics can solve this automatically by excluding recognized staff movement, but even manual counting can be adjusted with a rough staff-exclusion factor.

**6. What does this module recommend as the primary benchmark for interpreting a store's conversion rate?**
A) Always compare against a general-retail industry average, regardless of category
B) Benchmark primarily against the store's own rolling multi-week trend, using category benchmarks only as a secondary sanity check
C) Conversion rate should never be benchmarked against anything
D) Only compare against the single best-performing store in the district, with no other context

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Trend drift catches a real problem days or weeks before an absolute-number comparison against an industry average would.

**7. Which foot-traffic counting technologies does this module identify as reaching 95% or higher accuracy?**
A) Only manual counting reaches this accuracy level
B) Stereo-video sensors (98%+), LiDAR sensors (95%+), and AI video analytics (95-99%)
C) No counting technology reaches 95% accuracy
D) Only Wi-Fi and Bluetooth tracking reach this accuracy level

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These represent the higher-accuracy tier of counting technology, contrasted with lower-accuracy options like basic infrared beam sensors (75-85%).
