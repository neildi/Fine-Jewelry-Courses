# C12-M04 Fact-Check: Inventory KPIs: Sell-Through, Turn, GMROI

## Verification Status: Jewelry-specific inventory turn and GMROI benchmarks are CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sources, with one flagged outlier on turn requiring human review; category-specific aging tolerances and the tiered markdown ladder are CONFIRMED across multiple independent jewelry-trade sources

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Jewelry-specific inventory turn benchmark (1-2 turns/year generally, 0.7-1.5x for boutique/luxury stores).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sources: jewel360.com's "What Is a Good Inventory Turnover Rate for Your Jewelry Store?" (1-2x/year generally, 0.8-1.5x for boutiques), jewelrybuydirect.com's "How to Improve Inventory Turnover for Jewelry Businesses" (1-2 turns/year), Luxare's "Jewelry Store Reporting and Analytics Tracking" (1.0-1.5x/year), Retail Operations Toolkit's benchmarks page (1-3x/year), and instoremag.com's trade-press Brain Squad piece (0.7-1.2x/year for American retail jewelry specifically). Five independent sources converge closely on this range.

**2. MUST VERIFY: Outlier inventory-turn source (aislestock.com, 2.0-4.1x/year).**
Status: FLAGGED FOR HUMAN REVIEW. This source's figure is notably higher than the broadly corroborated 1-2x consensus above. The source itself distinguishes "traditional store-based chains at the low end" from "DTC online-led players turning faster," which may partly explain the discrepancy if this source's overall range blends both business models rather than representing a single-location brick-and-mortar store specifically. This module treats the 1-2x range as primary given its much broader corroboration (five independent sources vs. one), but the discrepancy itself should be resolved by a human reviewer, ideally by confirming which specific business model (traditional store-based vs. DTC/online) most closely matches the store this course is being used for.

**3. Jewelry-specific GMROI benchmark (mass-market ~1.0-1.1, luxury boutique 3.0-3.4).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across three independent sources: Toolio's "The Complete Guide to GMROI for Retail Brands" (Luxury & Jewelry "$1.10 for mass jewelry to $3.40 for luxury boutiques"), Apshan's "GMROI: Formula, Benchmarks and Fashion Guide" ("Jewelry: lower, often near 1, where high margins are offset by very slow turnover"), and Luxare's reporting-analytics page ("jewelry benchmarks sit around $1.00"). Three independent sources converge closely on a GMROI near 1.0 specifically for mass-market jewelry.

**4. General-retail GMROI "healthy" benchmark (2-3, with 3.5+ excellent), used for contrast.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced from Toolio's guide, which states this as the general retail rule of thumb across most categories (not jewelry-specific), explicitly used in this module only as a point of contrast against the jewelry-specific figure.

**5. Category-specific aging tolerances (bridal 12-18mo, fashion ~6mo, classic fine jewelry 12-24mo, seasonal must-clear-in-season).**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from jewel360.com's "Inventory Aging Reports: When To Mark Down Expensive..." article, a jewelry-specific operations source explicitly breaking down "normal" aging by category.

**6. The tiered markdown ladder (10-15% at 12-18mo, 20-30% at 18-24mo, 40%+ after 24mo).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across two closely aligned jewelry-specific sources with matching percentages and timeframes: jewel360.com's aging-reports article and a jewel360-affiliated LinkedIn post describing the identical three-tier structure. An older JCK trade-press source (2016, "Out With the Old, In With the New Inventory") independently corroborates the same general tiered-discipline pattern (an initial ~30% markdown followed by a step to 50%), though with somewhat different specific percentages and timing than the jewel360 sources, suggesting this general markdown-ladder approach has been a stable industry practice for at least a decade, even as the specific numbers used have varied somewhat by source and era.

**7. Bounty/spiff, bundling, and vendor consignment/return techniques for aged inventory.**
Status: CONFIRMED across multiple independent jewelry-trade sources: Abbott Jewelry Systems' "Aged Inventory Management" blog (bounty/spiff technique, a tiered markdown starting at 30% — a somewhat more aggressive starting point than the jewel360 sources, again illustrating some variation in specific numbers across sources even as the general approach is consistent), jewel360.com (bundling), and southernjewelrynews.com's "7 Steps to Manage Aging Inventory at full price" (staff training to feature aging pieces in presentations, vendor exchange arrangements between cooperative retailers).

**8. Practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL synthesis created for this module, not an externally sourced case.
