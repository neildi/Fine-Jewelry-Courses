# C12-M08 Outline: Reporting Up: The One-Page Store Review

## Module Metadata
- Course: C12 - Retail KPIs & Reporting for Jewelry Store Leaders
- Module: 8 of 8 (final module)
- Persona: P7 (First-Time Store Leader), P8 (Multi-Store Leader), per official course architecture
- Working title: Reporting Up: The One-Page Store Review
- Learning objective: Store leaders can apply the context-insight-action reporting structure, build a leadership-facing executive summary with its four required fields, compress a full dashboard into a genuinely useful one-page report, deliver a rehearsed verbal reporting script, and cascade a store-level target into fair, specific, transparent individual associate targets.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: Directly fulfills the two remaining C12 terminal outcomes: "set individual targets from store targets" and "present results upward" per this project's official course architecture, closing out the course.

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames this module as the point where all of this course's KPI and diagnostic work becomes communicable and actionable for leadership and for the team.
2. **The Three-Part Flow Every Good Report Follows** — Context, insight, action, with the explicit critique of stopping at context alone.
3. **The Four Required Fields of a Genuinely Useful Summary** — Current-period result, comparison, driver, decision; the never-end-on-an-unexplained-KPI discipline.
4. **Building the One-Page Version** — Compressing Module 6's dashboard into a genuinely selective one-page report.
5. **A Script for the Verbal Version** — The four-sentence rehearsed script for live/phone reporting.
6. **Setting Individual Targets From the Store Target** — Cascading-goals logic applied to store-to-associate target-setting.
7. **Keeping the Cascade Honest** — Visible reconciliation and regular review cadence as trust-preserving disciplines.
8. **Putting the Vocabulary to Work** — Explicit synthesis tying Modules 6, 7, and 8 into the complete reporting cycle, and closing the course.
9. **A Worked Scenario: The Report That Actually Got a Budget Approved** — Illustrative synthesis contrasting a diagnosed, specific ask against a vague one.
10. **Practice Exercise** — Writing a one-page report and rehearsing the verbal script.
11. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap, and closes out the course.

## Production Notes
- The context-insight-action three-part reporting structure is sourced directly from retailbusinessacademy.com's "Reporting Scripts" article, which explicitly names this exact flow ("Every high-impact report follows this simple flow: 1. Context → 2. Insight → 3. Action") along with the specific verbal executive-summary script quoted in this module ("Overall, the business is performing __________. Sales are __________, driven by __________...").
- The four-required-fields executive-summary structure (current-period result, comparison, driver, decision) and the "never end on an unexplained red or green KPI" discipline are sourced directly and specifically from AnyGen's "Retail KPI Presentation Template" page, which explicitly names these exact four fields for "the executive-summary slide" and states this discipline in nearly the same words used in this module.
- The "not a dashboard dump" framing and the broader 12-slide leadership-review structure (from which this module's one-page compression is derived) are sourced from the same AnyGen source, as well as its companion "Retail Sales Presentation Template" page, which independently corroborates a very similar structure (executive summary with actual/plan/YoY variance, drivers, and a decisions-and-owners closing section).
- The four required fields and the one-page-format recommendation for board-level reporting ("board-level reporting, a one-page format works best: overall network score, three key wins, three key risks, and recommended actions... Keep it decision-ready") are independently corroborated by getamply.co's "Retail Store Visit Report" article, which describes a very similar one-page structure in a different but related context (store-visit reporting rather than KPI/sales reporting), reinforcing the general one-page/decision-ready principle across two independent sources.
- The cascading-goals framework (starting from top-level organizational goals, breaking them into department/team/individual targets, ensuring visible traceability back to the top-level goal, and involving employees in shaping their own goals) is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent HR/goal-setting sources: Lattice's "How to Use Cascading Goals," AIHR's "Cascading Goals: 5 Examples + How-To for HR Leaders," and Leapsome's "Cascading Goals Framework." These are general business/HR sources, not retail- or jewelry-specific, but the underlying goal-cascading logic applies directly to a store-to-associate target-setting context.
- This module's application of the cascading-goals framework specifically to a jewelry store's sales-target-to-associate-target context (accounting for scheduled hours and role-specific targets, e.g., a bridal specialist's target differing from a general associate's) is this module's own original synthesis, not itself an externally sourced claim, though it reflects the confirmed general framework applied to a retail-specific context already established elsewhere in this course (e.g., C11-M06's scheduling-to-traffic content).
- The worked scenario (the diagnosed, specific staffing request that gets approved vs. a vague ask) and the practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses created for this module, not externally sourced cases.
