# C14-M07 Outline / Media Record

**Working title:** Talent Pipeline and Succession
**Alternates:** "Building the Bench Before You Need It"; "Succession Planning Across a District"

**Scenario hook:** A district leader loses a store manager with two weeks' notice and discovers the obvious internal successor was never told she was being developed, turning a plannable transition into a crisis.

**Section outline:**
1. Why This Matters — reader connects staff-development track record as an explicit qualification for multi-unit leadership roles.
2. The Bench You Can See vs. the Bench You Have — reader distinguishes informal impressions from a documented pipeline.
3. Building the District Talent Map — reader can build a five-column succession grid across stores.
4. What Belongs in a Development Conversation — reader can structure an explicit development conversation with a high-potential associate.
5. Succession Planning Across the District — reader can identify when to move talent between stores rather than let it stagnate or leave.
6. Objections, Mistakes and Edge Cases — reader can avoid over-promising and the sales-performance-as-proxy-for-management-potential trap.

**Facts used, with placement:**
- Signet DM requires 3-5 years multi-unit management experience plus "recruitment and staff-development track record" → Section 1
- ASM posting: "responsible for the selection and development of talent to drive store growth" → Section 1
- Sales Manager posting: "hiring quality talent to plan for and create talent bench" → Section 1
- Store Manager reports to District Manager reporting line (context for cross-store mobility) → Section 5
- Monthly business review as the review cadence for people results (from Module 6) → Sections 3, 6
- C13's individual-manager-level bench-strength framework (Module 10, "Building Bench Strength for Your Own Promotion") extended to district scale → Section 4

**Gaps not covered in evidence:** No named formal succession-planning software or 9-box grid methodology was found in the supplied research; the five-column grid format in this article is a reasonable synthesis, not a verbatim sourced template — flagged for reviewer awareness. No specific readiness-scoring rubric was found in evidence.
