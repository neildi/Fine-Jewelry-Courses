# C14-M03 Quiz: Comparative KPI Analysis Across Stores

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. Why do raw comparisons across stores (raw revenue, raw traffic) systematically mislead?**
A) Raw numbers are always the most accurate way to compare any two stores
B) A metric is only genuinely comparable across stores when its numerator, denominator, scope, period, and exclusions all remain consistent; raw numbers are driven as much by store size and format as by how well a store is actually run
C) Raw comparisons never mislead under any circumstances
D) Only revenue, never traffic, can ever be misleading when compared raw

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A compact urban shop and a large suburban location use space differently, and placing them in the same raw ranking hides useful differences rather than revealing them.

---

**2. What is the sales-per-square-foot formula, and what problem does it solve?**
A) Total employees divided by total stores, with no relationship to sales or space
B) Total sales divided by selling square footage, normalizing for store size so a compact urban store and a large suburban location can be compared meaningfully on the same footing
C) Sales per square foot has no standard formula and varies unpredictably by source
D) Total sales divided by the number of years a store has been open

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is confirmed as the single most widely cited comparative metric across an unusually large number of independent retail-metrics sources.

---

**3. What specific trap does this module warn against regarding the definition of "selling area" used in sales-per-square-foot calculations?**
A) There is no meaningful difference between different definitions of selling area
B) Comparing stores where one measures carpet area and another measures gross leasable area silently breaks the comparison, even if the resulting numbers look similar
C) Selling area should always include the stockroom and offices for a fair comparison
D) Square footage definitions never affect the accuracy of a sales-per-square-foot comparison

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Before benchmarking, a consistent definition of selling area must be agreed upon and applied uniformly at every location.

**4. What is the recommended build sequence for a genuinely usable multi-store district scorecard?**
A) Track every possible metric with no limit, since more data is always better
B) Standardize data inputs first, build a focused 6-8 KPI scorecard spanning revenue/labor/customer experience/operations, establish internal benchmarks (best location as the target) before external ones, and create a genuine review cadence
C) Scorecards require no standardization since every store's data is automatically comparable
D) Review cadence has no relationship to whether a scorecard is actually used

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This directly echoes the same 5-8 headline KPI dashboard-design discipline this project's own C12 course already established for a single store.

**5. Why should comparisons be segmented by store maturity, format, and region before drawing conclusions?**
A) Segmentation has no effect on the validity of a comparison
B) A newly opened store belongs in its own ramp-up cohort rather than being directly compared against a mature, fully-established location, since comparing them without accounting for maturity produces a misleading conclusion
C) Every store should always be compared against every other store identically, regardless of when it opened
D) Only format matters; maturity and region are irrelevant to fair comparison

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This mirrors the same partial ramp-quota principle this project's own C13 course already established for new hires, now applied to a store's own performance trajectory.

**6. Why does this module recommend diagnosing outcomes with paired measures rather than isolated numbers?**
A) A single metric always tells the complete story on its own
B) A store with strong sales per square foot but weak customer-experience scores tells a genuinely different story than one strong or weak on both, and reading any single metric in isolation risks missing exactly the pattern a paired reading reveals
C) Paired measures should never be used in retail analysis
D) Isolated numbers are always more reliable than paired measures

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This directly extends this project's own C12 course's driver-tree diagnostic approach to comparative analysis across an entire district.

**7. In the worked scenario, why did the smallest store initially appear to be the district's weakest performer, and what did the normalized metric reveal?**
A) The smallest store genuinely was the weakest performer, and the normalized metric confirmed this
B) Its raw revenue trailed other stores simply because of its smaller footprint; recalculating using sales per square foot revealed it was actually the second-highest performer district-wide, while a mid-sized store that looked adequate on raw revenue ranked last on the normalized metric
C) The normalized metric produced identical results to the raw-revenue ranking in every case
D) The district leader never recalculated using any normalized metric

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This illustrates exactly how raw comparisons can mask genuine underperformance while falsely flagging a well-performing but simply smaller store.

**8. What does this module identify as a key limitation or caveat regarding reported sales-per-square-foot benchmark ranges by category?**
A) Benchmark ranges are always identical and precise across every source consulted
B) Reported ranges vary considerably by retail format and category across sources, which is itself an argument for comparing a district's own stores primarily against each other and against category-specific benchmarks rather than a generic cross-category figure
C) Benchmark ranges have no practical value and should never be consulted
D) Only one single source exists for sales-per-square-foot benchmark data

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This module treats the general range as directionally confirmed while noting real variation in exact figures between sources.
