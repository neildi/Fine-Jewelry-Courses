# C14-M02 Quiz: The Store Visit: Agenda, Audit, Debrief

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. What is the five-part store-visit rhythm this module describes?**
A) Arrive, inspect, criticize, leave, forget
B) Prepare, Observe, Coach, Verify, Follow up
C) A store visit requires no consistent structure and should be entirely improvised
D) Only two steps exist: arrive and leave

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This structure is designed specifically to build the store manager's own capability rather than substitute for it, directly applying Module 1's "coach, don't fix" principle.

---

**2. What should a district leader do during the "Prepare" phase, before ever walking into the store?**
A) Nothing; preparation is unnecessary for an effective store visit
B) Review sales, conversion, payroll, and staffing results in advance, and specifically revisit the commitments and action items from the previous visit
C) Only review the store's physical address and parking availability
D) Preparation should happen only after the visit is already underway

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Starting a visit cold, with no reference to prior commitments, signals to the store manager that those commitments weren't actually being tracked.

---

**3. What does the "Observe" phase specifically recommend, before consulting any checklist?**
A) Immediately begin comparing the store against a checklist upon walking in
B) Watch the store through the customer's eyes first, assessing customer engagement, selling behaviors, leadership presence, and store standards as a genuine first-time visitor would
C) Skip observation entirely and go straight to the debrief
D) Only observe the back-of-store inventory area, ignoring the customer-facing floor

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This customer-eyes-first approach precedes any formal checklist comparison.

---

**4. What are the five core domains a structured store audit covers, confirmed across multiple independent retail-audit sources?**
A) Only sales figures matter; no other domains are relevant
B) Merchandising/visual standards, inventory/stock management, store operations, customer experience, and compliance/loss-prevention
C) Weather patterns, competitor pricing, and social media presence
D) There is no consistent domain structure across retail-audit sources

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: An unusually strong convergence across independent sources confirms this same five-category structure, which a district leader applies consistently across every store for comparability.

---

**5. Why does this module recommend capturing photos and specific written observations during an audit, rather than relying on general impression?**
A) Photos and written observations have no practical value during an audit
B) This makes a finding both fair and actionable, directly mirroring the evidence-based documentation discipline this course's own C13 content already established for performance reviews
C) Only verbal descriptions should ever be used, never photos or written notes
D) Evidence capture is only relevant for compliance audits, never for merchandising or customer-experience findings

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Specific, dated evidence is far more defensible and useful than a vague overall impression.

---

**6. How does this module recommend balancing in-the-moment coaching with the formal end-of-visit debrief?**
A) All coaching should be saved exclusively for the formal debrief, with no real-time coaching during the visit
B) Some coaching happens in real time when something is actually observed (asking questions before offering solutions), while the formal debrief synthesizes the pattern across the whole visit into a limited set of priorities
C) No coaching should ever happen during a store visit under any circumstances
D) The formal debrief should always cover every single issue found, with no limit on the number of priorities

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This balance mirrors the explicit rhythm recommended by named district-manager visit guidance.

---

**7. Why does this module recommend limiting the formal debrief to exactly two or three priorities, rather than an exhaustive list?**
A) Limiting priorities has no effect on whether a store manager makes real progress
B) A store manager walking away with too many priorities rarely makes real progress on any of them — the same "one development area, not five" principle this course established for individual coaching applies directly to a store-visit debrief
C) Store visits should never result in any specific priorities being set
D) An exhaustive list of every issue found is always more effective than a focused list

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This directly extends a coaching-focus principle already established elsewhere in this course to the store-visit context specifically.

---

**8. In the worked scenario, what specific obstacle did the district leader's questioning approach surface that a purely inspection-based visit would likely have missed?**
A) The store manager was simply unwilling to fix the staffing issue
B) A previously unmentioned scheduling-software constraint that had been blocking the staffing fix, revealed only because the district leader asked what was actually preventing the fix rather than re-issuing the same generic instruction
C) The conversion-rate decline had no identifiable cause at all
D) The district leader discovered the problem was entirely fabricated

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Because this visit surfaced the real underlying obstacle, the fix actually happened before the next visit, rather than becoming a third consecutive unaddressed commitment.
