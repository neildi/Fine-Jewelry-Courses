# C14-M03 Outline: Comparative KPI Analysis Across Stores

## Module Metadata
- Course: C14 - Multi-Store Leadership: Store Visits, Audits, Manager Development
- Module: 3 of 8
- Persona: P8 (Multi-Store Leader), per official course architecture
- Working title: Comparative KPI Analysis Across Stores
- Learning objective: Multi-unit leaders can explain why raw store comparisons mislead, apply sales-per-square-foot as a size-normalizing metric with a consistent definition, build a focused 6-8 KPI district scorecard following a specific standardization sequence, segment comparisons by appropriate cohorts (maturity, format, region), and diagnose outcomes using paired measures rather than isolated numbers.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: Directly fulfills the C14 terminal outcome "compare stores on normalized KPIs" per this project's official course architecture, and directly supports the course's stated assessment (a district scorecard).

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames comparative analysis as the numeric counterpart to Module 2's consistent store-visit structure.
2. **Why Raw Comparisons Mislead** — The core normalization problem and the consistency-of-definition principle.
3. **Sales Per Square Foot: The Single Most Useful Normalizer** — The formula, the consistent-definition discipline, and category-benchmark variation.
4. **Building a Focused, Consistent District Scorecard** — The standardize-build-benchmark-review sequence, tied to C12's 5-8 KPI dashboard discipline.
5. **Comparing Only Appropriate Cohorts** — Segmenting by maturity, format, region; tied to C13's ramp-quota principle.
6. **Diagnosing With Paired Measures, Not Isolated Numbers** — Tied to C12's driver-tree diagnostic approach.
7. **Putting the Vocabulary to Work** — The complete comparative-analysis sequence.
8. **A Worked Scenario: The Store That Wasn't Actually Underperforming** — Illustrative synthesis showing a normalized ranking reversing a raw-revenue conclusion.
9. **Practice Exercise** — Calculating and comparing sales-per-square-foot rankings against raw-revenue rankings for a real district.
10. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The core "raw comparisons mislead because stores differ in size/format" problem, and the "a metric is only comparable when its numerator, denominator, scope, period, and exclusions remain consistent" principle, are sourced directly from Shopify's "Sales per Square Foot by Industry: Benchmarks" article ("Compare stores that share a similar format and product mix... placing them in the same benchmark can hide useful differences") and tablepage.ai's "Retail Store Performance Metrics" article, which states this exact consistency-of-definition principle nearly verbatim.
- The sales-per-square-foot formula (total sales ÷ selling square footage) and its role as "the great equalizer" normalizing for store size are CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across an unusually large number of independent sources: Zerodha's "Retail Sector Analysis Part 2," Tableau's "Retail Industry Metrics & KPIs," Epos Now's "Retail Store KPIs," storeauditiq.com's "12 Retail KPIs Every Multi-Store Operator Should Track," spark.mishipay.com's "Multi-Location Retail Analytics," Pomegra's "Sales per square foot and store productivity," retail.town's "Using Sales Per Square Foot (SPF)," generatekpi.com's "Multi-Location Business KPIs," SBPI's "Core retail KPI Sales per Square Foot" PDF, and theoryhouse.com's "2025 Retail Performance Indicators" — an exceptionally strong, independent convergence across at least ten separate sources.
- The specific warning about inconsistent square-footage definitions (carpet area vs. gross leasable area vs. selling area) breaking comparisons is sourced directly from retail.town's "Using Sales Per Square Foot (SPF)" article, which names this exact common trap.
- The reported benchmark range variation by category/format ($200-1,200+ per square foot annually depending on category) is sourced from storeauditiq.com's "12 Retail KPIs" article (providing a detailed format-by-format benchmark table) and independently corroborated by spark.mishipay.com's article, which cites a similar but not identical range ($200-1,200/sqft across apparel, electronics, grocery). This module treats the general range as directionally confirmed across two sources while noting real variation in exact figures between sources, consistent with this project's practice of not overstating precision where sources genuinely differ.
- The district-scorecard build sequence (standardize data inputs, build a 6-8 KPI scorecard covering revenue/labor/customer experience/operations, establish internal-then-external benchmarks, create a review cadence) is sourced directly and specifically from generatekpi.com's "Multi-Location Business KPIs" article, which names this exact five-step implementation sequence.
- This module's explicit tie connecting the 6-8 KPI scorecard principle to this project's own C12 course's 5-8 headline KPI dashboard-design discipline is this module's own original synthesis, connecting the confirmed general multi-location KPI framework to this project's own previously-established, independently-verified content; not itself an externally sourced claim.
- The cohort-segmentation discipline (segmenting comparisons by store maturity, format, region, operating hours, selling area, merchandise mix, season, and promotional calendar; treating a newly opened store as belonging in a ramp-up cohort rather than a mature-store comparison) is sourced directly and specifically from tablepage.ai's "Retail Store Performance Metrics" article, which names this exact list of segmentation dimensions and this exact ramp-up-cohort principle.
- This module's explicit tie connecting the ramp-up-cohort principle to this project's own C13 course content on partial ramp-quota expectations for new hires is this module's own original synthesis; not itself an externally sourced claim, though it accurately reflects that previously-established, independently-verified content.
- The "diagnose outcomes with paired measures rather than isolated numbers" principle is sourced directly from the same tablepage.ai article, and this module's explicit tie to this project's own C12 course's driver-tree diagnostic approach is this module's own original synthesis connecting these two frameworks.
- The worked scenario (the small store that wasn't actually underperforming once normalized) and the practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses created for this module, not externally sourced cases.
