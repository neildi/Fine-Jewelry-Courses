# C14-M02 Outline: The Store Visit: Agenda, Audit, Debrief

## Module Metadata
- Course: C14 - Multi-Store Leadership: Store Visits, Audits, Manager Development
- Module: 2 of 8
- Persona: P8 (Multi-Store Leader), per official course architecture
- Working title: The Store Visit: Agenda, Audit, Debrief
- Learning objective: Multi-unit leaders can apply the five-part store-visit rhythm (prepare, observe, coach, verify, follow up), prepare using prior commitments and current KPIs, run a structured audit across five core domains with captured evidence, balance in-the-moment coaching with a focused end-of-visit debrief, and close every visit with named owners and specific follow-up timing.

## Production Notes
- The five-part store-visit rhythm (Prepare, Observe, Coach, Verify, Follow up) and its specific checklist items (review sales/conversion/payroll/staffing in advance; revisit prior commitments; observe through the customer's eyes before giving feedback; ask questions before providing solutions; coach in real time; recognize strong performance; agree on two or three clear priorities; document owners, timing, and follow-up plan) are sourced directly and specifically from runninggreatstores.com's "District Manager Store Visit Template & Guide" article, which names this exact five-part structure and provides this exact checklist.
- The five core audit domains (merchandising/visual standards, inventory/stock management, store operations, customer experience, compliance/loss-prevention) are CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent retail-audit sources: Shopify's "Store Audit Checklist," fieldpie.com's "Retail Audit Guide 2026" (explicitly naming five core domains: shelf/merchandising compliance, inventory/storage, store operations, customer experience, compliance/legal), Axonify's "Retail audit guide," and goaudits.com's "Retail Store Audits" article (categorizing checklists into Merchandising & Planograms, Health & Safety, Store Operations, Security & Loss Prevention, and Customer Service) — an unusually strong convergence across independent sources on this same five-category structure.
- The evidence-capture discipline (photos, specific observations, comparing expected standard against actual) is sourced directly from gopazo.com's "Retail Store Audit Explained" article, which names this exact sequence ("Capture Evidence: Record photos, observations and relevant context... Compare the expected state with what is actually happening"), and independently corroborated by goaudits.com's article and Industry Today's "A Guide to Retail Audits" (both recommending photo documentation for before/after comparison).
- This module's explicit tie connecting the evidence-capture discipline to this course's own C13 content on evidence-based performance documentation is this module's own original synthesis, connecting the confirmed general audit-evidence principle to this project's own previously-established, independently-verified content; not itself an externally sourced claim.
- The "ask questions before providing solutions" and "coach the Store Manager on what you observe in real time" in-the-moment coaching principles, alongside "agree on two or three clear priorities before leaving," are sourced directly from the same runninggreatstores.com article.
- This module's explicit tie connecting the "two or three priorities, not an exhaustive list" principle to Module 5's one-development-area coaching discipline (established in this project's own C13 course) is this module's own original synthesis; not itself an externally sourced claim, though it accurately reflects that previously-established content.
- The worked scenario (the visit that surfaced a real scheduling-software obstacle) and the practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses created for this module, not externally sourced cases.
