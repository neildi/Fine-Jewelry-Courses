# C14-M01 Outline: From Running a Store to Running Managers

## Module Metadata
- Course: C14 - Multi-Store Leadership: Store Visits, Audits, Manager Development
- Module: 1 of 8
- Persona: P8 (Multi-Store Leader), per official course architecture
- Working title: From Running a Store to Running Managers
- Learning objective: Store leaders transitioning to multi-unit roles can articulate the core "doing to enabling" mindset shift, recognize why single-store management skills don't automatically transfer, apply the four core multi-unit capabilities, build deliberate lead-from-a-distance and follow-up systems, and build a cross-store consistency system.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: This opening module lays the foundational mindset shift the rest of C14 depends on, directly preceding the course's stated terminal outcomes (structured store visits, comparative KPI analysis, coaching managers, compliance oversight, talent pipeline).

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames this as a genuinely different job, not a scaled-up version of single-store leadership, building directly on C13's toolkit.
2. **The Core Shift: From Doing to Enabling Others to Do** — The "doing vs. enabling" framing and the district-manager-multiplier distinction.
3. **Why Being a Great Store Manager Doesn't Automatically Transfer** — The no-operations-manual problem and the "running a district like one big store" trap.
4. **Four Specific Capabilities Multi-Unit Leadership Requires** — Developing others, letting go of control, big-picture focus, avoiding doing the store's job.
5. **Leading From a Distance** — The structural challenge of relationship-building without daily presence.
6. **Following Up Is Where the Real Work Happens** — The delegation-follow-up discipline specific to multi-unit scale.
7. **Consistency Across Stores Without a Single Daily Script** — The need for a deliberate cross-store consistency system.
8. **Putting the Vocabulary to Work** — The complete mindset-shift sequence.
9. **A Worked Scenario: The District Manager Who Kept Fixing Things** — Illustrative synthesis showing the cost of personally fixing problems during store visits.
10. **Practice Exercise** — Identifying a recent instance of personally solving a problem and reworking the approach into a coaching prompt.
11. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The "doing to enabling" core management-transition framing is sourced from ACM Queue's "The Evolution of Management" article and independently corroborated by Splunk's "Individual Contributors vs Managers" article, both describing this same fundamental shift from personal output to enabling others' output.
- The specific claim that a district manager's "primary job is to develop Store Managers who can run their locations without the DM present" is sourced directly from jobdescription.org's "District Manager Job Description, Salary & Career Outlook" article, a detailed, specific source on this exact role.
- The "struggling DM solves problems / great DM builds teams that solve problems before the DM hears about them" and "struggling DM visits to inspect / great DM visits to develop" framing is sourced directly and specifically from a LinkedIn post by a named multi-unit retail operations expert (Ginger Vandeventer), explicitly describing this "DM Multiplier Effect" distinction with matching paired-contrast language.
- The claim that skills making someone a great store manager "can actually work against them" in a district role, and the specific "5 critical shifts" framing (doing to delegating, managing one team to leading many, daily operations to strategic thinking), is sourced from a LinkedIn post by a named district-manager-development consultant (Aicha Bascaro), and independently corroborated by mohrretail.com's "4 Qualities of Successful Retail Multi-Unit Managers" article, which explicitly warns against "trying to run a district or region the same way you ran your single store."
- The specific claim that a store manager's job is "directed by the operations manual" while a district manager lacks this same daily guidance and must self-direct is sourced directly from americanfranchiseacademy.com's "This is Why District Managers Fail" article.
- The four specific multi-unit capabilities (develop others' strengths, let go of control, look at the big picture every day, avoid getting caught up in doing the store's job) are sourced directly and specifically from mohrretail.com's "4 Qualities of Successful Retail Multi-Unit Managers" article, which names these exact four capabilities as what "the most successful multi-unit managers are able to" do.
- The "leading from a distance" challenge and its distinction from daily-presence relationship-building is sourced from both americanfranchiseacademy.com's article ("lead and inspire from a distance") and mohrretail.com's article ("leading from a distance strategically... the lack of daily physical proximity").
- The "developing a process to follow up on delegated tasks is where the real leadership work happens" principle is sourced directly from americanfranchiseacademy.com's article ("know how to follow up properly... that's where the magic really happens").
- The cross-store consistency-system requirement is sourced directly from the same americanfranchiseacademy.com article ("have systems to achieve consistency across the district... something a unit manager doesn't need").
- This module's explicit connections to this project's own C13 (delegation) and C11 (daily/weekly/monthly operating rhythm) course content are this module's own original synthesis, connecting confirmed general multi-unit leadership research to this project's own previously-established, independently-verified prior course content; not itself an externally sourced claim.
- The worked scenario (the district manager who kept fixing merchandising problems) and the practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses created for this module, not externally sourced cases.
