# C14-M01 Fact-Check: From Running a Store to Running Managers

## Verification Status: The core mindset-shift, capability, and structural-challenge claims are all confirmed across multiple independent, credible multi-unit-retail-leadership sources, with no MUST VERIFY items identified in this module's research

## Claims Reviewed

**1. The "doing to enabling" core management-transition framing.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced from ACM Queue's "The Evolution of Management" article ("When you move into management, you step away from that direct line... your role is to mentor, motivate, and guide your team") and independently corroborated by Splunk's "Individual Contributors vs Managers" article, describing the same fundamental shift.

**2. The claim that a district manager's "primary job is to develop Store Managers who can run their locations without the DM present."**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from jobdescription.org's "District Manager Job Description, Salary & Career Outlook" article, a detailed, specific source stating this exact framing.

**3. The "struggling DM solves problems / great DM builds teams that solve problems before the DM hears about them" and "visits to inspect / visits to develop" framing (the "DM Multiplier Effect").**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly and specifically from a LinkedIn post by named multi-unit retail operations expert Ginger Vandeventer, explicitly using this "DM Multiplier Effect" language and these exact paired contrasts.

**4. The claim that store-manager skills "can actually work against them" in a district role, and the "5 critical shifts" framing.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced from a LinkedIn post by named district-manager-development consultant Aicha Bascaro ("The skills that made you a great store manager can actually work against you as a district manager... from doing to delegating, from managing one team to leading many, from daily operations to strategic thinking"), and independently corroborated by mohrretail.com's "4 Qualities of Successful Retail Multi-Unit Managers" article, which explicitly warns against "trying to run a district or region the same way you ran your single store."

**5. The claim that a store manager's job is "directed by the operations manual" while a district manager must self-direct.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from americanfranchiseacademy.com's "This is Why District Managers Fail" article, which explicitly states this exact distinction.

**6. The four specific multi-unit capabilities (develop others' strengths, let go of control, look at the big picture every day, avoid getting caught up in doing the store's job).**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly and specifically from mohrretail.com's "4 Qualities of Successful Retail Multi-Unit Managers" article, which names these exact four capabilities based on the source's own stated work with retailers nationally.

**7. The "leading from a distance" challenge and its distinction from daily-presence relationship-building.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced from both americanfranchiseacademy.com's article ("lead and inspire from a distance") and mohrretail.com's article ("leading from a distance strategically... the lack of daily physical proximity").

**8. The "following up on delegated tasks is where the real leadership work happens" principle.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from americanfranchiseacademy.com's article ("know how to follow up properly... that's where the magic really happens").

**9. The cross-store consistency-system requirement, unique to the multi-unit role.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from the same americanfranchiseacademy.com article ("have systems to achieve consistency across the district... something a unit manager doesn't need because they only have one unit to focus on").

**10. This module's explicit connections to this project's own C13 (delegation) and C11 (daily/weekly/monthly operating rhythm) course content.**
Status: ORIGINAL synthesis created for this module, connecting confirmed general multi-unit leadership research to this project's own previously-established, independently-verified prior course content. Not itself an externally sourced claim.

**11. Worked scenario (the district manager who kept fixing merchandising problems) and practice exercise.**
