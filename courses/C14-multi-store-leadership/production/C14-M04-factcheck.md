# C14-M04 Fact-Check: Coaching Managers on Coaching

## Verification Status: The "hats" framework, the B-player-investment principle, and the portfolio-diagnosis-then-coach principle are all sourced from named, specific sources; the GROW model's applicability to manager-coaching is independently corroborated by a dedicated coach-training source

## Claims Reviewed

**1. The "hats" framework (strategist, coach, financial analyst, operator) and the coach-hat pitfall ("Telling instead of asking; solving problems for your managers instead of teaching them how").**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly and specifically from eathealthy365.com's "The Responsibilities of a District Manager" article, which names this exact framework with this exact table structure (Role / Primary Focus / Common Pitfall to Avoid) and this exact pitfall description for the Coach role.

**2. The "invest disproportionate time in B-player managers, then have A-players mentor C-players" principle.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from the same eathealthy365.com article, which states this exact recommendation ("Invest your prime time in your 'B' players... A small improvement with them has a much bigger impact on your district's overall results than trying to fix a deeply broken store. Turn your 'B's into 'A's, and empower your 'A's to mentor your 'C's").

**3. The "look across the portfolio, recognize each store is unique, coach the manager on the specific driver" principle, and the "what store/store manager needs coaching on what metric" framing.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from a RetailWire discussion article ("Should district managers be held more accountable for store performance?"), which contains this exact framing in a named commentator's response within the discussion thread.

**4. The accountability-without-micromanagement tension (whether DMs are held accountable with the same rigor as store managers).**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced from the same RetailWire discussion, which explicitly poses this exact question as its central discussion topic, drawing multiple named industry commentators' responses.

**5. "Coaching other managers" and "teaching, training and developing" as core, explicitly named district-manager responsibilities, distinct from associate-level training.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent job-description and role-definition sources: Kohl's official "District Manager, Stores" careers page, AppleOne's "Retail District Manager Job Description," Indeed's "District Manager Job Description," and several real job postings (Circle K and others) consulted during this module's research, all explicitly naming manager-coaching or manager-development as a distinct core responsibility.

**6. The GROW model's applicability to manager-coaching without requiring formal coach certification.**
Status: CONFIRMED, independently corroborated by coachingcultureatwork.com's "Coach Training for Leaders and Managers" article, which explicitly confirms managers do not need formal coach certification to use coaching techniques in their day-to-day role, and that even a short introductory workshop provides a foundational understanding usable immediately — directly supporting this module's premise.

**7. This module's specific application of C13's coaching structures (observe-debrief-practice, observation-impact-alternative, GROW model) to the meta-coaching context via direct modeling followed by observed practice.**
Status: ORIGINAL synthesis created for this module, connecting the confirmed general meta-coaching principles above to this project's own previously-established, independently-verified C13 content. Not itself an externally sourced claim, but built directly on already-confirmed prior course content.
