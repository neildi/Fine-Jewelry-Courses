# C14-M03 Fact-Check: Comparative KPI Analysis Across Stores

## Verification Status: The sales-per-square-foot formula and normalization principle are confirmed across an exceptionally large number of independent sources (10+); the reported benchmark-range figures are directionally confirmed but vary somewhat in exact numbers between sources, which this module explicitly notes rather than overstating precision

## Claims Reviewed

**1. The core "raw comparisons mislead because stores differ in size/format" problem and the consistency-of-definition principle.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from Shopify's "Sales per Square Foot by Industry: Benchmarks" article ("Compare stores that share a similar format and product mix... placing them in the same benchmark can hide useful differences") and tablepage.ai's "Retail Store Performance Metrics" article, which states the consistency-of-definition principle nearly verbatim ("A metric is comparable only when its numerator, denominator, scope, period, and exclusions remain consistent").

**2. The sales-per-square-foot formula (total sales ÷ selling square footage) as the primary size-normalizing metric.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across an exceptionally large number of independent sources: Zerodha's "Retail Sector Analysis Part 2," Tableau's "Retail Industry Metrics & KPIs," Epos Now's "Retail Store KPIs," storeauditiq.com's "12 Retail KPIs Every Multi-Store Operator Should Track," spark.mishipay.com's "Multi-Location Retail Analytics," Pomegra's "Sales per square foot and store productivity," retail.town's "Using Sales Per Square Foot (SPF)," generatekpi.com's "Multi-Location Business KPIs," SBPI's "Core retail KPI Sales per Square Foot" PDF, and theoryhouse.com's "2025 Retail Performance Indicators." This represents an unusually strong, independent convergence across at least ten separate sources on an identical formula.

**3. The specific warning about inconsistent square-footage definitions (carpet area vs. gross leasable area vs. selling area) breaking comparisons.**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly from retail.town's "Using Sales Per Square Foot (SPF)" article, which names this exact common trap and recommends teams agree on and uniformly apply a single area definition.

**4. Reported benchmark-range variation by category/format.**
Status: PARTIALLY CONFIRMED WITH ACKNOWLEDGED VARIATION. storeauditiq.com's "12 Retail KPIs" article provides a detailed format-by-format benchmark table (e.g., apparel specialty $300-500 median, top quartile $700+; quick-service restaurant $700-1,200 median). spark.mishipay.com's article cites a somewhat different range (apparel $200-600/sqft, electronics $500-1,200/sqft, grocery $300-600/sqft). This module treats the general order-of-magnitude range as directionally confirmed across these two sources while explicitly noting real variation in exact figures between sources, consistent with this project's practice of not overstating precision where sources genuinely differ.

**5. The district-scorecard build sequence (standardize data inputs, build a 6-8 KPI scorecard covering revenue/labor/customer experience/operations, establish internal-then-external benchmarks, create a review cadence).**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly and specifically from generatekpi.com's "Multi-Location Business KPIs" article, which names this exact five-step implementation sequence in this exact order.

**6. This module's explicit tie connecting the 6-8 KPI scorecard principle to this project's own C12 course's 5-8 headline KPI dashboard-design discipline.**
Status: ORIGINAL synthesis created for this module, connecting the confirmed general multi-location KPI framework to this project's own previously-established, independently-verified content. Not itself an externally sourced claim, but built on already-confirmed prior course content.

**7. The cohort-segmentation discipline (segmenting by store maturity, format, region, operating hours, selling area, merchandise mix, season, promotional calendar; ramp-up cohort for new stores).**
Status: CONFIRMED, sourced directly and specifically from tablepage.ai's "Retail Store Performance Metrics" article, which names this exact list of segmentation dimensions and explicitly states "A newly opened store can still be analyzed, but it belongs in a ramp-up cohort rather than a mature-store comparison."
