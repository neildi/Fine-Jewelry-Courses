# Sizing, Timelines, Custom and Resizing Realities

## The ring that arrived on time but didn't fit

The proposal is in three weeks. The ring arrives in two — on time. And it doesn't fit. She slips it on and it spins, or it won't pass the knuckle, and the moment that should have been perfect is spent explaining that the store will resize it next week. It was avoidable. The associate never sized her properly, never asked about the timeline with a margin, and never said the one sentence about resizing that would have saved the day: *a ring that must fit on a specific date needs to be right the first time, because the timeline and the size are the same conversation.*

This module is about the practical realities that separate a smooth bridal sale from a beautiful ring that arrives wrong or late: sizing it right, stating the timeline honestly, turning a custom idea into a specification, and setting resizing expectations up front.

## Why this matters

Bridal postings in this project's research put this work in the job description. A bridal manager's duties include "support custom engagement ring projects, redesigns, upgrades," and a bridal sales specialist is expected to "translate client ideas into clear specifications for our design and production teams." Sizing, timelines, and custom are not back-office footnotes — they are named competencies, and they feed a metric you are judged on: the **custom design pipeline**.

They also feed trust. The couple who learns at the proposal that the ring doesn't fit or arrives late does not become a referral. The couple whose ring fits on the day, because you sized it, padded the timeline, and set expectations early, tells a different story entirely.

## Sizing it right the first time

A ring that must fit a specific finger on a specific date deserves a correct size, measured well. The basics:

- **Measure the correct finger, on the correct hand.** The ring finger on the left hand, unless they tell you otherwise. It is the most common sizing error in bridal, and it is free to avoid.
- **Measure at a stable time.** Fingers change size through the day and with temperature — they swell in heat and after activity, and shrink in cold. Measure mid-appointment, not after the couple has been outside in the cold or gripping a hot coffee.
- **Consider the band width.** A wider band sits tighter than a narrow one of the same labeled size. If the design is wide, say so before ordering, not after.
- **Account for the surprise factor.** If one partner is buying as a surprise and you don't have a true size, borrow a ring from the wearer's existing collection (with the buyer's help), size it, and add a note that a final resize may be needed after the proposal. A slight miss is recoverable; a wildly wrong size is a story the couple tells for years.

## Timelines: the number every couple asks for and every associate softens

Every bridal appointment contains a timeline question, and the temptation is to give the most optimistic answer — "we can do that." The discipline is to give the honest one, with margin, because the proposal date is not movable.

- **Off-the-shelf plus sizing** is usually the fastest path, but "in stock" is not "on her finger." Sizing, engraving, and any setting adjustment take real days. State them.
- **Custom work takes weeks, not days.** A custom ring — designed, approved, cast, set, and finished — runs on a production calendar that the workshop controls, not the counter. The course this catalog pairs with custom work (C8, Custom Design & Repair Workflow) covers the gates in detail; for this module, the floor rule is: quote the honest range with margin, and never promise a date the bench hasn't confirmed.
- **Rush is a real thing with real limits.** If the date is tight, say what is and isn't possible before the couple commits, not after. A couple told "we can't promise the custom ring by Saturday, but this in-stock piece can be sized by Thursday" gets to choose with open eyes.

The sentence that saves the day: **"tell me the date you need it by, and I'll tell you honestly what gets there."**

## Custom: from "something like this" to a specification

Custom is where a bridal specialist adds the most value — and where vague language costs the most. A couple says "something like this but with a thinner band and a warmer stone," and if those words go to the bench unrefined, the result will not match the picture in their heads.

The fix is to translate the idea into a specification before it leaves the counter, which is exactly what the job postings describe. The sequence:

1. **Pin the references.** Two or three photos or existing pieces the couple likes, and one thing each that they would change.
2. **Name the variables.** Shape, setting style, band width, metal, stone specs, and any sizing notes — written down, not remembered.
3. **Restate it in plain words and get a nod.** "So: an oval, rose gold, thinner band, bezel instead of prongs — is that the ring?" The couple's nod is the first approval gate.
4. **Hand off with a clear spec and a real date.** The bench gets the written spec, the couple gets the honest timeline and the deposit terms, and everyone gets the same picture.

C8 is the deep course on the full custom workflow — discovery, CAD, approval gates, deposits, change orders. This module's job is the bridal front end: turning "something like this" into a specification the bench can build and the couple has approved.

## Resizing realities, said before the sale

Resizing is where post-sale disappointment is manufactured, and almost all of it is avoidable by one honest sentence before the sale. The realities worth stating:

- **Most rings can be sized up or down a limited amount.** A plain band moves easily. But a ring covered in stones — an eternity band, a full pavé, or a tension setting — is a different animal: it may be resizable only slightly, or not at all, and the time to say so is before they buy it, not when they bring it back.
- **A stone-covered band must be ordered in the right size.** If the design can't be resized, the size becomes the order. This is why sizing discipline and setting choice are the same conversation (Module 3).
- **Finger size changes.** Weight changes, temperature, even time of day move a finger by a fraction of a size. A ring that fits today may need a quarter-size adjustment in a year; that is normal, not a defect, and saying so up front removes the surprise.
- **The proposal-day fix is the one to avoid.** When the ring doesn't fit on the day, the fix is a story the couple tells. When it fits, nobody mentions the sizing — and that silence is the win.

## On the floor: applying it this week

- **Check your sizing routine.** For one week, note which hand and finger you measure by default, and whether you consider band width and time of day. Fix the one most common slip.
- **Build a timeline cheat sheet.** List the honest lead times for: in-stock + sizing, engraving, and custom, with the bench-confirmed ranges. Say the padded number, not the optimistic one.
- **Practice the spec restatement.** For the next custom request, write the spec and restate it out loud ("an oval, rose gold, thinner band, bezel — is that the ring?") before it leaves the counter.
- **Say the resizing sentence up front** on every stone-covered band or tension setting: "this one can't be easily resized later, so let's get the size right now."

## Objections, mistakes and edge cases

| Situation | The trap | Better move |
|---|---|---|
| Surprise proposal, no true ring size | Guessing the size | Borrow an existing ring, size it, and note a post-proposal resize may be needed |
| Couple wants a custom ring in a week | Promising the impossible | Say what's possible and offer the honest alternatives |
| Stone-covered band, wrong size | Ordering it anyway | State it can't be easily resized; get the size right now |
| Couple says "whatever fits, you decide" | Ordering a guess | Measure properly and confirm; the finger is not a guess |
| Finger size changes later | Treating it as a defect | Normalize it up front: a later quarter-size tweak is common |
| Custom idea is vague | Sending vagueness to the bench | Translate to a written spec and get the couple's nod first |
| The date is tight | Softening the timeline to please | "Tell me the date — I'll tell you honestly what gets there" |
