# M07 Quiz — Couple Dynamics

10 questions testing the terminal outcome: managing two decision-makers. Answer key at the end.

---

**1. What are the two most common couple patterns?**
a) Rich and poor.
b) The vocal-one/quiet-one, and the two-who-disagree.
c) Young and old.
d) Local and out-of-town.

**2. One partner dominates the conversation. What should you do?**
a) Address everything to the talker.
b) Ask the quiet partner a direct, low-stakes question and hold space.
c) Ignore the quiet partner until they speak.
d) Ask the talker to be quiet.

**3. Why is taking a side in a couple's disagreement a mistake?**
a) It's always against policy.
b) It makes you the tie-breaker, and the "loser" resents both the outcome and the referee.
c) It slows the sale.
d) It's illegal.

**4. What is the reconcile-the-priorities move?**
a) Pick the cheaper option.
b) Isolate what each person actually values, then find a third option that holds both.
c) Flip a coin.
d) Let the louder partner decide.

**5. A parent insists "lab-grown isn't real." What do you do?**
a) Argue with the parent.
b) Surrender and show only natural.
c) Give accurate facts to everyone calmly, then re-center: "which feels right to you two?"
d) Ask the parent to leave.

**6. "We need to think about it" is genuine. What do you do?**
a) Push through the hesitation.
b) Honor it, send the favorites, and make the door easy to come back through.
c) Offer a bigger discount.
d) Take it personally.

**7. Why does referral volume compound the importance of couple dynamics?**
a) Referrals are the only metric.
b) A couple who both felt heard tells the story; one who felt steamrolled tells the opposite.
c) Referrals are unaffected by dynamics.
d) Couples never refer anyone.

**8. The couple defers every decision to you. What do you do?**
a) Decide for them to save time.
b) Narrow, then reflect the call back: "the final call is yours — what are you leaning toward?"
c) Refuse to help.
d) Pick the most expensive ring.

**9. When might a disagreement actually be about something else?**
a) Never — couples always argue about rings.
b) When the energy doesn't match the stated objection — a budget or timeline fear, or deeper hesitation.
c) Only on weekends.
d) When the ring is over $5,000.

**10. What is the sign that the quiet partner has a real opinion they aren't sharing?**
a) They smile at everything.
b) A tightened mouth or an extra-long look at a tray the talker dismissed.
c) They ask for water.
d) They check their phone.

---

## Answer key

1. **b** — The dominant/quiet split and the two-who-disagree are the two core patterns.
2. **b** — A direct, low-stakes question to the quiet partner brings them in without forcing.
3. **b** — Refereeing makes the "loser" resent the outcome and you.
4. **b** — Find the option that holds both priorities rather than picking a winner.
5. **c** — Calm facts for everyone, then hand the decision back to the couple.
6. **b** — Honoring a genuine hesitation and keeping the door open protects the relationship.
7. **b** — Both-felt-heard couples refer; steamrolled couples tell the opposite story.
8. **b** — Narrow helpfully, but keep ownership of the decision with the couple.
9. **b** — A mismatch between the objection and the energy signals the real issue is elsewhere.
10. **b** — Micro-signals like a tightened mouth or a lingering look are the quiet partner's language.
