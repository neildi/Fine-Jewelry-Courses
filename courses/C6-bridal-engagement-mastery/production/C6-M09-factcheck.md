# C6-M09 Fact-Check: Wedding Bands and Stacking

## Verification Status: Contour-fit and stacking principles strongly corroborated; wedding-band timing windows show genuine source-to-source variance

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Contoured/curved bands recommended for halo, three-stone, and vintage-style rings to avoid a visible gap.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across Queensmith ("curved or contoured wedding rings are often designed to fit neatly around a centre stone"), Stienhardt (curved/nesting band styles for engagement ring pairing), and JewelTrendPro's timing guide ("contouring prevents gaps" for halo/three-stone styles specifically).

**2. Metal-matching rationale (uneven wear from hardness differences; possible galvanic corrosion risk).**
Status: Sourced from JewelTrendPro's timing guide ("matching metals prevent galvanic corrosion") and Queensmith's stacking guide ("check metal density... to prevent harder metals from wearing down softer ones"); the thermal-expansion/buffer-band recommendation for mixed-metal stacks is sourced from a separate JewelTrendPro stacking guide. These are consistent with standard metallurgical principles regarding dissimilar-metal contact, though the galvanic-corrosion claim specifically is presented as a real but secondary consideration (the primary driver in all sources is wear/durability, not corrosion risk).

**3. Spacer bands to separate mixed metals or manage a stack's visual balance.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across Queensmith (spacer band to "prevent the stack from looking like one solid block of metal") and JewelTrendPro's stacking guide (buffer band between dissimilar metals for thermal/wear reasons) — independently corroborated for two distinct but related reasons (aesthetic and durability).

**4. Stacking principles (width variation, two-metal-tone limit, anchor-then-build approach).**
Status: STRONGLY CROSS-CORROBORATED across Queensmith, Stienhardt, and two separate JewelTrendPro stacking guides. The specific two-metal-tone-maximum guidance and the "anchor with your most significant piece" approach both appear independently across multiple sources with closely matching language and logic.

**5. Wedding-band shopping timeline windows (3-4 months in-stock; ~6 months with customization; 8 months-1 year fully custom/contour-matched).**
Status: MODERATE-TO-SUBSTANTIAL VARIANCE across eleven sources reviewed. SoFi and Borsheims cite 3-4 months (in-stock) and 6-7 months (general); Scott's Custom Jewelers cites 6-8 months general, 8-9 months for contour-matched custom; JewelTrendPro's four separate timing guides give varying specific breakdowns (one cites 12-16 months for fully custom, another cites 12-20 weeks for matching sets specifically); WedSites and PriceScope cite shorter 3-4 month general windows. This module synthesizes these into three representative bands (3-4mo / ~6mo / 8mo-1yr) that reasonably reflect the center of the reported range, while acknowledging real variance exists depending on which specific source and customization type is referenced.

**6. Contour-matched/fully custom wedding bands following similar timeline logic to Module 7's general custom-design process.**
Status: This is a reasonable extrapolation from Module 7's general custom-design timeline content combined with the sourced wedding-band timing guides that specifically flag contour-matched and heirloom-incorporating bands as needing the longest lead times (JewelTrendPro, Scott's Custom Jewelers) — not a directly sourced one-to-one claim, but a logical and clearly labeled connection between two related, separately-sourced bodies of guidance.

**7. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
