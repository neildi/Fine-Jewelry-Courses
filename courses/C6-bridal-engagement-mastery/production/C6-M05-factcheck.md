# C6-M05 Fact-Check: Metals and Settings

## Verification Status: Setting mechanics strongly cross-corroborated; metal hardness figures treated as approximate/comparative

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Gold karat composition (14k=58.3%, 18k=75%, 22k=91.7% gold) and hardness comparison (14k~150 HV, 18k~125 HV).**
Status: Sourced from Up-Stat's "Engagement Ring Metals" comparison. Karat-percentage figures are standard, uncontested industry math (karat/24 = gold percentage). The specific Vickers hardness figures are presented as approximate/comparative rather than universal constants, since actual alloy hardness varies by specific formulation (which other metals are used to fill the remainder) — this module frames them accordingly.

**2. Platinum's displacement/patina wear mechanism vs. gold's scratch-and-material-loss mechanism; permanent white color with no replating needed.**
Status: Sourced from Up-Stat, consistent with well-established, standard gemological/trade knowledge about platinum's density and wear behavior. This is uncontested industry-standard information.

**3. White gold rhodium plating and 1-3 year replating cycle.**
Status: Sourced from Up-Stat, consistent with widely-known, standard jewelry-industry practice regarding white gold maintenance. Uncontested standard trade knowledge.

**4. Rose gold hardness (~150 HV, comparable to 14k) attributed to copper content.**
Status: Sourced from Up-Stat.

**5. Nickel allergy prevalence (up to ~17% of women, ~3% of men) and nickel as the most common metal contact allergy worldwide.**
Status: Sourced from Mayo Clinic Minute reporting (citing NIH data) and corroborated by AAAAI and DermNet sources describing nickel as the most frequent cause of contact allergy worldwide. This is credible medical-source information; presented as general population data, not store-specific.

**6. Nickel-safe metal list (titanium, niobium, 18k+ yellow gold, platinum, sterling silver, palladium).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across Mayo Clinic, DermNet, and Gale.care sources, which independently list overlapping safe-metal options.

**7. Setting-type descriptions and durability trade-offs (prong, bezel, pavé, channel, halo, shared-prong halo, bezel halo).**
Status: STRONGLY CROSS-CORROBORATED across eight independent sources (The Diamond Price, A Star Diamonds — two articles, Sabyl's, Tanishq, GemPundit, Diamonds.pro, Mapplegems, Angara). All sources describe matching mechanics: prongs maximize light but wear at exposed tips; bezels fully protect the stone edge with superior security; pavé/channel add band sparkle with channel offering slightly better individual-stone protection; halos increase apparent size, with construction sub-types (shared-prong vs. bezel-halo) trading visible metal for protection.

**8. Prong inspection cadence recommendations.**
Status: VARIES SLIGHTLY by source — A Star Diamonds recommends 12 months minimum (18 months for bezel/channel); The Diamond Price recommends 6 months; Sabyl's says "annually." This module presents the range (6-12 months) rather than a single figure, consistent with the course's handling of variable practical-guidance numbers.

Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from any external source.
