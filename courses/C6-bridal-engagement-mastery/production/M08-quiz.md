# M08 Quiz — Sizing, Timelines, Custom and Resizing Realities

10 questions testing the terminal outcome: correct sizing, honest timelines, and resizing expectations. Answer key at the end.

---

**1. What is the most common sizing error in bridal?**
a) Measuring with a plastic sizer.
b) Measuring the wrong finger on the wrong hand.
c) Using millimeters instead of sizes.
d) Measuring twice.

**2. Why does a wider band sit tighter than a narrow one of the same labeled size?**
a) It doesn't — that's a myth.
b) More band surface against the finger means it feels tighter.
c) Wider bands are always sized smaller.
d) It's a manufacturer error.

**3. When should you measure for size?**
a) After the couple has been outside in the cold.
b) Mid-appointment, at a stable temperature and time.
c) First thing in the morning.
d) Right after they hold hot coffee.

**4. A couple wants a custom ring in one week. What do you do?**
a) Promise it to keep the sale.
b) Say what's honestly possible and offer alternatives, including in-stock.
c) Decline the sale.
d) Rush it without telling the bench.

**5. What is the honest-timeline rule?**
a) "We can do that."
b) Quote the optimistic number so they're happy.
c) "Tell me the date you need it by, and I'll tell you honestly what gets there."
d) Never discuss timelines.

**6. What is the first step in turning "something like this" into a specification?**
a) Send the vague words to the bench.
b) Pin the references — two or three photos or pieces they like, plus what they'd change.
c) Guess what they meant.
d) Draw it yourself without asking.

**7. When must you state the resizing limit before the sale?**
a) Never — it's after-sale business.
b) On a stone-covered band or tension setting, which can't be easily resized.
c) Only for gold rings.
d) Only when they ask.

**8. For a surprise proposal with no true size, what do you do?**
a) Guess the size.
b) Borrow an existing ring with the buyer's help, size it, and note a post-proposal resize may be needed.
c) Refuse the sale.
d) Order the average size.

**9. Why are the timeline and the size "the same conversation"?**
a) They aren't related.
b) A ring that must fit a specific date has to be sized right the first time — both feed the same deadline.
c) Sizes are set by timelines.
d) It's just a slogan.

**10. What should you do before a custom order leaves the counter?**
a) Restate the spec in plain words and get the couple's nod.
b) Email the bench without a spec.
c) Skip the approval to save time.
d) Let the couple finalize later.

---

## Answer key

1. **b** — The wrong finger/hand is the classic bridal sizing error.
2. **b** — Wider bands grip more finger surface and feel tighter.
3. **b** — Stable, mid-appointment measurement avoids temperature/time distortion.
4. **b** — Honest alternatives beat a promised date the bench can't meet.
5. **c** — Anchor on the date and state what actually gets there.
6. **b** — Pin references first so the bench builds the right picture.
7. **b** — Stone-covered and tension designs carry resizing limits that must be said up front.
8. **b** — Borrow-and-size with a post-proposal resize note is the honest path.
9. **b** — A fixed proposal date makes correct sizing and honest timeline one requirement.
10. **a** — A plain-language restatement and their nod is the first approval gate.
