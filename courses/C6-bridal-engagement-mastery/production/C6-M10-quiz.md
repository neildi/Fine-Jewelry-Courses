# C6-M10 Quiz: Appraisal, Insurance, and Aftercare

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly explaining value distinctions and insurance trade-offs, not memorizing exact dollar figures.

---

**1. What does an insurance appraisal's retail replacement value (RRV) actually represent?**
A) What the ring would sell for in a resale market
B) The cost to replace the item with an equivalent piece at a traditional retail jewelry store, accounting for materials, labor, and retail markup
C) The exact original purchase price with no adjustment
D) RRV and fair market value are always identical numbers

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: RRV is intentionally the highest standard valuation type, designed to let an insured client walk into a store and buy a true equivalent replacement, not to reflect resale value.

---

**2. Why might a client be confused if they compare their insurance appraisal number to what their ring might sell for?**
A) Because these numbers are never actually different
B) Because retail replacement value (used for insurance) is typically meaningfully higher than fair market value (used for resale), and both are accurate but answer different questions
C) Because fair market value is always higher than retail replacement value
D) Because insurance appraisals never reflect any real value at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Explaining this distinction proactively prevents a common misunderstanding, since these two accurate but different figures serve different purposes.

---

**3. What is the main limitation of standard, unmodified homeowners or renters insurance for an engagement ring?**
A) It provides unlimited coverage for any jewelry loss
B) It typically caps jewelry coverage at a sub-limit of roughly $1,000-2,500 and often covers theft only, not loss or accidental damage
C) It automatically covers the ring's full appraised value with no limitations
D) Standard homeowners insurance has no bearing on jewelry coverage at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This sub-limit and narrow coverage (theft-only in many cases) makes standard, unmodified homeowners insurance insufficient for most engagement rings.

---

**4. What is a key trade-off of adding a scheduled rider to an existing homeowners policy, compared to a standalone jewelry policy?**
A) Riders always provide broader coverage than standalone policies with no downsides
B) A rider raises coverage to full appraised value but may still exclude mysterious disappearance/accidental damage depending on the carrier, and a claim is typically reported to CLUE, which can affect future home insurance
C) Riders are always free with no premium cost
D) There is no real difference between a rider and a standalone policy

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A standalone policy is typically all-risk and doesn't affect the client's homeowners claims history, while a rider's terms and CLUE-reporting impact are real trade-offs worth explaining clearly.

---

**5. What is the professional consensus for how often a daily-worn engagement ring should receive professional cleaning and inspection?**
A) Once every ten years
B) Every six months, since this catches prong wear before a stone is actually at risk of falling out
C) Never; daily-worn rings require no professional maintenance
D) Only if the ring appears visibly damaged

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Six months is the professional consensus across multiple sources for rings worn daily, specifically because it catches prong wear while it's still a minor, inexpensive repair.

---

**6. Why is this maintenance cadence directly connected to Module 5's content on settings?**
A) It has no real connection to setting type
B) Prong wear is invisible until a stone is at risk, and setting type (prong vs. bezel) directly affects how much monitoring a ring needs — the six-month cadence exists specifically to catch that wear early
C) All settings require identical maintenance regardless of construction
D) Module 5 has no relevance to aftercare at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This module directly extends Module 5's point that prong wear is invisible until a stone is at risk, which is exactly why the recurring inspection cadence matters.

---

**7. Approximately how often should a client get a reappraisal to keep their insurance coverage amount accurate?**
A) Never; the original appraisal is valid forever
B) Every two to three years, since material prices (gold, diamonds) shift over time and coverage should reflect current replacement cost
C) Every single day
D) Reappraisal has no bearing on insurance coverage accuracy

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Periodic reappraisal ensures the insured value keeps pace with changing material costs, so the client isn't underinsured relative to current replacement cost.

**8. In the worked scenario, why does the associate raise the insurance conversation proactively rather than waiting for the client to ask?**
A) Because insurance is legally required to be mentioned at every sale
B) Because most clients don't realize standard homeowners insurance is insufficient, and raising it proactively prevents a future, potentially costly misunderstanding
C) Because the associate is required to sell insurance directly
D) Because clients never care about protecting a purchase this significant

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Proactively raising this, along with a written appraisal and the free six-month maintenance touchpoint, ensures the client leaves fully informed rather than discovering a coverage gap later.
