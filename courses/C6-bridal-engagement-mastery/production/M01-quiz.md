# M01 Quiz — The Modern Bridal Buyer's Journey

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: reading where a bridal buyer is in their research journey and adapting the appointment. Answer key with one-sentence explanations at the end.

---

**1. A couple's first question is "do you have super ideal cuts and hearts-and-arrows stones?" What does this tell you?**
a) They are beginners who need the 4Cs explained.
b) They have done deep research on round-diamond proportions and are past the basics.
c) They only want lab-grown diamonds.
d) They are price-shopping only.

**2. Which statistic best describes bridal's weight in a typical jewelry store's business?**
a) About 5 percent, a small add-on category.
b) About 35 percent (median value) of jewelers' total business, per a JCK retail panel.
c) It is larger than the entire fashion category combined.
d) Under 10 percent in most stores.

**3. A solo researcher walks in and clearly is not buying today. What is the best move?**
a) Push for a close so the visit isn't wasted.
b) Ignore them and help a warmer customer.
c) Educate, give them a spec card or written quote, and book a follow-up.
d) Tell them to come back when they are ready to buy.

**4. A client says "I read that I should only buy H color or better." What is the dignity-preserving response?**
a) "That's wrong; color doesn't matter."
b) "That's a good starting rule for a round brilliant — here's where it bends for rose gold or a cushion cut."
c) "Whoever told you that doesn't know diamonds."
d) "Color is the least important C, ignore it."

**5. What is the "loop," as this module describes the modern bridal journey?**
a) A straight path from ad to purchase.
b) Research online, visit, research again, then return to buy — often at a different store than the first visit.
c) A one-visit close, same as a decade ago.
d) A journey that skips in-store visits entirely.

**6. A family advisor keeps insisting "lab-grown diamonds aren't real." What is the safer move than arguing?**
a) Agree with them to avoid conflict.
b) Correct the advisor sharply in front of the buyer.
c) Validate the advisor's intent, then show the current reality to both of them.
d) End the appointment.

**7. Why does "assume research, confirm it gently" work better than asking "have you heard of the 4Cs?"**
a) It flatters the buyer into buying.
b) It credits the buyer's effort and moves the conversation forward instead of implying they know nothing.
c) It is shorter to say.
d) It avoids all product discussion.

**8. Which buyer is likely to have aligned on some basics before walking in?**
a) The solo researcher.
b) The couple shopping together.
c) The family-in-tow, always.
d) None of them.

**9. A buyer says "we've been to three stores — can you beat this quote?" Where are they in the journey?**
a) Early research.
b) Comparison shopping, late in the loop.
c) Not a serious buyer.
d) Looking for a free appraisal.

**10. What should a solo researcher leave with, instead of a hard sell?**
a) Nothing — they aren't buying today.
b) A written quote, spec card, or appointment so the loop keeps pointing back to your store.
c) A discount to force the decision.
d) A list of competitors to compare you against.

---

## Answer key

1. **b** — Proportion and hearts-and-arrows vocabulary signals deep research; they are past the 4Cs basics.
2. **b** — JCK's retail panel reports 35% (median) of jewelers' business is bridal.
3. **c** — The solo researcher needs low-pressure education and a concrete next step, not a close.
4. **b** — Correct the error while preserving the person's dignity, and show where the rule bends.
5. **b** — The loop describes researching, visiting, re-researching, then returning to buy.
6. **c** — Validate the advisor's intent and show current reality to both, keeping the buyer at the center.
7. **b** — It credits the buyer's effort and moves forward instead of implying ignorance.
8. **b** — A joint shopper has usually aligned on basics and is pressure-testing them.
9. **b** — Comparing quotes is late-loop behavior; respond with fair price and a reason to buy from you.
10. **b** — Give them something concrete so their at-home research keeps returning to your store.
