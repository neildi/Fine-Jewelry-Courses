# M06 Quiz — The Try-On

10 questions testing the terminal outcome: presenting the stone and reading the reaction. Answer key at the end.

---

**1. What are the three things to get right before the stone touches skin?**
a) Price, discount, and warranty.
b) Clean it, get the light right, and have the scope ready.
c) Specs, certificate, and history.
d) The room, the music, and the receipt.

**2. After placing the ring, what is the right amount to say?**
a) A full spec list so they're informed.
b) One honest observation, then silence.
c) Nothing — leave the room.
d) Keep talking so it isn't awkward.

**3. Why does filling the try-on silence with specs cost sales?**
a) Specs are always wrong.
b) It interrupts the emotional moment and can talk a decided couple back into indecision.
c) Clients dislike facts.
d) It's against store policy.

**4. Which signal means the real decision is happening between the couple?**
a) The wearer asks about the warranty.
b) The partner watches the wearer's face, not the ring.
c) The wearer reads the receipt.
d) The couple discusses price.

**5. What should you do when you see the partner watching the wearer's face?**
a) Interrupt to confirm specs.
b) Be invisible — the decision is happening between them.
c) Ask for the close immediately.
d) Offer a discount.

**6. Why is a hard close a mistake at the emotional peak?**
a) It's illegal in bridal.
b) It drags a feeling back into a negotiation; a quiet confirmation works better.
c) Bridal buyers don't close.
d) It always offends.

**7. The phone comes out for a photo. What does it usually mean, and what do you do?**
a) They're leaving; ignore it.
b) Committing the ring to memory/share — offer to take it and suggest the daylight spot.
c) They're checking prices; stop them.
d) It's a security risk; call a manager.

**8. When should you offer the scope or loupe?**
a) Before the hand, always.
b) After the hand, and only if the buyer is interested — it confirms with optics what they're feeling.
c) Never in front of the client.
d) Only for lab-grown.

**9. What does "let's see it in daylight" accomplish?**
a) It wastes time.
b) It re-engages a quiet couple with a reason to keep experiencing the stone.
c) It hides flaws.
d) It's required by GIA.

**10. The wearer is genuinely underwhelmed. What do you do?**
a) Push harder on the specs.
b) Ask what's missing — size, shape, height — and adjust, don't argue.
c) Tell them they're wrong.
d) End the appointment.

---

## Answer key

1. **b** — Clean, light, and scope-ready set the stage for the stone to show itself.
2. **b** — One observation, then silence; the stone sells itself.
3. **b** — Specs interrupt the moment and can reverse a near-decision.
4. **b** — The partner watching the wearer's face is watching the actual decision.
5. **b** — Stay out of the way; the decision is theirs.
6. **b** — A quiet confirmation of the moment beats a negotiation close.
7. **b** — It's commitment/sharing; help with the photo and the light.
8. **b** — After the hand, if interested; optics confirm the feeling.
9. **b** — It gives a quiet couple a reason to keep experiencing the stone.
10. **b** — Diagnose the missing element and adjust, rather than argue.
