# C6-M08 Quiz: The Proposal Logistics Conversation

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly matching logistics recommendations to a client's actual situation, not memorizing exact percentages.

---

**1. Why is in-store pickup often recommended over home delivery for a surprise-proposal ring?**
A) In-store pickup is always cheaper
B) It removes the risk of a delivery notification, package, or timing mishap alerting a partner who may intercept it or be home unexpectedly
C) Home delivery is illegal for engagement rings
D) There is no real difference between pickup and delivery for discretion purposes

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: When a client's home isn't reliably private (e.g., living with the partner), in-store pickup eliminates the discovery risk that comes with scheduling and package handling.

---

**2. What is the single most important practical step recommended before the actual proposal, regarding ring concealment?**
A) Never practice with the ring box beforehand
B) Do a dry run in the actual planned outfit at least 48 hours ahead, since a box that seems hidden standing still can shift, print through fabric, or become noticeable while moving
C) Always carry the ring box in a completely see-through bag
D) Concealment location doesn't matter as long as the ring is in the store's original box

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A dry run reveals problems (shifting, printing through fabric, noticeability while bending or hugging) that aren't apparent when just standing still.

---

**3. What is the critical constraint of a layaway plan that must be communicated clearly to a client with a proposal-timing goal?**
A) Layaway rings can be used to propose immediately after the first payment
B) The client cannot propose with the physical ring until the full balance is paid off
C) Layaway has no bearing on when a client can propose
D) Layaway always requires a credit check

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is confirmed consistently across every layaway source reviewed — possession only transfers once the balance is paid in full, so the term must be structured to clear well before any firm proposal date.

---

**4. Why do layaway deposit percentages, term lengths, and cancellation policies vary so much by store?**
A) They don't vary; every jeweler uses identical terms
B) Layaway terms are set individually by each retailer, so deposits can range from roughly 10-30%, terms from 60 days to a year, and cancellation policies from full refund to restocking fees or store credit only
C) Layaway terms are set by a single national regulatory body
D) Layaway always requires no deposit at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This module found genuine, meaningful variance across nine retailer-specific sources, which is why associates should walk clients through their own store's specific written terms rather than general industry assumptions.

---

**5. Which scenario is realistically eligible for same-day pickup, per this module?**
A) A fully custom halo design with a rare fancy-cut stone
B) An in-stock, ready-to-wear ring that requires no sizing and no engraving
C) Any ring at all, regardless of sizing or customization needs
D) Same-day pickup is never actually possible for engagement rings

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Same-day pickup is realistic specifically for in-stock pieces with no sizing or engraving needed; adding either of those extends the timeline meaningfully.

---

**6. What is the realistic minimum rush timeline for a fully custom engagement ring, even with an added rush fee?**
A) Always same-day, regardless of design
B) Generally two to four weeks minimum, since casting and hand-setting have physical time requirements that can't be meaningfully compressed further
C) Exactly one hour
D) Custom rings can never be rushed under any circumstance

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Casting and hand-setting are physically time-bound processes; being direct about this prevents an associate from making a promise the manufacturing process can't support.

---

**7. What do professional proposal planners typically provide, per this module?**
A) Only the ring itself, nothing else
B) Location scouting, vendor coordination (photographer, videographer, florist), a detailed run-sheet, and on-the-day logistics support
C) They only work with celebrities and are not available to average clients
D) Proposal planners have no relevance to a jewelry store's client relationships

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This service model is consistently described across every proposal-planning source reviewed, and building referral relationships with local planners extends what a jewelry store can offer bridal clients.

---

**8. In the worked scenario, why does the associate recommend in-store pickup rather than delivery for the client proposing this weekend?**
A) Because delivery is always slower than in-store pickup
B) Because the client lives with the partner they're proposing to, making home delivery carry real discovery risk from packages or delivery notifications
C) Because the store never offers home delivery under any circumstance
D) Because the ring needs additional sizing that can only happen in-store

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The recommendation is grounded directly in the client's specific living situation — cohabiting with the proposal recipient makes home delivery a meaningfully riskier option than in-store pickup.
