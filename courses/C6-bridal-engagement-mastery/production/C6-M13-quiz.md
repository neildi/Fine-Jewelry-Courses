# C6-M13 Quiz: Selling Bridal in Cruise and Port Retail Environments

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly applying transparency principles under this channel's specific pressures, not memorizing exact commission percentages.

---

**1. According to GIA's own consumer guidance, who typically earns a commission on cruise/port jewelry purchases?**
A) No one earns a commission; prices are set independently of sales volume
B) The shopping consultant, the store manager and salespeople, and often the cruise line itself
C) Only the individual salesperson, with no involvement from the ship or store management
D) Commission structures never apply in this specific retail channel

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: GIA's guidance is explicit that virtually everyone involved in the purchasing process, including the cruise line itself in many cases, typically earns some form of commission or fee.

---

**2. What does "duty-free" actually mean, and what does it NOT mean?**
A) Duty-free always means the item is discounted below fair market value
B) Duty-free means import/export duties weren't charged when goods entered the port destination; it says nothing about whether the underlying retail price itself is fair
C) Duty-free is a legal guarantee of the lowest possible price
D) Duty-free and tax-free are identical concepts with no distinction

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A store can be genuinely duty-free while still setting a base price high enough that even a "discount" leaves real markup intact — duty-free status is not itself evidence of a good deal.

---

**3. What is the appropriate response to a client who is running low on time before their ship departs and hasn't decided on a purchase?**
A) Emphasize that they must decide before the ship leaves or lose the opportunity
B) Offer a written quote with the price held, encourage them to take photos and compare, and let them decide later without pressure
C) Discourage them from ever considering the purchase again
D) Refuse to provide any information unless they decide immediately

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The ship's departure schedule should never be used as a sales tool; offering a considered, low-pressure path to a later decision respects the client's actual interests.

---

**4. What is the standard U.S. customs duty-free personal exemption for international purchases, and how does it differ for the U.S. Virgin Islands specifically?**
A) There is no meaningful exemption for any U.S. traveler
B) The standard exemption is $800 per person for most international destinations, but it's a notably higher $1,600 specifically for purchases made in the U.S. Virgin Islands
C) The exemption is always exactly the same regardless of destination
D) Exemptions only apply to jewelry, never to any other purchased goods

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is a specific, documented distinction worth knowing accurately, since giving a client incorrect customs information can create a real legal and financial problem for them later.

---

**5. What specific practice does consumer guidance warn travelers to watch for in receipt fine print?**
A) Receipts never contain any additional terms
B) Some receipts contain restrictive "sales contract" language that a client might inadvertently sign without realizing its implications
C) Fine print is always in the client's favor and never needs review
D) Return policies are never included on receipts

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is a specific, documented risk; providing unconditional, clearly written return terms and making sure a client actually understands what they're signing is part of ethical selling in this channel.

---

**6. How should an associate respond if a client wants to photograph a piece or get an independent appraisal before deciding?**
A) Discourage this as a sign of distrust
B) Welcome it — a fairly priced, legitimate piece holds up to that scrutiny, and only an unfairly priced one needs to avoid it
C) Refuse to allow photographs under any circumstances
D) Treat this request as grounds to end the sales conversation

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Encouraging independent verification is a direct, practical demonstration of confidence in fair pricing, and it's the opposite of a tactic used by stores relying on pressure or misdirection.

---

**7. Why does this module argue that transparency has outsized value specifically in the cruise/port retail channel?**
A) Because this channel has no meaningful reputational challenges
B) Because travel-advice communities (Reddit, Cruise Critic, The Points Guy, and similar sources) actively and visibly discuss cruise jewelry experiences, giving a genuinely trustworthy transaction real word-of-mouth value
C) Because word-of-mouth has no relevance in cruise/port retail
D) Because clients in this channel never discuss their purchases with anyone else

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Given how visible and active travel-forum discussion of cruise jewelry shopping is, a single well-handled, transparent transaction can generate meaningfully positive word-of-mouth in a channel with real, documented reputational baggage.

---

**8. In the worked scenario, what does the associate do instead of leaning into the client's two-hour time constraint?**
A) Reminds the client repeatedly that the ship is leaving soon to encourage a quick decision
B) Offers a written quote with the price held, encourages photos and comparison, and removes pressure to decide immediately
C) Refuses to discuss the ring further given the time constraint
D) Tells the client the price will increase if they don't decide immediately

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This directly applies the module's core principle of never using the departure clock as a sales tool, giving the client a genuine, low-risk path to a considered decision.
