# C6-M11 Quiz: Selling Bridal Across Demographics and Life Stages

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes appropriately adapting consultation approach, not memorizing exact statistics.

---

**1. What does the data on self-purchased engagement rings show, per this module?**
A) Self-purchasing is an extremely rare, marginal phenomenon with no real growth
B) The share of engagement rings financed solely by the bride grew meaningfully (from single digits to double digits of purchases) over recent years, and self-purchasers often spend more on average than grooms did
C) Self-purchasing only happens among a tiny handful of celebrities
D) Self-purchase data shows no meaningful trend in any direction

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Industry data (De Beers' Diamond Insight Report, reported by Rapaport) shows real growth in self-purchasing over time, with self-purchasing brides spending more on average than grooms did in the same period.

---

**2. What is a practical consultation adjustment recommended for a self-purchasing client?**
A) Always ask what her partner would think of the ring
B) Avoid defaulting to language that assumes a partner is choosing or paying, since the client has indicated this is their own purchase
C) Refuse to help a client who is buying for themselves
D) Assume self-purchasing clients need less product education than any other client

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Defaulting to partner-assuming language when a client has stated they're self-purchasing can feel dismissive of the framing they've already given; matching the client's own stated context is the better approach.

---

**3. What is a common but not universal pattern in how same-sex couples approach engagement ring shopping, per this module?**
A) Same-sex couples never shop together under any circumstances
B) Many same-sex couples shop and decide together as a matter of course, meaning the traditional "who buys/who's surprised" framework often doesn't map cleanly onto the relationship
C) Same-sex couples require an entirely different product line with no overlap with other bridal offerings
D) There is no meaningful difference in consultation approach for any couple

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Co-decision-making is frequently the default dynamic, which is why open questions about symbolism and shared values, rather than assumptions about roles, tend to work better than a script built around a single surprise-buyer.

---

**4. What is one recurring piece of guidance for second-marriage engagement ring clients, per this module?**
A) The ring should always be dramatically cheaper than a first-marriage ring
B) Budget considerations often skew higher, and it's worth being mindful of avoiding visual similarity to a previous ring
C) Second marriages should never involve an engagement ring at all
D) There is no meaningful difference in approach between a first and second engagement

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Clients in a second marriage are often further along financially and personally, and budget conversations often reflect that; avoiding a design that closely echoes a previous ring is also commonly cited etiquette guidance.

---

**5. Why should an associate approach a second-engagement conversation with warmth rather than awkwardness?**
A) Because second engagements are inherently less significant than first engagements
B) Because a second engagement deserves the same genuine enthusiasm as a first, and treating it as anything less can come across as unwelcome judgment
C) Because associates are required by policy to discourage second marriages
D) Because there is no difference in emotional significance between any two client interactions

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A client's second engagement is a meaningful, joyful moment for them, and an associate's job is to meet that with genuine warmth, not hesitation or unspoken judgment.

---

**6. What is the recommended approach when a client's language or context suggests a specific cultural bridal tradition the associate isn't deeply familiar with?**
A) Guess at what the tradition likely involves without asking
B) Ask a genuine, respectful question, such as whether there's a specific piece or style that's traditional for their family
C) Assume no tradition applies and proceed with a standard consultation
D) Avoid the topic entirely to prevent any possible mistake

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Asking a direct, respectful question is more useful and more respectful than guessing or assuming, especially since specific expectations vary significantly by family, region, and generation even within a shared broader tradition.

**7. Which of the following is an example of a major cultural bridal jewelry tradition mentioned in this module?**
A) There are no meaningful cultural variations in bridal jewelry traditions worldwide
B) The Chinese si dian jin ("four pieces of gold") tradition, in which the groom's family gifts the bride a coordinated gold jewelry set
C) All cultures use an identical single-ring tradition with no variation
D) Cultural bridal traditions have no relevance to jewelry retail whatsoever

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Si dian jin is one of several major traditions described in this module (alongside South Asian streedhan and Middle Eastern mahr/shabka traditions, among others), illustrating the real variation in bridal jewelry customs worldwide.

**8. In the worked scenario, how does the associate respond to a client who states she's buying her own ring?**
A) With visible surprise and a question about why her partner isn't involved
B) With straightforward enthusiasm, treating the consultation exactly as any other and moving forward on the client's own terms
C) By refusing to proceed until a partner is present
D) By assuming the client must not have a partner at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Responding with immediate, straightforward enthusiasm respects the client's stated framing and avoids requiring her to justify or explain her decision.
