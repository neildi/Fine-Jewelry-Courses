# M03 Quiz — Shape, Setting, Metal

10 questions testing the terminal outcome: guiding shape → setting → metal one decision at a time. Answer key at the end.

---

**1. What is the correct order of the three stacked decisions?**
a) Metal, then setting, then shape.
b) Shape, then setting, then metal.
c) Setting, then shape, then metal.
d) The order doesn't matter.

**2. What is the one binary question that sorts nearly every shape conversation?**
a) "Round or princess?"
b) "Brilliant cut or step cut?"
c) "Big or small?"
d) "White or yellow?"

**3. Why are step-cut buyers often guided toward higher clarity?**
a) Step cuts are always lower quality.
b) Their broad, flat facets reveal inclusions and color more readily than brilliant cuts.
c) Step cuts are cheaper, so clarity budget is freed.
d) Step cuts hide inclusions better.

**4. A client says "she's a nurse and works with her hands." Which setting should you surface first?**
a) A tall six-prong solitaire.
b) A bezel or low basket.
c) A tension setting.
d) A high halo.

**5. A couple wants more presence but has a fixed budget. What genuinely helps?**
a) A bigger stone they can't afford.
b) A halo, which makes the center read larger without adding carat cost.
c) A tension setting.
d) Cheaper metal.

**6. What is the honest trade-off between platinum and white gold?**
a) Platinum is simply better in every way.
b) White gold is always cheaper and better.
c) Platinum wears and patinas; white gold is rhodium-plated and re-plated over time.
d) There is no difference.

**7. Which gold is harder and more scratch-resistant for daily wear?**
a) 24k.
b) 18k.
c) 14k.
d) They are identical.

**8. What should you say before a buyer commits to a tension setting?**
a) Nothing — it's their choice.
b) That tension settings are harder to resize later.
c) That tension settings are the most durable.
d) That tension settings are being discontinued.

**9. A couple freezes and says "we need to think about it" after seeing the whole case. What happened?**
a) They didn't like any of the rings.
b) They were overwhelmed — shown everything at once with no way to choose.
c) The prices were too high.
d) They were never serious buyers.

**10. What does "guide, don't dump" mean in practice?**
a) Show every ring so nothing is missed.
b) Run one binary at a time, each narrowing the next, and share one practical trade-off per option.
c) Let the couple browse alone.
d) Only show three rings total.

---

## Answer key

1. **b** — Shape (silhouette) first, then setting (wear and engineering), then metal (color and durability).
2. **b** — Brilliant versus step cut sorts nearly everyone and constrains what follows.
3. **b** — Step cuts' flat facets are transparent windows; inclusions and color show more.
4. **b** — A bezel or low basket matches an active, hands-on lifestyle.
5. **b** — A halo adds perceived size without adding carat cost.
6. **c** — Honest maintenance framing builds trust; neither is "simply better."
7. **c** — 14k carries more alloy, so it is harder and more scratch-resistant.
8. **b** — Say it before the decision so resizing limits aren't a surprise.
9. **b** — Overwhelm, not disinterest, produces "we need to think about it."
10. **b** — Sequential binaries with a practical note per option guide without overwhelming.
