# C6-M05 Quiz: Metals and Settings

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly applying durability/maintenance trade-offs to lifestyle scenarios, not memorizing exact hardness numbers.

---

**1. What is the counterintuitive relationship between gold karat and durability, per this module?**
A) Higher karat always means more durable
B) Higher karat means richer color but generally softer metal — 14k is typically the hardest/most durable common option, not 22k
C) Karat has no relationship to hardness at all
D) 22k gold is always the best choice for daily wear

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Higher karat gold contains more pure gold and less of the harder alloy metals, making it softer — 14k is generally the most durable for daily wear despite being lower karat than 18k or 22k.

---

**2. How does platinum's wear mechanism differ from gold's?**
A) Platinum never shows any wear at all
B) Platinum displaces under wear (developing a patina) rather than scratching away and losing material, unlike gold alloys
C) Platinum is actually harder than all gold alloys
D) There is no real difference between platinum and gold wear patterns

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Platinum is softer than gold on a hardness scale but behaves differently under wear — it displaces rather than chips away, so most of the original metal stays with the ring over time.

---

**3. Why does white gold typically require replating every 1-3 years?**
A) White gold is a pure white metal that simply fades naturally
B) White gold is actually a yellow-gold alloy plated with rhodium for its white finish, and that plating wears off over time
C) Replating is only needed if the ring is damaged
D) White gold never requires any maintenance

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The bright white finish comes from rhodium plating over a naturally yellow-toned alloy; as the plating wears, the underlying tint starts to show through, requiring periodic replating.

---

**4. Why is it important to set expectations about white gold's replating requirement at the point of sale?**
A) It isn't important; clients never notice color changes
B) A client not told about replating in advance may feel misled later when the ring's appearance changes, even though this is normal expected maintenance, not a defect
C) White gold never actually changes appearance over time
D) Replating is illegal to charge for, so it must be disclosed for legal reasons only

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Setting this expectation upfront prevents a client from feeling deceived later when normal, expected plating wear becomes visible — it's a maintenance fact, not a flaw.

---

**5. Which metals are generally recommended for a client with known nickel sensitivity?**
A) Any white gold, regardless of specific alloy
B) Pure titanium, palladium, platinum, sterling silver, and 18-karat-or-higher yellow gold
C) Only sterling silver is ever safe for nickel allergies
D) Nickel allergy has no bearing on metal recommendations

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These metals are consistently identified across medical/dermatological sources as generally nickel-free or nickel-safe options, unlike some white gold alloys that may contain nickel.

---

**6. What is the primary trade-off of a prong setting, per this module?**
A) Prongs have no real durability concerns at all
B) Prongs maximize brilliance by lifting the stone for maximum light exposure, but the exposed tips gradually wear and require regular professional inspection (roughly 6-12 months) since wear is invisible until a stone is at risk
C) Prongs are always more durable than bezel settings
D) Prong settings never need any maintenance

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Prongs maximize light entry and brilliance, but their exposed tips wear gradually and invisibly, making regular professional inspection essential to catch wear before a stone is actually at risk of falling out.

---

**7. Why is a bezel setting often recommended for clients with active, hands-on lifestyles?**
A) Bezel settings have no real durability advantage over prongs
B) The continuous metal rim protects the stone's edge fully, with no exposed claw tips to wear down, making it the most durable, lowest-maintenance option for hands-on work
C) Bezel settings are only used for decorative, non-functional jewelry
D) Bezel settings always cost more than prong settings

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Because there are no exposed prong tips to wear thin, bezel settings consistently outperform prong settings on stone security, making them well-suited to hands-on lifestyles.

**8. In the worked scenario, why does the associate recommend a bezel setting to the nurse client?**
A) Because bezel settings are always the cheapest option
B) Because the client's own mentioned lifestyle detail (catching a ring on gloves) directly points to bezel's key advantage — no raised prong tips to catch on anything
C) Because prong settings are illegal in healthcare settings
D) Because the associate has no other setting options available

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The recommendation is grounded directly in a lifestyle detail the client offered naturally, matching it to the specific durability advantage (no exposed catching points) that bezel settings provide.
