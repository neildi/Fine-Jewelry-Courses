# C6-M01 Outline: The Modern Engagement Ring Buyer: Trends, Psychology, and the Path to Purchase

## Module Metadata
- Course: C6 - Bridal & Engagement Mastery
- Module: 1 of 14
- Persona: P1
- Working title: The Modern Engagement Ring Buyer: Trends, Psychology, and the Path to Purchase
- Learning objective: Associates can explain the directional 2026 bridal trends (shape, metal, lab-grown adoption) accurately, recognize that specific statistics vary meaningfully by source, know current design details by name, and use trend knowledge as a discovery-informed vocabulary rather than a predetermined sales script.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Explain 2026 engagement-ring buyer trends and use them to guide, not dictate, a client conversation."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames bridal's unique emotional/financial stakes and the fast pace of trend change.
2. **The Headline Shift: Away From Maximalism, Toward Intentionality** — Three converging directional trends (shape, metal, lab-grown) agreed upon across sources.
3. **A Necessary Caveat: Trend Data Varies by Source** — Explicit comparison of two sources' conflicting exact percentages (oval/round, yellow gold share); the "direction is reliable, magnitude varies" lesson.
4. **What's Actually Rising: Design Details Worth Knowing by Name** — Hidden halo, toi et moi, bezel, east-west, sourced from both trend reports.
5. **The Carat-Size Question: Sources Disagree Even on Direction** — Transparent presentation of directly conflicting claims (shrinking vs. growing average carat) with the same underlying cause.
6. **Buyer Psychology and Shopping Behavior** — Research-before-visit norm, ethical-sourcing growth, generational differences (Gen Z/Millennial/Gen X+ preference profiles).
7. **A Worked Scenario: Using Trend Data Without Leading With It** — Illustrative synthesis modeling discovery-first trend application.
8. **Practice Exercise** — Multi-generational client-profile trend-relevance exercise.
9. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- Trend statistics are sourced from two independent, credible 2026 industry publications: "Engagement Ring Trends 2026: What's In, What's Out, What's Coming Back" (The Diamond Price, published June 2026) and "Engagement Ring Trends 2026: What's Actually Selling Right Now" (Diavlia, last updated April 2026). These sources DIRECTLY CONFLICT on several specific percentages (oval vs. round share, yellow gold share, average carat size direction) while agreeing on overall direction for most trends. This module deliberately surfaces the conflict transparently rather than picking one source silently — this is a design choice, not an oversight, and should be preserved in any revision.
- The Diavlia source cites its own data as coming from The Knot's 2025 Real Weddings Study, Google Trends search volume, and its own order data; The Diamond Price source describes its own methodology as tracking real-time diamond listings, without the same named third-party survey backing for the ring-style statistics specifically (its diamond pricing methodology is separately published, but the ring-trend statistics' primary source is less explicitly documented in the fetched content).
- The generational preference breakdown (Gen Z/Millennial/Gen X+) is sourced specifically from The Diamond Price's "By Region and Demographic" section.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.

## Assessment Alignment
- Quiz items should test: correctly identifying agreed-upon directional trends despite magnitude disagreement, recognizing named design details (hidden halo, toi et moi, bezel, east-west), understanding why the carat-size trend is genuinely disputed between sources, and applying discovery-first trend usage in a scenario.
- Huddle card should focus on the "direction is reliable, exact numbers vary by source" lesson and the named design-detail vocabulary as the most portable takeaways.
- Factcheck should explicitly document both sources' conflicting figures side by side and flag that neither should be treated as more authoritative without further independent verification (e.g., against The Knot's original published study or GIA/Platinum Guild data directly).
