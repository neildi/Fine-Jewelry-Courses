# C6-M01 Fact-Check: The Modern Engagement Ring Buyer

## Verification Status: Sourced from two independent industry publications, with explicit, documented disagreement between them

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Lab-grown diamonds crossing 50% of new US engagement ring sales in 2025-2026.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across both sourced publications. The Diamond Price reports 52% in Q1 2026 (up from 18% in 2022); Diavlia reports lab-grown "crossed 50%" in 2025 per The Knot's 2025 Real Weddings Study (15,000+ respondents). Both sources agree on the threshold-crossing and general magnitude, differing only slightly on exact percentage and timing (2025 vs. Q1 2026). This is the most reliably corroborated claim in the module.

**2. Oval shape share vs. round shape share.**
Status: DIRECTLY CONFLICTING between sources. The Diamond Price reports oval overtaking round (32% vs. 29%) for the first time in over 50 years. Diavlia reports round still firmly leading (~55%) with oval at only 22% and rising. Both agree oval is the fastest-rising shape; they disagree sharply on whether it has actually overtaken round. MUST VERIFY: neither figure should be presented to a client or in external training material as a settled fact — the module correctly frames this as source disagreement rather than resolving it artificially.

**3. Yellow gold market share.**
Status: DIRECTLY CONFLICTING. The Diamond Price reports yellow gold at 44% (dominant, ending 20 years of white gold/platinum dominance). Diavlia reports yellow gold at roughly 30% (a strong, rising second choice, but white gold + platinum still combined around 55%). Both agree yellow gold has risen sharply from a much lower base (~15-22%) circa 2019-2020. MUST VERIFY: same caution as shape share above.

**4. Average carat size direction.**
Status: DIRECTLY CONFLICTING in DIRECTION, not just magnitude. The Diamond Price reports average carat size DECREASING (1.2ct in 2022 to 0.95ct in 2026). Diavlia reports average carat size INCREASING (1.2ct in 2022 to 1.5ct in 2025). This is the most significant disagreement in the module, and the module correctly presents it as a genuine, unresolved conflict rather than picking a side. MUST VERIFY: if this module is used for external training, consider seeking a third, more authoritative source (e.g., a direct citation of The Knot's original published Real Weddings Study, or GIA/Platinum Guild trade data) to attempt resolution, or continue presenting it as disputed.

**5. Named design-detail trends (hidden halo, toi et moi, bezel, east-west).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across both sources, which independently corroborate the same four trends as rising, even while disagreeing on shape/metal/carat magnitudes. This corroboration across otherwise-conflicting sources increases confidence in these specific claims.

**6. Generational preference breakdown (Gen Z/Millennial/Gen X+).**
Status: Sourced SOLELY from The Diamond Price's "By Region and Demographic" section; NOT independently corroborated by the Diavlia source, which does not provide an equivalent generational breakdown. MUST VERIFY: treat this specific breakdown as single-sourced and less independently confirmed than the claims corroborated by both sources.

**7. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from either source.
