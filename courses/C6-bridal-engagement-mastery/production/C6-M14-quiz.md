# C6-M14 Quiz: Selling Bridal in Luxury Hotel and Private Appointment Settings (Capstone)

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This capstone quiz emphasizes correctly integrating concepts across the full course, not memorizing new isolated facts.

---

**1. What are the main formats of private and hotel-based bridal selling described in this module?**
A) Only walk-in retail with extended hours
B) Hotel-suite trunk shows, formal hotel-brand partnerships (e.g., a hotel partnering with a specialist for in-suite viewings), and by-appointment private showrooms/ateliers
C) Exclusively online video consultations with no physical component
D) There is only one standard format used across the entire luxury segment

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These three formats share an absence of walk-in traffic and the presence of real lead time, distinguishing them sharply from Module 13's cruise/port environment.

---

**2. Why is discretion often the primary reason a client chooses a private appointment or hotel-based format, rather than a standard store visit?**
A) Discretion has no real bearing on why clients choose this channel
B) For clients with real privacy needs (public figures, high-profile families, or anyone valuing discretion around a major purchase), avoiding a public retail floor and managing information carefully is often the central value of the format
C) Private appointments are always cheaper than standard retail
D) Discretion is only relevant for celebrity clients and no one else

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Discretion isn't a courtesy add-on in this channel — it's frequently the specific reason a client is using this format at all.

---

**3. What is a concrete discretion practice described in this module?**
A) Publicly announcing a client's arrival and purchase details to hotel staff
B) Using unmarked or minimally branded transport/packaging, executing NDAs when appropriate, and sharing information on a strict need-to-know basis
C) Sharing full client purchase details with all store staff regardless of involvement
D) Discretion practices have no specific, concrete implementation; they are purely conceptual

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These are specific, actionable practices, not abstract principles — they directly protect a client's privacy in a channel where that's often the central expectation.

---

**4. What should be explicitly confirmed, not assumed, before taking high-value inventory to an off-site private showing?**
A) Nothing needs to be confirmed; standard in-store insurance automatically covers all off-site activity
B) Insurance coverage for off-premises transport and display, since limits and requirements vary by policy and need to be verified in advance
C) Off-site showings never require any special insurance consideration
D) Chain-of-custody logs are unnecessary for professional jewelers

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A standard jewelers block policy typically covers off-site transit and shows, but specific limits and requirements vary, making explicit confirmation a real operational necessity, not an assumption to skip.

**5. Why does this module argue that Module 5's setting/durability guidance and Module 9's wedding-band pairing guidance apply just as much in a private hotel-suite appointment as in standard retail?**
A) Because durability and pairing considerations only matter for lower-priced pieces
B) Because the physical realities of metal hardness, setting security, and band pairing don't change based on the sales environment or price point
C) Because private appointments never involve any product education
D) Because Module 5 and Module 9 content is not actually relevant to bridal selling

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The underlying physical and practical realities covered in earlier modules apply regardless of setting or price point — a luxurious environment doesn't change how metal or settings actually perform.

**6. Why does this module argue that ethical standards (from Module 12) apply with zero exceptions in a luxury private-appointment setting?**
A) Because wealthier clients require less honesty than other clients
B) Because a client in this channel typically has more resources and more alternatives, giving them more, not less, ability to walk away, which makes genuine trust even more commercially essential
C) Because ethical standards only apply to lower-priced transactions
D) Because private appointment clients are legally exempt from standard consumer protections

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: If anything, the greater optionality available to this client segment raises, rather than lowers, the stakes for honest, transparent selling.

**7. In the worked scenario, how does the specialist open the private hotel-suite consultation?**
A) By focusing entirely on the luxury of the setting itself, with minimal substantive discussion
B) By gathering the same design-brief information covered in Module 7, and being just as clear about realistic timelines and pricing as earlier modules require

Explanation: The suite and privacy are real value-adds, but they support the underlying consultation rather than substituting for the same substantive process covered throughout this course.

**8. What is the central lesson of this capstone module regarding how the private/luxury setting affects consultation substance?**
A) Substance and rigor can be relaxed in a more luxurious environment
B) The setting changes the pacing and privacy of the conversation, but the underlying advisory, sizing, timeline, and ethical standards from every prior module still fully apply
