# C6-M10 Outline: Appraisal, Insurance, and Aftercare: Protecting the Purchase for a Lifetime

## Module Metadata
- Course: C6 - Bridal & Engagement Mastery
- Module: 10 of 14
- Persona: P2
- Working title: Appraisal, Insurance, and Aftercare: Protecting the Purchase for a Lifetime
- Learning objective: Associates can explain the difference between retail replacement value and fair market value, walk a client through the trade-offs of all three insurance paths (unmodified homeowners, scheduled rider, standalone policy) without steering toward one option, and communicate the professional maintenance cadence connecting directly to Module 5's setting-durability content.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Explain appraisal and insurance options clearly enough that a client leaves knowing exactly how their purchase is protected."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames the protection conversation as preventing a future, worst-case-timing misunderstanding.
2. **Appraisals: Retail Replacement Value, Not Resale Value** — RRV vs. fair market value distinction; appraisal cost/timeline; why insurers require one.
3. **The Three Insurance Paths, and What Each Actually Covers** — Unmodified homeowners (sub-limit, theft-only), scheduled rider (full value but CLUE-reported, may exclude mysterious disappearance), standalone policy (all-risk, no CLUE impact, comparable cost).
4. **Aftercare: A Cadence, Not a One-Time Conversation** — Daily/weekly/6-month/1-2yr/5-10yr/2-3yr maintenance schedule, tied to Module 5's setting/white-gold content.
5. **A Worked Scenario: The Protection Conversation at Checkout** — Illustrative synthesis of proactively raising insurance and aftercare at the point of sale.
6. **Practice Exercise** — RRV-vs-fair-market explanation roleplay plus a three-path insurance walkthrough.
7. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- Retail replacement value vs. fair market value distinction is STRONGLY CROSS-CORROBORATED across Jewelers.org (official trade association guidance), La Jolla Gem Appraisal, Gemology Resources, and Antwerp Diamond Store — all four independently describe RRV as the standard/highest insurance valuation and fair market value as a lower, distinct resale-oriented figure.
- Appraisal cost ($50-200) and turnaround (up to one week) is sourced from CNBC's consumer guide, cross-consistent with Jewelers Mutual's general appraisal guidance.
- The three-path insurance framework (unmodified homeowners, scheduled rider, standalone policy) and each path's specific trade-offs (sub-limits of $1,000-2,500; CLUE reporting on riders; all-risk/no-CLUE-impact on standalone policies; 0.5-1.5% annual cost) is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across seven independent sources (Jewelers Mutual, CNBC, Hippo, InsuranceNewsNet, CaratYes x2, SuperJeweler, Insurance.com), with CaratYes providing the most granular side-by-side rate comparison.
- Maintenance cadence (daily wipe, weekly/monthly soap clean, 6-month professional inspection, 1-2yr rhodium replating, 5-10yr prong retipping, 2-3yr reappraisal) is STRONGLY CROSS-CORROBORATED across Joalys, CaratYes's maintenance schedule guide, and JewelryCarats — all three independently converge on six months as the professional consensus for daily-worn rings specifically.
- The "cheapest repair vs. most expensive failure" framing for prong wear is sourced directly from Joalys and echoed in similar language by CaratYes.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
