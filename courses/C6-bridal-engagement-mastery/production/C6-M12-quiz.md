# C6-M12 Quiz: Ethical Standards in High-Ticket Bridal Selling

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly applying disclosure rules and financing distinctions, not memorizing exact regulatory citations.

---

**1. Under the FTC's Jewelry Guides, what must accompany the word "diamond" when describing a lab-grown stone?**
A) Nothing; the word "diamond" alone is always sufficient
B) A clear, equally conspicuous qualifier (such as "laboratory-grown" or "laboratory-created") immediately preceding the word "diamond"
C) A qualifier is only required for stones over one carat
D) The word "cultured" may always be used alone with no further disclosure

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The FTC requires the qualifier to appear with the same visual and verbal prominence as "diamond" itself, whether in advertising, on a price tag, or in a verbal sales conversation.

---

**2. Which of the following terms is specifically prohibited when describing a lab-grown diamond, per the FTC Jewelry Guides?**
A) Laboratory-created
B) "Real," "natural," "genuine," or "precious"
C) Laboratory-grown
D) [Manufacturer name]-created

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These specific terms are prohibited because they would misleadingly suggest the stone is a mined, natural diamond.

---

**3. Does the FTC's disclosure requirement apply to small melee accent stones, or only to large center stones?**
A) Only to center stones over 1 carat
B) The obligation applies at every carat weight the Guides cover, including small melee accent stones
C) Melee stones are entirely exempt from disclosure requirements
D) Disclosure requirements only apply to loose stones, never to stones already set in a finished piece

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The disclosure obligation is not limited to large or prominent stones; it applies across all carat weights covered by the Guides, including small accent stones.

---

**4. What is the key difference between true 0% APR financing and deferred-interest financing?**
A) They are functionally identical with no real difference
B) True 0% APR means no interest accrues; deferred interest accrues silently from the purchase date and is charged retroactively in full if the balance isn't paid off by the deadline
C) Deferred interest is always a better deal for the consumer
D) True 0% APR always charges more interest than deferred interest

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: With deferred interest, even a small remaining balance at the deadline triggers a retroactive charge covering all interest accrued since the original purchase date — a materially different risk profile than true 0% APR.

---

**5. What does CFPB research indicate about the real-world risk of deferred-interest promotions?**
A) Deferred-interest promotions carry no meaningful risk for consumers
B) Roughly one in five deferred-interest promotional balances results in retroactive interest charges, and many affected consumers end up paying well over their original promotional balance
C) Deferred interest is illegal and therefore never actually offered by retailers
D) Consumers who use deferred interest promotions never pay any interest at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is real, documented consumer harm from federal regulatory research, not a rare or hypothetical edge case.

---

**6. What is the ethical practice recommended when a client asks about a "12 months no interest" financing promotion that is actually deferred interest?**
A) Simply confirm the promotion is available with no further explanation
B) Clearly explain that it's a deferred-interest plan, specify the retroactive-charge risk if the balance isn't paid in full by the deadline, and let the client decide with full information
C) Discourage the client from asking further questions about financing
D) Tell the client deferred interest and true 0% APR are the same thing

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Precise, honest explanation lets the client make a genuinely informed decision rather than assuming the safer, true-0%-APR version of the promotion.

---

**7. Which of the following is an example of a high-pressure sales tactic to avoid, per this module?**
A) Giving a client accurate information and time to think
B) Manufacturing false urgency, discouraging a second opinion, or continuing to press after a client has indicated they need more time
C) Proactively disclosing that a diamond is lab-grown
D) Explaining financing terms clearly and accurately

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These tactics prioritize closing the sale today over the client's actual informed interest, and are widely documented as both an ethical and reputational risk in jewelry retail.

---

**8. Why does this module argue that ethical selling and smart long-term selling are, in a referral-driven business, largely the same thing?**
A) Because ethics and commercial interest are always in direct conflict
B) Because a client who leaves informed and unpressured, even without buying today, is more likely to return and refer others than one who felt pushed into a decision
C) Because referrals have no connection to how a client was treated during the sales process
D) Because pressuring clients into immediate purchases always maximizes long-term referrals

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: In a business built on repeat clients and referrals, treating a client honestly and without pressure is both the ethical choice and, over time, the commercially smarter one.
