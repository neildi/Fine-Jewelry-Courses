# C6-M02 Quiz: Diamond Shape vs. Cut

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly distinguishing shape from cut and applying the cut-carat trade-off logic, not memorizing exact proportion numbers.

---

**1. What does "shape" refer to in a diamond, per this module?**
A) The quality of the faceting and light performance
B) The outline of the diamond as seen from above (round, oval, pear, emerald, etc.)
C) The certificate's overall grade
D) The diamond's country of origin

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Shape is the design choice — the outline as seen from above — distinct from cut, which refers to the quality of the faceting.

---

**2. What does "cut" refer to, as distinguished from shape in this module?**
A) The same thing as shape — they are interchangeable terms
B) The quality of the faceting: proportions, symmetry, and polish, which determines how well the stone handles light
C) Only the diamond's color grade
D) The number of facets a diamond has, regardless of quality

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Cut refers specifically to craftsmanship quality — proportions, symmetry, and polish — and every shape can be cut well or poorly.

---

**3. Why is cut widely considered the most important of the 4Cs?**
A) It is the cheapest factor to improve
B) It is the only one of the 4Cs determined by human craftsmanship rather than the stone's natural properties, and has an outsized effect on visible brilliance and apparent size
C) It has no real effect on how a diamond looks
D) It is the only factor that appears on a certificate

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Carat, color, and clarity are inherent to the rough stone; cut is what a skilled cutter does with it, and it significantly affects how brilliant and how large a diamond actually appears.

---

**4. Which three optical effects does a well-cut diamond optimize, per this module?**
A) Carat, color, and clarity
B) Brilliance, fire, and scintillation
C) Table, crown, and pavilion
D) Depth, girdle, and culet

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Brilliance (total light reflected), fire (dispersion into spectral colors), and scintillation (sparkle pattern from movement) are the three optical effects cutters balance when optimizing a diamond's cut.

---

**5. Why does an Excellent-cut 1.00ct round typically look larger than a Good-cut 1.00ct round of the same weight?**
A) There is no actual visual difference between them
B) A well-cut diamond distributes weight across the crown, maximizing face-up diameter, while a poorly-cut diamond hides weight in the pavilion or girdle
C) Good-cut diamonds are always physically larger in carat weight
D) Cut quality has no relationship to how a diamond's weight is distributed

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: An Excellent-cut 1.00ct round typically measures about 6.4-6.5mm across, while a Good-cut 1.00ct round might measure only 5.9-6.1mm — a visible difference caused by how the cutter distributed the stone's weight.

---

**6. According to this module, why does sacrificing cut quality to gain carat weight usually backfire?**
A) Larger poorly-cut diamonds always cost more than smaller well-cut ones
B) A well-cut smaller diamond often appears larger and more brilliant than a poorly-cut larger diamond, while frequently costing less
C) Carat weight is the only factor that affects a diamond's visible size
D) Cut quality has no effect on price at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is the single most common bridal buying mistake — an Excellent-cut 0.90ct diamond can appear larger and more brilliant than a Good-cut 1.10ct diamond while costing considerably less.

---

**7. How can cut quality help a budget-conscious client with color or clarity trade-offs?**
A) Cut has no relationship to color or clarity
B) An Excellent-cut diamond reflects enough light that minor color tints (G-H) and eye-clean inclusions (VS2-SI1) become much harder to detect face-up
C) Cut can fully hide any color or clarity issue regardless of severity
D) Clients should always prioritize color and clarity over cut

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A high cut grade can partially compensate for minor color tints and clarity inclusions, letting a client step down one grade in either category while maintaining a beautiful-looking stone.

---

**8. What is the "bowtie effect," and which shapes are most prone to it?**
A) A sparkle pattern found only in round brilliants
B) A dark, bow-tie-shaped shadow across the center of elongated shapes like oval, pear, and marquise, caused by inefficient light return from central facets
C) A flaw that only appears in lab-grown diamonds
D) A grading term with no visual manifestation

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The bowtie effect is a shadow specific to elongated fancy shapes; nearly all such stones show some degree of it, and the key evaluation question is whether it's subtle or dark and distracting.
