# C6-M11 Fact-Check: Selling Bridal Across Demographics and Life Stages

## Verification Status: Self-purchase and second-marriage guidance well corroborated from credible sources; several found LGBTQ+-specific statistics excluded due to unverifiable sourcing

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Self-purchase growth (7% to 14% of engagement rings financed solely by the bride, 2013-2017; $4,400 vs. $3,300 average spend).**
Status: Sourced from Rapaport's reporting (a well-established, credible diamond-industry trade publication) on De Beers' own Diamond Insight Report — a named, dated, primary industry source. This data reflects the 2013-2017 period specifically and is presented in this module as evidence of a historical growth trend, not as a claim about exact current-year percentages, since more recent equivalent data was not independently verified in this research pass.

**2. General fine-jewelry self-purchase preference (80% of U.S. adults more likely to buy for themselves than wait for a gift).**
Status: Sourced from PriceScope's reporting on a 2025 report from BriteCo, a specialized jewelry insurer with direct access to industry purchase-behavior data. This is a single-source figure for the general (not specifically bridal) self-purchase trend, presented as such.

**3. Same-sex couples frequently shopping and deciding on engagement rings together, rather than one partner surprising the other.**
Status: Corroborated qualitatively across Greenwich St. Jewelers' LGBTQ+ wedding ring guide (recommending an open conversation between partners before shopping) and Autostraddle's first-person guide (recommending partners "talk to your partner" as the first step). This module presents this as a common, well-documented pattern rather than a universal rule.

**4. IMPORTANT EXCLUSION: Several highly specific LGBTQ+-focused statistics and named quotes found during research were NOT included in this module.**
Status: Multiple JewelTrendPro articles returned during research contained very specific statistics (e.g., a claimed 86% figure attributed to "a 2022 study by the Williams Institute," a claimed 63%-vs-29% co-decision-making statistic attributed to "The Wedding Report, 2024," and a quote attributed to a named "GIA-Certified Jewelry Historian & Senior Curator") that could not be independently verified against a locatable primary source within this research pass, and the attribution details raised credibility concerns (GIA does not appear to have a role publicly described as stated). These specific statistics and quotes were deliberately EXCLUDED from this module's content. Only the more general, cross-corroborated qualitative pattern (co-decision-making as common) was retained, sourced from the more clearly credible Greenwich St. Jewelers and Autostraddle sources instead.

**5. Second-marriage etiquette guidance (higher typical budget, avoiding visual similarity to a previous ring, joint decision-making, retiring previous rings).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across four independent sources (Estate Diamond Jewelry/Ben Khordipour, The Knot, Alberthern, and Legacy Diamonds and Gemstones), all independently recommending the same core guidance points with closely matching reasoning (financial/life-stage maturity, avoiding reminders of previous relationships, joint financial involvement).

**6. Multicultural bridal jewelry traditions (South Asian streedhan; Chinese si dian jin; Middle Eastern mahr/shabka; Mediterranean stefana/gold coins).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across GIA's own official 4Cs blog (a highly credible primary industry-education source), Alromaizan, Menē, and Arthesdam's Singapore-specific guide, all independently describing matching or closely overlapping tradition names, terminology, and general descriptions.

**7. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from any external source.

## Remaining Items for Human/Company Review (not resolvable via further research in this pass)
- [ ] If the store wants to include more specific, quantified LGBTQ+ bridal-buyer statistics in future training materials, commission or locate a verified primary source (e.g., a specific, citable Williams Institute or Wedding Report publication) rather than relying on the unverifiable secondary claims found during this research pass.
