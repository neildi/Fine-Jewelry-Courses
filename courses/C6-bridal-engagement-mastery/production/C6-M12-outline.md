# C6-M12 Outline: Ethical Standards in High-Ticket Bridal Selling

## Module Metadata
- Course: C6 - Bridal & Engagement Mastery
- Module: 12 of 14
- Persona: P2
- Working title: Ethical Standards in High-Ticket Bridal Selling
- Learning objective: Associates can correctly apply FTC lab-grown/treated-stone disclosure requirements, accurately distinguish and explain deferred-interest financing from true 0% APR to clients, and recognize and avoid common high-pressure sales tactics, understanding the commercial as well as ethical case for each.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Adapt a bridal sales approach across client demographics, life stages, and sales channels... while holding a consistent ethical standard." (ethical-standard portion)

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames bridal's combination of high dollar value and high emotional stakes as raising the ethical bar.
2. **FTC Disclosure Requirements Are Not Optional** — Lab-grown diamond disclosure rules (equal conspicuousness, prohibited terms, melee-inclusive scope) and treated-stone disclosure rules, framed as federal law with real enforcement history.
3. **Deferred-Interest Financing: Understanding What You're Actually Offering** — True 0% APR vs. deferred interest mechanics; CFPB/NCLC consumer-harm data; the honest-disclosure practice.
4. **Recognizing and Avoiding High-Pressure Tactics** — False urgency, discouraging second opinions, store-favorable financing steering, ignoring stated hesitation; the commercial case for not pressuring.
5. **A Worked Scenario: The Financing Conversation Done Right** — Illustrative synthesis of an honest deferred-interest explanation at the point of sale.
6. **Practice Exercise** — Lab-grown disclosure and deferred-interest explanation roleplay, plus high-pressure-phrase identification.
7. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- FTC Jewelry Guides disclosure requirements (equal-conspicuousness standard, permitted qualifier terms, prohibited terms, melee-inclusive scope, treated-stone disclosure rule) are sourced directly from PRIMARY FEDERAL REGULATORY SOURCES: the FTC's own "In the Loupe" consumer guidance, the FTC's template diamond warning letter (an actual enforcement document), and the FTC's letters to diamond companies. This is the highest-confidence sourcing tier used in this course — direct primary-source federal regulatory text, not secondary reporting. Secondary trade-press coverage (National Jeweler, JCK) was used only to corroborate and contextualize, not as the primary basis for any specific rule stated.
- Deferred-interest vs. true 0% APR mechanics and consumer-harm statistics (roughly 1-in-5 promotional balances resulting in retroactive charges; over a third of affected consumers paying more than 150% of original balance) are sourced from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's own archived research bulletin and reporting citing CFPB's 2024 Issue Spotlight — both primary or near-primary federal regulatory sources, cross-corroborated by the National Consumer Law Center's "Deceptive Bargain" report and consistent secondary coverage (Bankrate, NerdWallet, CNBC, USA Today) all describing matching mechanics.
- High-pressure sales tactic examples (false urgency, discouraging second opinions, financing steering, ignoring stated hesitation) are drawn from Jewelry Secrets' consumer-facing warning guide, a long-running, specifically jewelry-retail-focused consumer advocacy source; the corrective "informed and unpressured client is more valuable long-term" framing is this module's own synthesis, consistent with the referral-driven business logic established throughout this course.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
