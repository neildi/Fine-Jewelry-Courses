# M05 Quiz — Budget Conversations That Keep the Romance

10 questions testing the terminal outcome: a warm, early, honest budget conversation. Answer key at the end.

---

**1. When should you ask about budget?**
a) Last, after the couple has picked a ring.
b) Early, in a normal voice, framed as "it helps me show you the best options."
c) Only if they bring it up.
d) Never — let them browse freely.

**2. Why do you ask for a "range" rather than a hard number?**
a) It is legally required.
b) It lowers the temperature and treats budget as a starting point, not a cap.
c) It lets you charge more.
d) It avoids the word "budget."

**3. A couple in casual clothes names a large budget. What does this illustrate?**
a) They are lying.
b) Why you should never assume budget from appearance.
c) That casual buyers always spend big.
d) That you should have up-sold harder.

**4. What are the two money-saving moves in the trade-off triage?**
a) Smaller stone and cheaper metal.
b) Color-against-warm-metal and eye-clean over flawless.
c) Skip the setting and the warranty.
d) Lab-grown and moissanite.

**5. Why is "eye-clean" the right clarity goal for most couples?**
a) Flawless diamonds are always visible to the eye.
b) Paying for a flawless grade over a clean stone that looks identical is spending money on a report, not on the hand.
c) Clarity doesn't matter at all.
d) Eye-clean stones are always cheaper.

**6. The dream ring is genuinely out of range. What are the two honest paths?**
a) Pretend it's close, or say "no."
b) A smaller/differently-specced stone now, or the upgrade plan later.
c) Discount until it fits, or lose the sale.
d) Push financing hard.

**7. How should financing be presented?**
a) As a rescue for a number they were never told.
b) As a service they can use, without promising approvals or rates.
c) Only after they ask three times.
d) As a guaranteed approval.

**8. What must you never promise about financing?**
a) That it exists.
b) Approvals, rates, or terms — those are the lender's to state.
c) That it's optional.
d) That it has no fees.

**9. A couple deflects "we haven't really thought about budget." What do you do?**
a) Drop it and guess.
b) Anchor them: "most couples I see choose between a few ranges — is there a monthly number or a total you'd like to keep under?"
c) Show only the cheapest rings.
d) Show only the most expensive rings.

**10. What keeps the "lasts a lifetime" framing from becoming a pressure tactic?**
a) It's always pressure — avoid it.
b) Using it to point the conversation at meaning, not to justify an upsell.
c) Only using it for expensive rings.
d) It's only for the closing moment.

---

## Answer key

1. **b** — Early and plainly, framed as helpful; last-minute budget asks feel like letdowns.
2. **b** — "Range" is lower-stakes than a hard number and keeps the conversation moving.
3. **b** — Appearance doesn't predict budget; read the number, not the wardrobe.
4. **b** — Warm metal hides a slightly warmer stone; eye-clean beats paying for paper.
5. **b** — Eye-clean is what's visible; flawless-over-eye-clean spends on the report.
6. **b** — A smaller/differently-specced stone now or the upgrade plan keeps both the relationship and the future sale.
7. **b** — A service, never a rescue; no promised approvals or terms.
8. **b** — Approvals, rates, and terms belong to the lender.
9. **b** — Anchor with normal ranges so the number stops being a secret.
10. **b** — Meaning, not upsell pressure, is the honest use of the framing.
