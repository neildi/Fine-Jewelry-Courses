# C6-M03 Fact-Check: GIA vs. AGS

## Verification Status: Strongly corroborated between two detailed sources; one recent claim flagged for independent verification

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Shared founder (Robert M. Shipley) and founding years (GIA 1931, AGS 1934).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across both sources. Whiteflash states "Both organizations were originally founded by the same group of jewelry professionals"; CaratWire specifically names Robert M. Shipley and both years.

**2. AGS founded as a retailer-ethics association, not originally a grading lab; AGS Laboratories opened in 1996 specifically to address the cut-grading gap.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT. Both sources describe AGS's original ethics-association purpose and the 1996 lab opening driven by the cut-grading gap GIA hadn't yet filled.

**3. GIA introduced its own round-brilliant cut grade in January 2006, a decade after AGS Laboratories opened.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT. CaratWire states this explicitly with the specific date; Whiteflash corroborates generally ("GIA was late to the party and continues to lag behind").

**4. Core methodology difference: GIA's categorical 5-tier proportion/appearance system vs. AGS's continuous 0-10 ASET-measured light-performance scale.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT and is the central, well-corroborated technical claim of this module. Both sources describe this distinction in detail and consistently.

**5. The 2022 GIA acquisition of AGS Laboratories' facility, IP, ASET methodology, and staff; AGS Laboratories ceasing standalone certification.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT. Both sources describe this event with matching details (late 2022 timing, Las Vegas facility, methodology transfer).

**6. The AGS Ideal Report supplement (2023+, ~$25 per stone) as the methodology's continuation within GIA reports.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT. Both sources describe this supplement; CaratWire provides the specific ~$25 price point.

**7. Fancy-shape cut-grading gap: GIA round-only vs. AGS covering princess, emerald, oval, cushion, radiant, Asscher.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across both sources, including the specific list of AGS-covered fancy shapes.

**8. Spend-based recommendation guideline with specific dollar thresholds ($4,000, $15,000, ~$25 supplement cost).**
Status: Sourced SOLELY from CaratWire's "Four-line buyer verdict by spend band" section; NOT independently corroborated by Whiteflash, which does not provide equivalent specific dollar thresholds.
