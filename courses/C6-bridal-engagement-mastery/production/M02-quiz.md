# M02 Quiz — Booking and Preparing the Appointment

10 questions testing the terminal outcome: converting an inquiry into a prepared appointment. Answer key at the end.

---

**1. A caller asks "how much is a 1-carat round diamond?" What is the best response?**
a) "I can't discuss price over the phone."
b) Give an honest range, then bridge to two specific appointment times.
c) "Just come in and we'll talk."
d) Quote the cheapest stone you have to get them in.

**2. Why does an appointment out-convert a walk-in for bridal?**
a) Walk-ins are never serious buyers.
b) The buyer has committed time, and you control the associate, environment, and selection.
c) Appointments always cost more.
d) Walk-ins only buy fashion jewelry.

**3. Which is the stronger bridge to book an appointment?**
a) "Stop by anytime."
b) "We're open until 8."
c) "I can show you the 1-carat in your budget next to two alternatives Thursday at 3 or Saturday at 11 — which works?"
d) "Call back when you're ready to buy."

**4. What are the five lines of pre-appointment discovery?**
a) Name, address, birthday, email, phone.
b) Who's coming; occasion/timeline; budget range; what they know; what they've seen.
c) Favorite color, metal, gemstone, designer, price.
d) None — discovery happens at the appointment.

**5. Why should you ask "will it be just you, or are you bringing anyone?" when booking?**
a) It's polite small talk.
b) It tells you which journey you're preparing for and how many chairs to stage.
c) It screens out time-wasters.
d) It's required by policy.

**6. What does speaking an agenda out loud in the first two minutes accomplish?**
a) It wastes time better spent selling.
b) It makes the next hour predictable for an anxious buyer and frames the visit as ending in a concrete deliverable.
c) It pressures the buyer.
d) It replaces the greeting.

**7. According to the module, what is the most reliable signal to a buyer that they are expected and important?**
a) A discount.
b) Being greeted by name and handed to the right associate.
c) A free gift.
d) A follow-up email.

**8. A buyer brings photos of a competitor's ring. What's the better move?**
a) Knock the competitor's quality.
b) Meet it: "we can do that, and here's how ours compares on the points that matter."
c) Refuse to look at the photos.
d) Suggest they buy from the competitor.

**9. A buyer no-shows their appointment. What should you do?**
a) Remove them from your book.
b) Follow up same day, re-offer two times, and keep the relationship warm.
c) Wait for them to call you.
d) Charge them a no-show fee.

**10. What does "stage the selection, not the whole case" mean?**
a) Show everything so they don't miss anything.
b) Present the relevant pieces plus one deliberate alternative at a different price point, so they can see what a step buys.
c) Show only the most expensive pieces.
d) Let the buyer browse the cases alone.

---

## Answer key

1. **b** — Dodging a price question backfires; answer honestly, then bridge to a specific time.
2. **b** — A booking selects committed buyers and lets you control associate, environment, and selection.
3. **c** — Two specific options with a reason to say yes beat an open invitation.
4. **b** — Who's coming, occasion/timeline, budget range, what they know, what they've seen.
5. **b** — It tells you which journey (solo/couple/family) to prepare for.
6. **b** — An agenda calms the anxious buyer and frames the visit around a concrete deliverable.
7. **b** — Name + right associate is the clearest signal of being expected.
8. **b** — Meet the comparison and differentiate on the points that matter.
9. **b** — A warm same-day follow-up with re-offered times keeps the relationship alive.
10. **b** — A curated selection with one alternative price point teaches value better than a full case.
