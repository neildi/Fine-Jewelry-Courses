# C6-M13 Fact-Check: Selling Bridal in Cruise and Port Retail Environments

## Verification Status: Strong cross-source agreement on core dynamics; module explicitly aligns with, rather than contradicts, professional consensus caution about this channel

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Multi-level commission structure (shopping consultant, store staff/management, and often the cruise line itself earning commission).**
Status: CONFIRMED via PRIMARY/HIGH-CREDIBILITY SOURCE — GIA's own official consumer guidance states this directly and explicitly ("Virtually everyone involved in your purchasing process... will be working on commission. This includes... the cruise ships"). Independently corroborated by multiple Reddit r/Cruise community threads and Grandsailor's shore-side-strategy guides.

**2. Specific seasonal placement fees (cited at $25,000+ per season) and sales kickback percentages (cited at roughly 10-15%).**
Status: Sourced from Grandsailor's shore-side-strategy content, a single-source-type figure (one travel-strategy publisher). These specific dollar/percentage figures are presented in this module as illustrative of the general, well-corroborated pattern (commission/fee-based placement) rather than as verified universal constants, since exact structures vary meaningfully by cruise line, destination, and specific commercial arrangement.

**3. Duty-free status does not equal discounted or fair pricing.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across five independent sources: GIA, Jewelers Mutual's "Smart Traveler's Guide," Grandsailor's port-shopping tourist-trap guide, Family Cruise Companion's Caribbean jewelry guide, and Serli & Siroan's Caribbean jewelry scam warning — all five independently draw this same distinction with closely matching reasoning.

**4. U.S. customs duty-free exemption figures ($800 standard international exemption; $1,600 specifically for U.S. Virgin Islands purchases).**
Status: Sourced from Family Cruise Companion's detailed guide, which explicitly cites U.S. Customs rules for this specific USVI exemption; this figure is consistent with standard, publicly documented U.S. Customs and Border Protection policy regarding the USVI's distinct, higher personal exemption compared to other international destinations.

**5. Documented scam/pressure patterns (rushed decisions before ship departure; inflated reference prices creating illusory discounts; restrictive sales-contract fine print; "free trinket" scams; appraisal-value misrepresentation).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across The Points Guy's official cruise-scam guide, Jewelers Mutual's traveler guide, Serli & Siroan's Caribbean scam warning, and multiple independent Reddit r/Cruise community threads — all describing overlapping, specific tactics with matching detail.

**6. Professional consensus caution regarding cruise-ship/port jewelry purchases generally.**
Status: The American Gem Society's consumer guidance explicitly and directly states "No, you should not buy jewelry on a cruise ship," and this caution is echoed by Houston Jewelry and Loan's consumer guide. This module does NOT contradict or soften this widely-held professional consensus; it explicitly acknowledges the channel's real, documented reputational and structural challenges and frames the associate's ethical obligation as actively countering the specific, well-documented failure modes described above, rather than denying that they exist or are common.

**7. Reddit/travel-forum community discussion as evidence of the channel's visible reputational scrutiny.**
Status: Directly evidenced by this module's own research process — multiple, independent Reddit r/Cruise threads specifically discussing jewelry-shopping risks and specific store names/warnings were located during research, supporting the claim that this channel receives active, visible public discussion and scrutiny in exactly the kind of forums referenced in the module.
