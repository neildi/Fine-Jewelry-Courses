# C6-M12 Fact-Check: Ethical Standards in High-Ticket Bridal Selling

## Verification Status: Core claims rest on primary federal regulatory sources (FTC, CFPB) — the highest-confidence sourcing tier used in this course to date

## Claims Reviewed

**1. FTC lab-grown diamond disclosure requirement (equal-conspicuousness standard; qualifier immediately preceding "diamond").**
Status: CONFIRMED via PRIMARY SOURCE — the FTC's own "In the Loupe: Advertising Diamond, Gemstones and Pearls" consumer guidance and the FTC's actual template diamond warning letter (an enforcement document sent to real companies in 2019) both state this requirement in the FTC's own words. This is a direct, primary regulatory source, not secondary reporting.

**2. Permitted qualifier terms (laboratory-grown, laboratory-created, [manufacturer name]-created, or other terms that clearly convey non-mined origin); "cultured" permitted only alongside a qualifier.**
Status: CONFIRMED via PRIMARY SOURCE (FTC's own guidance) and cross-corroborated by National Jeweler's "8 Key Changes the FTC Made to the Jewelry Guides" and JCK's "The FTC Gets Tough on Lab-Grown Diamond Disclosure," both trade-press sources with direct, accurate quotation of the FTC's actual rule text.

**3. Prohibited terms ("real," "natural," "genuine," "precious") for lab-grown stones.**
Status: Sourced from National Jeweler's "Creative Connecting: How to Handle Disclosures," which quotes a legal source directly describing this FTC prohibition; consistent with the general logic of the FTC's equal-conspicuousness and non-deception rules described in the primary FTC sources.

**4. Disclosure obligation applies to melee-sized accent stones, not just center stones, at every carat weight covered by the Guides.**
Status: Sourced from Gurudiam's detailed disclosure-requirements guide, which explicitly addresses this specific point; consistent with and a reasonable extension of the FTC's own general rule language ("any laboratory-created... diamond") which does not carve out an exception by size.

**5. Treated-stone disclosure requirement (disclose treatment and permanence; applies verbally or in writing).**
Status: Sourced from JCK's "The revised FTC guides: What's Permissible, What's Not," which directly quotes the FTC's rule text on treated diamonds, and National Jeweler's "Creative Connecting" article, which corroborates the verbal-or-written disclosure obligation via a quoted legal source.

**6. Deferred interest vs. true 0% APR mechanics (silent accrual from purchase date; full retroactive charge if balance remains at deadline).**
Status: CONFIRMED via PRIMARY/NEAR-PRIMARY SOURCE — the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's own archived newsroom bulletin explicitly describing this mechanism, cross-corroborated by CFPB's own consumer-facing "Ask CFPB" explainer and independently corroborated by Bankrate, NerdWallet, CNBC, and the National Consumer Law Center's "Deceptive Bargain" report — all describing matching mechanics with no contradiction found across any source.

**7. Consumer-harm statistics (roughly 1-in-5 deferred-interest promotional balances resulting in retroactive charges; over a third of affected consumers paying more than 150% of original balance; retroactive interest potentially reaching ~50% of original purchase cost for 25-35 month promotions).**
Status: Sourced from the CFPB's own archived research bulletin (which explicitly cites its 2015 CFPB study for the "more than a third pay over 150%" and "more than half pay more than the full promotional balance" figures) and NerdWallet's reporting citing an "earlier CFPB report" for the 25-35-month/50%-of-cost figure. The "roughly 1 in 5" figure specifically is cited by a secondary source (Monetary Jewelers) attributing it to "CFPB's Issue Spotlight on retail credit cards (2024)" — this specific 2024 figure was not independently located in a directly-accessed CFPB primary document during this research pass and should be treated as a secondary citation of a CFPB source rather than a directly verified primary-source figure.

**8. High-pressure sales tactic examples (false urgency, discouraging second opinions, financing steering, ignoring stated hesitation).**
Status: Sourced from Jewelry Secrets, a long-running, specifically jewelry-retail-focused consumer advocacy resource; this is a single-source-type claim (one consumer advocacy site) but the specific tactics described are consistent with the same tactics documented more generally in high-pressure-sales literature across retail industries broadly, and are used here as illustrative examples rather than an exhaustive or independently statistically-verified list.

**9. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from any external source.
