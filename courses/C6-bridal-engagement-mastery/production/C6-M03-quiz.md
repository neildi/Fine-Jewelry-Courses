# C6-M03 Quiz: GIA vs. AGS

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly explaining the methodological difference and current (post-2022) status of AGS, not memorizing exact dates.

---

**1. Who founded both GIA and AGS, and in what years?**
A) Two unrelated founders, decades apart
B) Both founded by gemologist Robert M. Shipley — GIA in 1931, AGS in 1934
C) Both founded in the same year by different people
D) AGS was founded first, in the 1800s

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Both organizations trace back to the same founder, Robert M. Shipley, with GIA founded in 1931 and AGS in 1934, though they were built for different original purposes.

---

**2. Why did AGS open its own diamond-grading laboratory in 1996?**
A) To compete directly with GIA on color and clarity grading
B) To fill a gap GIA hadn't yet addressed: a genuinely measured cut grade based on light performance, not just proportion categories
C) AGS Laboratories has always existed since 1934
D) To replace GIA entirely as the trade's primary grading body

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: AGS Laboratories opened specifically because GIA had not yet introduced a measured cut grade — GIA didn't add its own round-brilliant cut grade until January 2006, a decade later.

---

**3. What is the core methodological difference between GIA's and AGS's cut grading systems?**
A) There is no real difference; they use identical methods
B) GIA uses a categorical 5-tier system (Excellent through Poor) based on proportions and observer assessment; AGS measures light performance directly via ASET ray-tracing on a continuous 0-10 scale
C) AGS only grades color and clarity, never cut
D) GIA's system is numeric and AGS's system is categorical (the reverse of the actual difference)

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: GIA's cut grade is categorical and proportion-based; AGS's system directly measures light performance (brightness, contrast, dispersion, leakage) using ray-tracing technology on a continuous numeric scale.

---

**4. What happened to AGS Laboratories in 2022?**
A) It merged with an unrelated company and stopped all diamond-related work
B) GIA acquired its facility, intellectual property, ASET methodology, and research staff; AGS Laboratories stopped issuing standalone certificates
C) AGS Laboratories is still operating exactly as before, completely independent of GIA
D) AGS Laboratories was found to have fraudulent grading practices and was shut down

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: In late 2022, GIA acquired AGS Laboratories' facility, IP, ASET methodology, and staff. AGS Laboratories stopped issuing its own standalone certificates as a result, but the methodology continued.

---

**5. How can a client still access AGS's light-performance methodology today, after the 2022 closure?**
A) They cannot; the methodology is permanently unavailable
B) Through the "AGS Ideal Report," a supplement that can be added to a qualifying GIA report for roughly $25
C) Only by visiting the original AGS Laboratories building, which still operates unchanged
D) AGS methodology was never actually preserved after 2022

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Starting in 2023, GIA began offering the AGS Ideal Report as a supplement to qualifying GIA reports, continuing the same light-performance methodology under a different delivery format.

---

**6. Why does the fancy-shape cut-grading gap matter more in 2026 than it did five years ago, per this module?**
A) Fancy shapes have become less popular, making this irrelevant
B) GIA has never offered an overall cut grade for fancy shapes, while AGS's methodology covers oval and other fancy shapes — and oval is one of the fastest-rising shapes per Module 1's trend data
C) GIA started grading fancy shapes in 2025, making AGS unnecessary
D) This gap has no practical relevance to bridal selling

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Since GIA has never provided an overall cut grade for fancy shapes and AGS's methodology does cover shapes like oval, this gap becomes more practically relevant as oval's popularity rises, per Module 1's trend findings.

---

**7. Per this module's spend-based guideline, when should the AGS Ideal supplement be most strongly recommended?**
A) Never, regardless of price or shape
B) For round diamonds above roughly $15,000, diamonds marketed as "super ideal," any fancy-shape diamond, or lab-grown rounds, since it may be the only independent cut credential available
C) Only for diamonds under $1,000
D) Only for colored gemstones, never diamonds

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The guideline recommends the supplement most strongly at higher price points, for "super ideal" marketing claims, and for fancy shapes or lab-grown rounds where it may be the only available independent cut credential.

---

**8. What stays essentially the same between GIA and AGS reporting, per this module?**
A) Nothing is the same; every aspect differs completely
B) Color and clarity grading methodology and the fluorescence scale are essentially the same between the two systems — the meaningful difference is specifically in cut/light-performance measurement
C) Only clarity grading is shared; color grading differs completely
D) AGS never graded color or clarity at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Both labs use essentially the same methodology and master stone sets for color and clarity, and the same fluorescence scale — the real distinction is isolated to cut/light-performance measurement.
