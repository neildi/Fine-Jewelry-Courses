# C6-M13 Outline: Selling Bridal in Cruise and Port Retail Environments

## Module Metadata
- Course: C6 - Bridal & Engagement Mastery
- Module: 13 of 14
- Persona: P2
- Working title: Selling Bridal in Cruise and Port Retail Environments
- Learning objective: Associates can accurately explain the commission/marketing-fee structure underlying cruise/port jewelry retail, correctly distinguish duty-free status from discounted pricing, accurately state customs exemption amounts, and actively counteract departure-clock time pressure rather than leverage it, positioning transparency as this channel's key competitive differentiator.

## Production Notes
- The multi-level commission structure in cruise/port jewelry retail (ship-affiliated shopping consultants, store staff, and the cruise line itself often earning commission) is sourced directly from GIA's own official consumer guidance ("Cruise Ship Shopping: Tips for Buying Gems and Jewelry"), a highly credible primary industry-education source, and independently corroborated by multiple Reddit r/Cruise community threads and Grandsailor's shore-side-strategy guides describing specific seasonal placement fees (cited at $25,000+ per season) and sales kickback percentages (cited at roughly 10-15%). The specific dollar/percentage figures from Grandsailor are treated as illustrative of the general pattern rather than verified universal constants, since exact commission structures vary by cruise line and destination.
- The duty-free-does-not-equal-discount distinction is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across GIA, Jewelers Mutual's "Smart Traveler's Guide," Grandsailor's port-shopping guide, Family Cruise Companion, and Serli & Siroan's Caribbean jewelry scam guide — all five independently make this same core distinction with matching reasoning (inflated reference prices, taxes sometimes already built into sales price).
- Customs exemption figures ($800 standard U.S. international exemption; $1,600 specifically for U.S. Virgin Islands purchases) are sourced from Family Cruise Companion's detailed guide, cross-consistent with standard, publicly documented U.S. Customs and Border Protection policy on this specific USVI exemption.
- High-pressure/time-pressure warnings and specific scam patterns (rushed decisions before ship departure, "free trinket" scams, restrictive sales-contract fine print, appraisal-inflation tactics) are corroborated across The Points Guy, Jewelers Mutual, Serli & Siroan, and multiple Reddit r/Cruise community threads, all independently describing overlapping specific tactics.
- The professional consensus recommendation to generally avoid or be highly cautious about cruise-ship/port jewelry purchases is explicitly and directly stated by the American Gem Society ("Should You Buy Jewelry on a Cruise?" — answer: "No, you should not buy jewelry on a cruise ship") and Houston Jewelry and Loan; this module does not soften or contradict this widely-held professional caution, but frames the associate's role as actively countering the specific documented failure modes rather than denying the channel's real reputational challenges.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
