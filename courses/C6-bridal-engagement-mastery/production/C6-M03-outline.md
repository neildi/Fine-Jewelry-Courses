# C6-M03 Outline: GIA vs. AGS: Reading Certificates and Choosing the Right Lab

## Module Metadata
- Course: C6 - Bridal & Engagement Mastery
- Module: 3 of 14
- Persona: P2
- Working title: GIA vs. AGS: Reading Certificates and Choosing the Right Lab
- Learning objective: Associates can explain the GIA/AGS shared history and methodological difference (proportion-bucket vs. ASET light-performance measurement), explain what happened to AGS Laboratories in 2022 and why the methodology still matters, identify the fancy-shape cut-grading gap, and apply a spend-based guideline for recommending the AGS Ideal supplement.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Distinguish diamond shape from cut quality, and explain why GIA and AGS grade cut differently, especially for fancy shapes" (second half, completing Module 2's foundation).

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Bridges from Module 2's cut-quality foundation to how it's actually measured/certified.
2. **A Shared Origin, Then Two Different Missions** — Robert Shipley founding both; GIA (1931) as research institute vs. AGS (1934) as retailer-ethics association; AGS Laboratories opening in 1996 specifically to address the cut-grading gap GIA hadn't yet filled.
3. **The Core Methodological Difference: Proportion Buckets vs. Measured Light Performance** — GIA's categorical 5-tier system vs. AGS's continuous 0-10 ASET-measured scale; no clean conversion between systems.
4. **What Happened in 2022: AGS Laboratories Closed, But the Methodology Didn't Disappear** — GIA's 2022 acquisition of AGS's facility/IP/staff; the AGS Ideal Report supplement (2023+, ~$25) as the methodology's continuation.
5. **The Fancy-Shape Gap: Why This Matters More as Oval Rises** — GIA's round-only cut grading vs. AGS's fancy-shape coverage (princess, emerald, oval, cushion, radiant, Asscher); direct tie to Module 1's oval trend finding.
6. **A Practical Spend-Based Guide for Recommending the Supplement** — Four spend bands with specific recommendations, including the October 2025 GIA lab-grown reporting change.
7. **What Stays the Same Between the Two Systems** — Color/clarity methodology and fluorescence scale parity, to avoid overstating the distinction.
8. **A Worked Scenario: Explaining Why "AGS" Still Matters After the Lab Closed** — Illustrative synthesis addressing a likely client question directly.
9. **Practice Exercise** — Fancy-shape GIA report gap-identification exercise.
10. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.
