# C6-M07 Quiz: The Custom Design and CAD Workflow for Bridal

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly sequencing the workflow and applying the CAD-review order, not memorizing exact day counts.

---

**1. What is the correct order of the five-stage custom bridal design workflow, per this module?**
A) CAD design, consultation, sign-off, revisions, inspection
B) Consultation/design brief, CAD design and rendering, revision rounds, final sign-off and production, final inspection and delivery
C) Production, consultation, CAD design, revisions, delivery
D) There is no consistent order; every jeweler does this differently

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This sequence is strongly consistent across nine independent sources reviewed, all describing the same core stages in the same order.

---

**2. What typically happens once a client formally approves a CAD file?**
A) Nothing changes; the client can still request free changes indefinitely
B) The approval locks the file's specifications (prong height, band profile, metal weight, engraving placement), and further changes typically require rework, added cost, or added time
C) The file is automatically deleted and a new one must be created
D) Sign-off has no real significance in the process

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: CAD sign-off is the last opportunity to change a design as a digital file; once approved, the locked specifications proceed to casting, and further changes are no longer simple in-flight adjustments.

---

**3. Per this module's CAD review checklist, what should be checked before the aesthetic face-up view?**
A) Nothing; the face-up view should always be reviewed first
B) Stone size/shape against the actual order, proportions, prong placement, metal weight, seating depth, shank taper/gallery clearance, and engraving space
C) Only the price should be checked before anything else
D) CAD files don't require any technical review, only visual approval

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This technical-before-aesthetic review order helps catch problems (like an underweight shank or shallow stone seating) that a face-up-only view can hide.

---

**4. Why is walking a client through the same technical review sequence (rather than just asking "does it look good?") valuable?**
A) It isn't valuable; clients only care about appearance
B) It helps the client catch problems a purely aesthetic view can hide, like incorrect seating depth or metal weight
C) It slows down the process for no benefit
D) Technical details are irrelevant to the client's decision

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A face-up-only view can hide real technical problems, so walking through proportions, seating, and weight first helps ensure the approved file actually matches expectations.

---

**5. What is the most commonly cited total timeline range for a custom engagement ring, from first consultation to delivery?**
A) Always exactly 2 weeks
B) Roughly four to twelve weeks total, with four to six weeks cited most frequently as typical for moderate complexity
C) Always at least six months
D) Timeline has no real range; it's always the same for every design

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Sources consistently cite a 4-12 week range depending on complexity, with 4-6 weeks appearing most frequently as the typical figure for a moderately complex design.

---

**6. Which phase of the custom workflow is consistently described as the most time-intensive?**
A) The initial consultation
B) Fabrication (casting, stone setting, and finishing)
C) Final inspection and delivery
D) All phases take exactly the same amount of time

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Fabrication is consistently cited as the longest single phase, generally running two to six weeks depending on design complexity.

---

**7. In the worked scenario, why does the associate recommend the client approve the CAD file that day rather than requesting another small tweak?**
A) Because further tweaks are always free and instant
B) Because the client's proposal date is only ten days away, and additional changes at this point would push past that deadline given how much time production requires
C) Because the associate doesn't want to do any more work
D) Because CAD files expire after 24 hours regardless of deadline

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: With a fixed, near-term deadline, the associate gives a specific, honest picture of how much time remains for production and recommends locking in sign-off now to protect that timeline.

---
