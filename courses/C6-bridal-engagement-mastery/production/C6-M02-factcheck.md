# C6-M02 Fact-Check: Diamond Shape vs. Cut

## Verification Status: Sourced from two consistent, commercially-affiliated diamond-education publications

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Shape vs. cut definitional distinction.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across both sources. Rings of UK and Whiteflash both explicitly state the same distinction: shape is the outline (round, oval, etc.), cut is the quality of faceting/proportions. Whiteflash's author explicitly notes "cut" is also colloquially used to mean shape (e.g., "marquise cut") and recommends restricting "cut" to the proportions/quality meaning to avoid confusion — this module follows that same convention.

**2. Cut as the most important of the 4Cs (human-craftsmanship framing).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT. Both sources state this; Whiteflash directly quotes GIA's own website: "The allure of a particular diamond depends more on cut than anything else."

**3. Brilliance, fire, and scintillation definitions.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT, nearly identical definitions across both sources.

**4. Diamond anatomy terms and round-brilliant proportion ranges (table 54-58%, depth 59-62.5%, crown angle 34-35 degrees).**
Status: Sourced from Rings of UK. Whiteflash's own ideal-proportion references (via AGS Traditional Ideal Proportions and Whiteflash's "A CUT ABOVE" Super Ideal specifications) are directionally consistent but not identically stated with the same exact percentage ranges in the fetched content — treat Rings of UK's specific numbers as the primary source for this module, cross-referenced generally (not number-for-number) against Whiteflash.

**5. Cut-carat trade-off mechanics (visual size difference, price premium percentages 10-20% Excellent vs. Very Good, 25-40% vs. Good).**
Status: Sourced from Rings of UK specifically. Whiteflash corroborates the underlying mechanic (weight distribution affecting face-up size) but does not provide the same specific premium percentages in the fetched content. MUST VERIFY: the specific premium percentages (10-20%, 25-40%) are single-sourced from Rings of UK and should be treated as a general guideline rather than a precise, universal figure, consistent with how this course has handled other single-sourced statistics.

**6. Cut compensating for color/clarity (G-H color, VS2-SI1 clarity becoming harder to detect face-up with Excellent cut).**
Status: Sourced from Rings of UK. This is a widely-accepted general principle in diamond retail consistent with how light performance affects visual perception of tint and inclusions, though the specific grade ranges cited are single-sourced.

**7. Bowtie effect definition and shape susceptibility (oval, pear, marquise).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT. Rings of UK explicitly defines and discusses bowtie for oval, pear, and marquise; Whiteflash's fancy-shape proportion table and general fancy-shape discussion corroborates elongated shapes as the relevant category, though Whiteflash's fetched content does not use the specific term "bowtie" as prominently.
