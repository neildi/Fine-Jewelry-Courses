# C6-M09 Quiz: Wedding Bands and Stacking

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly matching band recommendations to setting type and applying timeline guidance, not memorizing exact percentages.

---

**1. What is the primary reason a contoured or curved wedding band is recommended for a halo or three-stone engagement ring?**
A) Contoured bands are always cheaper than straight bands
B) A straight band pushed against a raised, detailed setting often leaves a visible gap, while a contoured band follows the ring's profile and closes that gap
C) Contoured bands never need resizing
D) Setting style has no bearing on which band shape works best

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Halo, three-stone, and vintage-style rings with pronounced side detail benefit most from a contoured band specifically because it eliminates the visible gap a straight band would leave.

---

**2. Why is matching metal type generally recommended for bands worn together on the same finger?**
A) Metal type has no effect on wear or durability when rings touch
B) Prolonged contact between metals of different hardness can cause the softer metal to wear faster, and dissimilar metals in constant contact can in some cases contribute to galvanic corrosion
C) Matching metal is purely a legal requirement with no practical basis
D) Mixed metals always look identical to matched metals

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Metal-matching is recommended primarily for durability and wear reasons, not purely aesthetic ones — dissimilar hardness levels in constant contact cause uneven wear over time.

---

**3. What is the recommended solution when a client wants to intentionally mix different metals in a stack?**
A) Mixing metals should never be allowed under any circumstance
B) Use a spacer band between the dissimilar metals to reduce direct wear and give the eye a visual resting point
C) Mixed metals should always touch directly with no separation
D) Metal mixing has no solution and should be actively discouraged in all cases

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A spacer band protects against wear from friction between dissimilar metals while also preventing the stack from looking like one solid, undifferentiated block.

---

**4. Which stacking principle is consistently recommended across sources reviewed?**
A) Every band in a stack should be the exact same width
B) Vary band widths intentionally and generally limit a stack to two metal tones per hand
C) A stack should always include at least four different metal tones
D) Stacking has no consistent principles; any combination works equally well

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Intentional width variation and limiting to two complementary metal tones are the most consistently recommended stacking principles across multiple independent sources.

---

**5. What deadline should drive the wedding-band shopping timeline, per this module?**
A) The proposal date
B) The wedding date, not the proposal date — many clients don't think about this until surprisingly late
C) The date the engagement ring was purchased
D) Wedding bands have no meaningful timeline considerations at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The wedding-band shopping deadline is tied to the wedding date specifically, which is a genuinely important distinction to communicate since it's a separate timeline from the engagement ring purchase.

---

**6. Roughly how far in advance should a client begin shopping for a fully custom, contour-matched wedding band designed to nest against their engagement ring?**
A) The week of the wedding
B) Roughly eight months to a year in advance, following similar logic to Module 7's custom-design timeline
C) Timing has no bearing on custom contour-matched bands
D) Exactly two days before the wedding

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A contour-matched band effectively requires its own CAD design and revision process, so it follows the longer custom-design timeline logic rather than the faster in-stock timeline.

---

**7. Why is it valuable to proactively mention the wedding-band timeline at the original engagement ring sale?**
A) It has no real value and should be avoided
B) It plants the seed for the second sale months in advance, improving the odds the client returns to the same store rather than shopping elsewhere later
C) It is required by law to mention wedding bands at every engagement ring sale
D) Wedding bands are never purchased at the same store as the engagement ring

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Proactively raising this at the point of the engagement ring sale, well before the client would otherwise think to return, is a genuine driver of the wedding-band attach-rate KPI.

**8. In the worked scenario, why does the associate recommend a contoured band for the client's wedding band?**
A) Because contoured bands are always the cheapest option
B) Because the client's original engagement ring has a halo setting, and a contoured band avoids the visible gap that would occur with a straight band against that setting
C) Because the client specifically asked for a straight band
D) Because contour has no relationship to setting type in this scenario

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The recommendation is grounded directly in the specific setting style of the client's existing engagement ring, applying the contour-fit principle from earlier in the module.
