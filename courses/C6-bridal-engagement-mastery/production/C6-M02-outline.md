# C6-M02 Outline: Diamond Shape vs. Cut: Why They're Not the Same Thing

## Module Metadata
- Course: C6 - Bridal & Engagement Mastery
- Module: 2 of 14
- Persona: P1
- Working title: Diamond Shape vs. Cut: Why They're Not the Same Thing
- Learning objective: Associates can clearly distinguish shape from cut quality, explain the three optical effects cut optimizes, use diamond anatomy vocabulary correctly, and make a concrete, visual cut-carat trade-off argument to a budget-conscious client.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Distinguish diamond shape from cut quality, and explain why GIA and AGS grade cut differently, especially for fancy shapes" (first half; GIA/AGS specifics deferred to Module 3).

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames the shape/cut conflation as one of the most common client-facing points of confusion.
2. **The Core Distinction** — Definitional separation of shape (design choice) vs. cut (quality/craftsmanship factor).
3. **Why Cut Is Widely Considered the Most Important of the 4Cs** — Human-craftsmanship framing; visual/size impact.
4. **The Three Optical Effects Cut Is Actually Optimizing** — Brilliance, fire, scintillation definitions.
5. **Anatomy of a Diamond: The Vocabulary You Need** — Table, crown, girdle, pavilion, culet, depth; round-brilliant ideal proportion ranges.
6. **The Cut-Carat Trade-Off: The Single Most Common Buying Mistake** — Visual-size mechanic; Excellent-vs-Good premium percentages; concrete mm/carat comparison.
7. **Cut Can Partially Compensate for Color and Clarity** — Practical budget-reallocation trade-off.
8. **The Bowtie Effect: A Fancy-Shape-Specific Consideration** — Definition; tie-back to Module 1's oval-trend finding.
9. **A Worked Scenario: Explaining the Cut-Carat Trade-Off** — Illustrative synthesis using a visual-comparison sales technique.
10. **Practice Exercise** — Shape/cut clarification roleplay plus cut-carat pitch practice.
11. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- Sourced from two independent diamond-education publications: Rings of UK's "Diamond Cut Guide: Why Cut Quality Matters Most (2026)" and Whiteflash's "The 4 C's - Diamond Cut: Cut Grading and Buying Guide" (by Bryan Boyne, G.G.). These sources are LARGELY CONSISTENT with each other on core concepts (shape/cut distinction, brilliance/fire/scintillation definitions, cut-carat trade-off logic, bowtie effect), which increases confidence relative to Module 1's conflicting trend sources.
- Both sourced publications are RETAILER-OPERATED educational content (Rings of UK and Whiteflash both sell diamonds), which is standard and reputable in this space (both are established, credentialed businesses — Whiteflash's author is a G.G., Graduate Gemologist), but should be understood as educational content from parties with a commercial interest, not neutral academic sources. This is consistent with how diamond education is typically sourced industry-wide (GIA itself, while non-commercial, is also the trade's dominant credentialing body).
- Specific proportion percentage ranges (table 54-58%, depth 59-62.5%, crown angle 34-35 degrees for round brilliants) are consistent between sources and align with widely-published GIA/AGS-adjacent proportion guidance — treat as reliable general guidance, though exact "ideal" ranges have some variation depending on which lab/methodology is referenced (addressed further in Module 3).
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
