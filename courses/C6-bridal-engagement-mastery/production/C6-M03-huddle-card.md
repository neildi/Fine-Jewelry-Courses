# C6-M03 Huddle Card: GIA vs. AGS

## 5-Minute Pre-Shift Discussion

**The one idea to remember:** AGS Laboratories closed in 2022 — but the science didn't disappear. It's now the "AGS Ideal Report," a ~$25 supplement you can add to a GIA report.

## The Core Difference
- **GIA cut grade** = a category (Excellent, Very Good, Good...) based on proportions and a human panel's visual assessment.
- **AGS cut grade** = an actual MEASUREMENT of light performance (brightness, fire, leakage) on a 0-10 scale, with an ASET image showing exactly where light is going.

No clean conversion between the two — a GIA "Triple Excellent" could be AGS 0, 1, or 2.

## The Gap That Matters More Now
GIA has NEVER graded overall cut quality on fancy shapes — only round. AGS's methodology covers oval, cushion, emerald, princess, radiant, Asscher. With oval trending up (Module 1), this gap comes up more often.

## When to Recommend the AGS Ideal Supplement
- Round under ~$4,000 → skip it
- Round $4,000-$15,000 → recommend it
- Round above $15,000 or "super ideal" claims → strongly recommend it, show the ASET image
- Any fancy shape or lab-grown round → it's the ONLY independent cut credential available

## Today's Discussion Prompt
Next time a client asks about a fancy-shape stone's cut grade, practice explaining what the GIA report does and doesn't tell them.
