# C6-M04 Quiz: Natural, Lab-Grown, and Simulants

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly distinguishing the four options and advising without bias, not memorizing exact price/resale percentages.

---

**1. What are the two production methods for lab-grown diamonds, per GIA's own research?**
A) Mining and cutting
B) HPHT (High Pressure, High Temperature) and CVD (Chemical Vapor Deposition)
C) Heating and cooling only
D) There is only one production method

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: GIA's research describes HPHT (using pressure/temperature/metallic flux on a carbon source) and CVD (using a gas reactor and plasma to deposit diamond material on a seed) as the two production methods.

---

**2. Is moissanite the same thing as a "fake diamond" or inferior diamond substitute?**
A) Yes, it is simply a lower-quality diamond
B) No — moissanite is a genuinely different mineral (silicon carbide), independently graded, not trying to be sold as diamond
C) Moissanite and cubic zirconia are the exact same material
D) Moissanite is actually a type of natural diamond

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Moissanite is silicon carbide, a distinct mineral with its own gemological identity and independent grading tradition — not an inferior or "fake" diamond.

---

**3. Why is cubic zirconia generally a poor choice for a ring meant to be resized or repaired over time?**
A) CZ is actually harder than diamond
B) CZ can crack or cloud under the heat used in resizing/repair, unlike diamond or moissanite which are heat-resistant
C) CZ has no real durability differences from moissanite
D) Resizing has no effect on any gemstone

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: CZ lacks the heat resistance of diamond and moissanite, making it prone to damage during the heat-based processes used in ring resizing and repair.

**4. How does this module frame the price gap between natural and lab-grown diamonds?**
A) There is no real price difference
B) Lab-grown diamonds cost meaningfully less, though the exact percentage varies by source (roughly 60-80% savings range) — the direction is reliable even though the magnitude varies
C) Lab-grown diamonds always cost exactly 50% of natural
D) Natural diamonds are always cheaper than lab-grown

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Multiple sources agree lab-grown costs substantially less, with cited savings ranging from roughly 60% to 80% depending on the specific source and carat size — the module presents this as directionally reliable but not a single precise figure.

**5. What does this module say about resale value estimates for lab-grown vs. natural diamonds?**
A) Both retain identical resale value
B) Estimates vary enormously by source (from roughly 10% to 40% for lab-grown, 25% to 60% for natural), and one source explicitly states no verified industry-wide percentages exist — but the consistent finding is that lab-grown retains meaningfully less value than natural
C) Lab-grown diamonds always retain more value than natural
D) Resale value is not a topic worth discussing with clients

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The specific percentages vary widely across sources, and the module explicitly quotes a source stating no verified industry-wide figures exist — but every source agrees lab-grown retains less resale value than natural.

**6. What is the laser inscription on a lab-grown diamond's girdle, and why is it a reassuring detail rather than a downside?**
A) It has no real purpose
B) A permanent, physical identification mark (often including "laboratory-grown") that travels with the stone itself, ensuring disclosure survives even if separated from its certificate
C) It is only used to identify stolen diamonds
D) It is a flaw that reduces the diamond's value

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The laser inscription provides permanent, physical proof of origin directly on the stone, meaning disclosure isn't dependent on a certificate staying with the piece — a genuinely reassuring detail worth highlighting to clients.

**7. When a client asks "which would you get?", what does this module recommend as a balanced response?**
A) Confidently state your own personal preference as the correct answer
B) Explain how the decision depends on what matters most to the client specifically (value comparison vs. origin story vs. budget), and redirect to their own priorities
C) Refuse to answer the question at all
D) Always recommend the most expensive option

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The worked scenario models answering honestly about what different clients value, without imposing a personal verdict, and redirecting the conversation back to the specific client's own priorities.

**8. Why does this module recommend presenting moissanite with the same enthusiasm as a natural diamond, if a client's budget points that way?**
A) Moissanite is objectively better than diamond in every case
B) A client choosing moissanite for honest budget reasons deserves the same quality of service as any other client, and a shift in tone can signal bias even without explicit discouragement
C) Associates should never mention moissanite as an option
D) Enthusiasm has no effect on how a client perceives a recommendation

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Advising without bias means treating every genuinely appropriate option with equal respect and energy — a noticeable drop in enthusiasm for a lower-price option can communicate bias just as clearly as words would.
