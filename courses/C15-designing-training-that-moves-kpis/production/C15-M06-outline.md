# C15-M06 Outline / Media Record

**Working title:** Measuring Training: Pre/Post KPI Design
**Alternates:** "Closing the Loop on Module 1's Outcome"; "Baseline, Deliver, Check: The Minimum Measurement Design"

**Scenario hook:** A trainer delivers a well-designed module but has no baseline number captured before training started, making it impossible to credibly claim the training caused any later KPI change.

**Section outline:**
1. Why This Matters — reader connects the documented training-leadership KPI accountability (Director of Sales Training, L&D role) and this course's own stated KPI link to the need for measurement.
2. The Minimum Pre/Post Design — reader can build a five-element measurement table (KPI, baseline, training delivered, check-in point, post number).
3. Choosing a Realistic Check-In Window — reader can set an appropriate measurement window, informed by the three-month progress-review anchor from job-market evidence.
4. What to Do When the Numbers Don't Move — reader can run a three-question diagnostic before rewriting training.
5. Objections, Mistakes and Edge Cases — reader avoids substituting completion/satisfaction for KPI data and the missing-baseline trap.

**Facts used, with placement:**
- Director of Sales Training must demonstrate training ROI in sales/client-satisfaction terms → Section 1 (reused sourcing from C15-M01)
- Watches of Switzerland L&D role measured explicitly on conversion, ATV, client retention → Section 1 (reused sourcing from C15-M01)
- This course's own stated KPI link: new-hire ramp time, post-training KPI lift → Section 1
- JCK compensation guide reference to reviewing an associate's progress three months into the job → Section 3
- Cross-reference to C12 (KPI vocabulary) and C14 Module 3 (comparative-KPI diagnostic discipline) → Sections 2, 4

**Gaps not covered in evidence:** No specific named pre/post measurement template or statistical methodology was found verbatim in the supplied research; the five-element table and three-question diagnostic are original synthesis built to operationalize the documented KPI-accountability standard — flagged for reviewer awareness. The three-month figure is drawn from a general new-associate progress-review context, not a training-measurement-specific source, and is used here only as a directional anchor for window-setting, not a fixed rule.
