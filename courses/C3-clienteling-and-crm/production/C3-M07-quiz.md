# C3-M07 Quiz: Winning Back a Lapsed Client

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly matching dormancy tier to tone, sequencing the win-back framework, and avoiding discount-first mistakes.

---

**1. A client hasn't purchased or responded to outreach in 4 months but responded warmly to a text 5 months ago. Which dormancy tier does she fall into?**
A) Recently quiet
B) Genuinely lapsed
C) Long-dormant
D) Not lapsed at all — no action needed

**Correct answer: A**
Explanation: 4 months of quiet with a recent warm interaction places her in the "recently quiet" tier (3-6 months), which calls for a light, no-agenda touch rather than a full win-back campaign.

---

**2. Which is the correct order of the Win-Back Framework presented in this module?**
A) Offer, Reconnect, Acknowledge, Ask, Leave the door open
B) Reconnect, Acknowledge, Offer, Ask, Leave the door open
C) Acknowledge, Offer, Reconnect, Leave the door open, Ask
D) Ask, Reconnect, Offer, Acknowledge, Leave the door open

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Reconnect on the relationship first, briefly acknowledge the gap, then offer something specific, make an easy ask, and leave the door open regardless of the response.

---

**3. Why does the module recommend against leading a win-back message with a discount?**
A) Discounts are against most company policies
B) It signals desperation, trains bargain-hunting, and skips the relationship-rebuilding step
C) Clients rarely notice discounts in messages
D) Discounts should only be offered in person, never in writing

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The module lists three specific costs of discount-first messaging: it signals desperation, trains clients to expect discounts every time, and skips the relationship step that actually drives durable re-engagement.

---

**4. A long-dormant client (20 months of silence) receives a win-back message. Which approach is most appropriate?**
A) Assume the relationship picks up exactly where it left off and reference recent inside jokes
B) Reintroduce yourself clearly, reference the last specific interaction, and make no assumptions about continued relationship
C) Send a steep discount immediately to re-capture interest quickly
D) Skip win-back entirely since 18+ months is considered unrecoverable

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Long-dormant clients need the most care — a clear reintroduction and low-pressure invitation, without assuming the relationship has continued as-is.

---

**5. A client responds to a win-back text with "thanks for thinking of me!" but doesn't ask any follow-up questions or express interest in buying. What should the associate do?**
A) Immediately follow up with a specific product recommendation to close the interaction
B) Treat it as a successful relationship touch, log it, and continue on a normal cadence
C) Assume she's not interested and remove her from future outreach
D) Ask her directly why she hasn't purchased recently

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A polite, non-committal response is a successful relationship touch, not a failed sales attempt. It should be logged and followed by normal-cadence outreach, not treated as a missed close.

---

**6. A first win-back message gets no response. What does this module recommend for the next attempt?**
A) Follow up again within 1-2 days with more urgency
B) Never contact this client again
C) Wait several weeks and use a different reason for the next touch
D) Send the exact same message again immediately

**Correct answer: C**
Explanation: The module recommends patience over escalation — space the next attempt out and vary the reason, rather than following up quickly or repeating the same message.

---

**7. A client explicitly asks to be left alone after a win-back message. What is the correct response?**
A) Send one more message explaining the value they'd be missing
B) Honor the request immediately, and document it in the CRM so no one else reaches out again
C) Wait 6 months and try once more since preferences can change
D) Escalate to a manager to attempt to save the relationship

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Explicit opt-out requests must be honored immediately and completely, with clear CRM documentation to prevent any future team member from re-contacting the client.

---

**8. Which metric would best tell you whether your win-back approach is rebuilding real relationships rather than producing one-off transactions?**
A) Number of win-back messages sent per week
B) Reactivated client lifetime value
C) Average discount percentage offered
D) Number of associates trained on win-back scripts

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Reactivated client lifetime value shows whether clients who come back continue purchasing at levels similar to their pre-lapse history, indicating a rebuilt relationship rather than a single transactional win.

---
