# Module 2 Self-Assessment Quiz: The Clienteling Calendar

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: running a structured, appropriately-paced outreach cadence.

**1. What are the three timing points in the post-sale sequence described in this module?**
A. 7, 14, and 21 days
B. 30, 90, and 365 days
C. 1, 6, and 12 months
D. Immediately, then never again
**Answer: B** - A 30-day check-in, a 90-day cleaning reminder, and a 365-day anniversary-of-purchase note.

**2. What are the three dates worth capturing for the date-based outreach sequence?**
A. Store opening date, client's first visit, last purchase date
B. Client's birthday, partner's birthday, and anniversary
C. Only the anniversary date
D. Every holiday on the calendar
**Answer: B** - These three dates drive the highest-return recurring outreach in jewelry retail.

**3. According to this module, which channel tends to outperform for personal, time-sensitive clienteling messages?**
A. Direct mail
B. SMS, when the sender is a recognized person
C. Billboard advertising
D. Social media comments
**Answer: B** - SMS works well for short, personal, timely messages like reminders and repair updates.

**4. Why is a cleaning and inspection cadence considered a retention tool, not just a service?**
A. It has no real retention value
B. It brings the client back in regularly, creates repair-sale opportunities, and refreshes the client record
C. It only matters for insurance purposes
D. It should be avoided because it costs the store money
**Answer: B** - Stores running this cadence consistently report meaningfully higher repeat-purchase rates.

**5. What are the two rules for a win-back message to a lapsed client?**
A. Lead with the biggest possible discount, and repeat it often
B. Don't lead with a discount, and give the message a genuine reason to exist
C. Only reach out once and never again if there's no response
D. Always mention the client's last purchase price
**Answer: B** - Warmth plus a concrete reason (new arrivals, a cleaning invite) works better than a coupon.

**6. What does "following through" on a wishlist entry mean?**
A. Logging it once and considering the job done
B. Reaching out when something changes: a matching new arrival, an event, an approaching occasion, or after about 30 days
C. Deleting wishlist entries after 24 hours
D. Only acting on wishlist items for the store's biggest spenders
**Answer: B** - A wishlist entry is wasted if it's captured but never revisited.

**7. What is the recommended ceiling on intentional outreach touches per year for most clients?**
A. As many as possible, since more contact always helps
B. About 2 to 4 intentional touches per year
C. Exactly one touch per year, and no more
D. There is no reasonable limit
**Answer: B** - Going meaningfully beyond this causes unsubscribe rates to spike, per the sourced guidance.

**8. What is the appropriate use split between SMS and email in a clienteling cadence?**
A. They serve identical purposes and can be used interchangeably
B. SMS for short, time-sensitive, personal messages; email for longer content like newsletters or event invitations
C. Email should never be used in jewelry retail
D. SMS should only be used for mass promotional blasts
**Answer: B** - Matching the channel to the message type improves both response and client experience.

**9. Why should outreach be segmented rather than sent as one mass blast to the full client list?**
A. Segmenting has no real benefit
B. Tighter, relevant segments produce better response rates and fewer opt-outs, and opted-out clients should always be excluded
C. Mass blasts are required by most CRM platforms
D. Segmenting only matters for very large stores
**Answer: B** - A relevant message to the right group outperforms an irrelevant message to everyone.

**10. What is the central risk this module identifies with poorly run clienteling?**
A. Only under-contacting clients is a risk; over-contacting is never a problem
B. Both silence (forgetting a client) and over-contact (spamming a client) damage the relationship, and the fix is a calibrated, reason-based cadence
C. Clienteling should be avoided entirely to prevent any risk
D. Only large stores need to worry about outreach cadence
**Answer: B** - The calendar in this module is designed to avoid both failure modes at once.
