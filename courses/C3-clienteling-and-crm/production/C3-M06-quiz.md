# Module 6 Self-Assessment Quiz: Personalized Outreach Without Sounding Like Spam

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: writing outreach that reads as attentive rather than surveillant, while staying compliant.

**1. According to the research cited in this module, what are the two components of the "creepiness" reaction to personalized marketing?**
A. Price sensitivity and brand loyalty
B. An ambiguity assessment ("what is this, how do they know?") followed by a surveillance judgment
C. Font choice and color scheme
D. Message length and timing
**Answer: B** - This two-part emotional process explains most of the discomfort people report with personalized messages.

**2. What percentage of emotional discomfort did perceptions of ambiguity and surveillance explain, per the cited research?**
A. About 10%
B. Roughly 75%
C. Exactly 50%
D. Less than 5%
**Answer: B** - This makes ambiguity and surveillance perception the dominant drivers of discomfort, not personalization itself.

**3. What is the "attribution fix" described in this module?**
A. Removing all personal details from every message
B. Naming the specific source of a personal detail (e.g., "you mentioned during your visit...") to resolve the "how do you know that" ambiguity
C. Only sending messages from an anonymous store account
D. Sending the exact same message to every client
**Answer: B** - This closes the ambiguity gap that produces the creepiness reaction.

**4. What must a business have before sending marketing text messages under TCPA?**
A. Nothing; any past purchase is sufficient
B. Prior express written consent, including a clear disclosure and an affirmative opt-in action
C. Only a verbal agreement
D. A pre-checked box is sufficient
**Answer: B** - Verbal agreements, pre-checked boxes, and implied consent don't satisfy TCPA requirements.

**5. What must happen if a client replies "STOP" to a marketing text?**
A. Nothing changes; continue sending as normal
B. The opt-out must be honored immediately
C. Wait 30 days before complying
D. Ask the client to confirm in writing before honoring it
**Answer: B** - Failing to honor opt-outs promptly carries real legal and financial risk.

**6. What is a practical distinction this module draws between message types?**
A. There is no meaningful distinction between any type of text message
B. A personal, one-to-one relationship text differs from an automated marketing-platform campaign text, though both should be grounded in real client consent
C. Only email messages require any consent at all
D. SMS messages never require any form of consent
**Answer: B** - The strictest automated-dialing-system rules target mass campaigns, but any promotional content should still be grounded in real consent.

**7. What is the recommended frequency range for promotional SMS messages per month?**
A. As many as possible, with no upper limit
B. Roughly 1 to 4 messages per month
C. Exactly one message per year
D. At least 20 per month
**Answer: B** - Going meaningfully beyond this range tends to increase unsubscribe rates.

**8. What are "quiet hours" for SMS marketing, as described in this module?**
A. There are no restrictions on send times
B. No marketing texts should be sent before about 8am or after 9pm in the recipient's local time zone
C. Messages can only be sent on weekends
D. Quiet hours only apply to email, not SMS
**Answer: B** - Sending outside these hours risks being seen as intrusive and can violate industry regulations.

**9. Which of these is an example of a spam-trigger phrase to avoid?**
A. "New arrivals are here, want me to send a few photos?"
B. "Amazing deal, act now, limited time only!"
C. "It's been a few months, want to swing by for a cleaning?"
D. "I thought of you when this piece came in"
**Answer: B** - Urgency and financial-promise language like this raises spam-filter risk and rarely fits a genuine clienteling relationship anyway.

**10. Why does moderate, clearly-sourced personalization tend to work better than revealing many unexplained details at once?**
A. Personalization should always be avoided entirely
B. Clearly-sourced personalization avoids triggering the ambiguity and surveillance reaction that unexplained personalization can cause
C. More personalization always increases response rates regardless of framing
D. Clients never notice the difference
**Answer: B** - The goal is to make the "how do you know that" question unnecessary by answering it upfront.
