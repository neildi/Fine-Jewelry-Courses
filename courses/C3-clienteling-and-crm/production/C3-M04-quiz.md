# Module 4 Self-Assessment Quiz: Segmenting Your Book

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: segmenting a client book and calibrating service tiers appropriately.

**1. According to the figures cited in this module, roughly what share of revenue do a brand's top clients (variously defined as top 2-5%) tend to generate?**
A. Less than 5%
B. Around 40%
C. Exactly 10%
D. There is no meaningful concentration
**Answer: B** - Both the Endear framing and the KPMG-cited figure point to a similar, disproportionate revenue concentration among top clients.

**2. What is the real capacity risk for an associate managing a large client book without a system?**
A. There is no real limit to how many personal relationships one person can maintain
B. An associate can typically maintain personal contact with only a fraction (illustratively, around 30 out of 200) without support
C. Capacity limits only apply to store managers, not associates
D. CRM systems make personal relationships unnecessary
**Answer: B** - Beyond a certain number, attention naturally spreads too thin without a system to help prioritize.

**3. What are the five signals used to identify a VIC in this module's framework?**
A. Only total historical spend
B. Revenue concentration, purchase frequency, category depth, engagement signals, and relationship potential
C. Age, income, and location only
D. Only how many times a client has visited the store
**Answer: B** - Using all five together avoids the mistake of ranking clients by spend alone.

**4. Why does "relationship potential" matter as a segmentation signal?**
A. It doesn't matter; only historical spend should count
B. Some clients with modest spend so far are early in a growing relationship and worth investing in now
C. Relationship potential should only be considered for clients who already spend the most
D. It's impossible to assess without a formal survey
**Answer: B** - Today's promising newer relationship can become tomorrow's top client.

**5. According to Gogo Cheng, how are some brands adjusting the traditional VIC definition?**
A. Narrowing it to the top 0.5% only
B. Widening it from the traditional top 1-2% to the top 5-10%
C. Eliminating VIC tiers entirely
D. Applying it only to first-time buyers
**Answer: B** - This reflects fewer new entry-level buyers and a need to protect a larger share of revenue.

**6. What are the four categories of VIC treatment described in this module?**
A. Discounts, coupons, free shipping, and loyalty points
B. Accessibility, service, experiences, and recognition
C. Advertising, social media, email, and print
D. Speed, price, quality, and convenience
**Answer: B** - These scale down to realistic, budget-appropriate versions at an independent jewelry store.

**7. What is the risk if a top associate with strong client relationships leaves the store?**
A. There is no risk; relationships transfer automatically
B. Institutional knowledge about top clients can be lost if it only ever lived in that associate's memory
C. Only the associate's personal career is affected
D. Client relationships are unaffected by staff changes
**Answer: B** - This is why Tier 1 reasoning should be documented in a shared CRM.

**8. What is the recommended structure for tiering a client book?**
A. Treat every client identically regardless of any signal
B. A three-tier structure with decreasing intensity of personal attention, while still maintaining outreach at every tier
C. Only the top 1% should receive any attention at all
D. Tiers should never be revisited once set
**Answer: B** - Lower tiers still receive consistent, well-timed outreach; they simply receive a different intensity of personal touch.

**9. What caution does Gogo Cheng raise about focusing only on the very top of a client book?**
A. There is no real risk to focusing exclusively on VICs
B. The relationship skills used on VICs are applicable to everyday customers too, and neglecting the broader book has become riskier as fewer new buyers arrive
C. Non-VIC clients should be actively discouraged from returning
D. VIC programs should be eliminated entirely
**Answer: B** - The smartest brands extend some of that same attentiveness more broadly, not just to the top tier.

**10. Why should client tiering be revisited periodically rather than set once?**
A. Tiering should never change once established
B. Purchase pace, engagement, and relationship potential shift over time, so a client's tier can and should change
C. Revisiting tiers is only necessary for very large stores
D. Once a client reaches Tier 1, they can never be reassessed
**Answer: B** - A static, one-time tiering exercise quickly becomes inaccurate as client behavior evolves.
