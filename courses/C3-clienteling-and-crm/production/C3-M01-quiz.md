# Module 1 Self-Assessment Quiz: The Client Profile — What to Capture, What to Leave Out

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: building an accurate client profile while avoiding assumptions and unnecessary data collection.

**1. Why does a client profile matter more in jewelry than in most retail categories?**
A. Jewelry clients never return to the same store
B. Jewelry purchases are tied to recurring life events (birthdays, anniversaries), making the same client a likely future buyer
C. Jewelry stores are legally required to keep detailed files
D. It doesn't matter more; all retail categories are the same
**Answer: B** - Recurring occasions make a remembered client far more valuable over time than a one-time anonymous buyer.

**2. Which of these is one of the seven core data fields recommended for a jewelry client profile?**
A. Social media follower count
B. Partner name and contact details
C. Political affiliation
D. Home security system details
**Answer: B** - Many jewelry purchases are for a partner who isn't present, making this detail highly useful for future outreach.

**3. When is a natural moment to ask a client for personal details like a birthday or anniversary date?**
A. Only during a formal annual customer survey
B. During an engagement-ring sizing, a repair drop-off/pickup, or right after a purchase
C. Only if the client brings it up first, unprompted
D. Never; personal details should not be requested
**Answer: B** - These are moments where the conversation is already happening naturally and the request doesn't feel intrusive.

**4. Why should you capture information about a client even if they don't make a purchase that visit?**
A. It's not worth capturing anything without a completed sale
B. What they looked at, liked, and who they're shopping for is valuable for future follow-up regardless of today's outcome
C. Only paying customers deserve a profile
D. It's required by law in all cases
**Answer: B** - A browser today can become a customer tomorrow if the right details support a later follow-up.

**5. What does INSTORE's "You're so cute together!" example illustrate?**
A. Complimenting clients always improves sales
B. The risk of making assumptions about a client's relationship or situation
C. All comments about relationships should be avoided entirely
D. Clients enjoy compliments about their appearance
**Answer: B** - An incorrect assumption, spoken aloud or written into a file, damages trust and can embarrass the client.

**6. What does the GDPR's data-minimization principle require, in general terms?**
A. Collecting as much data as possible for future use
B. Collecting only data that is adequate, relevant, and limited to what's necessary for a specific purpose
C. Avoiding all data collection entirely
D. Storing data indefinitely regardless of purpose
**Answer: B** - This principle applies well beyond GDPR jurisdictions as a practical, trust-building standard for any client file.

**7. Which of these notes is an example of a judgment that should NOT be recorded, versus useful information?**
A. "Anniversary in September; prefers yellow gold"
B. "Client probably can't really afford this piece"
C. "Ring size 6.5, taken during sizing on [date]"
D. "Prefers text over email"
**Answer: B** - This is a subjective judgment about means, not information the client shared or a fact needed to serve them.

**8. What should you do if a client declines to share a requested detail, like their birthday?**
A. Guess the detail from other information and record it anyway
B. Respect the decline, note only that it was declined, and don't push further in that visit
C. Ask again immediately using different wording
D. Refuse to continue helping the client
**Answer: B** - Respecting a decline preserves trust; the client's choice not to share is itself information worth respecting.

**9. Why is recording a client's purchase history with amounts useful, done correctly?**
A. It tells you roughly what a client is comfortable spending again, without implying judgment about their means
B. It should be used to publicly rank clients by wealth
C. It has no legitimate use in a jewelry client profile
D. It should only be recorded for the store's most expensive clients
**Answer: A** - Purchase history is objective information, useful for future recommendations, and distinct from a subjective judgment.

**10. What is "good data hygiene" for a client profile, beyond initial capture?**
A. Never updating a profile once it's created
B. Updating records at the point of contact, and periodically reviewing for accuracy and outdated information
C. Deleting all client data after each purchase
D. Only updating profiles once a year, regardless of new information
**Answer: B** - Accurate, current information is more valuable than a large but stale or inaccurate profile.
