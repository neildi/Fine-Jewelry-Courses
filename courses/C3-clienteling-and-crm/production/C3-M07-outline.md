# C3-M07 Outline: Winning Back a Lapsed Client

## Module Metadata
- Course: C3 - Clienteling & CRM
- Module: 7 of 8
- Persona: P2
- Working title: Winning Back a Lapsed Client
- Learning objective: Associates can segment lapsed clients by dormancy tier, draft respectful win-back outreach that reconnects on relationship before offering anything, and avoid discount-first messaging that erodes long-term value.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Re-engage a lapsed client respectfully, without sounding like a discount blast."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Positions win-back as cheaper/faster than new-client acquisition; contrasts relationship-based win-back vs. corporate discount blasts.
2. **What "Lapsed" Actually Means — and Why It's Not One Category** — Three dormancy tiers: recently quiet (3-6 mo), genuinely lapsed (6-18 mo), long-dormant (18+ mo), each requiring a different tone.
3. **The Win-Back Framework: Reconnect Before You Reoffer** — Five-step sequence: Reconnect, Acknowledge, Offer, Ask, Leave the door open.
4. **Why Discounting First Backfires** — Three specific costs: signals desperation, trains bargain-hunting, skips relationship-rebuilding.
5. **Reading the Response — and the Non-Response** — Guidance for warm response, polite non-committal response, no response, and explicit opt-out requests.
6. **Connecting Win-Back to the Rest of Your Clienteling Practice** — Ties back to Module 6's Four-Part Test and forward to Module 8's referral engine.
7. **Sample Win-Back Messages by Situation** — Five worked examples across all three dormancy tiers plus response-handling examples.
8. **Common Mistakes in Win-Back Outreach** — Over-referencing the gap, assuming static taste, escalating pressure, forgetting CRM logging.
9. **Metrics Worth Tracking** — Reactivation rate, time-to-reactivation, reactivated client lifetime value; ties to course KPI link.
10. **Practice Exercise** — Draft three win-back messages, one per dormancy tier, using the five-step framework.
11. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Ties to Prior Modules

- Reuses the Module 6 Four-Part Test (specificity, reason to exist, value before ask, easy exit) as the underlying quality check for win-back messages.

## Production Notes
- Keep dormancy-tier framing central — this is the module's primary new mental model, distinct from segmentation (Module 4) which is about value/priority, not time-since-contact.
- Avoid recommending specific discount percentages or promotional mechanics; keep the discount-timing guidance principle-based (mention only after relationship touch, if at all).
- Persona P2 skews toward associates managing larger, more established books — content should assume some existing lapsed-client volume, not a brand-new associate with few contacts yet.

## Assessment Alignment
- Quiz items should test: correctly identifying dormancy tier from a scenario, sequencing the five-step win-back framework, recognizing discount-first mistakes, and choosing appropriate response-handling per the non-response guidance.
- Huddle card should focus on the three dormancy tiers and the "reconnect before you reoffer" principle as the most portable takeaways.
- Factcheck should verify: no specific discount percentages or legal claims are asserted, metrics guidance is framed as general best practice, and terminology stays consistent with Module 4 (segmentation) and Module 6 (Four-Part Test).
