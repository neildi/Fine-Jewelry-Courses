# Module 3 Self-Assessment Quiz: Getting the Most from Your CRM

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: running a consistent daily CRM habit and a weekly hygiene review.

**1. What does a daily "what has today already promised" check cover?**
A. Only the store's total sales for the day
B. Repairs/orders due today, today's appointments (with client files reviewed), and follow-ups due today
C. Only new client sign-ups
D. Nothing; this check isn't necessary
**Answer: B** - This prevents the worst kind of moment: a client expecting something you didn't know you owed them.

**2. According to WJewel's guidance, when should a client detail (price, date, size, name) be logged into the CRM?**
A. At the end of the week
B. Before the associate serves the next person
C. Only if the client makes a purchase
D. Only once a month during a review
**Answer: B** - Logging in real time prevents details from being lost to memory.

**3. What does "recorded wish lists get followed up; the ones in a notebook do not" mean?**
A. Notebooks are more reliable than CRMs
B. Information that isn't in the shared system effectively doesn't exist for future follow-up
C. Wish lists should never be recorded at all
D. Only paper records should be trusted
**Answer: B** - If a detail lives only in one person's private notes, it won't get acted on by the team or the system.

**4. What should a short daily team huddle cover?**
A. A full hour-long meeting about store strategy
B. Yesterday's number, today's expected clients, one piece worth highlighting, and any blockers
C. Only disciplinary issues
D. Nothing; huddles are unnecessary
**Answer: B** - A brief, consistent huddle keeps the whole team aligned without taking much time.

**5. What is the 14-day rule mentioned in this module's weekly review?**
A. Clients must be contacted every 14 days regardless of context
B. Any open thread with no activity for 14+ days or more should be flagged and given one of three treatments
C. CRMs should be wiped clean every 14 days
D. New clients have 14 days to make a purchase or be deleted
**Answer: B** - This catches quietly stalling threads before they become fully lost opportunities.

**6. What are the three treatments for a flagged, stale client thread?**
A. Ignore it, delete it, or escalate it to a manager
B. Follow up now, schedule a specific future follow-up, or close it out
C. Always close it immediately
D. Always follow up immediately regardless of context
**Answer: B** - Each stale thread gets a deliberate decision rather than being left in limbo.

**7. Why is "follow up with Sarah" considered a weak reminder compared to a more specific one?**
A. It's not weak; general reminders work just as well
B. Vague reminders lack a clear next action and tend not to get done effectively
C. Reminders should never include a client's name
D. Specific reminders take too much time to write
**Answer: B** - A reminder like "send Sarah photos and ask about her anniversary date" is far more actionable.

**8. What does Gem Logic's quote about "shops that look effortless" emphasize?**
A. Exceptional individual talent is required to run a good store
B. Consistent, ordinary daily habits, not heroic effort, are what make a store run smoothly
C. Effortless-looking stores don't actually have any real systems
D. Only large stores can achieve this level of organization
**Answer: B** - Small, consistent habits compound into a well-run operation over time.

**9. What happens to a client's information if it only ever lives in one associate's memory?**
A. Nothing changes; memory is just as reliable as a written record
B. It doesn't transfer to colleagues, and it's lost entirely if that associate is unavailable
C. It automatically becomes part of the CRM eventually
D. Client information should only ever live in memory for privacy reasons
**Answer: B** - This is exactly the failure mode the daily and weekly habits in this module are designed to prevent.

**10. What is the main difference between the daily and weekly CRM routines in this module?**
A. There is no meaningful difference between them
B. Daily routines handle today's specific commitments; weekly routines catch broader drift like stale threads and incomplete records
C. Weekly routines replace the need for any daily habit
D. Daily routines are only for managers, and weekly routines are only for associates
**Answer: B** - Both are necessary; one prevents today's surprises, the other prevents slow, silent decay in the client book.
