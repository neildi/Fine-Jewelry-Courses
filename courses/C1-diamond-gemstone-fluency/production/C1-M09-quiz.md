# Module 9 Self-Assessment Quiz: Treatment and Origin Disclosure

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: applying FTC-style disclosure rules correctly across diamonds, colored stones, and pearls.

**1. What are the FTC's three disclosure triggers for a gemstone treatment?**
A. Price, weight, and shape
B. Not permanent, requires special care, or significantly affects value
C. Country of origin, lab name, and report number
D. Color, clarity, and cut
**Answer: B** - If a treatment meets any one of these three criteria, it must be disclosed under the FTC Jewelry Guides.

**2. Under what federal statute are the FTC Jewelry Guides ultimately enforced?**
A. The Sherman Antitrust Act
B. Section 5 of the FTC Act (15 U.S.C. § 45), prohibiting unfair or deceptive acts or practices
C. The Lanham Act
D. There is no federal enforcement mechanism
**Answer: B** - The Jewelry Guides are interpretive guidance enforced through Section 5's broad prohibition on unfair or deceptive commercial practices.

**3. Roughly how large can civil penalties be for a company that knowingly violates an existing FTC order?**
A. There are no penalties at all
B. Up to approximately $43,792 per violation
C. A flat $100 fine
D. Penalties only apply to individuals, never companies
**Answer: B** - The FTC can pursue civil penalties up to roughly $43,792 per violation for knowing violations of an existing order or an already-identified unfair/deceptive practice.

**4. Why is heat-treated ruby or sapphire commonly disclosed even though it may not strictly trigger the mandatory FTC test?**
A. It is a marketing gimmick
B. It is standard, expected trade practice since the vast majority of rubies and sapphires are heat-treated and clients increasingly ask
C. Heat treatment is illegal without disclosure
D. It has no bearing on trade practice
**Answer: B** - While heat treatment is generally stable, permanent, and may not strictly trigger mandatory disclosure, disclosing it anyway is standard, expected practice given how common the treatment is.

**5. Approximately how much can a confirmed Kashmir origin add to a fine sapphire's value, based on real auction examples?**
A. No meaningful difference
B. Potentially into the hundreds of thousands to millions of dollars for exceptional examples, compared to unconfirmed-origin stones
C. Exactly double the price, always
D. Kashmir origin actually decreases value
**Answer: B** - Real auction results (e.g., a 35.09 ct Kashmir sapphire estimated at $8.3-12 million) demonstrate that confirmed rare geographic origin can be one of the largest value multipliers in the colored-stone trade.

**6. What is the Kimberley Process?**
A. A gemstone treatment method
B. An international certification scheme, launched in 2003, to prevent conflict diamonds from entering the legitimate diamond trade
C. A diamond cut-grading system
D. A pearl grading standard
**Answer: B** - The Kimberley Process is an intergovernmental initiative requiring valid certificates for rough diamond shipments to confirm they are conflict-free.

**7. How does the Kimberley Process differ from a colored-stone geographic origin claim like "Kashmir sapphire"?**
A. They are the same thing
B. The Kimberley Process is a conflict-free sourcing certification for rough diamonds; colored-stone origin claims are value-driving provenance statements verified by gemological labs
C. The Kimberley Process only applies to colored stones
D. Origin claims for colored stones are legally required on every report
**Answer: B** - These are related but distinct compliance concepts: one addresses ethical sourcing/conflict prevention for diamonds, the other addresses documented geographic provenance affecting colored-stone value.

**8. A client asks if an emerald has been treated. What is the best response?**
A. Deny any treatment to avoid a difficult conversation
B. Confirm the oiling treatment plainly, explain it's standard trade practice, and give specific care guidance
C. Refuse to answer
D. Change the subject to price
**Answer: B** - Disclosure should be delivered as plain, confident information, naming the treatment, normalizing it as standard practice, and following with practical care guidance.

**9. Can an associate state a specific geographic origin for a stone based on visual appearance alone?**
A. Yes, an experienced associate can determine origin by eye
B. No, origin can only be confirmed through laboratory documentation from a credentialed source
C. Only for diamonds, not colored stones
D. Origin claims never require any documentation
**Answer: B** - Origin is not visually determinable; an associate should never state a specific geographic origin without lab documentation, given the significant legal and financial stakes involved.

**10. Why does disclosure tone matter as much as disclosure content?**
A. It doesn't; only the words used matter
B. Delivering disclosure as plain, confident information (rather than a nervous confession) builds trust and supports the sale rather than undermining it
C. Tone is irrelevant to compliance
D. Associates should always apologize when disclosing a treatment
**Answer: B** - A defensive or apologetic tone can make a standard, legitimate treatment sound like a hidden flaw, while calm, confident disclosure reinforces trust and professionalism.
