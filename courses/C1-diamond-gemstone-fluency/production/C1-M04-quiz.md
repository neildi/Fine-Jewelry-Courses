# Module 4 Self-Assessment Quiz: Lab-Grown, Natural, Simulant

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: delivering a compliant, non-defensive disclosure across all three stone categories.

**1. What is the fundamental difference between a lab-grown diamond and a diamond simulant?**
A. Lab-grown diamonds are cheaper
B. A lab-grown diamond is chemically real diamond; a simulant is a different material entirely
C. Simulants are always synthetic sapphire
D. There is no meaningful difference
**Answer: B** - Lab-grown diamonds share identical chemistry, optics, and physical properties with natural diamonds; simulants like moissanite and cubic zirconia are entirely different materials that merely resemble diamond visually.

**2. Name the two primary lab-grown diamond production methods.**
A. Heating and cooling
B. HPHT and CVD
C. Irradiation and annealing
D. Fracture filling and coating
**Answer: B** - High-Pressure High-Temperature (HPHT) and Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD) are the two established methods for growing gem-quality diamond in a lab.

**3. Which growth method typically takes just a few weeks, versus a few months for the other?**
A. HPHT takes weeks; CVD takes months
B. CVD typically takes weeks; HPHT can take a few months
C. Both take the same amount of time
D. Neither has a defined growth time
**Answer: B** - CVD generally grows a diamond in a few weeks due to its layer-by-layer deposition process, while HPHT, replicating high-pressure natural conditions, can take a few months to reach a desired size.

**4. What is the FTC's core disclosure requirement for lab-grown diamonds at point of sale?**
A. Disclosure is optional if the price is clearly lower
B. The stone must be disclosed as laboratory-grown, laboratory-created, or an equivalent qualifier immediately before the word "diamond"
C. Disclosure is only required for stones over 1 carat
D. Only online retailers must disclose, not in-store sales
**Answer: B** - The FTC's Jewelry Guides require clear and conspicuous disclosure, using an approved qualifier positioned immediately before the word "diamond," regardless of sales channel or stone size.

**5. What happened in April 2019 regarding lab-grown diamond disclosure?**
A. The FTC banned lab-grown diamonds entirely
B. The FTC sent formal warning letters to eight companies over inadequate disclosure
C. GIA stopped grading lab-grown diamonds
D. AGS introduced a new lab-grown scale
**Answer: B** - The FTC sent warning letters to eight companies advertising simulated or lab-created diamonds without adequately disclosing they were not mined, establishing real enforcement precedent for this rule.

**6. How does the trade actually distinguish lab-grown from natural diamonds at scale, since they look identical to the eye?**
A. It cannot be done reliably at all
B. Dedicated screening equipment, often using short-wave UV light and detecting specific nitrogen-related features, is used across the trade
C. Only a jeweler's loupe is needed
D. Fluorescence alone reliably distinguishes them
**Answer: B** - Approximately 40 different screening instruments exist in the trade, many using short-wave UV light to detect characteristics like specific nitrogen-related features that differ between natural and lab-grown material.

**7. What should appear as the receipt line-item description for a lab-grown diamond sale?**
A. Just "diamond"
B. "Laboratory-grown diamond" plus carat weight, certifying lab, and report number
C. A generic SKU number only
D. The word "synthetic"
**Answer: B** - The qualifier must appear as the actual line-item description, not buried in a footnote, alongside the carat weight, certifying laboratory, and report number.

**8. A client asks if disclosure rules only apply in the US. What's the accurate answer?**
A. Yes, disclosure is a purely American requirement
B. No, major markets including the US, EU, and India all require some form of clear lab-grown disclosure, though the legal mechanisms differ
C. Disclosure rules only apply to online sales
D. Only GIA-graded stones require disclosure
**Answer: B** - Clear disclosure expectations exist across major jurisdictions, even though the specific legal citation and enforcement mechanism (such as the US FTC Act Section 5) differ by country.

**9. Why is moissanite's "rainbow flash" sometimes cited online as a way to detect a "fake diamond"?**
A. It is not a real optical property
B. Moissanite's higher refractive index (2.65) causes more colorful fire than a diamond's, which is a real distinguishing trait, not a flaw
C. It only appears in cubic zirconia
D. It indicates the moissanite is defective
**Answer: B** - Moissanite's refractive index is higher than diamond's, producing more colorful flashes of dispersed light, an accurate physical distinguishing trait rather than a defect or marketing myth.

**10. A CVD-grown diamond shows evidence of a post-growth HPHT treatment on its report. What does this mean?**
A. The stone is fraudulent
B. This is a normal, disclosed practice where HPHT treatment is used after CVD growth to improve or stabilize color
C. The stone is actually natural
D. This voids the report entirely
**Answer: B** - It is an established, legitimate practice for some CVD-grown stones to receive a post-growth HPHT treatment specifically to enhance or stabilize color, and GIA notes this treatment on the report rather than treating it as a defect or fraud.
