# Module 6 Self-Assessment Quiz: The Colored Stone Top 10

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: identifying the ten stones' hardness, treatments, and care requirements.

**1. What is the Mohs hardness of sapphire and ruby?**
A. 6
B. 7.5
C. 9
D. 10
**Answer: C** - Both are corundum varieties with a Mohs hardness of 9, second only to diamond.

**2. Why does emerald's 7.5-8 Mohs hardness understate its real-world fragility?**
A. It doesn't; emerald is actually very durable
B. Emeralds commonly contain internal fractures (jardin) that make them more fragile in practice than the hardness number suggests
C. Emerald is actually softer than 7.5
D. Emeralds are never treated
**Answer: B** - The hardness number measures scratch resistance only; emerald's typical internal fracturing (jardin) creates a real fragility risk hardness alone doesn't capture.

**3. What do the F1, F2, and F3 grades on an emerald report indicate?**
A. The stone's color intensity
B. The degree of clarity enhancement (oiling): minor, moderate, and significant respectively
C. The stone's country of origin
D. The stone's cut quality
**Answer: B** - GIA's emerald clarity-enhancement scale grades the visual impact of oil/resin filling as minor (F1), moderate (F2), or significant (F3).

**4. Where is tanzanite found?**
A. Multiple locations across East Africa
B. Only in a single small area near Merelani, Tanzania
C. Primarily in Sri Lanka
D. It is a lab-grown material with no natural source
**Answer: B** - Tanzanite has exactly one commercial source worldwide: a roughly 7-square-kilometer area near Merelani, Tanzania, making it geographically among the rarest gemstones.

**5. What does it mean that tanzanite is "trichroic"?**
A. It has three different hardness levels
B. The crystal shows three distinct colors depending on the viewing angle
C. It can only be cut into three shapes
D. It requires three separate treatments
**Answer: B** - Trichroism (a form of pleochroism) means the rough crystal displays three different colors along three different axes, commonly blue, violet, and a reddish-brown/burgundy tone before heat treatment.

**6. What does heat treatment typically do to tanzanite?**
A. Nothing; tanzanite is never heat treated
B. It removes the brownish pleochroic axis, leaving the stone showing a consistent blue-to-violet color
C. It changes the stone's hardness
D. It makes the stone trichroic
**Answer: B** - Nearly all commercial tanzanite is heat treated to eliminate the brownish axis, revealing the well-known, market-desired blue-violet color consistently.

**7. What is beryllium diffusion?**
A. A cleaning method for sapphires
B. A high-temperature treatment (over 1,800°C) that diffuses beryllium into corundum, capable of creating or enhancing certain colors like vivid orange and yellow
C. A method for measuring sapphire hardness
D. A synonym for basic heat treatment
**Answer: B** - Beryllium diffusion is a more advanced corundum treatment than simple heat treatment, using extreme temperatures to diffuse beryllium into the crystal lattice and produce specific color effects.

**8. Why does beryllium diffusion require disclosure?**
A. It doesn't; it's identical to standard heat treatment
B. It significantly affects the stone's color and value, meeting the FTC's disclosure trigger
C. Only lab-grown stones require disclosure
D. Disclosure is optional for corundum specifically
**Answer: B** - Because beryllium diffusion can create or dramatically enhance color, it meets the "significantly affects value" trigger under FTC disclosure rules, and the trade formally committed to disclosure norms following a documented history of controversy.

**9. Which two cleaning methods should always be avoided on an oiled emerald?**
A. Warm water and a soft cloth
B. Ultrasonic and steam cleaning
C. Any form of cleaning at all
D. Only ultrasonic cleaning needs to be avoided
**Answer: B** - Ultrasonic vibrations can extend fractures and dislodge oil, while steam heat can cause the oil to sweat out; both should be avoided in favor of warm soapy water and a soft cloth.

**10. Why might a citrine stone actually be heat-treated amethyst?**
A. This is a common industry deception
B. Heat treatment of amethyst to produce citrine's yellow-orange color is a normal, disclosed industry practice
C. Amethyst and citrine are chemically unrelated
D. This only happens with lab-grown quartz
**Answer: B** - Amethyst and citrine are both quartz varieties, and heating amethyst to produce citrine's color is standard, disclosed trade practice, not a deceptive substitution.
