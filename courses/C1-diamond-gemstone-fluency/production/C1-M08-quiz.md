# Module 8 Self-Assessment Quiz: Metals, Marks and Alloys

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: explaining metal trade-offs and reading quality marks correctly.

**1. What percentage of pure gold is in 14K gold?**
A. 41.7%
B. 58.3%
C. 75.0%
D. 91.6%
**Answer: B** - 14K represents 14 out of 24 parts pure gold, or approximately 58.3%.

**2. Why might 14K be a better choice than 18K for an everyday ring?**
A. 14K is always cheaper regardless of durability
B. 14K contains more alloy metal, making it more scratch- and dent-resistant for daily wear
C. 14K is actually purer than 18K
D. There is no meaningful difference between them
**Answer: B** - Lower karat gold contains more non-gold alloy metal, which increases hardness and scratch resistance, making it a practical choice for pieces worn daily.

**3. Why is white gold not naturally bright white?**
A. It always contains a blue dye
B. Pure gold is yellow, and mixing it with white alloy metals produces a pale, creamy off-white, not a true bright white
C. White gold is a completely different metal from yellow gold
D. It only appears white under specific lighting
**Answer: B** - White gold is still a gold alloy; blending gold with white metals like palladium, nickel, or silver produces a pale off-white base color, not the bright white achieved through rhodium plating.

**4. What does rhodium plating on white gold actually do?**
A. Nothing functional; it is purely decorative dye
B. It provides the bright white color and adds a hard, protective, scratch-resistant surface layer
C. It changes the karat of the gold underneath
D. It permanently prevents any color change forever
**Answer: B** - Rhodium plating both brightens white gold's appearance and adds durability and a protective barrier, though the layer wears down over time and requires periodic reapplication.

**5. What is the FTC's legal definition of gold vermeil?**
A. Any gold-colored jewelry regardless of construction
B. Gold electroplated over sterling silver, with a minimum 10-karat gold layer at least 2.5 microns thick
C. Solid 24K gold
D. Gold-plated brass with no minimum thickness requirement
**Answer: B** - Vermeil has a specific FTC legal definition requiring a sterling silver base, at least 10-karat gold, and a minimum 2.5-micron gold layer thickness, distinguishing it from unregulated gold-plated jewelry.

**6. How does gold-filled construction differ from gold-plated?**
A. They are identical terms for the same process
B. Gold-filled uses a thicker gold layer mechanically bonded to a base metal; gold-plated uses a much thinner electroplated layer
C. Gold-filled contains no actual gold
D. Gold-plated always lasts longer than gold-filled
**Answer: B** - Gold-filled jewelry uses a substantially thicker gold layer (commonly around 5% of total weight) mechanically bonded via heat and pressure, making it more durable than the thin electroplated layer used in standard gold-plated jewelry.

**7. What minimum platinum percentage is required for an unqualified platinum mark?**
A. 50%
B. 75%
C. 95%
D. 100%
**Answer: C** - An unqualified platinum mark generally requires at least 95% pure platinum, or a qualifying alloy with iridium or ruthenium at that purity level.

**8. What does the EU Nickel Directive regulate?**
A. The karat purity of gold jewelry
B. The rate of nickel release from items intended for prolonged skin contact, such as earrings and rings
C. Platinum marking requirements
D. Hallmarking assay office procedures
**Answer: B** - The EU Nickel Directive (now part of REACH) sets specific nickel release limits (0.5 micrograms/cm²/week for most jewelry, 0.2 for piercing posts) to protect against nickel allergy reactions from prolonged skin contact.

**9. Why do many modern white gold alloys use palladium instead of nickel?**
A. Palladium is always cheaper than nickel
B. Palladium reduces the risk of allergic skin reactions compared to nickel-containing alloys
C. Palladium changes the karat rating
D. There is no meaningful reason; it is purely a marketing choice
**Answer: B** - Nickel is a common contact allergen, so many white gold formulations use palladium instead specifically to reduce the risk of skin irritation for sensitive wearers.

**10. What are the three compulsory elements of a UK hallmark?**
A. Karat number, price, and store name
B. Sponsor's/maker's mark, metal and fineness mark, and assay office mark
C. Country of origin, weight, and date only
D. UK hallmarks only require a single karat stamp
**Answer: B** - A full UK hallmark requires the sponsor's or maker's mark, the metal and fineness mark, and the assay office mark identifying the independent testing body, making it a stricter system than the US trademark-plus-karat-stamp approach.
