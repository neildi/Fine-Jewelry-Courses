# Module 3 Self-Assessment Quiz: AGS vs. GIA Nomenclature

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: converting between AGS and GIA grading systems accurately in a live conversation.

**1. On the AGS 0-10 scale, which number represents the best grade?**
A. 10
B. 0
C. 5
D. It varies by category
**Answer: B** - AGS uses 0 as the highest possible grade and 10 as the lowest, consistently across cut, color, and clarity.

**2. How many cut grades does AGS assign compared to GIA?**
A. AGS has 5, GIA has 11
B. AGS has 11, GIA has 5
C. Both have 5
D. Both have 11
**Answer: B** - AGS assigns eleven distinct cut grades (0-10) while GIA assigns five (Excellent through Poor), meaning AGS draws finer distinctions.

**3. What is ASET, and what does it measure?**
A. A chemical test for diamond authenticity
B. A color-coded light-performance viewing tool showing where light returns to the eye versus leaks
C. An alternate name for the GIA report
D. A carat-weight measuring device
**Answer: B** - ASET (Angular Spectrum Evaluation Tool/Technology) is AGS's light-performance device, using color coding (red/green for return, white/black for leakage) to map how a diamond handles light.

**4. What does "AGS Triple Zero" actually mean?**
A. An official single grade AGS prints on every top-tier report
B. A trade-slang term for a stone scoring 0 in cut, color, and clarity simultaneously
C. A stone with three separate AGS reports
D. The same thing as GIA's Triple Excellent
**Answer: B** - Triple Zero is informal trade terminology describing three separate zero grades achieved together, not an official single designation stamped on the report.

**5. Is GIA's "Triple Excellent" the same tier as AGS's "Triple Zero"?**
A. Yes, they are interchangeable terms
B. No, they measure different category combinations (craftsmanship vs. cut/color/clarity)
C. No, because GIA doesn't have a top tier
D. Yes, but only for round brilliants
**Answer: B** - GIA Triple Excellent covers cut, polish, and symmetry; AGS Triple Zero covers cut, color, and clarity. They are both "top tier" concepts but not measuring the same three things.

**6. What happened between GIA and AGS in 2022?**
A. AGS stopped grading diamonds entirely
B. GIA acquired AGS's light-performance technology and began offering it as a supplemental report
C. The two labs merged into one organization
D. GIA adopted the 0-10 scale
**Answer: B** - GIA began offering the AGS Ideal Report as a digital supplement to its own grading reports, incorporating AGS's light-performance methodology while keeping both labs' core grading systems separate.

**7. A client asks for an exact number-to-letter conversion between AGS and GIA grades. What should you do?**
A. Provide a precise conversion table from memory
B. Explain that no official crosswalk exists, give a general directional comparison, and offer to compare the physical stones
C. Refuse to discuss the other lab's grading at all
D. Tell the client AGS grades don't matter
**Answer: B** - Neither lab publishes an official conversion table; the honest approach is directional language plus a physical stone comparison, not an invented precise conversion.

**8. Which region is AGS's grading and membership network most concentrated in?**
A. India and the Gulf
B. The United Kingdom
C. The United States
D. Equally global with GIA
**Answer: C** - AGS is a predominantly US-focused laboratory and guild membership organization; GIA has substantially broader international reach.

**9. What is the key difference between the Ideal-Scope and the ASET?**
A. They measure completely unrelated things
B. The Ideal-Scope uses a simpler two-color system (red/black) while ASET uses three colors for more detailed analysis (red/green/blue)
C. ASET is used only by GIA
D. The Ideal-Scope is more detailed than ASET
**Answer: B** - The Ideal-Scope is a simpler reflector tool with two-color coding, while AGS's ASET separates direct and reflected light into two additional colors for a more detailed read.

**10. Who founded AGS, and what is that person's connection to GIA?**
A. Robert M. Shipley, who also founded GIA three years earlier
B. AGS and GIA had no founding connection
C. GIA founded AGS as a subsidiary
D. AGS was founded by a former GIA employee with no other connection
**Answer: A** - Robert M. Shipley founded GIA in 1931 and AGS in 1934, making the two organizations historically connected through their shared founder despite being separate entities today.
