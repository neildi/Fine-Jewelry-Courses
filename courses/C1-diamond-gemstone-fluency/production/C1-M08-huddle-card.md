# Huddle Card: Metals, Marks and Alloys

**Headline:** White gold isn't naturally white, gold-plated isn't vermeil, and karat isn't a quality hierarchy, it's a durability trade-off.

**3 key points:**
1. 14K vs. 18K is not "better vs. worse," it's purity vs. durability. 14K resists scratches better for everyday wear.
2. White gold is naturally pale yellow. Rhodium plating makes it bright white AND protects it, but wears off over time, that's why re-plating exists.
3. Vermeil = gold over STERLING SILVER with a legal minimum thickness. Gold-plated = thin gold over brass, no legal minimum. Not the same thing.

**Script to practice today:**
"White gold is naturally a pale off-white, not bright white. That bright, reflective look comes from a rhodium coating on top, which wears down with normal wear and gets refreshed with a simple re-plating service."

**Question to ask the team:**
Do we know, off the top of our heads, which pieces in our case are vermeil vs. gold-filled vs. gold-plated, and can we explain the difference to a client?
