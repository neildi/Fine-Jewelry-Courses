# Module 7 Self-Assessment Quiz: Pearls, Types, Grading and Care

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: selling a pearl strand confidently using the seven value factors.

**1. What is the fundamental difference between a natural pearl and a cultured pearl?**
A. Cultured pearls are artificial/fake
B. Both form through the same biological nacre-secretion process; natural pearls start by accident, cultured pearls start when a technician deliberately inserts an irritant
C. Natural pearls are made of a different material
D. Cultured pearls are always round; natural pearls never are
**Answer: B** - Both types are genuine, biologically formed pearls; the only difference is whether the initiating irritant occurred naturally or was deliberately introduced by a person.

**2. Who is credited with creating the first cultured pearl, and when?**
A. Kokichi Mikimoto, 1893
B. GIA, 1931
C. Robert Shipley, 1934
D. An unknown French jeweler in the 1700s
**Answer: A** - Kokichi Mikimoto successfully produced the first hemispherical cultured pearls on July 11, 1893, founding the modern cultured pearl industry.

**3. What is the key mechanical difference between bead nucleation and tissue nucleation?**
A. They produce chemically different materials
B. Bead nucleation inserts a round bead plus tissue; tissue nucleation uses only a tissue graft with no bead
C. Tissue nucleation is only used for diamonds
D. There is no meaningful difference
**Answer: B** - Bead nucleation uses a round bead (often mother-of-pearl) plus donor tissue, providing a round template; tissue nucleation uses only tissue, with no bead core.

**4. Why are most freshwater cultured pearls not perfectly round?**
A. Freshwater mussels are defective
B. They are typically tissue-nucleated, without a round bead template to guide the shape
C. Freshwater pearls are always fake
D. They are intentionally cut into irregular shapes
**Answer: B** - Without a bead core, tissue-nucleated freshwater pearls develop more organically, resulting in oval, button, drop, or baroque shapes more often than round.

**5. List the seven GIA pearl value factors.**
A. Carat, cut, color, clarity, polish, symmetry, fluorescence
B. Size, shape, color, luster, surface quality, nacre quality, matching
C. Hardness, treatment, origin, cut, clarity, color, carat
D. Mollusk type, farm location, harvest date, size, price, grade, certificate
**Answer: B** - These seven factors are pearls' equivalent of the diamond 4Cs, and every price difference between two strands traces back to one or more of them.

**6. What is the regulated minimum average nacre thickness for Tahitian cultured pearls?**
A. 0.1mm
B. 0.8mm
C. 5.0mm
D. There is no regulated minimum
**Answer: B** - Tahitian cultured pearls are required by regulation to carry a minimum average nacre thickness of 0.8mm, one of the few pearl grading points backed by a specific regulatory number.

**7. How does a gemological lab actually verify whether a pearl is natural or cultured?**
A. By visual inspection alone
B. Through X-ray radiography showing internal structure, plus trace-element analysis (EDXRF)
C. By weighing the pearl
D. There is no reliable scientific method
**Answer: B** - X-ray radiography reveals internal growth structure (natural pearls show concentric rings with no solid nucleus; bead-cultured pearls show a central bead core), and EDXRF trace-element testing helps confirm freshwater vs. saltwater origin.

**8. Why are pearl grading scales like "AAA" not directly comparable between two different retailers?**
A. They are actually standardized industry-wide
B. There is no single governing body enforcing what letter grades mean, so each retailer's scale is its own internal system
C. AAA always means the same thing everywhere
D. Only GIA-certified pearls carry letter grades
**Answer: B** - Unlike diamond grading, pearl letter-grade scales are retailer-specific rather than government- or single-body-regulated, so associates should fall back on the seven underlying value factors for accurate comparisons.

**9. What are conch and melo pearls, and why are they unusual?**
A. Common cultured pearl types sold in most jewelry stores
B. Rare, natural, non-nacreous pearls that cannot be commercially cultured
C. A brand name for Akoya pearls
D. Synthetic pearl imitations
**Answer: B** - Conch and melo pearls form naturally in non-nacre-producing mollusks, have a distinctive porcelain-like surface, and have no commercially viable culturing method, making them exceptionally rare and typically seen only in high-end estate or auction contexts.

**10. What should a client do with pearls regarding perfume, hairspray, and lotion?**
A. Apply these products after putting the pearls on
B. Apply these products before putting pearls on, since they can dull nacre luster over time
C. These products have no effect on pearls
D. Only avoid hairspray; perfume and lotion are fine anytime
**Answer: B** - Perfume, hairspray, and lotion should be applied before pearls go on, since they can dull the nacre's luster with repeated exposure over time.
