# Module 5 Self-Assessment Quiz: Fluorescence, Inclusions

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: reframing fluorescence and inclusions as identifying characteristics rather than defects.

**1. Which of the following is NOT one of the five GIA fluorescence intensity grades?**
A. Faint
B. Medium
C. Excellent
D. Very Strong
**Answer: C** - GIA's five fluorescence grades are None, Faint, Medium, Strong, and Very Strong; "Excellent" is a cut grade term, not a fluorescence grade.

**2. What physical structure causes blue fluorescence in the vast majority of fluorescent diamonds?**
A. Boron atoms randomly distributed
B. The N3 defect, three nitrogen atoms surrounding a vacancy in the crystal lattice
C. A crack in the diamond's surface
D. Excess carbon atoms
**Answer: B** - GIA's own peer-reviewed research identifies the N3 defect (three nitrogen atoms around a missing carbon atom) as the cause of blue fluorescence in the large majority of fluorescent diamonds.

**3. What percentage of diamonds submitted to GIA show a negative hazy or oily appearance from fluorescence?**
A. About 25-35%
B. Fewer than 0.2%
C. About 50%
D. Nearly 100%
**Answer: B** - GIA's own studies found this negative appearance in fewer than 0.2 percent of fluorescent diamonds, making it a rare exception rather than a common outcome.

**4. What is the key difference between fluorescence and phosphorescence?**
A. They are the same phenomenon with different names
B. Fluorescence only occurs during UV exposure; phosphorescence persists as an afterglow after the UV source is removed
C. Fluorescence is rarer than phosphorescence
D. Phosphorescence is graded on GIA reports; fluorescence is not
**Answer: B** - Fluorescence stops immediately when the UV light is removed, while phosphorescence continues as an afterglow, sometimes for several minutes; phosphorescence is also far rarer (under 1% of diamonds) and is not listed on standard GIA reports.

**5. Why can strong blue fluorescence sometimes make a lower-color-grade diamond appear whiter in daylight?**
A. It permanently changes the color grade
B. The blue fluorescence visually offsets some of the diamond's yellow tint under UV-rich lighting
C. It has no effect on appearance at all
D. It only happens in lab-grown diamonds
**Answer: B** - Under normal, UV-rich lighting conditions, blue fluorescence can visually counteract yellow body color, making a warmer-graded stone appear whiter than the grade alone would suggest, per GIA's own studies.

**6. Which fluorescence color is most likely to intensify, rather than offset, a diamond's body color?**
A. Blue
B. Yellow or orange
C. White
D. All colors behave identically
**Answer: B** - Unlike blue fluorescence, yellow or orangy fluorescence tends to intensify existing body color rather than counteract it, which is why jewelers often flag this combination for clients specifically buying near-colorless white diamonds.

**7. Is phosphorescence a reliable, standalone way to prove a diamond is lab-grown?**
A. Yes, it is a definitive test
B. No, it is one data point gemologists may consider, not a standalone proof, since it is more commonly seen in some lab-grown diamonds but not exclusive to them
C. Phosphorescence never occurs in lab-grown diamonds
D. Only fluorescence, not phosphorescence, is ever relevant to origin
**Answer: B** - While phosphorescence is more commonly observed in certain HPHT lab-grown diamonds, it is not a definitive, standalone proof of origin and should be treated as one consideration among several scientific methods.

**8. Why is an inclusion considered evidence that a diamond is natural?**
A. Lab-grown diamonds never have inclusions
B. Natural inclusions are a byproduct of geologic formation and help identify a specific stone, similar to a fingerprint
C. Inclusions only appear in low-clarity diamonds
D. Inclusions indicate the diamond has been treated
**Answer: B** - Inclusions form as part of a diamond's natural crystallization process and make each stone's plot unique, which is why GIA describes them as identifying rather than purely negative features.

**9. A client asks if their inclusion will "get worse" over time. What is accurate?**
A. Yes, inclusions can grow
B. No, inclusions are permanent features formed during the diamond's original crystallization and do not change or worsen over time
C. Only surface-reaching inclusions can worsen
D. It depends on the setting metal
**Answer: B** - Inclusions are fixed, permanent characteristics from the diamond's formation; they do not grow, spread, or change after the stone is cut and polished.

**10. Roughly what fraction of all diamonds exhibit fluorescence?**
A. Nearly 100%
B. Roughly a quarter to a third
C. Less than 1%
D. Exactly half
**Answer: B** - Approximately 25-35% of diamonds submitted to GIA exhibit some level of fluorescence, making it a common, not rare, identifying characteristic.
