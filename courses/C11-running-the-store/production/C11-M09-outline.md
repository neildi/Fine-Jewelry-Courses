# C11-M09 Outline: Compliance Calendar: Insurance, Records, Filings

## Module Metadata
- Course: C11 - Running the Store
- Module: 9 of 10
- Persona: P1/P7 (First-Time Store Leader, per official course architecture)
- Working title: Compliance Calendar: Insurance, Records, Filings
- Learning objective: Store leaders can maintain an insurance-schedule refresh cadence, correctly track the two distinct Form 8300 deadlines (15-day filing and January 31 payer statement), apply AML/OFAC customer-identification obligations, apply G7 diamond-origin documentation requirements at intake, and build all of the above into the store's own SOP-structured compliance binder.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: Directly fulfills the C11 terminal outcome "maintain a compliance calendar covering insurance, records, and filings" per this project's official course architecture, and directly supports the course's stated capstone assessment (the store's own SOP binder).

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames compliance discipline as the paper-side counterpart to floor operations, with real financial and legal consequences for gaps.
2. **Insurance: Keeping the Schedule Current, Not Just Filed** — Quarterly/half-yearly schedule refresh, January export, annual physical inventory retained 7 years off-premises.
3. **Form 8300: The Federal Cash-Reporting Deadline** — The 15-day filing window and the separate January 31 payer-statement deadline.
4. **AML, OFAC, and G7 Origin Documentation** — USA PATRIOT Act/Bank Secrecy Act customer-ID and OFAC screening obligations, plus G7 origin documentation for non-industrial diamonds ≥0.50 carats.
5. **The Compliance Calendar: What Happens When** — Distinguishing event-triggered items (Form 8300, origin documentation at intake) from calendar-triggered items (insurance refresh, AML audit, annual inventory).
6. **Building the Store's Own Compliance Binder** — Direct application of Module 8's seven-element SOP structure to each compliance obligation.
7. **A Worked Scenario: The Missed January 31 Deadline** — Illustrative synthesis showing the distinction between event-triggered and calendar-triggered compliance failures.
8. **Practice Exercise** — Building a one-page compliance calendar and cross-checking it against the store's actual current dates.
9. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- This module draws directly and extensively on this project's own Fine-Jewelry-Retail-Workflows-and-Operating-Systems.md research file (section 7.3 and section 9), which is itself grounded in named primary sources: Jewelers Mutual (insurer inventory-list refresh cadence, annual documented itemized physical inventory retained 7 years off-premises), the IRS (Form 8300 payer statements furnished by January 31), and this project's own Fine-Jewelry-Career-Guide.md (Global Fine Jewelry Retail Career, Operations, and Competency Framework), which explicitly names the USA PATRIOT Act, Bank Secrecy Act, OFAC sanctions screening, Form 8300 cash-receipt filing (>$10,000), and G7 sanctions requiring origin documentation for non-industrial diamonds 0.50 carats and larger.
- The specific 15-day Form 8300 filing window was directly cross-checked against current IRS.gov sources during this module's production (Instructions for Form 8300, IRS Form 8300 reference guide, and IRS newsroom guidance FS-2021-03 and FS-2023-19) and is CONFIRMED consistent with the January 31 payer-statement requirement already cited in this project's own prior research file.
- The G7 origin-documentation requirement for non-industrial diamonds ≥0.50 carats reflects this project's own prior research (Fine-Jewelry-Career-Guide.md), which itself notes this is a recent and fast-moving regulatory area; the module explicitly flags the Jewelers Vigilance Committee's Sanctions Resource Center as the recommended source for staying current, since specific carat thresholds and country-scope details in G7/Russian-diamond sanctions regimes have been subject to revision and phase-in changes.
- The distinction between event-triggered and calendar-triggered compliance items is this module's own original organizing synthesis, built directly from the underlying obligations already documented in this project's prior research files; it is not itself an externally sourced claim, though every individual obligation it categorizes is independently sourced.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.

## Assessment Alignment
- Quiz items should test: correctly applying the insurance-schedule refresh cadence and 7-year off-premises retention rule, correctly distinguishing the two separate Form 8300 deadlines (15-day filing vs. January 31 payer statement), correctly identifying AML/OFAC/G7 origin-documentation obligations, correctly categorizing compliance items as event-triggered vs. calendar-triggered, and correctly applying the seven-element SOP structure to a compliance obligation.
- Huddle card should focus on the two distinct Form 8300 deadlines and the event-triggered vs. calendar-triggered distinction as the two most operationally important takeaways to avoid a costly compliance miss.
- Factcheck should flag the G7/Russian-diamond origin-documentation requirement as the item most likely to have changed since this project's research was compiled, given how fast international sanctions regimes evolve, and should direct the store leader to the Jewelers Vigilance Committee's Sanctions Resource Center for a current check before finalizing store policy.
