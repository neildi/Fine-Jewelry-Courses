# C11-M08 Fact-Check: Writing SOPs Your Team Will Actually Use

## Verification Status: The five specific SOP-failure modes and the systems-design reframe are unusually well-corroborated across multiple independent general-business-process sources using nearly identical language; the seven-element structure and drafting/piloting techniques are sourced from retail-specific SOP guides

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Five specific, repeated SOP-failure modes (wrong author; excessive length; poor accessibility; staleness; lack of ownership/accountability).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sources analyzing why SOPs go unused: tallyfy.com's "Standard operating procedures: why most fail and how to fix" article (explicitly naming all five of these exact failure modes in nearly this same language: "written by the wrong people," "too long," "buried in shared drives," "never updated," "no accountability"), scribehow's "7 Common SOP Challenges and Practical Fixes for Each" article, and ideagen.com's "The real reason your SOPs and policies aren't being followed" article. This is unusually strong, multi-source, independently corroborated consensus on a specific, named set of failure modes, though none of these sources are jewelry- or even retail-specific; they are general business-process-management sources.

**2. The systems-design (not training/discipline) reframe for chronically-ignored procedures.**
Status: Sourced from two related articles by consultevo.com, both making the same specific diagnostic point about unclear execution points, scattered information, and added friction without added value as the real, addressable causes. These are general business-process/consulting sources, not retail- or jewelry-specific, though the underlying diagnostic principle is domain-general.

**3. The seven-element SOP structure (Task, Owner, Timing, Steps, Standard, Exception, Proof).**
Status: Sourced directly from gopazo.com's "Retail SOP Guide," a retail-specific source explicitly proposing this exact structure and terminology. This is the single most directly retail-relevant structural source used in this module. The direct callback connecting this structure to Module 2's opening sequence and Module 4's high-value verification protocol is this module's own original synthesis, not itself an externally-sourced claim, though it accurately reflects those modules' previously-established and independently fact-checked content.

**4. "Document what your best people already do" drafting technique and pilot-with-a-relative-novice testing practice.**
Status: Sourced from shoplworks.com's "What Is a Retail SOP?" guide (explicit "Document what your best people already do" and "Pilot it, then revise based on feedback... look for the steps people can't actually follow") and Smartsheet's "How to Write Standard Operating Procedures" guide (explicitly recommending testing with people of "different levels of knowledge" including "those with no knowledge of the tasks"). Both are independently corroborating, credible sources for these specific practices.

**5. Accessibility (SOPs living where the task actually happens) and change-triggered (not calendar-triggered) revision.**
Status: Sourced from shoplworks.com's guide ("revisit it whenever something changes that affects how the task is done") and tallyfy.com's article (the "buried in shared drives" failure mode and its corresponding fix).

**6. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from any external source.
