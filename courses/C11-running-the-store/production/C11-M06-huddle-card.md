# C11-M06 Huddle Card: Scheduling to Traffic and Peak Coverage

## 5-Minute Pre-Shift Discussion

**The one idea to remember:** Schedule to the CURVE, not the clock. A flat schedule that's "technically staffed all day" often misses exactly when clients are actually here.

## Map the Real Curve First
Pull 4-6 weeks of real POS/traffic data. Don't guess. Don't copy last year blindly. Plot it hour by hour.

## Traffic Peak ≠ Purchase Peak
Sometimes people browse at one time and BUY at another. You may need coverage for both, not just one.

## The Anchor Technique
Never start a shift exactly when the rush hits. Anchor shift starts 15 min to an hour AHEAD of the climb. Stagger arrivals — don't have everyone start at once.

## Two Hard Floors (Non-Negotiable)
1. Module 2's two-person minimum for open/close and floor coverage — security requirement, not a preference
2. C9's 30-40 min per real client conversation — if you want genuine discovery quality during a rush

## SPLH: Your Own Number, Not Someone Else's
General retail SPLH benchmarks vary wildly ($40-80 to $150-250) and aren't jewelry-specific. Track YOUR store's actual baseline. Watch if it improves after a schedule change — that's the real signal.

## Dead Zones Are for Dead-Zone Work
Inventory counts, merchandising review, cleaning — schedule these into your genuinely quiet hours, not competing with your real peak.

## Today's Discussion Prompt
Look at last Saturday's actual traffic vs. our schedule that day. Did coverage actually match when people were here?
