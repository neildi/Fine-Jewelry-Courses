**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

**1. What are the five numbers in the weekly review structure this module recommends?**
A) Payroll cost, rent, utilities, insurance premium, and tax liability
B) Weekly turnover (with prior-period comparison), net profit margin, repair pipeline status (completed/open/overdue), customer and old-gold purchase activity, and top-ten/bottom-ten sellers
C) Only total sales for the week, with no other context
D) Number of new hires, number of terminations, number of promotions, number of complaints, and number of compliments

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This specific five-number structure, reviewed in a fixed weekly slot, gives a manager a complete health check in under fifteen minutes.

**2. Why does the weekly turnover number need to be compared against the same week last year and the previous four weeks, rather than viewed alone?**
A) Comparison context is unnecessary; the raw number alone is always sufficient
B) A single number without comparison context is close to meaningless — a manager needs to know whether the week is normal, unusually strong, or a warning sign
C) Comparisons should only be made against next year's projected numbers
D) Turnover numbers should never be compared across different time periods

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Context transforms a raw number into an actionable signal about whether the store is on track.

**3. What are the three KPIs this course's terminal assessment structure anchors ongoing store-leader success to?**
A) Employee satisfaction, customer satisfaction, and social media engagement
B) Sales versus plan, shrinkage rate, and visual-merchandising audit score
C) Total inventory value, total square footage, and total staff count
D) Store hours, holiday sales only, and website traffic

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Each of these three KPIs traces directly back to the operational disciplines built in earlier modules of this course.

**4. Which earlier modules does this course tie the shrinkage-rate KPI back to?**
A) Module 1 only
B) Module 4's high-value verification protocol and Module 7's vendor and repair pipeline visibility
C) Module 6's scheduling content exclusively
D) No earlier module relates to shrinkage rate

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A rising shrinkage number is rarely a mystery once a manager traces it back to which specific control from these modules slipped.

**5. What design principle does this module apply to the scorecard itself, drawing on Module 8?**
A) The scorecard should be as exhaustive and detailed as possible, regardless of length
B) The scorecard should be a single page, reviewed in a fixed time slot and sequence every week, applying the same low-friction simplicity principle established for SOPs
C) The scorecard should be reviewed only once per quarter
D) There is no specific design principle; format doesn't matter

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Removing the friction of re-deciding what to check each week is often the real reason an unstructured review quietly stops happening.

**6. Why does this module emphasize reading multi-week trends rather than single-week numbers?**
A) Single-week numbers are always more informative than trends
B) A gradually climbing shrinkage rate or declining merchandising score over several weeks is an actionable early-warning signal worth tracing to its source before it shows up in a worse monthly result
C) Trends should only be reviewed once a year
D) Multi-week trends are irrelevant to store performance

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Catching drift on the weekly scorecard, before it compounds into a monthly problem, is the entire point of running the review weekly.

**7. What should a store leader do when the scorecard reveals a recurring gap, such as repairs consistently running overdue?**

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This closes the loop between the weekly scorecard, the SOP binder, and the compliance calendar as different views of the same operating system.

**8. In the worked scenario, how did the manager catch the aged-stock problem before the monthly inventory reconciliation?**
A) They waited for the monthly cycle as usual and dealt with it then
B) The weekly top-ten/bottom-ten sellers list showed the same three slow movers sitting in the bottom ten for five consecutive weeks, prompting an immediate targeted markdown or sales push

Explanation: This illustrates the scorecard's core value: catching a drift five weeks earlier than the monthly cycle would have, before it became a larger aged-stock problem.
