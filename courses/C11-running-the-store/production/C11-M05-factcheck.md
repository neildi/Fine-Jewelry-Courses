# C11-M05 Fact-Check: Visual Merchandising Standards and Case Zoning

## Verification Status: Placement/zoning and lighting-temperature principles well-corroborated across multiple independent jewelry-merchandising sources; one specific statistic (40% perceived-quality figure with named academic/industry citations) traced to a single vendor-site source, not independently verified against the original studies

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Hot-zone/cold-zone placement model and eye-level center/side positioning logic.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent jewelry-merchandising sources: PlanoHero's "Jewelry Merchandising" guide (explicit hot-zone/cold-zone terminology and definitions), jewel360's "Visual Merchandising for Jewelry" and "10 Visual Merchandising Tips" articles, and tuplestrategy.com's two merchandising articles (explicit eye-level center/side/corner logic, including the specific point about natural pairings like wedding bands beside engagement rings). This is a well-corroborated, multi-source consensus.

**2. Lighting standards (warm white LED, 2700-3000K range; CRI 95+; angled lighting to eliminate shadow/glare).**
Status: Sourced from suntopdisplay.com's "How Jewelry Display Cases Influence Customer Buying Decisions" article (explicitly citing Philips Lighting 2020 research on warm-white LED performance for jewelry) and independently corroborated by datallen.com's "Jewelry Displays for Retail Stores" article (specifying a similar 3000-4000K warm-white range) and NoblePack's "How to Display Jewelry" guide (the warm-vs-cool-tone-by-metal-type principle and shadow/glare-elimination point). IMPORTANT SOURCING NOTE: the specific claim that "ambient factors... collectively influence a shopper's perceived product quality by up to 40%," with its named citation to the Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services (2021), comes from suntopdisplay.com alone within this research pass — a display-case manufacturer/vendor site, not the original academic journal itself. This specific statistic and citation have NOT been independently verified against the original study in this research pass and should be treated as a vendor-cited claim pending independent verification, distinct from the broader, well-corroborated lighting-temperature principles.

**3. One-focal-point-per-zone principle, odd-number grouping (three, five, seven), and the 20/40/40 price-tier space-allocation rule.**
Status: Sourced from iigsouth.com's "Visual Merchandising for Jewellery Stores: The Complete Guide" (the focal-point and odd-number grouping principles specifically) and independently corroborated by Palmer Retail Solutions' "16 Display Tips for Jewelry Stores" and kuhnsdiamondjewelers.com's "10 Jewelry Display Ideas That Actually Sell" (both describing the same top-tier/mid-tier/lower-tier space-allocation logic, with kuhnsdiamondjewelers.com using the specific "20/40/40" phrasing).

**4. Grouping-by-collection/occasion/price-point logic; the "don't cluster slow movers" principle; front-row/back-row price-tier positioning.**
Status: Sourced from Stuller's "Jewelry Showcasing" blog article (a major jewelry-supply-industry source, directly credible for this specific practical guidance) and Palmer Retail Solutions' "Use These Merchandising Techniques" article, both independently confirming these practices.

**5. The explicit reconciliation of merchandising principles with Module 2's case-security discipline.**
Status: This is this module's own reasoned synthesis, directly extending previously-established and independently fact-checked C11 Module 2 content (see C11-M02 factcheck: one-item-out-at-a-time discipline, every slot filled with merchandise or a marker) rather than a new externally-sourced claim requiring independent verification here.
