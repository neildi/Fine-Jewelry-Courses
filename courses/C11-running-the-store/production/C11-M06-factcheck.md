# C11-M06 Fact-Check: Scheduling to Traffic and Peak Coverage

## Verification Status: Core scheduling-to-traffic disciplines well-corroborated across multiple independent general-retail sources; SPLH figures explicitly flagged as varying substantially by source and not jewelry-specific; two key constraints (security minimum, compressed-time benchmark) directly extend this project's own previously-established and fact-checked content

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Data-driven traffic-mapping discipline (pulling several weeks of POS/door-count data and plotting hour-by-hour patterns).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent retail-scheduling sources: TCP Software's "Employee Scheduling in Retail" article, shiftsynch.org's "How to Schedule Retail Staff Based on Foot Traffic" guide, and getdor.com's "Retail Peak Hour Staffing" and "Retail Staffing Optimization" articles. All are general-retail (not jewelry-specific) sources, though the underlying data-driven discipline is domain-general and directly applicable.

**2. General retail peak-time patterns (lunch hours 11am-2pm, after-work 4-7pm, weekend afternoons heaviest overall).**
Status: Sourced from fieldpie.com's "Store Schedule Guide" and turnozo.com's "Retail Scheduling Software" article, both independently describing similar general peak windows. This module explicitly notes these are general-retail patterns requiring confirmation against a store's own actual data, not universal assumptions.

**3. The traffic-peak-vs-purchase-peak distinction.**
Status: Sourced directly from xshift.ai's "Retail Holiday Staffing" article, which explicitly states "peak traffic and peak sales do not always align... you may see heavy browsing from 10 AM-12 PM but peak purchases from 2-5 PM."

**4. The "anchor" technique (starting shifts 15 minutes to an hour ahead of a projected traffic climb) and staggered-arrival scheduling.**
Status: Sourced from getdor.com's "Retail Peak Hour Staffing" article (explicit "build the schedule backward from your peaks... layer in the rest of the team about an hour ahead of your peak") and shiftsynch.org's guide (the specific "15-minute anchor" terminology).

**5. Sales-per-labor-hour (SPLH) benchmarks.**
Status: Sourced from getmyroster.com's "Retail Staff Scheduling" guide (citing $150-250 for "retail" generally) and turnozo.com's article (citing $40-80). IMPORTANT SOURCING NOTE: these figures vary substantially between the two sources and are general-retail benchmarks, not jewelry-specific. Given the dramatically different average ticket size between typical general retail and fine jewelry, this module explicitly declines to present either specific figure as jewelry-applicable, and instead recommends store-specific baseline tracking as the transferable practice.

**6. Dead-zone task scheduling (non-customer-facing work scheduled into genuinely low-traffic hours).**
Status: Sourced from shiftsynch.org's guide ("Schedule Tasks for Dead Zones... Inventory, window displays, and deep cleaning should never happen during power hours"). This module's specific connection of this principle to the inventory/merchandising work established in Modules 3-5 of this course is this module's own original synthesis, not itself an externally-sourced claim.

**7. The application of Module 2's two-person security minimum and C9's compressed-time staffing benchmark as scheduling constraints.**
Status: This is this module's own reasoned synthesis, directly extending previously-established and independently fact-checked content from both this course (see C11-M02 factcheck) and this project's separate C9 course (see C9-M05 factcheck, which itself flagged the 30-40-minute figure as sourced from a single trunk-show planning source, not a universal constant). Not a new externally-sourced claim requiring independent verification here.

**8. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from any external source.
