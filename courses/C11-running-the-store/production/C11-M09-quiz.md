# C11-M09 Quiz: Compliance Calendar: Insurance, Records, Filings

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. How often should a store refresh the itemized inventory list it provides to its insurance broker, at minimum?**
A) Only when a claim is filed
B) At minimum every quarter or half-year, plus a full export of the list and photo archive every January
C) Once, when the policy is first written, with no further updates needed
D) Every five years

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A schedule that hasn't been updated recently creates a real coverage gap that's invisible until a claim is contested.

---

**2. How long must a documented, itemized annual physical inventory be retained, and where?**
A) One year, on the store's own premises
B) Seven years, stored off-premises
C) No specific retention period is required
D) Indefinitely, but only in digital form on the store's local server

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Off-premises storage ensures a single event at the store (fire, flood, robbery) can't destroy both the merchandise and the only record of what was lost.

---

**3. What are the two distinct Form 8300 deadlines a store leader must track separately?**
A) There is only one deadline: filing at year-end
B) Filing within 15 days of a qualifying cash transaction, and furnishing payer statements to the customer by January 31 of the following year
C) Filing within 30 days and furnishing statements within 60 days
D) Both obligations are due on the same fixed date each year regardless of when the transaction occurred

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These are functionally different obligations — one is event-triggered (tied to the transaction date), the other is calendar-triggered (a fixed year-end anchor) — and tracking them as one item is a common source of missed deadlines.

---

**4. What cash amount triggers a Form 8300 filing obligation?**
A) Any cash receipt over $1,000
B) Any cash receipt over $10,000 in a single transaction or in related transactions
C) Only cash receipts over $100,000
D) Form 8300 applies only to credit card transactions, not cash

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This threshold applies across the jewelry trade specifically because high-value cash transactions are a recognized money-laundering risk point.

---

**5. What broader regulatory obligations do dealers in precious metals and stones carry beyond Form 8300 cash reporting?**
A) No obligations beyond Form 8300 exist
B) USA PATRIOT Act and Bank Secrecy Act requirements for a documented customer identification program, plus OFAC sanctions-list screening
C) Only state-level sales tax reporting
D) Obligations that apply only to publicly traded jewelry companies

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These obligations require maintaining customer identification records and screening transactions against the Treasury's OFAC sanctions list, independent of the cash-specific Form 8300 requirement.

---

**6. What does the G7 origin-documentation requirement specifically require for certain diamonds?**
A) No documentation is required for any diamonds
B) Documentary proof of origin for non-industrial diamonds of 0.50 carats and larger, driven by restrictions on Russian-origin diamonds entering G7 markets
C) Origin documentation only for diamonds purchased directly from Russia
D) A verbal confirmation of origin is sufficient; no written documentation is needed

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A store that can't produce origin documentation on request for a qualifying stone risks real business-continuity exposure, including an inability to resell or insure the piece.

---

**7. Why does this module treat G7 origin documentation as an intake-time control rather than a calendar-cycle item?**
A) Because origin documentation only needs to be checked once a year
B) Because it's a per-transaction control that must happen at every qualifying intake, the same category of discipline as the high-value verification protocol from Module 4
C) Because origin documentation is optional and can be skipped for time-sensitive sales
D) Because it only applies to online sales, not in-store transactions

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Like the high-value verification protocol, this is a control applied every time a qualifying transaction occurs, not on a fixed calendar schedule.

---

**8. In the worked scenario, what specifically caused the missed January 31 deadline, and what was the fix?**
A) The store never filed Form 8300 at all; the fix was to start filing
B) The 15-day filing was tracked correctly, but there was no year-end anchor point for the separate January 31 payer-statement deadline; the fix was adding a fixed December reminder to review the prior year's filings
C) The store was never actually required to furnish payer statements
D) The fix was to stop accepting cash payments over $10,000 entirely

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This illustrates the core distinction in this module: event-triggered and calendar-triggered compliance items need separate tracking mechanisms, not just general vigilance.
