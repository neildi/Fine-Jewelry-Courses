# C11-M06 Quiz: Scheduling to Traffic and Peak Coverage

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. What is the starting discipline this module recommends before building any schedule?**
A) Simply repeat last year's schedule with no changes
B) Pull several weeks of real POS transaction and door-count data and plot traffic hour by hour, rather than scheduling from habit or instinct
C) Schedule based entirely on general retail industry averages with no store-specific data
D) Ask employees to guess when the store is busiest

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This data-driven starting point is confirmed across multiple independent retail-scheduling sources and is the foundation every other technique in this module builds on.

---

**2. Why does this module caution that "traffic peaks and purchase peaks don't always align"?**
A) Traffic and purchases always happen at exactly the same time in every store
B) A store might see heavy browsing at one time of day but the actual majority of purchases close at a different time, meaning floor coverage and closing support may need to be staffed for two different peaks, not one
C) Purchase timing has no relationship to staffing needs
D) Only traffic data matters; purchase timing is irrelevant to scheduling

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This distinction means a schedule built only around browsing traffic might miss exactly when closing-focused support is most needed.

---

**3. What is the "anchor" technique described in this module?**
A) Starting every shift at the exact moment a rush begins
B) Starting shifts roughly 15 minutes to an hour ahead of a projected traffic climb, so the floor is genuinely staffed and settled before the rush actually arrives
C) Anchoring means never changing shift start times regardless of traffic data
D) The anchor technique applies only to closing shifts, never to opening shifts

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This specific technique prevents staff from still getting organized as clients are already walking in.

---

**4. According to this module, what two constraints from earlier in this course apply on top of traffic-driven scheduling?**
A) There are no additional constraints beyond raw traffic data
B) Module 2's two-person security minimum for opening/closing and general floor coverage, and C9's compressed-time staffing benchmark (30-40 minutes per full client conversation) for high-traffic periods
C) Only sales targets matter; security and conversation-quality benchmarks are irrelevant to scheduling
D) These constraints only apply during holiday seasons, never during normal weeks

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These are real, non-negotiable floors beneath which staffing should never fall, regardless of how quiet traffic data suggests a given hour might be.

---

**5. How does this module recommend treating sales-per-labor-hour (SPLH) figures cited in general retail sources?**
A) As a precise, universal jewelry-industry standard that every store should match exactly
B) As a general-retail benchmark, not a jewelry-specific standard, given how dramatically average ticket size differs between typical general retail and fine jewelry retail; the transferable practice is tracking a store's own actual SPLH baseline
C) SPLH has no relevance to jewelry retail scheduling at all
D) All retail categories have identical SPLH targets regardless of ticket size

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Given the wide variation in cited SPLH figures across general-retail sources, and the very different average ticket sizes in jewelry retail, store-specific tracking is the genuinely useful, transferable discipline.

---

**6. What does this module recommend scheduling into a store's lowest-traffic "dead zone" hours?**
A) The store's most important client-facing sales activities
B) Non-customer-facing tasks like inventory counts, merchandising review, and deep cleaning — work that was established in earlier modules of this course
C) Dead zones should simply be left unstaffed with no activity at all
D) High-value client appointments should always be scheduled during dead zones

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This directly connects the inventory and merchandising work from Modules 3-5 to a specific, deliberate place in the schedule, rather than letting it compete with genuine peak-hour coverage.

---

**7. In the worked scenario, what problem did the flat, evenly-spread schedule actually have, despite technically having coverage every hour?**
A) The schedule had no real problem and needed no changes
B) It didn't account for a clear, consistent Saturday peak between 11am and 3pm, while Tuesday afternoons ran consistently quiet — coverage wasn't actually matched to when clients were really in the store
C) The schedule had far too many total hours and needed to be cut across the board
D) The problem was entirely unrelated to traffic patterns

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: "Technically staffed every hour" is not the same as "staffed to match real demand," which is exactly the distinction this module's redesign illustrates.

---
