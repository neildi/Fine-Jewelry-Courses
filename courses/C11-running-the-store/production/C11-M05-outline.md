# C11-M05 Outline: Visual Merchandising Standards and Case Zoning

## Module Metadata
- Course: C11 - Running the Store
- Module: 5 of 10
- Persona: P1/P7 (First-Time Store Leader, per official course architecture)
- Working title: Visual Merchandising Standards and Case Zoning
- Learning objective: Store leaders can apply hot-zone/cold-zone placement logic, the one-focal-point-per-zone and odd-number-grouping principles, jewelry-specific lighting standards, and price-tier merchandising rules, while maintaining the case-security discipline established in Module 2.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: Directly fulfills the C11 terminal outcome "maintain visual merchandising standards and case zoning" per this project's official course architecture; the course's stated KPI link explicitly includes a "visual-merchandising audit score."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames a showcase as simultaneously a security container and a sales tool, requiring both disciplines together.
2. **Hot Zones and Cold Zones: Where the Eye Actually Goes First** — Predictable attention patterns within a case and their placement implications.
3. **One Focal Point Per Zone, Grouped in Odd Numbers** — The focal-point principle, odd-number grouping, and the 20/40/40 price-tier space allocation rule.
4. **Lighting: Not Decorative, Functional** — Warm-white LED color temperature, CRI, and angle as functional (not aesthetic) choices specific to jewelry.
5. **Grouping That Guides the Client, Not Just Organizes Inventory** — Collection/occasion/price-point grouping logic; the "don't cluster slow movers" principle; front-row vs. back-row price positioning.
6. **Where Merchandising Meets Security** — Explicitly reconciling this module's principles with Module 2's case-security discipline.
7. **A Worked Scenario: The Case That Looked Empty But Wasn't** — Illustrative synthesis showing a merchandising redesign improving engagement without changing merchandise.
8. **Practice Exercise** — Redesigning a real showcase against this module's principles.
9. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The hot-zone/cold-zone placement model (top-right and center as hot zones; bottom-left as a cold zone suited to statement pieces or steady sellers) and eye-level center/side positioning logic (center commands the most attention from the most angles; sides support natural pairings/add-on sales) are CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent jewelry-merchandising sources: PlanoHero's "Jewelry Merchandising" guide (explicit hot-zone/cold-zone terminology), jewel360's "Visual Merchandising for Jewelry" and "10 Visual Merchandising Tips" articles, tuplestrategy.com's two merchandising articles (explicit eye-level center/side/corner logic), and suntopdisplay.com's "How Jewelry Display Cases Influence Customer Buying Decisions" article (explicitly citing the Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services, 2021, on ambient factors affecting perceived product quality by up to 40%, and Philips Lighting 2020 research on warm-white LED performance for jewelry).
- The one-focal-point-per-zone principle, odd-number grouping (three, five, seven), and the 20/40/40 price-tier space-allocation rule are sourced from iigsouth.com's "Visual Merchandising for Jewellery Stores: The Complete Guide" (the focal-point and odd-number grouping principles specifically) and Palmer Retail Solutions' "16 Display Tips for Jewelry Stores" and kuhnsdiamondjewelers.com's "10 Jewelry Display Ideas That Actually Sell" (both independently describing the same top-20%/next-40%/remaining-40% space-allocation logic, though kuhnsdiamondjewelers.com uses the specific phrase "20/40/40").
- The lighting standards (warm white LED in the 2700-3000K range; CRI 95+; angled lighting to eliminate shadow/glare) are sourced from suntopdisplay.com (explicitly citing Philips Lighting 2020 research) and independently corroborated by datallen.com's "Jewelry Displays for Retail Stores" article (specifying the same 3000-4000K warm-white range for enhancing gold/pearl luster) and NoblePack's "How to Display Jewelry" guide (the warm-vs-cool-tone-by-metal-type principle and shadow/glare-elimination point). IMPORTANT NOTE: the specific "40% perceived quality" figure and named academic/industry citations (Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services 2021; Philips Lighting 2020) come from a single source (suntopdisplay.com, itself a display-case manufacturer/vendor site) within this research pass and have not been independently verified against the original cited studies; this figure should be treated as a vendor-cited claim pending independent verification, not a fully confirmed academic finding.
- The grouping-by-collection/occasion/price-point logic, the "don't cluster slow movers" principle, and the front-row/back-row price-tier positioning are sourced from Stuller's "Jewelry Showcasing" blog article (a major jewelry-supply-industry source) and Palmer Retail Solutions' "Use These Merchandising Techniques" article, both independently confirming these practices.
- The explicit reconciliation of merchandising principles with Module 2's case-security discipline is this module's own reasoned synthesis, directly extending previously-established and fact-checked C11 Module 2 content (see C11-M02 factcheck) rather than a new externally-sourced claim.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
