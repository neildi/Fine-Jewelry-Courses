# C11-M01 Quiz: From Sales Floor to Store Leadership: What Actually Changes

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. What is the single biggest shift practitioners describe when moving from a sales-floor role into store leadership?**
A) The job stays essentially the same, just with more paperwork added on top
B) Success stops being about personal sales output and becomes about the output of the entire team, which the individual-selling skills that earned the promotion don't automatically prepare someone for
C) Store leaders are no longer expected to understand sales technique at all
D) The shift only affects compensation, not actual day-to-day responsibilities

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This disorienting shift is the most consistently named surprise among people who've actually made this transition, and treating the new role as "selling, plus extra paperwork" is a common early mistake.

---

**2. According to this module, when does P&L and financial/operational accountability typically arrive for a new store manager?**
A) Only after several years of gradual, supervised buildup
B) Immediately, with no gradual ramp-up period, arriving alongside the shift to team-output accountability
C) P&L accountability never actually applies to store managers, only to regional executives
D) Financial accountability is optional and can be delegated away entirely

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Experienced retail leaders describe managing "the math, the mentoring, and the numbers" as arriving all at once, with no separation between people-leadership and financial-accountability work.

---

**3. What specific jewelry-retail responsibilities does this module identify as part of a store manager's immediate financial/operational scope?**
A) Store managers in jewelry retail have no financial responsibilities of any kind
B) Cash handling and deposit accuracy, inventory accuracy and shrinkage control, vendor relationships and purchasing decisions, and regular reporting on sales/labor/inventory to ownership
C) Only sales targets, with no responsibility for cash, inventory, or vendor relationships
D) Financial scope is identical across every retail industry with no jewelry-specific elements

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This scope is confirmed consistently across multiple real jewelry-retail job postings, representing a genuinely wide financial and operational responsibility beyond anything a sales-floor role touches directly.

**4. What does the "first month is for watching, not changing" principle recommend for someone newly stepping into store leadership?**
A) Immediately implement as many visible changes as possible in the first week to establish authority
B) Spend the first month observing how the floor actually operates and where real strengths and gaps exist, before making changes based on that observation rather than a pre-formed plan
C) Avoid making any changes at all, ever, regardless of what observation reveals
D) Changes should always be made before any observation occurs

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is a specific discipline of gathering accurate information before acting on it, which an eager new manager is specifically at risk of skipping in a rush to prove themselves.

---

**5. According to this module, what actually builds a team's genuine trust in a new leader?**
A) An early, impressive individual sale or a friendly first impression
B) How the leader responds to the first real crisis or problem, handled calmly and fairly
C) Trust is established automatically upon promotion and requires no further action
D) Trust depends entirely on being the most knowledgeable person in the store at all times

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A team is specifically watching for how a new leader handles the first real problem, not an early sales win, and this response is what actually establishes or undermines trust.

---

**6. Why does this module argue that being "the calmest," not "the smartest," is the more important leadership quality under pressure?**
A) Knowledge and expertise are irrelevant to effective leadership
B) A team takes its emotional cue directly from whoever is leading it, especially under real pressure, making calm composure more consequential than being the most knowledgeable person present
C) Being calm means avoiding any real engagement with problems as they arise
D) This principle only applies to leaders with more than ten years of experience

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This reflects the practical reality that a team's emotional state during a crisis often mirrors their leader's, making composure a genuine leadership skill in itself.

**7. What is the key difference between praising an associate for "hitting target" versus praising them for "staying with a customer through an objection"?**
A) There is no meaningful difference; both forms of praise produce identical results
B) Praising the outcome (hitting target) teaches an associate to chase the number by any means, while praising the specific behavior (staying through the objection) teaches the underlying skill that reliably produces good outcomes over time
C) Outcome-based praise is always more effective at building long-term capability
D) Behavior-based praise should never be used in a retail coaching context

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This distinction directly determines whether coaching builds a team's real, lasting capability or simply narrows their attention onto the metric itself.

**8. In the worked scenario, why did the store manager's response to the miscommunicated custom order matter more than any individual sale that week?**
A) It didn't actually matter; only sales performance affects how a team perceives a new leader
