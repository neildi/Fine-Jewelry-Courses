# C11-M07 Quiz: Vendor, Repair and Custom Pipeline Management

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. Why are memo and consignment goods treated as a distinct liability category rather than simple owned inventory?**
A) Memo and consignment goods are identical to owned inventory with no meaningful difference
B) These goods are liabilities while in the store's possession, generating a payment due only after the item is actually sold or consumed, rather than being owned outright from day one
C) Memo and consignment goods should never be tracked separately from owned inventory
D) Only consignment goods carry any special tracking requirement; memo goods are treated as fully owned

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This distinction matters specifically for what the store actually owes a vendor versus what it already owns outright, and a store leader is responsible for this being tracked accurately.

---

**2. Why does a store leader need to ensure every associate working with a specific vendor's line understands the vendor terms in effect?**
A) Vendor terms are irrelevant to associates and only matter to store leadership
B) Vendor terms (minimum purchase commitments, consignment splits) directly shape real floor decisions, and an associate operating without this knowledge can make a commercially reasonable-sounding promise the store's actual agreement doesn't support
C) All vendor relationships use identical terms, so no specific understanding is needed
D) Vendor terms only matter after a sale has already been completed

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This directly reuses the vendor-terms principle already established elsewhere in this project's course catalog, applied here to ongoing pipeline management.

---

**3. What is the difference between "pipeline-level" repair management and "job-by-job" repair management, according to this module?**
A) There is no meaningful difference between the two approaches
B) Pipeline-level management means holding visibility across every job simultaneously (completed/on-track/overdue counts), while job-by-job management only tracks individual jobs closely, which doesn't scale past a handful of repairs
C) Job-by-job management is always superior to pipeline-level management
D) Pipeline-level management means ignoring individual job details entirely

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A leader who can name roughly how many jobs are in each state at any given moment is actually managing the pipeline, not just individual jobs.

---

**4. What specific weekly report structure does this module recommend for repair-pipeline visibility?**
A) A report covering only jobs that are already overdue, with no other categories
B) A report covering how many repair jobs are completed and ready for pickup, how many are open and within deadline, and how many are overdue, with overdue jobs specifically flagged for immediate follow-up
C) A report that only tracks total revenue from repairs, with no status breakdown
D) Weekly reporting is unnecessary if daily huddles already cover repair status

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This three-count structure gives a leader genuine pipeline visibility rather than requiring individual job-by-job tracking.

---

**5. How does this module recommend applying the same pipeline-visibility principle to custom orders?**
A) Custom orders require no pipeline-level visibility since each one is unique
B) Knowing which custom orders are awaiting approval, which are in active production and on schedule, and which have slipped — with particular attention to proactive client updates throughout production, not just at pickup or when already overdue
C) Custom orders should only be reviewed once, at the very end of production
D) Client updates on custom orders should only happen if the client specifically asks for one

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This mirrors the repair-pipeline visibility principle, applied to the higher-stakes context of custom orders.

---

**6. Why does this module emphasize recognizing dependencies between vendor relationships and the repair/custom pipelines, rather than managing each area separately?**
A) Vendor relationships, repairs, and custom orders never actually intersect in practice
B) A custom order or repair can be blocked by a vendor-dependent component or shipment, meaning the actual bottleneck sometimes sits in a different category than where the job appears to be tracked
C) Cross-category dependencies are too rare to be worth tracking
D) Only vendor relationships need to be tracked; repairs and custom orders are entirely independent

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Recognizing these dependencies is what allows a leader to catch a blocked job early, rather than discovering the real cause of a delay only when a client complains.

**7. In the worked scenario, how did the store leader catch the custom engagement ring's vendor-dependency problem before it became a crisis?**
A) The bench jeweler reported the issue immediately when it first occurred
B) The store leader's regular weekly pipeline review, which looks across the full pipeline rather than only individual job statuses, surfaced the nine-day wait on a vendor shipment before it reached the point of missing the promised date
C) The client discovered the problem and reported it to the store
D) The issue was never actually caught and the promised date was missed with no warning

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This illustrates directly why pipeline-level review, rather than relying on individual jobs to surface problems on their own, catches issues while there's still time to act.
