# C11-M04 Quiz: Monthly Reporting, Planning, Inventory Reconciliation

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. What does the monthly business review with district or ownership leadership cover, according to this module?**
A) Only sales numbers, with no discussion of people or staffing results
B) Daily, weekly, and monthly business AND people results together, with strategic plans built jointly rather than the store leader simply reporting numbers upward
C) Only staffing schedules, with no financial content
D) The monthly review has no defined content and varies randomly

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This joint, combined focus on both business results and people results is what distinguishes the monthly review from a simple numbers report.

---

**2. What is the store leader's specific monthly financial responsibility, beyond simply tracking results?**
A) Store leaders have no real financial responsibility at the monthly level
B) Ensuring the store actually hits its monthly budgeted contribution — taking ownership of getting there, not just monitoring whether it happened
C) Financial responsibility only applies at the annual level, never monthly
D) Monthly financial tracking is optional and can be skipped without consequence

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is a specific, active ownership responsibility confirmed across real jewelry-retail job postings, distinct from passive reporting.

---

**3. What is the purpose of rotating departmental audits (a different category counted each month)?**
A) To create suspicion toward specific individual employees
B) To ensure every category of merchandise gets a dedicated, focused audit across the year rather than being reviewed with diluted attention every time, as a structural protection built into the system
C) Rotating audits serve no real purpose and are purely a matter of preference
D) Rotating audits are only used to punish underperforming departments

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is explicitly a structural, system-level protection, not a reflection of distrust toward any specific person.

---

**4. Why should the person who receives incoming shipments be kept structurally separate from the person who counts inventory?**
A) This separation has no real security or accuracy benefit
B) This deliberate segregation of duties is a structural safeguard, reducing the risk that a single person's error or dishonesty becomes the sole basis for inventory records, not a reflection of distrust toward any specific individual
C) Only one person should ever be responsible for all inventory-related tasks, with no separation
D) Segregation of duties only matters for very large, multi-location retailers

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is a deliberate internal-control practice designed to protect the integrity of records regardless of any individual's intentions.

---

**5. What is the correct four-step sequence for the high-value verification protocol described in this module?**
A) Adjust the system record immediately, then count once to confirm
B) Two people count independently; the count sheets are compared; a third person recounts any differences; only after that recount does the system get adjusted
C) A single person counts once and immediately updates the system
D) The sequence has no defined order and can be performed in any sequence

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This specific sequence prevents a single person's error or dishonesty from being the sole basis for adjusting records on the store's highest-value merchandise.

---

**6. What does the monthly inventory reconciliation process produce as its key output?**
A) A general, unspecific impression of whether inventory seems accurate
B) A specific missing-item report generated by comparing a physical count (via full-inventory, case-count, or partial-scan POS modes) against system records
C) Reconciliation produces no actionable output of any kind
D) Only a total dollar value, with no item-level detail

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This specific, named list of discrepancies is what should actually drive the rotating-audit and high-value-verification work, rather than those processes running independently.

---

**7. How does monthly reconciliation discipline relate to quarterly and annual compliance obligations?**
A) Monthly and quarterly/annual obligations are completely unrelated to each other
B) Quarterly insurer list refreshes, the annual itemized inventory, and Form 8300 payer statements all depend directly on the monthly reconciliation work having happened consistently and accurately
C) Quarterly and annual compliance can be completed accurately even if monthly reconciliation was skipped entirely
D) Only annual compliance matters; quarterly and monthly cycles are optional

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Treating the monthly cycle as optional quietly makes later quarterly and annual compliance work far harder and less accurate.

---

**8. In the worked scenario, why did the store leader choose to flag a process gap rather than simply rushing the report through by the deadline?**
A) Rushing an inaccurate report through would have been the better choice to avoid any difficult conversation
B) Working through the reconciliation properly and honestly flagging the specific gap (no mid-month checkpoint) that nearly caused the slip is what actually prevents the same near-miss from recurring next month
C) Process gaps should never be flagged to district leadership under any circumstances
D) The deadline itself was not actually important and could have been missed without consequence

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Honest process improvement, rather than a rushed and potentially inaccurate submission, is the leadership behavior this module recommends.
