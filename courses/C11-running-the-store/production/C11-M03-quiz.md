# C11-M03 Quiz: Weekly Counts, Coaching, Client-Book Review

**1. What is the specific structure of the published weekly business review model described in this module?**
A) A review conducted at a different, unpredictable time each week, covering whatever seems most urgent
B) A fixed time slot (ideally Monday morning), running through the same five specific reports in the same sequence each week, completed in under fifteen minutes when nothing unusual surfaces
C) An annual review covering the entire year's data at once
D) A review that only covers sales figures, with no other business data included

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The value of this structure is consistency — the same slot and same five reports every week surface real problems within a week of them starting, rather than much later.

---

**2. What are the five specific reports included in the weekly business review model?**
A) Only sales figures, repeated five times
B) Weekly turnover split (product vs. gift-card), net profit margin, repairs pipeline status (completed/open/overdue), customer and old-gold purchase data, and top/bottom sellers
C) Employee attendance records only
D) Only inventory counts, with no financial data included

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: These five reports together give a store leader a genuine, comprehensive weekly snapshot of the business.

**3. Why is annual-only inventory counting considered inadequate for jewelry retail, according to this module?**
A) Annual counting is actually the recommended best practice and should be the only method used
B) A category with this much value concentrated in a small footprint needs regular, weekly-to-monthly inventory control to catch errors as they happen, rather than letting them accumulate undetected until one stressful annual count
C) Inventory counting has no real relationship to loss prevention
D) Weekly counting is only relevant for stores with very low-value merchandise

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The weekly cycle-count model (one category per week) exists specifically to catch errors incrementally rather than all at once.

**4. What is the correct sequence to follow before declaring an inventory discrepancy a loss or theft?**
A) Immediately report it as theft and begin a formal investigation
B) Recount first, check whether the piece is in a repair box or out on approval with a client, check recent sales around that item number for a possible scan error, and only as a last resort write it off as a loss
C) Skip directly to writing off the item, since recounting wastes time
D) Assume every discrepancy is always a simple counting error with no further investigation needed

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This sequence catches common, non-criminal explanations (recount errors, items in repair, scan errors) before any conclusion about theft is drawn.

**5. Why does this module caution against automatically attributing every inventory disappearance to a simple miscount?**
A) Miscounts never actually occur in real jewelry retail operations
B) Investigating clusters of discrepancies quickly and thoroughly, rather than dismissing them individually, is important because patterns can reveal a real, ongoing problem that isolated dismissal would miss
C) Every disappearance should always be assumed to be theft without further investigation
D) This caution has no practical relevance to actual store operations

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Balancing careful individual investigation with attention to patterns across multiple discrepancies is the practical discipline this module recommends.

**6. How does the weekly team meeting differ in focus from the daily huddle described in Module 2?**
A) There is no meaningful difference between the two meetings
B) The weekly meeting focuses on accountability — checking whether clienteling contact preferences are actually being honored and thank-you notes are genuinely going out, reviewing the repair pipeline, and running a scheduled training topic — distinct from the daily huddle's day-to-day focus
C) The weekly meeting should cover only administrative paperwork with no client-related content
D) The daily huddle and weekly meeting should always cover identical content

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The weekly meeting's accountability focus complements, rather than duplicates, the daily huddle's more immediate, day-specific content.

**7. What does this module recommend for a store leader's weekly client-book review, beyond simply asking the team if they followed up with clients?**
A) Accepting the team's word that follow-ups happened, with no independent verification
B) Actively spot-checking specific client records against the store's clienteling cadence (such as 2-2-2) and following up directly with anyone who has fallen behind
C) Client-book review should only happen once per year, not weekly
D) Client follow-up has no measurable cadence and cannot be reviewed systematically

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Active spot-checking, rather than simply assuming compliance, is what makes this a genuine leadership responsibility rather than a passive check-in.

**8. In the worked scenario, what did the correct discrepancy-investigation sequence reveal about the missing diamond stud pair?**
A) The pair had genuinely been stolen and a theft investigation was warranted
B) A check of recent sales around that item number revealed a scanning error — the pair had been sold correctly but scanned under an adjacent item's code, meaning no actual loss occurred

Explanation: This illustrates exactly why skipping the investigation sequence in favor of an immediate theft assumption would have created unnecessary anxiety and mistrust over what was actually a simple scanning error.
