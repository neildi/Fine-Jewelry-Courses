# C11-M05 Quiz: Visual Merchandising Standards and Case Zoning

**Instructions:** Answer all questions.

---

**1. According to the hot-zone/cold-zone model described in this module, where should a store's highest-margin pieces typically be placed?**
A) In the bottom-left corner of the case, regardless of margin
B) In the hot zones — the top-right corner and center of the case, and specifically eye-level center, since these positions command the most attention from the most angles
C) Placement has no measurable effect on which pieces get attention
D) High-margin pieces should always be hidden in the back of the case

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This placement logic is confirmed across multiple independent jewelry-merchandising sources and directly shapes which pieces get the most natural visual attention.

---

**2. What is the "cold zone" in a display case best suited for, according to this module?**
A) The cold zone should never be used for any merchandise at all
B) Large, statement pieces or items with steady, dependable demand that don't need extra visual help to get noticed
C) Only the most expensive items in the entire store
D) Cold zones and hot zones serve an identical merchandising purpose

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Statement pieces and steady sellers can succeed in the cold zone without the extra visual boost that pieces needing more help would require.

---

**3. Why does this module recommend exactly one focal point per display zone, with merchandise grouped in odd numbers?**
A) Multiple focal points and even-numbered groupings are always more effective
B) Multiple competing focal points in the same small area create visual confusion and cancel each other out, while odd-numbered groupings (three, five, seven) tend to read as more intentional and curated than even numbers
C) The number of focal points and grouping size have no real effect on how a display reads
D) Focal points should always be avoided entirely in jewelry merchandising

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This principle is confirmed across multiple merchandising sources and directly affects whether a client's eye has somewhere clear to land.

---

**4. What does the "20/40/40" price-tier space-allocation rule recommend?**
A) All items should receive exactly equal display space regardless of price or priority
B) The top 20% of items (most expensive or most prioritized) get solo space or a featured vignette; the next 40% get low-density trays with real breathing room; the remaining 40% get a tidier, higher-density presentation, but never jammed together
C) Only the cheapest 20% of items should ever be displayed
D) This rule applies only to inventory counting, not display space

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This tiered space allocation ensures the most important pieces get genuine visual priority without neglecting the presentation quality of lower-tier merchandise.

**5. Why is lighting described in this module as "functional, not decorative" for jewelry specifically?**
A) Lighting has no measurable effect on how jewelry is perceived
B) Warm white LED lighting in the 2700-3000K range with a high color-rendering index affects perceived product quality, gold-tone appearance, and gemstone color accuracy, making lighting choices a functional merchandising decision rather than just an aesthetic one
C) Any lighting temperature works equally well for all types of jewelry
D) Only natural sunlight should ever be used to display jewelry

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is confirmed across multiple independent merchandising sources specifically focused on jewelry display, distinguishing jewelry's lighting needs from many other retail categories.

**6. What does this module recommend regarding slower-moving pieces in a display case?**
A) Cluster all slow-moving pieces together in a single dedicated display
B) Sprinkle underperforming pieces among more popular items of similar style or material, since clustering "slowpokes" together makes the whole case look unwanted
C) Remove slow-moving pieces from display entirely
D) Slow-moving pieces should always be placed in the most prominent hot zone

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This specific trade guidance recognizes that grouping struggling pieces together compounds the problem rather than solving it.

**7. How does this module explain the relationship between visual merchandising principles and the case-security discipline established in Module 2?**
A) Merchandising and security are competing priorities that require constant trade-offs
B) Good merchandising and good security reinforce each other — an uncluttered, well-organized case is easier to visually audit at a glance and easier to retrieve a single piece from without disturbing the rest
C) Security discipline should always be sacrificed in favor of better merchandising
D) Merchandising principles have no real relationship to case security at all

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This module explicitly reconciles the two, showing that a cluttered, poorly organized case is actually harder to secure, not just less attractive.

**8. In the worked scenario, what specifically changed to improve engagement with the underperforming bridal case?**
A) The merchandise itself was replaced with different, more expensive rings
B) The case was reorganized into odd-numbered groupings with one clear focal point (an elevated statement ring), and lighting was repositioned to eliminate a shadow — the merchandise itself didn't change

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This illustrates that merchandising principles alone — without changing the actual inventory — can measurably affect how a case performs.
