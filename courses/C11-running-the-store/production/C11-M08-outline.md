# C11-M08 Outline: Writing SOPs Your Team Will Actually Use

## Module Metadata
- Course: C11 - Running the Store
- Module: 8 of 10
- Persona: P1/P7 (First-Time Store Leader, per official course architecture)
- Working title: Writing SOPs Your Team Will Actually Use
- Learning objective: Store leaders can identify the five specific, repeated failure modes behind unused SOPs, diagnose ignored procedures as systems-design problems rather than training failures, draft procedures using the seven-element structure (Task, Owner, Timing, Steps, Standard, Exception, Proof), document real best-practice rather than theoretical process, and maintain SOP accessibility and currency.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: Directly fulfills the C11 terminal outcome "write SOPs your team will actually use" per this project's official course architecture; the course's stated assessment explicitly requires producing "the store's own SOP binder."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames this module as the documentation layer that makes every prior module's specific correct procedure durable past any one person's memory.
2. **Why Most SOPs Fail: The Same Five Problems, Repeatedly** — Wrong author, excessive length, poor accessibility, staleness, and lack of ownership/accountability.
3. **The Deeper Point: An Ignored SOP Is Often a Design Problem, Not a Discipline Problem** — The systems-design reframe for chronically-ignored procedures.
4. **Writing an SOP That Will Actually Get Followed** — The seven-element structure (Task, Owner, Timing, Steps, Standard, Exception, Proof), with direct callbacks to how Modules 2 and 4 already modeled this structure.
5. **Document What Your Best People Already Do** — Observation-based drafting and piloting with a relative novice before full rollout.
6. **Keep It Accessible and Keep It Current** — Physical/practical accessibility and change-triggered (not calendar-triggered) revision.
7. **A Worked Scenario: The SOP Nobody Followed, Rewritten** — Illustrative synthesis showing a rewritten cash-reconciliation SOP reducing discrepancies.
8. **Practice Exercise** — Drafting a one-page SOP for an existing course procedure and testing it on a colleague.
9. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The five specific, repeated SOP-failure modes (wrong author/never done the work; excessive length; poor accessibility/buried documentation; staleness/never updated; lack of per-step ownership and accountability) are CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple independent sources specifically analyzing why SOPs go unused: tallyfy.com's "Standard operating procedures: why most fail and how to fix" article (explicitly naming all five of these exact failure modes), scribehow's "7 Common SOP Challenges and Practical Fixes" article, and ideagen.com's "The real reason your SOPs and policies aren't being followed" article. This is unusually strong, multi-source, independently corroborated consensus on a specific, named set of failure modes.
- The systems-design (not training/discipline) reframe for chronically-ignored procedures is sourced from two related articles by consultevo.com ("Why Teams Treat Ignored SOPs as Urgent Instead of Structural" and "Why SOPs Nobody Follows Are a Systems Problem, Not a People Problem"), both making the same specific diagnostic point: unclear execution points, scattered information across tools, and added friction without added value are the real, addressable causes, not simple non-compliance.
- The seven-element SOP structure (Task, Owner, Timing, Steps, Standard, Exception, Proof) is sourced directly from gopazo.com's "Retail SOP Guide," a retail-specific source explicitly proposing this exact structure. This module's direct callback connecting this structure to Module 2's opening sequence and Module 4's high-value verification protocol is this module's own original synthesis, not itself an externally-sourced claim.
- The "document what your best people already do" drafting technique and the pilot-with-a-relative-novice testing practice are sourced from shoplworks.com's "What Is a Retail SOP?" guide (explicit "Step 2: Document what your best people already do" and "Step 3: Pilot it, then revise based on feedback... look for the steps people can't actually follow") and Smartsheet's "How to Write Standard Operating Procedures" guide (explicitly recommending testing with "people who perform the tasks daily, those with different levels of knowledge, and those with no knowledge of the tasks").
- The accessibility and change-triggered-revision content is sourced from shoplworks.com's guide ("revisit it whenever something changes that affects how the task is done") and tallyfy.com's article (the "buried in shared drives" failure mode and its fix).
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
