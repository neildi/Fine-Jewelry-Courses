# M16 Huddle Card — Jade

**One headline:** "Jade" is two different minerals, and jadeite has its own treatment language. Never say "natural jade" — say **jadeite or nephrite**, and **Type A, B or C**.

**3 key points**
1. **Two minerals, one name.** Jadeite = pyroxene, granular, harder (6.5–7), capable of vivid chromium-driven imperial green. Nephrite = amphibole, felted fibrous, softer but **tougher** — the toughest natural gem material, and the prehistoric tool stone.
2. **Type B is the one that bites.** Type A = natural (surface wax is accepted). **Type B = bleached and polymer-impregnated — it degrades, yellowing and clouding with age.** Type C = dyed. IR spectroscopy is the test. Disclose every time, and never present B or C as an heirloom piece.
3. **Texture and translucency are the beauty.** Jade is a rock, not a crystal. Teach clients to look at grain evenness and how far light travels into the stone — not just colour.

**1 script to practice today**

When a client asks "is this real jade?":
"There are three questions in that. First, is it jade at all — a lot of green stones get called jade. Second, jadeite or nephrite: they're two different minerals with different prices. Third, and this is the one that really decides the value — if it's jadeite, is it Type A, B or C? Type A is natural; Type B is bleached and resin-impregnated and it will change with age. Only a lab can tell us that third one, so for anything significant we get a report."

**1 question to ask the team**

"Go through every piece we've labelled simply 'jade.' For each one: is it jadeite or nephrite, do we have a report, and do we know the Type? Anything we can't answer becomes 'jade, type unconfirmed' until we do."

**Bangle rule to remember:** measure the **hand** (thumb tucked across the palm, widest point), match to the **inner** diameter, and never force one on. And when a client brings in a broken bangle — keep the pieces, offer repair or re-cut, and let her decide. That belief means something.
