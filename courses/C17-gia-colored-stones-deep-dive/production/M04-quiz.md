# M04 Quiz — Physical Properties & Durability

**1. Durability has three dimensions. Which of the following is NOT one of them?**
a) Hardness (resistance to scratching).
b) Toughness (resistance to chipping/breaking).
c) Price.
d) Stability (resistance to heat, light, chemicals).

**2. Why is 7 an important Mohs number for daily-wear ring stones?**
a) It is the hardness of diamond.
b) Household dust contains quartz (Mohs 7), so stones at or below 7 will accumulate fine dust scratches over years of daily wear, while stones above 7 generally resist dust abrasion.
c) Stones below 7 cannot be faceted.
d) 7 is the legal threshold for "precious" stones.

**3. A client asks why you are recommending a sapphire over a tanzanite for an everyday ring when both are "precious." The best answer is:**
a) Tanzanite is synthetic.
b) Sapphire is 9 Mohs, tough, and heat-safe, while tanzanite is 6–6.5 Mohs, has perfect cleavage, is brittle, and is not an ideal everyday stone for active wear — it's a beautiful dress piece.
c) Sapphire costs more.
d) Tanzanite isn't real.

**4. Which colored stone is 8 Mohs — hard — but has perfect basal cleavage and chips easily if knocked, making it less tough than its Mohs number suggests?**
a) Sapphire.
b) Spinel.
c) Topaz.
d) Jade.

**5. Emerald is 7.5–8 Mohs, but is considered moderately durable for everyday rings. Why?**
a) It's softer than the table says.
b) Emerald commonly contains natural inclusions and fractures (jardin), and a knock can propagate a fracture; emeralds are also routinely clarity-enhanced with oils/resins that can be damaged by heat or solvents.
c) Emerald dissolves in water.
d) Emerald fades in sunlight.

**6. Which property does a refractometer measure?**
a) Specific gravity.
b) Refractive index (how much light bends as it enters the gem — a number that is diagnostic for most gem species).
c) Hardness.
d) Cleavage.

**7. Which of the following is safe in an ultrasonic cleaner?**
a) Emerald (oiled).
b) Opal.
c) Untreated ruby or sapphire.
d) Lead-glass-filled ruby.

**8. Which home-cleaning method is safe for every species in the store, including pearls, opal, and emerald?**
a) Boiling water and bleach.
b) Ammonia soak overnight.
c) Warm soapy water and a soft toothbrush.
d) Ultrasonic at home.

**9. Which of these describes pearl care correctly?**
a) Pearls are hard and durable; clean them with any household cleaner.
b) "Last on, first off" — put pearls on after perfume/hairspray, take them off first, and wipe with a soft cloth; avoid ultrasonic, steam, acid, chlorine, and abrasives.
c) Pearls survive steam cleaning perfectly well.
d) Pearls should be soaked in vinegar monthly.

**10. If a client shows you a red stone they bought online and asks you to confirm it is ruby, the right answer is:**
a) "It's red, so it's a ruby — definitely."
b) "I can tell you it looks like ruby, but I can't confirm at the counter — red spinel, garnet, glass, doublets, and synthetic ruby can all look similar. A gemologist uses a refractometer and microscope to be sure; I can send it to a lab."
c) "That's definitely a garnet."
d) "You got scammed."

---

## Answer key

1. **c** — price is not a durability property.
2. **b** — quartz in dust makes 7 the critical scratch-resistance threshold.
3. **b** — sapphire (9, tough, stable) vs tanzanite (6–6.5, cleavage, brittle) — explain the difference without sounding like you're talking her out of tanzanite.
4. **c** — topaz is the textbook example of high hardness + poor toughness due to perfect cleavage.
5. **b** — emerald's jardin + oil/resin filling reduces effective durability despite decent Mohs rating.
6. **b** — refractometer measures refractive index by critical angle.
7. **c** — untreated corundum is ultrasonic-safe; oiled emerald, opal, and glass-filled ruby are not.
8. **c** — warm soapy water + soft brush is universal and cannot harm any gem in the store.
9. **b** — last-on-first-off, soft cloth, no chemicals/heat.
10. **b** — never identify by color at the counter; offer to send to a lab.
