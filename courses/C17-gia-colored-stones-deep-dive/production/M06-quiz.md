# M06 Quiz — Inclusions and the Micro-World

**1. What does a darkfield microscope do?**
a) Heats stones to reveal treatments.
b) Illuminates the gem from the side against a dark background, making inclusions appear bright against a black field — the standard gemological microscope setup.
c) Measures refractive index.
d) Grades color automatically.

**2. Which category describes an inclusion that was already present in the host rock before the gem crystal grew around it?**
a) Epigenetic.
b) Syngenetic.
c) Protogenetic (e.g., a calcite crystal in Mogok ruby that the ruby grew around as the marble recrystallized).
d) Fluorescent.

**3. The famous jagged three-phase inclusion (liquid + gas bubble + halite salt cube) is considered a classic diagnostic for which gem origin?**
a) Zambian emerald.
b) Thai ruby.
c) Colombian emerald (Muzo, Chivor, Coscuez).
d) Kashmir sapphire.

**4. Which inclusion is the most famous diagnostic feature of Russian (Ural Mountains) demantoid garnet?**
a) Three-phase jagged inclusions.
b) Horsetail inclusions (fibrous chrysotile/byssolite radiating from a chromite crystal).
c) Curved striae.
d) Calcite crystals.

**5. In a heat-treated sapphire, which of the following is an expected altered-inclusion feature under magnification?**
a) Curved growth striae.
b) Large gas bubbles trapped in glass.
c) Dissolved or coarsened rutile silk, "snowflake" inclusions around altered crystals, and circular discoid fractures around crystals from differential thermal expansion.
d) Cobalt-blue coating on the girdle.

**6. Under 10×, you see bright blue/orange "flash effect" at surface-reaching fractures, plus large gas bubbles and flattened bubbles at the interface between filler and corundum. What do you strongly suspect?**
a) Heat-only treated sapphire.
b) Lead-glass-filled ruby (GIA-CS-06 four detection features).
c) Natural untreated ruby.
d) Hydrothermal synthetic.

**7. Which single visual feature, if seen under magnification, should make you strongly suspect a Verneuil (flame-fusion) synthetic ruby or sapphire?**
a) Curved growth striae (curved color bands) with spherical gas bubbles.
b) Three-phase inclusions.
c) Horsetail inclusions.
d) Rutile silk.

**8. Chevron / zig-zag growth patterns, nail-head spicules, and metal platelets (from the autoclave) are classic features of which synthetic?**
a) Verneuil synthetic ruby.
b) Hydrothermal synthetic emerald/beryl.
c) Synthetic opal.
d) Glass.

**9. Modern GIA colored-stone origin reports rely on which combination of evidence?**
a) Color and carat weight alone.
b) Microscopy (inclusions) plus trace-element chemistry (LA-ICP-MS) plus spectroscopy (FTIR/UV-Vis/Raman), compared against a reference collection built from field-gemology samples.
c) Just the 10× loupe.
d) X-ray only.

**10. A client asks you to confirm the origin ("is this a Kashmir sapphire?") of an unreported stone at the counter. The right answer is:**
a) "Yes, it looks like Kashmir to me — the blue is velvety."
b) "I'd need to examine it properly and likely send it to a lab to give you an origin report; origin can't be called by color or by a quick loupe look, and I don't want to guess."
c) "All blue sapphires are from Kashmir."
d) "If it's blue it must be Sri Lankan."

---

## Answer key

1. **b** — darkfield = side illumination against black, the standard gem-microscope setup.
2. **c** — protogenetic (predates host); syngenetic = forms with host; epigenetic = forms after host.
3. **c** — Colombian emerald jagged three-phase (with halite cube) is the classic.
4. **b** — horsetail inclusions radiating from chromite.
5. **c** — altered silk, snowflake crystals, discoid fractures are classic heat features. (Curved striae = Verneuil; glass bubbles = filled; cobalt coating is a different treatment.)
6. **b** — flash effect + large gas bubbles + flattened bubbles = the four GIA-CS-06 lead-glass-fill indicators (plus durability warning).
7. **a** — curved striae + spherical bubbles are the Verneuil signature. Natural corundum has straight/angular color bands, not curved.
8. **b** — hydrothermal synthetics (emerald/beryl/quartz) show chevron growth, nail-head spicules, and autoclave-metal platelets.
9. **b** — microscopy + chemistry + spectroscopy, compared to field-collected reference samples.
10. **b** — never guess origin at the counter; refer to the lab.
