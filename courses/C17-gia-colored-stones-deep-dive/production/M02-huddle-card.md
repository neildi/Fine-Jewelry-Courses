# M02 Huddle Card — How Colored Stones Form

**One headline:** Colored stones from different origins look different because they formed in different rocks — basalt, pegmatite, hydrothermal veins, marble, or sedimentary deposits. That difference is geological, not quality marketing.

**3 key points**
1. **Five environments to know:** Alkali basalt (dark sapphire), pegmatite (aquamarine, tourmaline, kunzite), hydrothermal (emerald, imperial topaz), metamorphic marble (bright ruby, Ceylon/Kashmir sapphire, jadeite in a subduction variant), and sedimentary/supergene (opal, turquoise, placer gravels).
2. **Basalt vs. metamorphic sapphire** is the most useful counter distinction: iron-rich basalt sapphires are dark/inky; low-iron metamorphic sapphires are bright royal blue with silk. Price difference reflects rarity and consumer preference, not brand.
3. **Origin stories sell.** "This Mozambique ruby formed in rock shaped 500 million years ago as Gondwana split apart" is a client-sayable story. You don't need a geology degree — you need to know what kind of rock produced the stones in your case.

**1 script to practice today**

Client: "Why is this sapphire three times the price of that one when they look almost the same?"

Associate: "They're both natural sapphire, but they formed in different rock. The brighter one grew in marble-like rock low in iron, which gives it that vivid blue with fine silk — stones like that are rarer. The darker one formed in iron-rich volcanic basalt, which gives a deep navy look many clients love. Let me show you both in daylight — which color do you actually prefer?"

**1 question to ask the team**

"Which sapphires in our case are basalt-field (Thai/Cambodia/Australian) and which are metamorphic (Ceylon/Madagascar/Burma)? Let's pull one of each so we can show clients the difference side by side."
