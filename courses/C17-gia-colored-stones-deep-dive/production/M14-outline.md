## Title
**Emerald: Black Shale, Jardin, and the Treatment You Must Disclose**

## Scenario
A client inherits her grandmother's emerald ring, takes it to a jeweler for cleaning, and it comes back dull and cloudy — the ultrasonic has stripped the oil out of the fissures. The stone is not damaged beyond repair; it needs re-oiling and an apology. It is also the single most common emerald complaint in the trade, and it is entirely preventable: emerald is the one major species where "put it in the ultrasonic" is always the wrong answer.

## H2 outline
1. **The ultrasonic story (hook ~250w).** Establishes the module's hard rule and sets up why: emerald is characteristically fractured, that fracturing is routinely treated with a filler, and the filler is what cleaning destroys.

2. **Beryl, chromium and vanadium (~300w).** Emerald is the green variety of beryl (Be₃Al₂Si₆O₁₈). Green comes from **Cr³⁺ and/or V³⁺** substituting in the aluminium site; iron, when present, adds blue and *quenches* the red fluorescence you see in chromium-dominant stones — which is why the Colombian/Zambian difference is partly a fluorescence difference. Constants: Mohs 7.5–8, RI ~1.577–1.583, birefringence 0.006, SG ~2.72, uniaxial (–), dichroic (blue-green / yellow-green). Toughness is the weak point, not hardness: emerald is brittle and almost always fractured.

3. **Colombia: the black-shale anomaly (~500w).** The only major emerald deposit in the world hosted by **sedimentary rock** — Cretaceous black shale, with mineralization in calcite–fluorite–pyrite–albite veins. This is why Colombian material is geologically distinct and why it looks the way it does (warm, slightly yellowish-green, low iron, red under UV from chromium). Mines and characters: **Muzo** (the benchmark, slightly yellowish-green), **Coscuez**, **Chivor/Somondoco**, **La Pita**, **Peñas Blancas**. Two Colombian specialties: **trapiche emerald** (a six-rayed pattern of dark carbonaceous impurity between growth sectors) and ***gota de aceite*** ("drop of oil" — a roiled, oil-in-water growth structure prized as a Colombian indicator).

4. **Beyond Colombia (~700w):**
   - **Zambia (Kagem, Musakashi)** — the world's largest emerald producer by volume; schist-hosted, more iron, a cooler bluish-green, typically less fluorescence than Colombian. Gemfields operates Kagem and runs formal auctions.
   - **Brazil** — Belmont (an integrated, mine-to-market operation and one of the few traceable programs in colored stones), Nova Era, Itabira, Goiás, Bahia; pegmatite/schist-related.
   - **Ethiopia** — emerald found near Shakiso/Kenticha in Oromia, reaching the market around 2016–2017; noteworthy because the material is chromium-dominant with low iron and can look Colombian, which makes origin reporting important.
   - **Afghanistan (Panjshir)** — artisanal tunnels, high-quality small stones, famously difficult logistics.
   - **Zimbabwe (Sandawana)** — small but vividly colored stones.
   - **Pakistan (Swat/Mingora), Russia (Ural Mountains), Madagascar (Mananjary), Tanzania, Nigeria, China** — secondary to minor sources.
   - Note: **Hpakant is a jadeite locality, not an emerald locality** — don't confuse the two Myanmar names.

5. **Clarity enhancement: the oil-to-polymer spectrum (~600w).** Because virtually all emerald is fractured, clarity enhancement is standard. Traditional **cedarwood oil** has a refractive index close to beryl's, so it makes fissures nearly invisible; it also dries out over time, which is why emerald needs periodic re-oiling. Modern fillers include **synthetic resins and polymers** (epoxy, and trade products such as Opticon/ExCel/PermaSafe), which are more durable but can yellow, craze, or be harder to remove. **GIA's F0–F3 scale** (McClure et al. 1999) classifies the *degree* of enhancement: F0 none, F1 minor, F2 moderate, F3 significant. Detection: careful microscopy in reflected light often shows a slightly different luster or a flash along filled fissures, and in some light the filler reads as a faint iridescence. Disclosure is mandatory at every level, and the F-grade belongs on the invoice.

6. **Synthetics: flux and hydrothermal (~500w).** Emerald has been synthesized commercially since the mid-20th century, and both major methods are convincing:
   - **Flux-grown** (Chatham, Gilson, Lennix, Biron, Seiko and others): flux fingerprints and veils, phenakite crystals, sometimes platinum or metallic inclusions from the crucible; RI/SG generally overlap natural.
   - **Hydrothermal** (Lechleitner, Linde, Regency, Tairus, Malossi): seed plates, chevron or nail-head spicules of phenakite, growth zoning — and, importantly, **RI and SG noticeably lower than natural** (roughly 1.560–1.570 and ~2.65 vs natural ~1.577–1.583 and ~2.72). That lower reading is the fastest screening test you have.
   - Neither method produces the natural inclusion scenes you learned in M06 (mica plates, actinolite/tremolite needles, pyrite, calcite, three-phase inclusions in Colombian material) — absence of *any* natural inclusions is itself suspicious.
   - **Soudé emerald** (a doublet or triplet with green cement in the middle) is an assembled stone, must be disclosed as assembled, and must never be cleaned by immersion.

7. **Care: the absolute rule (~250w).** **No ultrasonic. No steam. Ever.** Warm soapy water and a soft brush only; dry immediately. No solvents, no acids, no heat, no sudden temperature change, no pressure setting. Removal before any bench work that involves heat; re-oiling intervals depend on wear (annual for a ring is a reasonable client-facing rule). Have the stone checked and re-oiled by a qualified bench jeweler or gemologist, and never let anyone promise a filled emerald is "as good as new" after stripping.

8. **Value factors (~350w).** Color first (most prized is a slightly bluish-green to pure green of medium-dark tone and strong saturation; the trade's "Muzo green" descriptor). Then clarity — with the emerald exception: **inclusions are expected and forgiven**; what you pay for is *transparency* and liveliness, not loupe-cleanliness. Then origin (Colombia commands the largest premium, Zambia and Brazil anchor the commercial market, Ethiopia is a newer premium-quality entrant). Then size and cut (emerald cut exists to protect a brittle stone and to show off color), then treatment (F0 and F1 command real premiums over F2/F3).
