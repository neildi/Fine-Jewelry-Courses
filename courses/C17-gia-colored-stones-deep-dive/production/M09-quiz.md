# M09 Quiz — Treatments and Disclosure

**1. Under the FTC Jewelry Guides (per Overton 2004), when a salesperson uses the word "ruby" or "emerald" without qualifier, what should the buyer be able to assume?**
a) That the stone is synthetic.
b) That the stone is natural, and that any non-permanent or value-affecting treatment has been clearly disclosed before sale.
c) That the stone is glass filled unless stated otherwise.
d) That the stone is untreated in every case.

**2. Which treatment produces flash effect (bright blue/orange flashes at fractures in reflected light), large gas bubbles, and flattened bubbles at a filler/corundum interface?**
a) Standard heat treatment.
b) Lead-glass filling of ruby.
c) Irradiation of blue topaz.
d) Beeswax coating on turquoise.

**3. What do F0, F1, F2, and F3 indicate on a GIA emerald report?**
a) Four colors of emerald.
b) The four major emerald sources.
c) The GIA clarity-enhancement grades: F0 = no indication of clarity enhancement; F1 = minor; F2 = moderate; F3 = significant (oil/resin filling).
d) Four different emerald cuts.

**4. Why can't beryllium-lattice-diffused sapphires be reliably identified by a 10× loupe alone?**
a) Because they have the same chemical composition as natural sapphire — Be penetrates the entire lattice rather than sitting in a thin surface layer, so there is no tell-tale color concentration at facet edges; detection requires LA-ICP-MS/LIBS chemistry.
b) Because they are always the same color as natural sapphire.
c) Because they are always more included than natural.
d) Because Be diffusion makes them softer.

**5. Which of the following is the safest cleaning method for oiled emerald?**
a) Boil in ammonia.
b) Ultrasonic cleaner.
c) Steam cleaner.
d) Warm soapy water with a soft brush, followed by a soft cloth; avoid ultrasonic/steam/harsh chemicals; annual re-oiling/checkup.

**6. Which of the following is TRUE of commercial irradiated blue topaz?**
a) It is dangerously radioactive.
b) The blue color is induced by irradiation (usually followed by annealing), is permanent in normal wear, and commercial material is held until residual radiation is at safe background levels per NRC regulation; ultrasonic/steam are generally safe.
c) The color fades within weeks.
d) It is a simulant, not real topaz.

**7. What visible features suggest a coated tanzanite or mystic topaz?**
a) Three-phase inclusions.
b) Horsetail inclusions.
c) Bubbles in a thin coating layer; iridescent film on the girdle or pavilion facets; color that abrades at facet edges.
d) Curved striae.

**8. B-jade (bleached jadeite) is best detected by which method?**
a) The tooth test.
b) Visual appearance alone.
c) Infrared (IR) spectroscopy, which detects the polymer resin; visual features can include pitted/orange-peel surface and overly clean texture but are not definitive.
d) Smell.

**9. A lead-glass-filled ruby is brought in for a prong retipping (torch work). What is the bench jeweler's concern?**
a) The ruby will fade.
b) The lead glass melts at ordinary jewelers' torch temperatures; the stone can be destroyed during repair unless removed from the mounting first.
c) The ruby will become more valuable.
d) There is no concern.

**10. When you disclose a treatment at the counter, which two pieces of information should you always communicate?**
a) The color and carat weight.
b) The price and the salesperson's commission.
c) What the treatment is, and what (if any) care rules or durability limits come with it — plus any price effect relative to untreated material.
d) The history of the mine.

---

## Answer key

1. **b** — unqualified species name implies natural; treatments requiring special care/disclosure must be stated.
2. **b** — lead-glass fill (the four features in GIA-CS-06).
3. **c** — F0–F3 clarity enhancement grading.
4. **a** — Be penetrates the full lattice; chemistry required for ID.
5. **d** — warm soapy water, soft brush, annual check.
6. **b** — permanent, safe, normal care after regulatory holding period.
7. **c** — coating bubbles, iridescent film, abraded edges.
8. **c** — IR for polymer; visual features are suggestive only.
9. **b** — glass melts at torch temperatures; stone must come out before retipping.
10. **c** — what it is, what care it needs, and the price/value context.
