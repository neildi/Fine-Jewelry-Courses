# M05 Quiz — Color and Light

**1. What is the approximate wavelength range of visible light?**
a) 100–300 nm.
b) 380–750 nm.
c) 1000–5000 nm.
d) 1–10 meters.

**2. Which statement best describes where a gem's color comes from?**
a) Color is a dye inside the gem.
b) A gem's color is the mix of visible wavelengths it transmits/reflects after selectively absorbing other wavelengths — change the light source and the apparent color can shift.
c) Color is entirely an illusion in the viewer's eye with no physical basis.
d) Color is determined only by the gem's weight.

**3. Which trace element produces the red of ruby AND the green of emerald?**
a) Iron.
b) Titanium.
c) Chromium (Cr³⁺).
d) Copper.

**4. The blue of a classic blue sapphire is produced primarily by which mechanism?**
a) Copper ions.
b) Chromium replacing aluminum.
c) Intervalence charge transfer between Fe²⁺ and Ti⁴⁺.
d) A color center from irradiation.

**5. Tanzanite is famously trichroic. What does that mean in practice?**
a) It is three times as hard as other gems.
b) It shows three different colors along three different crystal directions, which is why it can look blue, violet, or brownish depending on viewing angle and light — and why the cutter must orient the table carefully to show blue face-up.
c) It changes from green to red in different lights, like alexandrite.
d) It contains three different minerals.

**6. How does pleochroism differ from the alexandrite (color-change) effect?**
a) Pleochroism is about viewing direction under a single light source; the alexandrite effect is about the stone appearing different colors under different light sources (daylight vs incandescent).
b) They are the same thing.
c) Pleochroism only occurs in synthetics.
d) The alexandrite effect only occurs in diamonds.

**7. What causes the six-rayed star on a star ruby or star sapphire?**
a) A surface engraving.
b) Light reflecting from three sets of oriented rutile-silk needles (at 120° to one another) inside the cabochon.
c) Fluorescence.
d) A coating applied to the back.

**8. Play-of-color in precious opal is produced by which optical mechanism?**
a) Chromium ions absorbing yellow.
b) Diffraction and interference of white light by a regular three-dimensional lattice of uniform silica spheres about 150–300 nm in diameter.
c) Copper platelets.
d) Fluorescence from opal cement.

**9. Which of the following is TRUE about color-change "alexandrite"?**
a) Any color-change gemstone should be called alexandrite.
b) True alexandrite is chrysoberyl colored by chromium; color-change garnet, color-change sapphire, and color-change diaspore/zultanite are different species and should be identified by their correct species name.
c) Alexandrite is a synthetic stone.
d) Alexandrite is only found in Russia.

**10. Before closing a colored-stone sale, the best lighting practice is to:**
a) Show the stone only under the brightest showcase spotlight.
b) Show the stone under a single warm light to make it look rich.
c) Show the stone under multiple light sources — including a daylight-equivalent (6500K) source or window light — so the client sees how it will look outside the store.
d) Dim the lights so the client can't see inclusions.

---

## Answer key

1. **b** — 380–750 nm (violet to red).
2. **b** — selective absorption + transmission/reflection; color is not purely "in the stone" but depends on the spectrum illuminating it.
3. **c** — chromium produces both ruby red and emerald green; the surrounding lattice geometry shifts the absorption spectrum.
4. **c** — Fe²⁺/Ti⁴⁺ intervalence charge transfer produces the classic blue (iron alone produces yellow/green).
5. **b** — trichroism (three colors in three directions); orientation by the cutter and careful multi-light showing at the counter matter.
6. **a** — viewing direction vs. light source — two different optical effects.
7. **b** — oriented rutile silk in three directions; cabochon must be cut perpendicular to c-axis to produce a centered, sharp star.
8. **b** — ordered silica spheres diffract/interfere with light to produce the colored flashes; sphere size determines which colors are seen.
9. **b** — true alexandrite is Cr-colored chrysoberyl; other color-change stones must be labeled by their actual species.
10. **c** — show in multiple lights, including daylight equivalent, before close — this is the protocol that prevents "it looked different at home" returns.
