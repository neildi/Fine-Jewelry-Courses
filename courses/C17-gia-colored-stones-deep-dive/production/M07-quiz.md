# M07 Quiz — Cut

**1. How does colored-stone cutting differ from diamond cutting?**
a) Colored stones are cut larger than diamonds.
b) Diamonds are cut for light return (brilliance/fire) at defined ideal angles; colored stones are cut to maximize face-up color first, with weight retention and symmetry as additional priorities — light return is secondary.
c) There is no difference; they use the same cut grade.
d) Colored stones are never cut by hand.

**2. Why are dark basalt sapphires often cut with shallower pavilions than pale Ceylon sapphires?**
a) To save rough.
b) To make them less likely to chip.
c) To let more light pass through and reduce extinction, preventing the stone from appearing over-dark face-up.
d) Because shallower cuts cost less.

**3. A strongly pleochroic stone like tanzanite must be oriented by the cutter so that:**
a) The table faces perpendicular to the optic axis, showing the rich violet-blue face-up rather than the brownish/purplish pleochroic direction.
b) The table is parallel to the c-axis for extra weight.
c) The girdle is as thin as possible.
d) The culet is off-center.

**4. Which orientation rule applies to a star ruby or cat's-eye chrysoberyl cabochon?**
a) The dome must be cut off-axis to maximize weight.
b) The cab base must be perpendicular to the c-axis so the rutile silk reflects a centered star or eye on the dome.
c) It doesn't matter — stars appear regardless of orientation.
d) The dome must be faceted.

**5. A "window" (fish-eye) in a faceted colored stone is caused by:**
a) A coating.
b) Pavilion angles that are too shallow for the gem's refractive index — light passes straight through instead of reflecting back, creating a see-through area in the center.
c) An inclusion.
d) Heat treatment.

**6. Which of the following best describes "extinction" in a colored stone?**
a) A vivid sparkle in daylight.
b) Dark, near-black dead patches face-up, typically from overly deep pavilions, dark bodycolor, or orientation effects.
c) The phenomenon in star sapphires.
d) An oil-filled fracture.

**7. Why are many high-value rubies, sapphires and emeralds cut with thick girdles and deep pavilions ("bellys"), even when that creates a window or extinction?**
a) Cutters are unskilled.
b) Weight retention — because fine colored stones are sold by the carat and rough is rare/expensive, cutters often retain extra weight even at some cost to appearance, especially on high per-carat goods.
c) The thick girdle improves color.
d) All colored stones must be cut that way.

**8. A client has inherited a 2.5 ct commercial Thai sapphire in a heavy native cut with a large window and dark extinction. She asks whether recutting it is a good idea. The best advice is:**
a) Always recut it immediately.
b) Never recut colored stones.
c) Refer her to a gemologist for a before/after value estimate: recutting can transform a poorly cut stone but costs carat weight, and it's worth doing only if the value/appearance gain exceeds the weight lost plus labor.
d) Recut it yourself at the bench.

**9. What does "calibrated" mean in commercial colored stones?**
a) The stone has a lab report.
b) The stone is cut to standard dimensions (e.g., 7×5 mm oval, 6 mm round) that drop into mass-produced mountings without custom setting — hence a calibrated premium.
c) The stone is a synthetic.
d) The stone was cut by a robot.

**10. Which of the following is a proper way to describe a colored stone's cut at the counter?**
a) "It has an excellent cut grade, same as a diamond."
b) "It faces up bright and evenly colored — the cutter oriented it to show the best blue, and the symmetry and polish are good — let me show you in daylight."
c) "It has no window at all so it's the best."
d) "The deeper the better."

---

## Answer key

1. **b** — colored stones are cut for face-up color first; diamonds are cut to ideal angles for light return.
2. **c** — shallower pavilions reduce extinction in dark material.
3. **a** — table perpendicular to optic axis for best face-up color in pleochroic stones.
4. **b** — cab base perpendicular to c-axis; misorientation slides the star off-center.
5. **b** — shallow pavilion = light leaks out = window.
6. **b** — dark dead patches (deep pavilions/over-dark material). Some extinction is normal in colored stones; full extinction in daylight is a problem.
7. **b** — weight retention on high-value rough; the cutter balances weight vs. appearance.
8. **c** — professional before/after value estimate; recut only when the math works.
9. **b** — standardized dimensions fitting mass-produced mountings.
10. **b** — describe face-up appearance (color, life, symmetry) in plain language; colored stones don't carry a single diamond-style cut grade.
