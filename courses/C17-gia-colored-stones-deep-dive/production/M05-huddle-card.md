# M05 Huddle Card — Color and Light

**One headline:** Color is not inside a gem — color is the light the gem doesn't absorb. Change the light, change the apparent color. Always show colored stones in multiple lights before close.

**3 key points**
1. **Five color mechanisms (Fritsch–Rossman):** transition-metal ions (Cr gives ruby red, Cu gives Paraíba neon blue, Fe gives peridot green), charge transfer (Fe²⁺+Ti⁴⁺ gives sapphire blue), color centers (irradiated blue topaz, smoky quartz), band theory (blue diamond from boron), and physical phenomena (dispersion, scattering, diffraction/interference — opal play-of-color, star, cat's-eye, moonstone schiller).
2. **Pleochroism ≠ color-change.** Pleochroism = different colors from different viewing angles under the same light (tanzanite, iolite, andalusite are trichroic). Alexandrite effect = different colors under different light sources (daylight vs. incandescent). Both must be shown deliberately.
3. **Hue/tone/saturation** is the professional color vocabulary — not "nice blue." Hue = color name (violetish-blue, greenish-blue); Tone = lightness/darkness; Saturation = intensity/grayness.

**1 script to practice today**

When showing any colored stone, after the client has admired it under showcase lighting:
"Let me also show you this in natural light — colored stones look slightly different in daylight than they do under our spotlights, and I want you to see exactly how it will look when you're wearing it to work or to dinner."

(Then walk to a window or use the 6500K lamp. If it's a tanzanite, alexandrite, color-change garnet or iolite, also show it in warm light and present the shift as a feature.)

**1 question to ask the team**

"Do we have a 6500K daylight lamp at the counter for showing colored stones? If not, who is walking stones to the window and how often — can we make that a standard step before any colored-stone close?"
