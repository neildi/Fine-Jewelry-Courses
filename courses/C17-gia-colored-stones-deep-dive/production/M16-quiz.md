# M16 Quiz — Jade

**1. What is the mineralogical difference between jadeite and nephrite?**
a) Jadeite is an amphibole, nephrite is a pyroxene.
b) Jadeite is a pyroxene (NaAlSi₂O₆); nephrite is an amphibole in the tremolite–actinolite series.
c) They are the same mineral with different colours.
d) Jadeite is a variety of quartz.

**2. Which statement about hardness and toughness is correct?**
a) Jadeite is both harder and tougher.
b) Nephrite is both harder and tougher.
c) Jadeite is harder; nephrite is tougher — among the toughest natural gem materials.
d) Nephrite is harder; jadeite is tougher.

**3. What causes the finest "imperial" green in jadeite?**
a) Iron.
b) Chromium.
c) Manganese.
d) Copper.

**4. Which is the world's premier commercial source of top-quality jadeite?**
a) Hetian, Xinjiang.
b) British Columbia, Canada.
c) Hpakant, Kachin State, Myanmar.
d) Cowell, South Australia.

**5. What is Type B jade?**
a) Natural, untreated jadeite.
b) Jadeite that has been bleached and polymer-impregnated.
c) Dyed jadeite.
d) A synthetic jade.

**6. What is the definitive test for Type B jade?**
a) A loupe.
b) A refractive index reading.
c) Infrared spectroscopy, which shows the polymer absorption peaks.
d) A scratch test.

**7. Which of the following is true of Type B jade over time?**
a) It becomes more valuable.
b) The polymer resin can yellow, craze and cloud — Type B is not permanently stable.
c) It turns into Type A.
d) Nothing changes.

**8. How do you size a bangle?**
a) Measure the wrist and match to the outer diameter.
b) Measure the widest part of the hand with the thumb tucked across the palm, and match that to the bangle's inner diameter.
c) Any bangle fits any hand if you use lubricant.
d) Measure the length of the fingers.

**9. Which of these is commonly sold as "jade" but is not jade?**
a) Serpentine/bowenite ("new jade," "Korean jade").
b) Aventurine quartz ("Indian jade").
c) Vesuvianite/californite ("California jade").
d) All of the above.

**10. What is the right response when a client brings in a broken jade bangle?**
a) Laugh and offer to throw the pieces away.
b) Take it seriously — a common belief is that the bangle took the blow meant for the wearer; offer repair or re-cut options and let the client decide.
c) Tell her it was probably fake anyway.
d) Refuse to look at it.

---

## Answer key

1. **b** — Jadeite is a pyroxene; nephrite is an amphibole in the tremolite–actinolite series. Two different minerals sharing one trade name.
2. **c** — Jadeite is harder (Mohs ~6.5–7 vs ~6–6.5); nephrite's felted fibre structure makes it the tougher material, which is why it was the prehistoric tool stone.
3. **b** — Chromium. Iron contributes to duller and bluish greens; manganese is associated with lavender. The vivid imperial green is a jadeite colour, not available in nephrite.
4. **c** — Hpakant in Kachin State, Myanmar. Guatemala (Motagua Valley) is the other commercially significant jadeite source; Hetian, BC, Cowell and pounamu are all nephrite.
5. **b** — Bleached to remove iron staining, then polymer-impregnated to restore translucency and hold the structure. Type C is dyed; B+C is common.
6. **c** — IR spectroscopy is definitive. At the counter you can only *suspect* from waxy luster, an orange-peel pitted surface under magnification, and age-related yellowing.
7. **b** — Type B degrades. This is the key client-facing fact: it is a legitimate disclosed product, but it is not an heirloom material.
8. **b** — You are measuring the hand, not the wrist, and matching to the **inner** diameter. Never force a bangle on.
9. **d** — All three are common simulants, along with hydrogrossular garnet, dyed chalcedony/quartzite, glass and plastic. Maw-sit-sit is a related rock but not jadeite.
10. **b** — Cultural context is part of the job. Handle it respectfully, offer options, and let the client decide what happens to the pieces.
