# M11 Huddle Card — Ruby

**One headline:** "Ruby" covers the widest price band in the gem trade — from untreated Mogok material at six figures a carat to lead-glass composite at tens of dollars. Know which one you are holding, and disclose the difference before the client has to ask.

**3 key points**
1. **Marble vs basalt explains almost everything.** Marble-hosted (Mogok, Montepuez, Luc Yen, Winza, Jegdalek) is low-iron and glows red under UV. Basalt-hosted (Pailin, Chanthaburi, Muling) is iron-rich, darker and inert. Fluorescence is a clue to origin — never a proof.
2. **Heat is standard; glass is a different product.** Standard heat is permanent, industry-standard, and carries normal corundum care. Lead-glass filling is a manufactured composite: no acid, no ultrasonic, no steam, no torch, never recut — and mandatory disclosure.
3. **Color first, origin second.** A mediocre stone from a famous source is still mediocre. The best Mozambique ruby beats a poor Mogok ruby, and saying so honestly is what survives a second opinion.

**1 script to practice today**

When a client arrives with a cheap "ruby" she found online:
"That's a different product that shares the name. Most rubies under a few hundred dollars a carat are either heavily treated, glass-filled composite, or lab-created — and glass-filled ruby can't go in an ultrasonic or near a torch. Here's what changes the price: treatment, origin, whether there's a lab report, and the color itself. Let me show you the stone in your budget that I'd actually put in a ring she wears every day."

**1 question to ask the team**

"Pull every ruby in the case priced over $2,000. Which ones have a lab report, and which are we describing as 'Burmese' or 'unheated' on nothing but a supplier's verbal claim? Anything we can't document goes back to 'ruby, heated' until we can."

**Care rule to repeat until it sticks:** Filled ruby = warm soapy water only. No dip, no ultrasonic, no steam, no torch, no recut.
