# M08 Huddle Card — Synthetics, Simulants, and Assembled Stones

**One headline:** "Synthetic" means same chemistry/structure as the natural gem but grown in a lab; "simulant" is a different material that just looks similar; "assembled" means two or three pieces bonded together. All three must be disclosed.

**3 key points**
1. **Verneuil red flag:** curved growth striae + spherical gas bubbles = flame-fusion synthetic corundum until proven otherwise. Flux synthetics show wispy veils and platinum platelets; hydrothermal synthetics show chevron growth and nail-head spicules.
2. **Doublet/triplet red flag:** always look at the girdle in profile — a join line, bubbles in glue, or a red ring (GTD) reveals a composite.
3. **"Ruby" by itself means natural ruby.** Use "synthetic ruby" / "lab-created ruby" / "lab-grown ruby" for man-made; brand names (Chatham, Kyocera, Inamori) require "synthetic/created" alongside. When in doubt, refer to the lab — never identify by color alone.

**1 script to practice today**

When a client asks "is this real?":
"'Real' is a tricky word — the more important distinction is natural vs. lab-grown. This one is natural (mined) [OR: this one is lab-created, same chemical composition but grown in a factory, which is why it's priced differently; OR: this one is a doublet — a thin slice of natural opal with a protective top and backing, making it more durable for everyday wear]. Either way I'll show you exactly what you're buying."

**1 question to ask the team**

"Which of our current vendors/suppliers use the terms 'created,' 'lab-grown,' 'Chatham,' or 'doublet/triplet' on their invoices — and are we using those same words on our tags and at the counter consistently? Let's do a quick tag audit this week."
