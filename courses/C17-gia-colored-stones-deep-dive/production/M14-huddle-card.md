# M14 Huddle Card — Emerald

**One headline:** Emerald's problem is toughness, not hardness — it is brittly fractured, the fractures are filled, and the filler is the most fragile part of the stone. No ultrasonic. No steam. Ever.

**3 key points**
1. **Colombia is geologically unique.** Nearly all emerald forms in pegmatite or schist; Colombia's forms in Cretaceous black shale, which is why it is low-iron, warm, and red under UV. Zambia leads the world on volume (Kagem), Colombia on premium.
2. **Learn the F-scale and say it out loud.** F0 none, F1 minor, F2 moderate, F3 significant. F1 is normal — presenting it as a defect is inaccurate. F0 and F1 carry real premiums, so the grade goes on the invoice and in the conversation.
3. **Synthetics hide in plain sight, but the constants give them away.** Flux = fingerprints, phenakite, platinum. Hydrothermal = seed plates, phenakite spicules, zoning — **and RI ~1.560–1.570 / SG ~2.65 versus natural ~1.577–1.583 / ~2.72**. An emerald with no natural inclusions at all is suspicious.

**1 script to practice today**

When handing over an emerald:
"This is an F1 — minor clarity enhancement, which is completely normal for emerald and it's stated right here on the report. One thing I want to be firm about: no ultrasonic and no steam, ever. Warm soapy water and a soft brush. The stone is hard enough to wear every day, but it's brittle, and the treatment is the part that can't take the heat. Bring it in once a year and we'll check and re-oil it."

**1 question to ask the team**

"Find the F-grade on every emerald in the case. Any stone where we can't produce that grade from a report or supplier invoice gets pulled to 'emerald, clarity enhanced' in our descriptions until we can."

**One trap to stop walking into:** Ethiopian emerald (Shakiso/Kenticha) is chromium-dominant and genuinely Colombian-looking. "Looks Colombian" is not "is Colombian." Report or nothing.
