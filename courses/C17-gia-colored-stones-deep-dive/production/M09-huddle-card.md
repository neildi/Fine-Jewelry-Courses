# M09 Huddle Card — Treatments and Disclosure

**One headline:** Treatments are common, many are permanent and industry-standard — but treatments that change durability or materially affect value (glass fill, lattice diffusion, coatings, dyeing, polymer-impregnation, assembled stones) must be disclosed before sale, with the care rules attached.

**3 key points**
1. **Heat-only corundum is permanent, standard, and cared for like untreated corundum.** Most commercial ruby/sapphire is heated — you don't need to alarm clients, but you should say so on the paperwork.
2. **Lead-glass-filled ruby is a composite**, not a normal ruby. Flash effect + bubbles = refer for confirmation. Warm soapy water only; no ultrasonic/steam/acid/torch work on these stones.
3. **Emeralds are almost always clarity-enhanced (oiled/resin-filled F0–F3).** This is standard — disclose the grade, give warm-soapy-water care, and offer annual re-oiling/checkups.

**1 script to practice today**

When selling any stone with a treatment on the report:
"This stone is [heated / clarity-enhanced / oiled / filled / coated / irradiated], which is [standard for the species / an enhancement that makes it more affordable]. It's permanent / stable, and care is straightforward: [care instructions for this treatment]. I'll put that on your receipt so you have it."

**1 question to ask the team**

"Pull every lead-glass-filled, coated, dyed, or Be-diffused stone in our case today. Do we have the correct disclosure language on every tag and on the receipt? Do our care sheets match the treatment? Let's fix any gaps before another filled ruby goes home without its care instructions."
