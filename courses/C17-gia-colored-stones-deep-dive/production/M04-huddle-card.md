# M04 Huddle Card — Physical Properties & Durability

**One headline:** Durability has three parts — hardness (scratch resistance), toughness (chip/break resistance), and stability (heat/light/chemical resistance). Many "precious" colored stones are strong in one dimension and weak in another; your job is to match the stone to the client's lifestyle, not to the client's color preference alone.

**3 key points**
1. **Mohs ≠ everything.** Topaz (8 Mohs) chips from cleavage; emerald (7.5–8) is fracture-prone from its inclusions; nephrite jade (6–6.5) is one of the toughest gems. Dust (quartz, 7 Mohs) scratches everything at or below 7 over time.
2. **Cleaning defaults.** Warm soapy water + soft toothbrush is safe for every species in the store. Ultrasonic/steam is appropriate for hard, tough, untreated, unfilled stones (corundum, spinel, garnet, aquamarine, diamond) — NOT for emerald (oiled), opal, tanzanite, lead-glass-filled ruby, pearl, amber, malachite, or turquoise.
3. **Pre-sale care conversation beats a post-sale return.** Before handing over an opal, tanzanite, emerald, or pearl ring, say one sentence about wear and offer a protective setting if appropriate. That one sentence prevents the "it came back cracked" scenario.

**1 script to practice today**

Client: "I love this tanzanite for my engagement ring — can it be an everyday ring?"

Associate: "It's absolutely beautiful, and I want you to know what you're getting into. Tanzanite sits lower on the hardness scale than sapphire and has a natural cleavage plane, so a hard knock can chip it — it's really a dress piece rather than a gym-and-dishes ring. If you love the color for an everyday ring, I'd set it in a bezel for protection, or I can show you sapphires in a similar blue that can take daily wear without much thought. Either way you'll have something beautiful — the choice is really about how you live in the ring."

**1 question to ask the team**

"Pull our top three selling colored stones right now — for each one, what do we currently say about durability and care at point of sale? Let's make sure we're all saying the same thing and that we're not missing warnings on tanzanite, emerald, opal, or pearl."
