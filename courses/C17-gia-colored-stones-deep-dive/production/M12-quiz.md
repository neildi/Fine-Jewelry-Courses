# M12 Quiz — Blue Sapphire

**1. What produces the blue color in blue sapphire?**
a) Chromium, the same element that colors ruby.
b) Iron and titanium together, through intervalence charge transfer.
c) Copper.
d) Cobalt.

**2. Why does heat treatment change the blue in corundum?**
a) It adds chromium.
b) It removes all inclusions.
c) Because the color depends on the oxidation-state pairing of Fe and Ti, which heat can shift.
d) It dyes the stone.

**3. What is geuda?**
a) A variety of synthetic sapphire.
b) Sri Lankan milky corundum rough that becomes good blue after heat treatment.
c) The trade name for Kashmir material.
d) A type of star sapphire.

**4. Which statement about Kashmir sapphire is accurate?**
a) It is currently mined in large volumes.
b) It was discovered in the 1880s and was essentially exhausted by the 1930s.
c) It is the standard commercial-grade material sold in most jewelry stores.
d) It is the trade name for dark Australian sapphire.

**5. What is the key difference between Yogo and Rock Creek Montana sapphire?**
a) Yogo is a synthetic; Rock Creek is natural.
b) Yogo is naturally a uniform cornflower blue needing no heat, but small; Rock Creek material is routinely heat treated.
c) They are the same deposit under two names.
d) Rock Creek is darker and never treated.

**6. Which of these is a detection feature of cobalt-glass-filled sapphire?**
a) Curved growth striae.
b) A strong red fluorescence under long-wave UV.
c) Bright blue/orange flash effect along fractures, plus large and flattened gas bubbles.
d) A conchoidal fracture.

**7. Why is beryllium-diffused sapphire difficult to identify?**
a) Because the color is only on the surface and fades.
b) Because it penetrates the entire lattice and detection requires trace-element chemistry (LA-ICP-MS/LIBS), not a loupe.
c) Because it is identical to natural sapphire in every respect.
d) Because it only occurs in ruby.

**8. Which synthetic-sapphire feature is the classic giveaway at 10×?**
a) Strong pleochroism.
b) Curved growth striae and rounded gas bubbles.
c) A cobalt spectrum.
d) Rutile silk.

**9. What is the honest answer when a client asks "is this sapphire heated?" and there is no lab report?**
a) "No, all our sapphires are unheated."
b) "Yes, everything is heated."
c) "I don't know — the report is the answer. Let's find out."
d) "It doesn't matter."

**10. Which pairing of trade color descriptor to source character is most conventional?**
a) Velvet = Ceylon; cornflower = Kashmir; royal = Montana.
b) Cornflower = Ceylon (brighter, lighter); royal = Burmese (richer, more saturated); velvet = Kashmir (soft, glowing).
c) Royal = Ceylon; velvet = Australia; cornflower = Burma.
d) All three terms mean the same thing.

---

## Answer key

1. **b** — Iron *and* titanium together, via Fe²⁺–Ti⁴⁺ intervalence charge transfer. Neither element does it alone; iron alone tends toward weak, greenish or too-dark blue.
2. **c** — Because the chromophore is an Fe/Ti ion pair, changing the oxidation state changes the color. This is why heat is so effective and why geuda transforms.
3. **b** — Geuda is Sri Lankan milky, low-value rough that heat treatment turns into attractive blue. Much affordable blue sapphire began as geuda.
4. **b** — Discovered in the 1880s at high altitude with a very short mining season; essentially exhausted by the 1930s. Today's Kashmir material is estate and collection material, and the report is everything.
5. **b** — Yogo (igneous dike) is naturally cornflower and needs no heat, but the rough is small. Rock Creek, Gem Mountain and Missouri River are alluvial gravels whose rough is routinely heated.
6. **c** — Flash effect plus gas bubbles, with blue concentrated along the fracture network; the cobalt spectrum is the confirmatory lab test.
7. **b** — Be penetrates the whole lattice at high temperature. There is no rim to see. Detection is chemical, not visual — so send it to a lab rather than guessing.
8. **b** — Curved striae and rounded/elongated gas bubbles are the Verneuil signature. Note that this distinguishes *synthetic* corundum; diffusion-treated natural corundum needs chemistry.
9. **c** — Never convert a supplier's verbal claim into your disclosure. No report means no answer.
10. **b** — Cornflower (Ceylon), royal (Burma), velvet (Kashmir) are the conventional associations. They are trade descriptors, not standardized grades.
