# M01 Huddle Card — Before the Grader

**One headline:** Colored stones are graded differently than diamonds — species, color (described, not letter-graded), treatments (common and must be disclosed), and origin (a price factor) — so diamond vocabulary fails you on colored stones.

**3 key points**
1. The diamond 4Cs vocabulary does not transfer 1:1. Color is described (hue/tone/saturation) not letter-graded; clarity is species-specific; cut is for color orientation; origin changes price; treatments are standard practice for many species.
2. There are four GIA doors — G&G for peer-reviewed detection/treatment science, Research & News for field reports and market updates, the Gem Encyclopedia for quick species questions, and the GIA Library for broken URLs. Use the Encyclopedia first, then go deeper.
3. Never guess origin, treatment or value on a colored stone you did not sell. "That's a family story worth confirming at the lab" is a better answer than a wrong guess.

**1 script to practice today**

Client: "Is this a natural sapphire?"

Associate: "It is natural sapphire, and like nearly all sapphires in the market it's likely heat-treated — that's a standard, permanent, disclosed treatment, not a mark against the stone. Let me confirm the specific report on this piece rather than guessing, and I can walk you through exactly what was or wasn't done."

**1 question to ask the team**

"Who in our store is the strongest on colored stones? When you get a colored-stone question you don't know, who do you go to — and what is one question you've had to escalate in the last month that we should all know the answer to?"
