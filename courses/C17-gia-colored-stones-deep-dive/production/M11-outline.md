## Title
**Ruby: Pigeon's Blood, Marble and Basalt — and the Filled Stone You Must Disclose**

## Scenario
A client has been shopping online and arrives with screenshots: a 3 ct "pigeon's blood Burmese ruby" for $900, and a 1.2 ct unheated Mozambique ruby from your case at $14,000. She wants to know why yours costs fifteen times more and whether the $900 stone is a bargain. Neither number is a typo — they are two different products that share a species name. One is almost certainly lead-glass-filled composite or a synthetic; the other is a natural, unheated, lab-documented stone from the most important ruby source on earth. This module gives you the vocabulary to explain the gap without mocking her research.

## H2 outline
1. **Two rubies, fifteen times the price (hook ~250w).** The counter scenario above. Sets up the module's thesis: ruby's price range is wider than any other gem species because "ruby" spans untreated Mogok marble material at $1M/ct to glass-filled composite at $10/ct.

2. **Why ruby is red: chromium in corundum (~350w).** Corundum Al₂O₃; Cr³⁺ substituting for Al (roughly 0.1–3 wt% Cr₂O₃) absorbs in the yellow-green and blue, transmitting red; the same Cr³⁺ produces the red fluorescence that makes fine marble ruby glow. Trace Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ is the enemy — iron quenches fluorescence and adds brown/blue modifiers. RI 1.762–1.770, birefringence 0.008, SG ~4.00, Mohs 9, uniaxial (–), no cleavage but conchoidal fracture and parting. Pleochroism: purplish-red / orangy-red — which is why cutters orient for the purer direction.

3. **The marble/basalt split: the single most useful idea in ruby (~450w).** Marble-hosted (metamorphic, low Fe, high Cr): Mogok, Mong Hsu, Montepuez, Luc Yen, Jegdalek, Snezhnoe, Winza. Strong red fluorescence under LW/SW UV and, in the best stones, visible "glow" in daylight — this is the physical basis of the pigeon's-blood look. Basalt-hosted (alkali basalt, high Fe): Thailand/Cambodia (Pailin, Chanthaburi), Australia, Nigeria, China (Muling), Kenya (Mangare). High iron ⇒ weak or inert fluorescence, darker garnet-red brownish body color. Practical consequence: **fluorescence is a clue to origin, never a proof** — a lab decides origin from inclusions and chemistry; an associate uses the UV lamp to frame a conversation, not to make a call.

4. **Source by source (~900w)** — the core reference table plus short notes on each:
   - **Mogok, Myanmar** — marble-hosted, the historical benchmark; "pigeon's blood," fine rutile silk that scatters light, strong fluorescence, dolomite/calcite marble. Restricted access (see legal note).
   - **Mong Hsu, Myanmar** — 1990s boom; dark blue cores, typically low-temperature flux-assisted heat treated to dissolve the core; tons of commercial material.
   - **Montepuez, Mozambique** — discovered 2009, the dominant world source by volume; amphibole-hosted, low-Fe, often strongly fluorescent, can rival Mogok at the top end; Gemfields runs the formal auction.
   - **Madagascar** — Vatomandry, Andilamena, Didy/Zahamena (2012 rush); variable quality, often treated.
   - **Vietnam (Luc Yen, Quy Chau)** — marble-hosted, often strong pinkish-red; also notable fancy sapphire.
   - **Winza, Tanzania** — 2007–08 rush; distinctive, clean, strongly fluorescent red-to-pink material; short-lived.
   - **Jegdalek (Afghanistan), Snezhnoe (Tajikistan)** — marble-hosted, historically important, limited modern supply.
   - **Pailin (Cambodia) / Chanthaburi (Thailand)** — basalt-hosted, higher Fe, darker; also the world's heat-treatment and trading capital.
   - **Longido (Tanzania)** — ruby-in-zoisite (anyolite): opaque, carving and bead material, not faceting goods.
   - **Muling (China)** — basalt-hosted, commercial dark red.
   - Short note: Nepal, Kenya, Malawi, Greenland (True North/Red) as minor/emerging.

5. **Treatment: heat, flux, and the glass-filled composite (~600w).** Recap from M09 with ruby specifics. Standard heat (dissolves silk, improves clarity/color) is permanent, industry-standard, must be disclosed but does not change care. Low-temperature flux-assisted heat (Mong Hsu, much Mozambique/Madagascar material) heals fissures with precipitated corundum; disclosed as "indications of heating with residues." **Lead-glass filling** is the line: heavily fractured low-grade rough + high-lead glass ⇒ a manufactured composite. Detection at 10×: **flash effect** (blue/orange flashes along fractures in reflected light), **large gas bubbles**, **flattened bubbles at the glass/corundum interface**, lower luster/light polish on filled areas; flash at facet junctions. Care: no acid (lemon juice, jewelry dip), no ultrasonic, no steam, no torch (~500 °C melts it), warm soapy water only, bench work only by a jeweler told it is filled, never recut. Disclosure is mandatory (Overton 2004 / FTC Jewelry Guides).

6. **Synthetics and look-alikes, briefly (~350w).** Recap M08 with a ruby lens: Verneuil (curved striae, gas bubbles, since 1902), flux (Chatham, Kashan, Ramaura — flux fingerprints, platinum crystals), hydrothermal (Lechleitner overgrowth, Tairus — growth zoning, seed plate), and the modern "lab-created ruby" disclosure rule (FTC: must be called laboratory-grown/created, never just "ruby"). Natural look-alikes: red spinel (single refraction, no pleochroism, RI ~1.718), pyrope/almandine garnet, red tourmaline (rubellite), and glass/CZ.

7. **Red spinel and the "rubies" that were never ruby (~300w).** The Black Prince's Ruby (a ~170 ct spinel in the Imperial State Crown) and the Timur Ruby (a ~352 ct spinel) — historic "Balas rubies" from Badakhshan. Why it matters on the floor: clients read these stories online; the honest version is that spinel spent centuries being mistaken for ruby, is now a respected species in its own right, and GIA now offers origin determination for red spinel.

8. **Value factors and budget tiers (~450w).** Color first (hue/tone/saturation; "pigeon's blood" = vivid, slightly pinkish-to-pure red with strong fluorescence, GIA applies defined criteria); then origin (only once quality is there); clarity (silk is expected and even desirable — it proves natural and softens the look; a loupe-clean ruby is a red flag); size (big jumps past 1, 3, 5 ct); treatment (unheated commands large premiums over heated; filled trades at a small fraction). Honest budget framing table: <$500/ct ⇒ expect treated/small/filled-or-synthetic risk; $500–2,000/ct ⇒ heated commercial; $2,000–10,000/ct ⇒ fine heated, small unheated; $10k+ ⇒ fine unheated, documented origin.

9. **Legal and ethical context (~250w).** The Tom Lantos Block Burmese JADE Act (Pub. L. 110-286, 2008) prohibited US import of Burmese-origin **jadeite and rubies**, including jewelry containing them; the broader Burma sanctions program was terminated in 2016, and Myanmar Gem Enterprise was later re-sanctioned (OFAC, 2021). Practical rule: **ask your compliance officer before buying or importing Burmese-origin material**, and never assume a 2016-era answer is current. Traceability: Gemfields' Montepuez auctions and a handful of integrated operations are the exception, not the rule.
