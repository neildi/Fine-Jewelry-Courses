# M15 Quiz — Pearls

**1. What is nacre made of?**
a) Solid aragonite with no organic content.
b) Aragonite platelets stacked in layers and bound by the organic protein conchiolin.
c) Calcite and quartz.
d) Conchiolin only.

**2. What is the legal distinction between "pearl" and "cultured pearl"?**
a) None — the terms are interchangeable.
b) Under the FTC Jewelry Guides, the unqualified word "pearl" means a *natural* pearl; cultured pearls must be identified as cultured.
c) "Cultured" means imitation.
d) "Pearl" means freshwater and "cultured" means saltwater.

**3. Which cultured pearl type is mostly tissue-nucleated, making it largely solid nacre?**
a) Akoya.
b) South Sea.
c) Tahitian.
d) Freshwater.

**4. Which of the GIA 7 Pearl Value Factors is the single most important?**
a) Size.
b) Shape.
c) Luster.
d) Matching.

**5. What are the GIA 7 Pearl Value Factors?**
a) Carat, cut, color, clarity, and four others.
b) Size, shape, color, luster, surface, nacre, matching.
c) Luster, nacre, origin, treatment, size, shape, matching.
d) Color, clarity, cut, carat, luster, nacre, matching.

**6. What are "chocolate pearls"?**
a) Freshwater pearls dyed brown.
b) Treated-color **Tahitian** cultured pearls (*Pinctada margaritifera*), produced by a bleaching-type process rather than dye.
c) A variety of freshwater mussel.
d) Synthetic pearls.

**7. Why does a laboratory use X-ray computed microtomography (μ-CT) rather than a loupe for some pearls?**
a) It is cheaper.
b) Beadless cultured pearls (keshi, tissue-nucleated freshwater) have no bead to find, so the growth architecture must be mapped in three dimensions.
c) Because pearls are radioactive.
d) It measures color.

**8. What is the Mohs hardness of pearl, and what practical rule follows from it?**
a) 9 — so it can go in the ultrasonic.
b) Roughly 2.5–4.5 — so pearls are last on, first off, wiped with a soft damp cloth, and never ultrasonically cleaned.
c) 7 — so it is safe with chemicals.
d) 5 — so it can be stored loose with diamonds.

**9. Keshi pearls are:**
a) Natural pearls from wild oysters.
b) An all-nacre by-product of culturing — therefore cultured pearls.
c) Imitation pearls.
d) A type of mabé pearl.

**10. A client asks "are these real?" about a cultured pearl strand. The best answer is:**
a) "No, they're cultured."
b) "Yes — they're real cultured pearls. Almost every pearl sold today is cultured; a natural pearl is rare and I'd want a lab report before calling one that."
c) "Yes, they're natural pearls."
d) "It doesn't matter."

---

## Answer key

1. **b** — Aragonite platelets in stacked layers, bound by conchiolin. Light interfering across those layers is what produces luster, orient and iridescence.
2. **b** — A legal vocabulary rule, not a quality judgment. Cultured pearls are real pearls; they just have to be labelled as cultured.
3. **d** — Most freshwater pearls are tissue-nucleated rather than bead-nucleated, so they are essentially solid nacre — which is why they wear well and tolerate drilling.
4. **c** — Luster. A moderate-sized pearl with superb luster beats a large chalky one in both appearance and value.
5. **b** — Size, shape, color, luster, surface, nacre, matching. Note that luster ranks first in importance even though it sits fourth in the list.
6. **b** — Per GIA's published studies (Wang et al. 2006; Du Toit 2008; GIA 2016) they are treated-color **Tahitian** cultured pearls, treated by a bleaching-type process, distinct from older silver-nitrate dyeing. Disclosure is still required.
7. **b** — Beadless cultured pearls look superficially like naturals because there is no bead. μ-CT maps the internal cavities and growth architecture that distinguish them.
8. **b** — Pearls are the softest thing in most jewelry cases, which is why the whole care routine exists: last on, first off, wipe, no ultrasonic or steam, restring periodically.
9. **b** — Keshi form when a nucleus is rejected or a beadless sac forms. All nacre, often superb luster, and still a product of culturing.
10. **b** — Honest, accurate, uses the required vocabulary, and sets up the natural-vs-cultured distinction without overclaiming.
