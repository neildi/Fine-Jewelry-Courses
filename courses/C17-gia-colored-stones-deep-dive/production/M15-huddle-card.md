# M15 Huddle Card — Pearls

**One headline:** Say **cultured pearl**. It is the law (FTC: unqualified "pearl" means natural), it is accurate, and it is the moment you get to teach the client something real.

**3 key points**
1. **Luster beats size.** GIA's seven factors are size, shape, color, luster, surface, nacre, matching — and luster is the most important. A 7 mm strand with mirror reflections beats a 9 mm chalky one every time. Teach clients to look at reflections, not millimetres.
2. **Know the four and what makes each different.** Akoya (*Pinctada fucata*, 2–10 mm, thin nacre, classic round white). South Sea (*P. maxima*, 9–20 mm, thick nacre, white/silver/golden). Tahitian (*P. margaritifera*, 8–18 mm, dark with peacock and aubergine overtones). Freshwater (*Hyriopsis*, mostly tissue-nucleated so mostly solid nacre).
3. **Natural vs cultured is an X-ray question.** RTX finds the bead; μ-CT handles the beadless cases (keshi, tissue-nucleated freshwater). Never call a pearl natural without a lab report.

**1 script to practice today**

For the estate strand on your counter:
"These are real cultured pearls — cultured means a farmer helped the oyster start, but the oyster made the pearl. Almost every pearl you see today is cultured; a genuinely natural pearl is rare enough that I'd want a laboratory report before I called one that. What I'm looking at now is the luster and the nacre — and whether the string is sound, because silk doesn't last forever."

**1 question to ask the team**

"How many of us say 'cultured pearl' automatically? Every listing, tag and appraisal in the store should carry the word. Let's audit the case tickets and website copy for bare 'pearl' and fix them."

**Care, in one breath:** Last on, first off. Wipe with a soft damp cloth. No ultrasonic, no steam, no soaking, no solvents. Restring when the silk goes. Store flat. And wear them — they dull in a box far faster than on a neck.
