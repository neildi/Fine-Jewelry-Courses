# M06 Huddle Card — Inclusions and the Micro-World

**One headline:** Inclusions are the gem's internal landscape — they tell origin, treatment, natural/synthetic, and history. But reading them takes a microscope and training; guessing at the counter is where expensive mistakes happen.

**3 key points**
1. **Diagnostic inclusions to recognize at the loupe level:** three-phase jagged inclusions point toward Colombian emerald; horsetail inclusions point toward Russian demantoid; rutile silk in corundum suggests natural and possibly unheated; calcite/dolomite points toward marble-hosted (Mogok) ruby.
2. **Treatment red flags:** flash effect (blue/orange flashes at fractures) plus gas bubbles = suspect lead-glass-filled ruby. Curved color bands = Verneuil synthetic until proven otherwise. Dye concentration along fractures = dyed material. None of these are proof on one feature alone, but they all trigger referral.
3. **Origin cannot be called by color or by a 10× glance.** A lab combines microscopy + trace-element chemistry (LA-ICP-MS) + spectroscopy against a field-collected reference library. "Let me send it to the lab" is always a professional answer.

**1 script to practice today**

Client (on an estate piece): "Can you tell me where this ruby is from?"

Associate: "I can tell you what I see under the loupe in a minute, but I want to be straight with you — origin calls don't happen from a quick look at the counter. The lab uses a microscope, chemical analysis, and spectroscopy compared against known reference stones. If origin matters for this piece, I can send it to GIA for an origin report — it takes a week or two and gives you a definitive answer rather than my best guess."

**1 question to ask the team**

"When do we send a colored stone out for a lab report today, and when do we give an opinion at the counter? Let's agree as a team on a threshold — for example, any estate piece above $X, any stone where the client is asking origin in writing, any stone with a synthetic/treatment red flag — so we're consistent with each other and with our clients."
