# M02 Quiz — How Colored Stones Form

10 questions. Answer key at the end.

---

**1. A dark, inky blue sapphire from Thailand and a bright royal blue sapphire from Sri Lanka are both corundum (Al₂O₃). Why do they look different?**
a) They have different crystal structures.
b) They formed in different host rock environments (iron-rich basalt vs. low-iron metamorphic rock), which changes trace-element chemistry and the presence of rutile silk.
c) One is natural and one is synthetic.
d) Dark sapphires are always older.

**2. Which trace element produces the red color in ruby?**
a) Iron.
b) Titanium.
c) Chromium.
d) Copper.

**3. Which geologic environment is most likely to produce dark blue and parti-color fancy sapphires that are commonly heat-treated to lighten color?**
a) Pegmatite.
b) Alkali basalt.
c) Marble-hosted metamorphic rock.
d) Sedimentary opal fields.

**4. Aquamarine, morganite, Paraíba-type tourmaline, kunzite, and spessartine garnet typically form in which environment?**
a) Alkali basalt.
b) Pegmatite (coarse-grained late-stage granite).
c) Hydrothermal veins in black shale.
d) High-pressure metamorphic serpentinite mélange.

**5. Colombian emeralds (Muzo, Chivor, Coscuez) formed in which geologic environment?**
a) Pegmatite pockets in granite.
b) Hydrothermal veins in Cretaceous black shale and limestone.
c) Alkali basalt xenocrysts.
d) Placer gravels.

**6. Why do Kashmir sapphires show a characteristic "velvet" appearance?**
a) They are always heavily included with fractures.
b) Submicroscopic rutile and dust-like inclusions scatter light, creating a soft velvety blue.
c) They are irradiated.
d) They are doublets with a blue top.

**7. Jadeite jade forms in which geologic environment?**
a) Pegmatite.
b) Marble-hosted regional metamorphism.
c) High-pressure/low-temperature subduction-zone serpentinite mélange.
d) Basalt lava flows.

**8. Which of the following is TRUE of opal?**
a) It is a crystalline variety of quartz.
b) It forms in pegmatites alongside aquamarine.
c) It is amorphous (non-crystalline) silica with water content; play-of-color comes from diffraction by ordered silica spheres.
d) It is harder than sapphire.

**9. Why do Sri Lankan gem gravels (illam) contain such a mix of species (ruby, sapphire, spinel, chrysoberyl, garnet, zircon)?**
a) The deposits are synthetic.
b) They are placer (alluvial) deposits that concentrate durable gems eroded from multiple upstream primary sources.
c) The gravels are artificially stocked by dealers.
d) All those minerals form together in basalt lava.

**10. Red beryl (bixbite) from Utah is one of the rarest gems on Earth. How does it form?**
a) In hydrothermal veins in black shale, like emerald.
b) In marble, like Mogok ruby.
c) As a vapor-phase mineral in topaz rhyolite (volcanic gas reaction with lava).
d) In pegmatites alongside aquamarine.

---

## Answer key

1. **b** — Host-rock chemistry controls trace elements and inclusions; same mineral species, different colors/texture.
2. **c** — Chromium (Cr³⁺) replaces aluminum and produces red in corundum.
3. **b** — Alkali basalt sapphires are high-iron, dark, commonly heat-treated.
4. **b** — Pegmatites yield large, often clean crystals of these species.
5. **b** — Colombian emeralds form in sedimentary-hosted hydrothermal veins (black shale/limestone), with V/Cr color and three-phase inclusions.
6. **b** — Submicroscopic rutile silk and dust-like inclusions produce the velvet texture (described by Schwieger 1990 and Atkinson/Kothavala 1983).
7. **c** — Jadeite forms in HP/LT subduction-zone serpentinite mélange (Myanmar Hpakant, Guatemala Motagua).
8. **c** — Opal is amorphous silica, not crystalline quartz; the ordered sphere array causes play-of-color (see M05).
9. **b** — Placer/alluvial gravels mix material from multiple upstream primary sources; Sri Lanka's illam is the classic example.
10. **c** — Red beryl is a vapor-phase mineral in topaz rhyolite at the Wah Wah Mountains (Shigley et al. 2003), not a hydrothermal or pegmatite gem.
