## 1. The nine §5 open items — resolved

| # | Item | Module | Resolution | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Black Prince's Ruby carat weight | M11 | **170 carats** (34 g), irregular cabochon red spinel in the Imperial State Crown. Confirmed. | Royal Collection / Historic Royal Palaces record; widely cited (e.g., Wikipedia "Black Prince's Ruby" sourced to the Royal Collection). |
| 2 | Timur Ruby carat weight | M11 | **352.5 carats** (variously 352.5 / 352.54 ct), inscribed unfaceted red spinel, mounted 1853 in the "Timur Ruby" necklace, Royal Collection. Confirmed. | Royal Collection; Christie's lot essay ("Timur Ruby … weighing 352.5 carats"). |
| 3 | GIA "pigeon's blood" criteria wording | M11 | GIA does **not** publish a scientific colorimetric definition. GIA's Ruby Quality Factors page: *"Historically, the term 'pigeon's blood' described the red to slightly purplish or pinkish red color of rubies with a soft, glowing, red fluorescence,"* and notes trade terms "can be subject to misinterpretation." | GIA Ruby Quality Factors — https://www.gia.edu/ruby-quality-factor |
| 4 | GIA padparadscha criteria wording | M12, M13 | GIA describes padparadscha as **"light to medium pinkish orange to orange-pink"**. The Laboratory Manual Harmonization Committee (LMHC) wording: *"a corundum variety of any geographic origin whose colour is a subtle mixture of pinkish orange to orangey pink with pastel tones and low to medium saturation"* in standard daylight. No single universal boundary; labs differ at the margins. | GIA gem encyclopedia / quality-factor pages; LMHC Information Sheet #4 (padparadscha). |
| 5 | Cobalt-glass-filled sapphire primary citation | M12 | **Correction:** there is no single G&G article for cobalt-glass-filled sapphire. Primary sources are (a) **GIT (2007)** "New blue treated sapphire" (first identification; treater's name "Super Diffusion Tanusorn"), and (b) **Hughes et al., "Cobalt-Doped Glass-Filled Sapphires — An Update"** (Lotus Gemology). GIA G&G's "Cobalt-Coated Sapphire" (Schneider & Jasso, Fall 2019, 55(3)) covers the *coating*, a distinct treatment. | lotusgemology.com; gia.edu Fall 2019 Lab Notes. |
| 6 | Diana/Catherine sapphire origin attribution | M12 | **Ceylon** origin confirmed by the maker: Garrard's own account: "the deep, intense blue of the **12-carat oval Ceylon sapphire** … Lady Diana chose her sapphire engagement ring from Garrard in 1981." | Garrard — https://www.garrard.com (House journal, Princess Diana engagement ring). |
| 7 | Rockefeller Emerald sale price and date | M10, M14 | **18.04 ct** rectangular step-cut Colombian emerald; **Christie's New York, 20 June 2017**; sold for **US$5,511,500** (~US$305,000/ct), a world per-carat record for emerald; bought by Harry Winston. | Christie's sale record; trade coverage. |
| 8 | Ethiopian emerald discovery vs first-market year | M14 | Shakiso/Kenticha deposit (Seba Boru district) discovered by local diggers in **2016**; **first Shakiso-area emeralds reached international markets in late 2016** (Renfro et al. 2017). Earlier, smaller Dubuluk material reached the trade 2011–2012. | GIA G&G Spring 2017 "A New Discovery of Emeralds from Ethiopia"; G&G Spring 2019 "Land of Origins." |
| 9 | 1863 jadeite/nephrite separation | M16 | **Alexis Damour**, French mineralogist, in **1863** distinguished jade into the two mineral species jadeite and nephrite. Confirmed. | mindat.org; International Gem Society (Jill Hobbs); standard mineralogy history. |
