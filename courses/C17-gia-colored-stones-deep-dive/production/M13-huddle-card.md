# M13 Huddle Card — Fancy Sapphire and Phenomenal Corundum

**One headline:** Fancy corundum is where treatment risk concentrates, because the premium colors — vivid orange, intense pink, padparadscha — are exactly what beryllium diffusion can manufacture, and a loupe cannot tell the difference.

**3 key points**
1. **Know the chromophores.** Pink = Cr (less than ruby). Orange/padparadscha = Cr + Fe. Yellow = Fe³⁺ and/or color centers. Green = yellow + blue together. Color-change = Cr + V. White = no chromophores at all.
2. **Be diffusion changed the rules.** It penetrates the whole stone, leaves no rim, and is too light for routine XRF. Only LA-ICP-MS or LIBS finds it. Emmett et al. (2003) is the paper. Disclosure is mandatory and permanent-treated material circulates for decades.
3. **Stars are a trade-off, not a bonus.** Sharp stars need heavy silk; heavy silk clouds the body. Say that out loud before the client decides she wants a transparent stone with a perfect star.

**1 script to practice today**

For the client who found a cheap padparadscha online:
"Padparadscha is the most treated color in corundum. Around 2000 a lot of vivid orange and pink sapphire came on the market that turned out to be beryllium-diffused — Be goes all the way through the stone, so there's no rim to see and no way to catch it with a loupe. That's why the laboratory report matters more than the photograph here. Let's get it looked at by a recognized lab before you commit."

**1 question to ask the team**

"List every fancy sapphire in the case in the orange–pink–yellow range priced over our threshold. Which have reports from a recognized lab — GIA, AGL, GRS, Gübelin, SSEF, Lotus? Anything without one gets described as 'sapphire, heated' until we can document it."

**Two phrases to stop using:** "It's natural, the silk looks right" (silk does not rule out diffusion) and "white sapphire is just like a diamond" (it is not — RI 1.77 vs 2.42).
