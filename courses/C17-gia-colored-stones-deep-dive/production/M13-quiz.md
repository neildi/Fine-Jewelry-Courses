# M13 Quiz — Fancy Sapphire and Phenomenal Corundum

**1. What causes the pink color in pink sapphire?**
a) Iron and titanium.
b) Chromium, at a lower concentration than in ruby.
c) Vanadium.
d) Copper.

**2. Which fancy-corundum color carries the highest beryllium-diffusion risk?**
a) Pastel lavender.
b) Vivid orange, intense pink and padparadscha.
c) Colorless (white) sapphire.
d) Green sapphire.

**3. Why was beryllium diffusion so much harder to detect than the older titanium surface diffusion?**
a) Because beryllium is colorless.
b) Because Be penetrates the entire stone, so there is no surface rim and no diagnostic feature at 10×; detection needs LA-ICP-MS or LIBS.
c) Because it only affects synthetic stones.
d) Because it fades after a year.

**4. What is the practical rule for using the word "padparadscha"?**
a) Use it whenever the stone looks pinkish-orange.
b) Use it only when a recognized lab report has used it; otherwise describe the color.
c) Use it only for Sri Lankan stones.
d) Avoid it entirely — the name is obsolete.

**5. What causes asterism in star sapphire?**
a) Color zoning.
b) Oriented rutile silk needles lying in three directions at 120° in the basal plane, seen in a cabochon cut parallel to that plane.
c) A coating on the dome.
d) Beryllium diffusion.

**6. Why is there a trade-off between a sharp star and a transparent body?**
a) Sharp stars need heavy silk, and heavy silk clouds the stone.
b) Transparent stones cannot be cut as cabochons.
c) The star is painted on.
d) There is no trade-off.

**7. Which is a tell for a synthetic star sapphire?**
a) A slightly off-center star.
b) Fine, sleepy silk.
c) Unnaturally even silk, a razor-sharp star, and an unnaturally uniform body color.
d) A twelve-ray star.

**8. What is true of most commercial color-change sapphire?**
a) It is almost all natural.
b) It is mostly synthetic, produced as an alexandrite imitation.
c) It is always beryllium diffused.
d) It comes only from Kashmir.

**9. What is the honest one-sentence comparison between white sapphire and diamond?**
a) They are visually identical and white sapphire is better value.
b) White sapphire is harder than diamond.
c) White sapphire is a real natural Mohs-9 gem and much cheaper, but with a far lower refractive index and dispersion it is noticeably less brilliant and dulls with wear.
d) White sapphire is synthetic.

**10. What is the store-wide buying rule this module recommends?**
a) Never buy fancy sapphire.
b) Anything vivid in the orange–pink–yellow range at a modest price gets a laboratory report before it gets a price.
c) Only buy stones with no report.
d) Buy on the supplier's verbal claim.

---

## Answer key

1. **b** — Chromium, at lower concentration than in ruby. The ruby/pink-sapphire boundary is a tone and saturation judgment, not a chemistry change — which is why it is contested between labs.
2. **b** — The premium colors are the diffusion targets. Pastel and parti material is comparatively low risk.
3. **b** — Full penetration means no rim, no edge concentration, and nothing diagnostic to see. Beryllium is also too light for routine XRF, so even well-equipped labs missed it initially.
4. **b** — Sell the stone, document the name. If the report says padparadscha, say padparadscha; if not, describe the color and price it as a pinkish-orange sapphire.
5. **b** — Three sets of exsolved rutile needles at 120°, viewed through a cabochon oriented with its base parallel to the basal plane; overlapping generations can produce twelve rays.
6. **a** — The silk that makes the star is the same silk that clouds the body. A client wanting both a gemmy transparent stone and a razor star is asking for two things that pull against each other.
7. **c** — The synthetic tells are unnatural evenness and uniformity — not sharpness alone, since plenty of natural stars are sharp.
8. **b** — Verneuil-grown color-change sapphire has been made for decades as an alexandrite imitation. Natural material exists but is uncommon — refer before calling it natural.
9. **c** — Honest comparison is the sale. Presented as a budget diamond substitute, it becomes a complaint within a year.
10. **b** — Report before price. The value difference between natural and diffused material in those colors is far too large to guess.
