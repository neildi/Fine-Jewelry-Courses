# M03 Quiz — Finding Them: Mining, Recovery, and Field Gemology

**1. What is the difference between a primary and a secondary colored-stone deposit?**
a) Primary deposits are large; secondary are small.
b) Primary deposits are the rock the gem formed in; secondary deposits are alluvial/placer gravels where gems are concentrated after eroding out of older rock.
c) Primary deposits only form in basalt; secondary only in marble.
d) Primary deposits produce rubies; secondary produce sapphires.

**2. Why are secondary (alluvial) deposits so often the site of colored-stone mining?**
a) They always produce higher quality stones than primary deposits.
b) Rivers and gravity naturally concentrate dense gems in gravels, and they can be mined with relatively simple equipment.
c) They are the only deposits that produce gem corundum.
d) They are always close to major cities.

**3. Which Indian city is the dominant global colored-stone cutting center?**
a) Mumbai.
b) Delhi.
c) Jaipur.
d) Bangalore.

**4. What is the central purpose of GIA field gemology expeditions?**
a) To buy rough for GIA's inventory.
b) To collect known-origin reference samples directly from miners/mines, enabling the lab to do geographic origin determination, and to document new deposits.
c) To produce marketing videos.
d) To train local miners.

**5. Why doesn't the Kimberley Process apply to colored stones?**
a) Colored stones are never mined in conflict zones.
b) The KP was designed specifically for rough diamonds in response to conflict-diamond campaigns; colored-stone supply chains are far more fragmented across small artisanal sources and multiple species, and no equivalent intergovernmental treaty exists.
c) Colored stones have their own separate version.
d) It does apply, but most people don't know it.

**6. Which mine is the documented example GIA has profiled as a fully integrated Brazilian emerald operation tracing a stone from blast to finished gem?**
a) Kagem (Zambia).
b) Belmont (Minas Gerais, Brazil).
c) Coscuez (Colombia).
d) Capão (Ouro Preto, Brazil).

**7. In what decade was the Montepuez ruby deposit in Mozambique discovered?**
a) 1980s.
b) 1990s.
c) 2000s (2009).
d) 2010s.

**8. When a client asks "what mine is this sapphire from?" about a commercial parcel stone with a country-level origin report, the most honest answer is usually:**
a) Name a famous mine (e.g., "Kashmir"), since that is what they want to hear.
b) Even with an origin report naming country, you almost never can identify the specific mine from a finished stone; country/region is what the lab establishes.
c) Tell them it is from the cheapest source to under-promise.
d) Refuse to sell the stone.

**9. Which of the following best describes the relationship between artisanal mining and "ethical" sourcing?**
a) Artisanal mining is always unethical.
b) Corporate mining is always ethical.
c) It is more complex than a simple binary — some artisanal mining supports communities under fair-trade/ASM co-op programs, some is informal and high-risk, and corporate mining varies in labor and environmental standards; per-stone traceability needs to be documented, not assumed from origin.
d) Country of origin tells you everything you need to know about ethics.

**10. A jig (the device Pardieu shows at Pailin) separates sapphires from lighter gravel using what principle?**
a) Magnetic attraction.
b) Gravitational separation by density — corundum (SG ~4) sinks while quartz/feldspar (SG ~2.6) wash away.
c) Color sorting by eye.
d) Chemical dissolution of the gravel.

---

## Answer key

1. **b** — primary = source rock; secondary = eroded/placer gravels.
2. **b** — density concentration + low-tech accessibility make placers the dominant mining target historically.
3. **c** — Jaipur (Rajasthan) is the global colored-stone cutting powerhouse per Lucas et al. 2016.
4. **b** — reference-sample collection is the central scientific purpose (documentary content is a byproduct).
5. **b** — KP is diamond-specific; colored stones lack an equivalent treaty framework.
6. **b** — Belmont mine (Lucas et al. 2015 Belmont journey-from-mine-to-market piece).
7. **c** — 2009 (first documented by McClure/Koivula G&G GNI Fall 2009; Gemfields production began in 2012).
8. **b** — origin reports establish country/region, not specific mine; guessing the mine name is an avoidable error.
9. **c** — honest, non-binary; traceability requires evidence, not assumptions from scale or country.
10. **b** — jigs work on specific-gravity differences (corundum SG ≈4, quartz SG ≈2.6).
