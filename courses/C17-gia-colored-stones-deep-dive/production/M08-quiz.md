# M08 Quiz — Synthetics, Simulants, and Assembled Stones

**1. What is the difference between a synthetic ruby and a ruby simulant?**
a) There is no difference — both are fake.
b) A synthetic ruby has the same chemical composition and crystal structure as natural ruby (it is corundum, Al₂O₃) but was grown in a lab. A simulant is a different material (glass, garnet, spinel) that merely looks like ruby.
c) A synthetic ruby is always glass.
d) A simulant is always a synthetic.

**2. Which two features under 10× are classic indicators of a Verneuil (flame-fusion) synthetic ruby or sapphire?**
a) Three-phase inclusions and calcite crystals.
b) Horsetail inclusions and zircon halos.
c) Curved growth striae (curved color bands) and spherical gas bubbles.
d) Chevron growth and nail-head spicules.

**3. Chatham, Kashan, Ramaura, and Knischka are historically associated with which synthetic growth process?**
a) Verneuil.
b) Hydrothermal.
c) Flux-melt (slow growth from a molten solvent, producing wispy/veil flux inclusions and platinum platelets from the crucible).
d) Skull melt.

**4. Chevron/zig-zag growth patterns, nail-head spicules, phenakite crystals, metal platelets from the autoclave, and a visible seed plate are characteristic of which synthetic?**
a) Hydrothermal synthetic emerald (e.g., Biron, Tairus).
b) Verneuil ruby.
c) Opalite plastic.
d) Garnet-topped doublet.

**5. When viewed perpendicular to the surface under magnification, most synthetic opal (Gilson/Kyocera and successors) shows which diagnostic feature?**
a) Three-phase inclusions.
b) A columnar "lizard-skin" structure (regular columns unlike natural opal's more random sphere arrangement).
c) Curved striae.
d) Horsetail inclusions.

**6. How do you detect a garnet-topped doublet (GTD) under 10×?**
a) It shows curved striae.
b) A join line is visible at or near the girdle when viewed in profile/immersion, and table-down on a white surface the red garnet top shows a "red ring" at the edge; the garnet top is hard while the glass pavilion is soft.
c) It has three-phase jagged inclusions.
d) It smells of glue.

**7. Which of the following is TRUE about the Chelsea color filter?**
a) If it shows red, the stone is definitely natural emerald.
b) If it shows green, the stone is definitely glass.
c) It is a quick screening tool only — Cr-colored natural emeralds and rubies (and cobalt glass/synthetics) typically show pinkish-red through it, while many iron-rich rubies and pastes show green; it is never a verdict on its own.
d) It can identify the origin of any gem.

**8. Under FTC Guides (per Overton 2004), when a sales associate uses the word "ruby" without qualifier, what does the consumer have a right to assume?**
a) That it is synthetic.
b) That it is a doublet.
c) That it is natural ruby; synthetic origin, assembled construction, and material treatments must be disclosed clearly before sale.
d) That it is glass.

**9. A customer brings in a large "opal" she bought cheaply. From the side you see three distinct layers — a transparent dome cap, a thin colored layer showing play-of-color, and a dark backing. What is it?**
a) Solid black opal.
b) Boulder opal.
c) An opal triplet (clear cap + thin precious opal layer + backing).
d) Synthetic emerald.

**10. You examine an estate "ruby" under 10× and see no obvious synthetic features, no curved striae, no bubbles, but the price the client says she paid is far below natural-ruby market value. The professional move is:**
a) Tell her it's definitely natural and make an offer.
b) Tell her it's definitely synthetic based on price.
c) Refer it to a gemologist / send to a lab for a formal identification report — absence of obvious inclusions at 10× is not proof of synthesis, and price alone is not identification.
d) Refuse to look at it.

---

## Answer key

1. **b** — synthetic = same chemistry/structure, lab-grown; simulant = different material, lookalike.
2. **c** — curved striae + spherical bubbles are the Verneuil signature. (Three-phase = Colombian emerald; horsetail = Russian demantoid; chevron/nail-head = hydrothermal emerald.)
3. **c** — flux-melt brands.
4. **a** — hydrothermal synthetic emerald (Biron/Tairus).
5. **b** — columnar/lizard-skin structure (Schmetzer/Henn 1987).
6. **b** — join line + red ring + mixed hardness.
7. **c** — screening only, never a verdict.
8. **c** — unqualified species name implies natural; synthetics/treatments/assembled stones must be disclosed.
9. **c** — opal triplet (three layers).
10. **c** — when in doubt, refer.
