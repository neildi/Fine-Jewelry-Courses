# M12 Huddle Card — Blue Sapphire

**One headline:** Blue needs iron *and* titanium working together — which is why heat treatment works so well on sapphire, why geuda becomes blue in a furnace, and why no one should be guessing "unheated" without a report.

**3 key points**
1. **Know the sources by look.** Kashmir = velvet and effectively gone since the 1930s. Burma = rich royal blue. Ceylon = bright cornflower, the market's backbone, home of geuda. Madagascar = huge volume, commercial to fine. Montana = Yogo (untreated, small) vs Rock Creek (routinely heated). Australia/Thailand = basalt, iron-rich, dark.
2. **Heat is normal; glass and diffusion are not.** Heated sapphire is permanent, standard, and fine to wear and clean normally. Cobalt-glass-filled sapphire is a composite: flash effect, gas bubbles, blue along the fractures, mandatory disclosure, composite care. Be-diffusion cannot be seen at all — it needs chemistry.
3. **Origin and color words are trade language, not grades.** "Ceylon" can mean Sri Lanka *or* a bright cornflower color — say which. "Kashmir" is a report question, never a look question.

**1 script to practice today**

For the bridal client who wants "Kate Middleton's ring":
"That ring is a twelve-carat Ceylon sapphire — Sri Lankan, beautiful, and exactly the kind of stone I can find for you. The Kashmir sapphires you read about are a different thing entirely: that deposit was essentially mined out by the 1930s, so today it's estate and collection material that trades with a laboratory report. What I'd love to do is find you a Ceylon or fine Madagascar sapphire with real presence — let's look at three."

**1 question to ask the team**

"How many stones in the case are we calling 'Ceylon' or 'unheated' on a supplier's verbal claim rather than a report? Make a list — those descriptions revert to 'blue sapphire, heated' until we can document them."

**Care rule:** Clean heated sapphire is generally ultrasonic- and steam-safe. Filled, diffused or heavily fractured sapphire is not — warm soapy water only, and hardness is not toughness.
