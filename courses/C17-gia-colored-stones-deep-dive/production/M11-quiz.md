# M11 Quiz — Ruby: Pigeon's Blood to Montepuez

**1. What element is responsible for ruby's red color?**
a) Iron.
b) Titanium.
c) Chromium.
d) Copper.

**2. Why do marble-hosted rubies (Mogok, Montepuez, Luc Yen) typically show stronger red fluorescence than basalt-hosted rubies (Pailin, Muling)?**
a) Because marble is softer than basalt.
b) Because marble-hosted deposits are low in iron, and iron quenches chromium's fluorescence.
c) Because basalt-hosted rubies contain more chromium.
d) Fluorescence has nothing to do with the host rock.

**3. Which ruby source has been the dominant world supplier by volume since its 2009 discovery?**
a) Mogok, Myanmar.
b) Montepuez, Mozambique.
c) Chanthaburi, Thailand.
d) Winza, Tanzania.

**4. Which of these is NOT one of the four classic detection features of lead-glass-filled ruby?**
a) Flash effect — bright blue/orange flashes along fractures.
b) Large gas bubbles trapped in the fill.
c) Flattened bubbles at the glass/corundum interface.
d) A strong red fluorescence under long-wave UV.

**5. A client wants to size a ring set with a ruby you suspect is lead-glass-filled. What is the right move?**
a) Size it normally with a torch; ruby is Mohs 9.
b) Put it in the ultrasonic after sizing.
c) Do not apply heat — the glass fill softens around 500 °C; confirm whether the stone is filled and have bench work done cold by a jeweler who knows.
d) Recut the stone to a smaller size first.

**6. What does the GIA report comment "indications of heating with residues" most commonly indicate?**
a) The stone is unheated.
b) Low-temperature flux-assisted heat treatment, common on Mong Hsu and much Mozambique/Madagascar ruby.
c) Beryllium lattice diffusion.
d) Lead-glass filling.

**7. The Black Prince's Ruby and the Timur Ruby are famous historical "rubies" that are actually:**
a) Garnets.
b) Red spinels.
c) Synthetic rubies.
d) Red tourmalines.

**8. Why is a large, loupe-clean ruby offered at a low price a warning sign rather than a bargain?**
a) Because clean rubies are always small.
b) Ruby is a normally included species; unusual clarity at a low price usually means synthetic or glass-filled material.
c) Because the seller made a pricing error.
d) Clean rubies are less valuable than included ones.

**9. The Tom Lantos Block Burmese JADE Act of 2008 (Pub. L. 110-286) restricted US import of which Burmese materials?**
a) All Burmese gemstones.
b) Rubies and sapphires.
c) Jadeite and rubies, including jewelry containing them.
d) Only jadeite.

**10. Put the ruby value factors in the correct order of importance.**
a) Origin, size, color, clarity, treatment.
b) Color first, then origin (only once quality is there), then clarity, size and treatment.
c) Carat weight first, color second.
d) Treatment first, because unheated always wins.

---

## Answer key

1. **c** — Chromium (Cr³⁺). The same ion produces both the red body color and the red fluorescence; iron is the element that quenches it.
2. **b** — Marble-hosted rubies are low-iron, so chromium's fluorescence is not suppressed. Basalt-hosted rubies incorporate more iron, which quenches fluorescence and darkens the body color.
3. **b** — Montepuez, Mozambique. Discovered in 2009, it is the most important ruby source in the world by volume and the best material rivals Mogok quality.
4. **d** — Fluorescence is not a fill-detection feature. The four are flash effect, large gas bubbles, flattened bubbles at the interface, and a luster/polish difference on filled areas.
5. **c** — Heat destroys glass fill. Confirm fill status first, then have any bench work done cold by a jeweler who has been told.
6. **b** — Flux-assisted low-temperature heat; residue from the flux precipitating corundum into fissures. Standard, permanent, and disclosed.
7. **b** — Red spinel. "Balas ruby" from Badakhshan was the historic name; spinel is now a respected species in its own right and GIA offers origin determination for red spinel.
8. **b** — Ruby is a characteristically included species, and fine rutile silk is part of the classic look. Loupe-clean and cheap means synthetic or filled until proven otherwise.
9. **c** — Jadeite and rubies only, including jewelry containing them — not sapphire, spinel or other Burmese gems. Note that the surrounding sanctions picture changed after 2016; check current compliance status.
10. **b** — Color dominates for every colored stone. Origin matters only once the quality is there; a pale, included stone from a famous source is still a pale, included stone.
