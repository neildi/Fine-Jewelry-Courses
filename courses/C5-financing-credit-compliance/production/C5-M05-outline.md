# C5-M05 Outline: AML Basics for Jewelry Retail

## Module Metadata
- Course: C5 - Financing, Credit & Compliance
- Module: 5 of 6
- Persona: P2
- Working title: AML Basics for Jewelry Retail
- Learning objective: Associates can explain why jewelry dealers have AML obligations, connect Form 8300 to the broader AML framework, and recognize day-to-day habits (clienteling, accurate records, pattern-noticing) that support AML compliance without mistaking this module for a complete AML program.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Explain, at a foundational level, why jewelry dealers have AML obligations and how Form 8300 fits into a broader compliance picture."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Widens the lens from C4's Form 8300 module to the broader AML framework it sits within.
2. **Why Jewelry Dealers Have AML Obligations in the First Place** — BSA/FinCEN designation logic; Cartier job-spec evidence that AML is a frontline duty, not a back-office abstraction.
3. **What This Actually Means for Day-to-Day Work** — Four overlapping habits: genuine clienteling, precise Form 8300 compliance, accurate inventory/provenance records, noticing unusual patterns beyond cash amount alone.
4. **This Is a Foundational Overview, Not a Complete AML Program** — Explicit scope limitation; what a real AML program requires (written program, compliance officer, tailored training, independent testing) that this module cannot provide.
5. **A Worked Scenario: Noticing a Pattern Worth Escalating** — Illustrative synthesis of a cash-clustering/rapid buy-return pattern worth escalating.
6. **Practice Exercise** — Store-specific "what would actually stand out" reflection plus escalation-path discussion.
7. **Key Takeaways** — Four-point recap.
