# C5-M01 Quiz: Consumer Financing Basics: How Retail Installment Credit Works at the Counter

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly applying the two register-level compliance rules to specific scenarios.

---

**1. How does a retail installment contract typically differ from a general-purpose credit card, per this module?**
A) There is no real difference between the two
B) It's typically tied to a specific purchase and merchant financing program, rather than being a revolving line usable anywhere
C) It always has a lower interest rate than a credit card
D) It can only be used for purchases under $1,000

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A retail installment contract is a credit agreement tied to a specific purchase through a merchant program (e.g., a store-branded financing card), distinct from a general-purpose revolving credit card.

---

**2. What must happen before a financing sale can proceed, regardless of which application channel is used?**
A) The customer must pay a deposit in cash first
B) The credit agreement must be in the consumer's hands before the sale proceeds
C) A manager must personally approve every application
D) The customer must have financed with the store before

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Regardless of application channel (online, in-store Direct to Device, etc.), the credit agreement must be in the consumer's hands before the sale proceeds — they need to see the terms before agreeing to them.

---

**3. A customer buys an in-stock ring and wears it out of the store the same day, financed through the store's program. What is the correct charge approach?**
A) Use Authorization Only, then wait to charge later
B) Charge the account today, since the customer is receiving the item today
C) Refuse to finance same-day purchases
D) Split the charge into two separate transactions

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: You can charge a financed account for products the customer is receiving at the time of sale — since the ring is being taken today, charging today is correct.

---

**4. A customer places a custom order that will take four weeks to produce and wants to finance it. What is the correct charge sequence?**
A) Charge the full amount immediately today
B) Use Authorization Only today to hold the credit, then process a Forced Purchase once the item is finished and delivered
C) Wait until delivery to do anything, with no authorization beforehand
D) Require the customer to pay cash instead of financing custom orders

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Future delivery must be transacted using Authorization Only (holding the credit without charging) followed by Forced Purchase once the item is actually ready and delivered — not a full charge on the day of the deposit.

---

**5. Why does this module emphasize NOT charging the full amount immediately for a custom order that hasn't been delivered yet?**
A) It has no real practical consequence either way
B) If something goes wrong before delivery (a delay, a design change, a cancellation), unwinding a completed charge is harder than canceling an authorization that was never converted
C) It is only a suggestion, not an actual rule
D) Immediate charging is always required by financing partners

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Charging immediately for undelivered goods creates a harder problem if anything changes before delivery — the Authorization Only/Forced Purchase sequence keeps the charge timeline matched to when the customer actually receives value.

---

**6. Which of the following is a typical financing-partner report that retailers use for reconciliation, per this module?**
A) A Customer Satisfaction Survey
B) A Credit Card Transaction Report
C) An Inventory Shrinkage Report
D) A Weekly Crime Bulletin

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Financing partners typically provide a Credit Card Transaction Report specifically used for reconciliation, alongside other reports like Application Summaries and Daily Funding Reports.

**7. According to this module, do all fine jewelry employers offer consumer financing?**
A) Yes, it is universal across every segment of the industry
B) No — luxury maison postings (e.g., Cartier, Tiffany, Van Cleef & Arpels, Bulgari) generally don't mention financing at all in associate job descriptions
C) Only independent jewelers offer financing
D) Financing is only available online, never in physical stores

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Luxury maisons generally do not mention financing or credit in their associate job postings, reflecting a genuine segment difference from chain and independent retailers that commonly rely on financing as a core sales tool.

**8. What should an associate do if they're uncertain whether their specific employer offers financing tools described in this module?**
A) Assume every employer offers identical financing tools
B) Confirm what payment tools are actually available at their specific employer rather than assuming universal applicability
C) Tell customers financing is never available anywhere
D) Ignore the question entirely

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Since financing availability varies significantly by industry segment, associates should confirm their specific employer's actual payment tools rather than assuming this module's content applies universally.
