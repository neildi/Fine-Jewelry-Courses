# C5-M03 Quiz: Layaway vs. Financing vs. Buy Now, Pay Later

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly distinguishing the three tools and applying the decision framework to specific customer scenarios.

---

**1. Which of the three "pay over time" tools involves NO credit at all?**
A) Financing
B) Layaway
C) BNPL
D) All three involve credit equally

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Layaway is not a credit product — the store holds the item, the customer makes scheduled payments, and ownership transfers only at final payment, with no interest or credit check involved.

---

**2. A customer wants to take an item home today but has no interest in applying for credit. Which option should NOT be recommended?**
A) Financing
B) Layaway
C) BNPL
D) None of these fit — recommend nothing

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Layaway requires the customer to wait until final payment before taking possession, which conflicts with wanting the item today — it's the wrong fit for this specific combination of needs.

---

**3. Per the decision framework in this module, what is the first question to ask when deciding which option to recommend?**
A) What is the customer's favorite metal type?
B) Does the customer need to leave with the item today?
C) How long has the customer shopped at this store?
D) What time of year is it?

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Whether the customer needs same-day possession is the first filter — if yes, layaway is off the table, narrowing the choice to financing or BNPL.

---

**4. A customer is buying a modest-priced item and wants to split payment over about six weeks without a longer financing commitment. Which option best fits?**
A) Layaway
B) Traditional deferred-interest financing
C) BNPL
D) None of these are appropriate for smaller purchases

**Correct answer: C**
Explanation: BNPL typically fits smaller purchases with a short payoff horizon (often around six weeks), distinct from the longer timelines typical of layaway or traditional financing.

---

**5. Why does this module recommend presenting the option that actually fits the customer's situation, rather than defaulting to whichever tool is easiest for the associate to process?**
A) It has no real effect on business outcomes
B) A properly matched option produces better completion rates and stronger repeat-client relationships than a mismatched one
C) Only financing should ever be recommended regardless of fit
D) Customers never notice which option they were given

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A customer who ends up with the option that actually fits their situation is more likely to complete the purchase successfully and return as a repeat client than one pushed into a mismatched option.

---

**6. Which job-spec evidence does this module cite to show that layaway is treated as a distinct process from financing, not a synonym?**
A) A posting that never mentions layaway at all
B) A job posting listing "process sales, returns, layaways, and financing applications" as separate line items
C) A posting stating layaway and financing are identical
D) A posting that only mentions financing

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Real job postings list layaway and financing as separate, distinct duties, reflecting that they are structurally different processes, not interchangeable terms for the same thing.

---

**7. What compliance framework governs BNPL products, per this module (recapping Module 2)?**
A) Form 8300
B) Regulation Z (Truth in Lending Act)
C) The FTC Guides on treatment disclosure
D) No compliance framework applies to BNPL

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: BNPL products are treated as subject to compliance obligations under Regulation Z, which implements the Truth in Lending Act — a framework introduced in Module 2 and recapped here.

---

**8. A customer wants a larger purchase with a longer payoff horizon and is comfortable with credit. Which option best fits, per this module's framework?**
A) Layaway
B) Traditional financing
C) BNPL
D) None of these fit larger purchases

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A larger purchase with a longer intended payoff horizon and comfort with credit points toward traditional financing, distinct from BNPL's shorter-term structure or layaway's no-credit approach.
