# C5-M04 Quiz: FTC Disclosure Rules: Treatments, Lab-Grown, and Advertising Claims

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly applying disclosure principles, not memorizing regulatory citations.

---

**1. According to JVC's framing cited in this module, what do the FTC Guidelines represent for a jewelry associate?**
A) A purely optional marketing suggestion
B) "The legal frame around what you can promise a financed client"
C) A set of rules that apply only to online sales
D) Guidance that only applies to store managers, not associates

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: JVC specifically frames its guidance on the FTC Guidelines as the legal frame around what you can promise a financed client, tying product-claim compliance directly to the financing relationship covered elsewhere in this course.

---

**2. What is the core disclosure principle for a treated gemstone, per this module?**
A) Treatments never need to be disclosed since they're standard practice
B) If a stone has been treated, that treatment must be disclosed to the customer, not left for them to discover or assume doesn't apply
C) Only irradiated stones require disclosure; other treatments don't
D) Disclosure is only required if the customer specifically asks

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The core principle is affirmative disclosure — treatments are legitimate trade practice, but the customer has a right to know what was done to the stone, and this shouldn't be left for them to discover or infer later.

---

**3. Why is it insufficient to let a customer simply infer whether a diamond is natural or lab-grown from its price?**
A) Price is always a perfectly reliable indicator with no ambiguity
B) The distinction must be clearly and affirmatively disclosed, not left ambiguous or implied through price alone
C) Lab-grown diamonds are never sold at a discount to natural ones
D) This distinction doesn't actually matter to most customers

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The natural-vs-lab-grown distinction must be clearly and affirmatively stated — relying on the customer to infer it from price risks creating a false impression even without an explicit false statement.

---

**4. Which of the following would be an example of an unsupportable advertising or verbal claim, per this module?**
A) Accurately stating a stone's grading report results
B) Telling a customer "this is worth double what you're paying" without a specific, verifiable basis for that claim
C) Explaining a treatment that was performed on a stone
D) Disclosing that a diamond is lab-grown

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A value claim like "worth double" needs a specific, verifiable basis to be supportable — making it without one is exactly the kind of unsupported claim this module warns against.

---

**5. What should an associate do if they're unsure whether a specific claim about a piece is supportable?**
A) Repeat it anyway since other associates say it
B) Bring the question to a manager before repeating it to a customer
C) Make the claim more exaggerated to be safe
D) Avoid selling the piece entirely

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: If there's uncertainty about whether a claim can be supported, that's specifically the kind of question to raise with a manager before using it in a customer conversation.

---

**6. Why does this module argue that disclosure problems compound in a financed sale compared to a cash sale?**
A) Financed sales have no real difference from cash sales in this respect
B) A financed sale involves an ongoing credit relationship and additional parties (the financing partner) who may field complaints about a claim the store made
C) Cash sales are actually more likely to generate disclosure disputes
D) Financing partners never get involved in product-claim disputes

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A financed sale creates an ongoing relationship plus an additional party (the financing partner) that may become involved if a customer disputes a product claim months into the term — a cash sale doesn't carry that same compounding exposure.

---

**7. In the module's worked scenario, what makes the "correct" explanation of a lab-grown diamond better than the "incorrect" one?**
A) It uses more technical jargon
B) It states the lab-grown origin plainly and explains why it matters to the customer's decision, rather than being technically true but misleading by omission
C) It avoids mentioning the diamond is lab-grown at all
D) It focuses only on the price, not the origin

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The correct version affirmatively states the lab-grown origin and connects it to the price and the customer's decision, rather than giving a technically accurate but incomplete answer that leaves the customer to assume otherwise.

Explanation: Since this module describes federal regulatory content with real enforcement consequences, it explicitly recommends verifying directly against ftc.gov and considering legal review before relying on it for real compliance training.
