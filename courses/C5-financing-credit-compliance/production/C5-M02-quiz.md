# C5-M02 Quiz: Running a Financing Application at the Point of Sale

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly explaining the deferred-interest mechanic and handling declines appropriately.

---

**1. In a deferred-interest promotion ("0% for 18 months"), what happens if a customer pays off the ENTIRE balance before the promotional period ends?**
A) They still owe some interest regardless
B) The accrued interest is waived entirely
C) They owe double the interest
D) The promotion is automatically canceled

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: If the full balance is paid within the promotional window, the interest that accrued in the background during that period is waived entirely — this is the "deferred" part of deferred interest.

---

**2. If a customer has a small balance remaining when an 18-month deferred-interest promotion ends, what typically happens?**
A) They only owe interest on the small remaining balance, going forward from that point
B) They are typically charged interest retroactively on the ENTIRE original purchase amount, back to the purchase date
C) The promotion automatically extends for another 18 months
D) Nothing happens; the remaining balance is forgiven

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is the mechanic that catches customers off guard — even a small remaining balance at the end of the promotional period typically triggers retroactive interest on the full original amount, not just the remainder.

---

**3. Why does this module emphasize explaining the deferred-interest mechanic precisely, rather than just saying "0% interest for X months"?**
A) It doesn't actually matter how it's explained
B) The retroactive full-balance interest charge is the most common source of customer confusion and complaints in this category of credit
C) Customers never ask follow-up questions about financing terms
D) It's only relevant for purchases under $1,000

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The headline "0% for X months" without explaining the retroactive-interest trigger is exactly the kind of incomplete explanation that leads to customer complaints when they don't pay off the balance in time.

---

**4. How does a typical BNPL (Buy Now, Pay Later) product generally differ from traditional deferred-interest store financing?**
A) They are identical products with different names
B) BNPL typically splits a purchase into a small number of equal installments over a short period, usually without the deferred-interest mechanic
C) BNPL always has a longer promotional period than deferred-interest financing
D) BNPL is never subject to any federal compliance requirements

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: BNPL is a related but structurally distinct product — typically short-term, equal installments, generally without the deferred-interest retroactive-charge mechanic that traditional store financing programs use.

---

**5. What should an associate do when a customer's financing application is declined?**
A) Guess at the specific reason for the decline and explain it to the customer
B) Avoid speculating on the reason, direct the customer to the financing partner's required notification, and pivot to alternative store options
C) Tell the customer the decision can be appealed directly to the associate
D) Immediately end the conversation with no further options offered

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The specific reason for a decline is communicated by the financing partner through a legally required notification — associates should not speculate, and should instead pivot naturally to alternatives like layaway.

---

**6. Can an associate override or influence a financing partner's credit decision?**
A) Yes, with manager approval
B) No — and associates should never suggest to a customer that they can, since it sets up a false expectation
C) Yes, but only for repeat customers
D) Only for purchases under a certain amount

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Associates genuinely cannot override a financing partner's credit decision, and suggesting otherwise to a customer creates a false expectation that will only lead to disappointment.

---

**7. What is the purpose of checking the Daily Funding Report, per this module?**
A) It has no real operational purpose
B) It confirms financed sales from the prior day have actually been funded into the store's account, similar to confirming a check has cleared
C) It only matters for the annual audit
D) It replaces the need for any other financial reconciliation

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The Daily Funding Report is the financing equivalent of confirming a check cleared — it's part of the daily financial-reconciliation rhythm alongside cash-drawer and deposit checks.

---

**8. How should an associate use the Customer List's available-credit information?**
A) Share it freely with any staff member who asks
B) Use it as a discreet clienteling tool in direct conversation with that specific customer only, never disclosed inappropriately to others
C) Post it publicly for transparency
D) Ignore it entirely since it has no sales relevance

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Available-credit information is a useful, discreet clienteling tool for informing how you present a follow-up opportunity to that specific customer — it should never be shared with anyone other than the customer themselves.
