# C8-M05 Quiz: Pricing Repairs and Custom Work

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly applying transparent pricing and approval practices, not memorizing any one store's specific deposit amount.

---

**1. What are the three essential components of a sustainable repair or custom-work price?**
A) The client's original purchase price, their emotional attachment, and a discount
B) Labor, materials, and margin
C) Only materials, since labor should never be charged separately
D) A single arbitrary total with no underlying components

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Labor covers skilled bench time and quality control; materials cover metal, stones, consumables, and vendor charges; margin covers operating costs and sustainable profit.

---

**2. What is the operational difference between an estimate and a quote?**
A) There is no meaningful difference; the terms are always interchangeable
B) An estimate is a good-faith projection based on visible conditions, while a quote is a fixed price for a defined, agreed scope of work
C) A quote is always lower than an estimate
D) Estimates should never be used for repair work

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Using the right term matters because an estimate may change if hidden conditions are discovered, while a quote creates a clear shared commitment on a fully defined scope.

---

**3. What should an associate do if a hidden condition discovered during bench inspection expands the repair scope beyond the original estimate?**
A) Complete the expanded work and bill the client afterward without further contact
B) Pause, document the finding, explain the updated scope and quote, and obtain the client's approval before doing additional work
C) Ignore the hidden condition and proceed only with the original work regardless of safety or durability
D) Cancel the job automatically with no explanation

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This estimate-to-updated-quote approval process prevents disputes, respects the client's choice, and follows directly from the intake discipline established in Module 2.

---

**4. When is a repair deposit especially appropriate?**
A) Never; all repairs must always be paid only at pickup
B) When a job requires new materials, outside-vendor work, or significant specialized bench time, because the deposit covers real committed costs and is applied toward the final invoice
C) Only when the client appears wealthy
D) Only for repairs that take less than one hour

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A deposit protects specific, recoverable costs and confirms commitment to a fully explained scope; it should be framed as a credit toward the actual work, not an arbitrary barrier.

---

**5. What is the consistent core pattern across real custom-jewelry deposit policies reviewed for this module?**
A) Custom work begins with no deposit, and all payment is due only after completion
B) A deposit is required before custom work begins; design labor becomes non-refundable once performed; a meaningful production payment (commonly 30-50% or more) is collected once the scope/design is agreed
C) Every jewelry business uses exactly the same deposit amount and refund policy
D) Deposits are only used for repair work, never for custom design

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The exact amount, refundability timeline, and revision allowance vary genuinely by business, but this core sequence is consistently supported across numerous independent real business policy pages.

---

**6. Why do custom policies commonly include a defined number of CAD revision rounds rather than unlimited revisions?**
A) Because clients should never be allowed to review a CAD model
B) Because unlimited revisions have no ceiling on design labor cost; defining included rounds and disclosing any additional-fee trigger protects both client expectations and the store's labor investment
C) Because CAD revisions take no real time or labor
D) Revision limits only exist to make clients unhappy

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Clear limits make the process more transparent: clients know what is included, and the store can explain further fees before additional work is performed rather than surprising them later.

---

**7. Why may a client changing their selected stone after CAD approval require a new design fee?**
A) Stone changes never affect the CAD model in any way
B) The model was built around the original stone's specific dimensions, so a new stone can require a new model and new design labor; the fee reflects that actual new work, not a penalty
C) A new fee is always illegal whenever a stone changes
D) The store should silently substitute the new stone without checking the model

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A stone's dimensions directly affect the setting design, so a change after approval may genuinely require technical remodelling rather than a simple substitution.

---

**8. In the worked scenario, why does the associate call the client before proceeding from a simple solder repair to a full re-shank?**
A) Because the associate wants to delay the repair unnecessarily
B) Because the original amount was an estimate based on the visible crack; the bench inspection revealed broader thinning, so the more extensive repair needs a new quoted scope, timeline, and client approval before work proceeds
C) Because a full re-shank is always illegal to perform
D) Because simple solder repairs are never allowed

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is the estimate-versus-quote discipline in practice: document the hidden condition, explain why the scope changed, and obtain informed approval before moving forward.

---
