# C8-M04 Fact-Check: Common Bench Repairs

## Verification Status: Ring-sizing timeline factors are among the most heavily cross-corroborated topic areas in this course; chain/clasp minute-level figures sourced from a single detailed source

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Ring sizing mechanics and timelines (down-sizing 2-4 days for plain gold, same-day/24-48hr possible for simple cases; up-sizing 7-14 days for ≥1.5 size changes; metal-type and stone-configuration effects).**
Status: STRONGLY CROSS-CORROBORATED across at least six independent sources: Pawn Shop Greensburg PA ("sizing running two to four business days" for simple cases, "stone replacement or full restoration stretching to a couple of weeks"), Repair Jewelry Solutions' bench-time table (matching 2-4 day gold figure, 3-7 days for platinum/stones), JewelTrendPro's engagement-ring-resizing guide (specifically citing 3-5 days for down-sizing ≤2 sizes and 7-14 days for up-sizing ≥1.5 sizes), Oahlan Jewelry's comprehensive guide (the most detailed single source, independently covering every factor — direction, metal, stones, design detail — this module presents, with closely matching timeframes), Seven Oaks Jewelers' own stated in-house process timeline (7-10 business days, a somewhat longer but still consistent figure from an actual practicing jeweler), and Wedding Frontier's guide (1-3 weeks range). This qualitative factor set (direction, metal, stones, design detail all affecting timeline in the same direction) is remarkably consistent across all sources despite some numeric variance, representing one of the best-corroborated topic areas encountered in this course.

**2. Platinum and hard-metal resizing limitations (platinum slower/more specialized; tungsten carbide generally not resizable; titanium/stainless steel limited).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across Oahlan Jewelry's detailed metal-by-metal breakdown and Repair Jewelry Solutions' bench-time table (both independently noting platinum requires laser welding and more skill); cross-consistent with this course's own C7 sibling content on hard-metal and eternity-band resizing limitations, referenced directly in this module.

**3. Prong retipping vs. rebuilding distinction and timelines (retipping 1-5 business days, some same-day/1-2 day quotes; five-step process sequence).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across six sources: My Jewelry Repair's three dedicated service pages (prong repair, retipping, replacement — all quoting "3-5 Business Days After Approval"), Rick Terry Jewelry ("one to three business days at most jewelry repair shops"), Sunshine Jewelry Philadelphia ("5 days or less"), Opulent Opal ("Same day to 1-2 days" for straightforward retipping), iTouch Diamonds Houston ("within a week or less"), and Jewelry Secrets' informal blog ("1 day to 3 days" for simple prong work). My Jewelry Repair's pages, while commercial service pages with a promotional purpose, provide the most detailed, consistent five-step process breakdown (assess, clean/prepare, apply metal, finish/polish, final inspection) and were used as the primary structural reference for this module's process description.

**4. Chain and clasp repair bench-time estimates (single-link re-solder 15-25 min; multi-link replacement 45-75 min; clasp replacement 20-40 min).**
Status: Sourced directly from JewelTrendPro's gold-necklace-chain-repair cost guide, which provides a detailed labor-time table with these specific minute-level figures. This is a SINGLE SPECIFIC SOURCE for these exact figures, though the general qualitative pattern (chain/clasp work being meaningfully faster than sizing or prong work) is consistent with the broader pattern found across all other repair-timeline sources reviewed in this module, which don't provide competing minute-level figures for direct comparison.

**5. The risk of rushing heat/metal-manipulation/stone-removal work, and same-day service being unrealistic for complex repairs specifically.**
Status: Sourced directly from Pawn Shop Greensburg PA's guide, which states this explicitly ("Same-day service on complex repairs is a myth, because any work involving heat, metal manipulation, or stone removal needs careful, deliberate handling, and rushing it is how stone[s are lost]"). Cross-consistent with the general "don't rush repairs" caution found in Oahlan Jewelry's and Jewelry Secrets' guides, both of which independently caution against expecting unrealistic same-day turnaround for anything beyond the simplest jobs.
