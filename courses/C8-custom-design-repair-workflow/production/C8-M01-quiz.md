# C8-M01 Quiz: The Repair and Custom Design Continuum

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly applying the continuum framework, not memorizing every specific job-type example.

---

**1. How does this module frame the relationship between "repair" and "custom design" work?**
A) They are entirely separate departments with no shared infrastructure or personnel
B) They exist on a single continuum, from simple bench repairs through moderate restoration, redesign/remounting, to fully custom builds, often sharing the same intake system and bench jewelers
C) Repair work is always more complex than custom design work
D) Custom design and repair never involve the same staff members

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Most jewelry stores use one consistent operational system and often the same personnel across this entire range, rather than treating repair and custom design as unrelated silos.

---

**2. What core intake elements should apply to every job, regardless of where it sits on the complexity continuum?**
A) Only custom design jobs need a job ticket; simple repairs don't require one
B) A unique job ticket, a clear scope of work, photographic documentation of starting condition, and a tracked status
C) Photographic documentation is only necessary for antique or high-value pieces
D) Job tracking is unnecessary for jobs expected to take less than a day

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This core intake discipline is what prevents jobs from getting lost or miscommunicated, and it applies consistently regardless of a job's complexity or expected timeline.

---

**3. What is a common job-status sequence used across jewelry-specific repair-tracking software?**
A) There is no consistent status sequence used across the industry
B) A sequence such as intake, awaiting approval, in progress, quality check, and ready for pickup
C) Jobs are only ever tracked as "done" or "not done," with no intermediate stages
D) Status tracking is only relevant for custom design work, never for repairs

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This general sequence is consistently used across multiple independent jewelry-repair-software platforms, providing both staff and clients visibility into where a job actually stands.

---

**4. How does a bench jeweler's role typically span the repair-to-custom-design continuum?**
A) Bench jewelers only ever perform one type of work and never move between repair and custom design tasks
B) A bench jeweler often performs routine repairs, assesses structural integrity, and increasingly uses CAD/3D printing alongside traditional hand skills to help produce custom-design pieces, moving fluidly across the continuum
C) CAD and 3D printing have completely replaced traditional bench-jeweler hand skills
D) Custom design work is always handled by a completely separate team from repair work

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This fluid movement across the continuum, within a single bench jeweler's role, is exactly why this course treats repair and custom design as related skills rather than separate tracks.

---

**5. What client-communication touchpoints should apply across the continuum, scaled to each job's timeline?**
A) No communication is necessary until the job is fully complete
B) Intake confirmation, an approval step when scope/pricing needs sign-off, updates if scope or timing changes, a completion notification, and a pickup/shipping confirmation
C) Only custom design jobs require any client communication at all
D) A single communication at the very end is sufficient for any job type

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This consistent cadence, scaled appropriately to each job's actual complexity and timeline, should apply to both a five-day repair and a ten-week custom build alike.

---

**6. Why does chain-of-custody logging matter across the entire continuum, not just for complex custom builds?**
A) Chain-of-custody tracking is only relevant for the most expensive pieces
B) It's the specific mechanism that lets a store confidently answer "where is my piece right now?" at any point, and it protects the store if a piece's condition or whereabouts is ever questioned, regardless of job complexity
C) Chain-of-custody logging is unnecessary for any in-house work
D) Only outsourced repairs require any chain-of-custody documentation

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Every handoff — from counter to storage, to an outside specialist, and back to the client — should be logged against a single job ticket, and this matters regardless of how simple or complex the underlying job is.

---

**7. What is the correct response when a job's actual complexity turns out to exceed its initial assumption (e.g., a simple repair reveals a need for more extensive work)?**
A) Proceed with the more extensive work immediately without informing the client
B) Pause, document the new finding, and return to the client with an updated scope and price for their sign-off before continuing
C) Ignore the new finding and complete only the originally requested work regardless of quality concerns
D) Cancel the job entirely without further discussion

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This documented approval step protects both the client (who deserves informed consent about a bigger job) and the store (which needs a clear, agreed record before doing additional work).

---

**8. In the worked scenario, why does the bench jeweler pause and seek approval rather than simply proceeding with a full re-shank?**
A) Because the client explicitly forbade any additional work
B) Because the job's complexity and scope changed from the original assumption, and the client deserves a documented, informed choice about the new cost and timeline before work proceeds
C) Because re-shanking a ring is illegal without written authorization
D) Because the bench jeweler doesn't actually have the skill to perform a re-shank

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This reflects the module's core principle that scope changes require documented client approval, regardless of where on the continuum a job started.

---
