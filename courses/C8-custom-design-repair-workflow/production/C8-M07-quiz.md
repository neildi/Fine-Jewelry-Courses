# C8-M07 Quiz: Quality Control and the Client Handoff

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly applying the inspection sequence and warranty-transparency discipline, not memorizing every checklist item.

---

**1. What are the four core areas covered by a systematic final inspection before a piece is returned to a client?**
A) Only a quick visual glance is necessary; no systematic sequence is needed
B) Stone security, surface/finish under magnification, function, and specification match against the original scope
C) Only the price and paperwork need to be checked before handoff
D) Final inspection is only necessary for custom pieces, never for repairs

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This consistent four-part sequence should be run the same way every time, catching issues a casual glance would miss.

---

**2. Why does this module emphasize "do not skip sections because the first few checks looked fine"?**
A) Because skipping sections is always acceptable if the piece looks fine overall
B) Because a piece can pass one check (like stone security) while still failing another (like function or spec match), so a systematic, complete process is needed to catch defects a partial check would miss
C) Because inspection sequences have no real bearing on final quality
D) Because only the first check in any sequence actually matters

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Different defect types show up at different inspection stages, so completing every step regardless of earlier results is what actually catches problems.

---

**3. Why is comparing the finished piece against the original intake photos and condition notes an important final-inspection step?**
A) This comparison serves no real purpose and can be skipped
B) It directly demonstrates that the agreed work was performed correctly and that anything documented as pre-existing is unchanged, closing the loop opened by Module 2's intake documentation
C) Intake photos are only useful for marketing purposes, not quality verification
D) This comparison is only relevant for custom design work, never for repairs

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This comparison is the practical payoff of the intake documentation discipline covered earlier in this course, verifying the work matches what was agreed and disclosed.

---

**4. What do repair and craftsmanship warranties typically cover, and what do they typically exclude?**
A) Warranties cover absolutely everything that could happen to a piece, with no exclusions
B) Warranties typically cover defects in the specific workmanship performed (like a solder joint failing under normal wear), while excluding normal wear and tear, damage from misuse, pre-existing conditions, and often any service performed by a third party
C) Warranties never actually cover anything and are purely decorative
D) Warranties only cover pieces that have never been worn

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This consistent scope, confirmed across multiple real jewelry-business warranty documents, is exactly what needs to be explained accurately to a client rather than oversold.

---

**5. Why might a "lifetime" warranty or guarantee not actually provide unconditional, permanent coverage?**
A) "Lifetime" always means completely unconditional coverage with no exceptions
B) Some lifetime guarantees specifically require documented professional inspection on a set schedule (commonly every six months) to remain valid, and missing that cadence can void coverage the client believed was permanent
C) Lifetime warranties are illegal and therefore never actually offered
D) Lifetime coverage has no relationship to any inspection requirement

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This conditional structure is a real, documented pattern (for example, in major retailers' lifetime diamond/gemstone guarantees), and clients need to understand it clearly to actually keep their coverage valid.

---

**6. What is the typical remedy under most repair or craftsmanship warranties, per this module?**
A) An unlimited, open-ended replacement guarantee for any issue whatsoever
B) A capped remedy limited to re-performing the specific defective work or refunding the original service fee
C) Warranties never actually specify any remedy at all
D) The remedy is always full replacement of the entire piece regardless of the defect

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Understanding this cap prevents an associate from implying a broader guarantee than the store's actual policy provides.

---

**7. What should a genuine client handoff include, beyond simply returning the finished piece?**
A) Nothing beyond a quick verbal confirmation that the work is done
B) A direct comparison to the intake photos, care guidance specific to the piece, and a clear, accurate explanation of the warranty's actual scope
C) Handoffs should be as brief as possible with no additional information shared
D) Care guidance and warranty explanations are unnecessary if the client doesn't ask

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Proactively providing this information, rather than waiting to be asked, is what turns a routine pickup into a moment that builds genuine client confidence and trust.

---

**8. In the worked scenario, why does the technician complete the full inspection and handoff process even at the end of a long day?**
A) Because store policy requires staying late regardless of circumstances
B) Because a shortcut taken to save a few minutes risks a stone loss, a warranty dispute, or a client leaving without understanding what they own — costs that far outweigh the time saved
C) Because customers never notice whether inspection steps were skipped
D) Because custom pieces never actually require any final inspection

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This reflects the module's core principle that the inspection and handoff discipline holds regardless of time pressure, since the cost of a shortcut is genuinely higher than the time it would save.
