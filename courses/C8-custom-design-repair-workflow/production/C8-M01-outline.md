# C8-M01 Outline: The Repair and Custom Design Continuum: From Simple Fixes to Full Builds

## Module Metadata
- Course: C8 - Custom Design & Repair Workflow
- Module: 1 of 8
- Persona: P1
- Working title: The Repair and Custom Design Continuum: From Simple Fixes to Full Builds
- Learning objective: Associates can place an incoming job on the repair-to-custom-design continuum, explain why the same intake discipline and client-communication cadence apply across that entire range, and recognize when a job's complexity changes mid-stream and requires a documented, approved scope update.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: Foundational module establishing vocabulary and operational framework for the entire course.

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames repair and custom design as one continuum, not separate departments, setting up Modules 2-8.
2. **The Continuum, From Simple to Complex** — Simple bench repairs, moderate repairs/restoration, redesign/remounting, fully custom design, each with forward references to later modules.
3. **Why the Same Infrastructure Serves the Whole Continuum** — Consistent job-ticket/status-tracking systems; bench jewelers working fluidly across the spectrum; consistent client-communication cadence.
4. **Chain of Custody: Why This Matters Across the Whole Continuum** — Logging every handoff against a single job ticket, regardless of job complexity.
5. **A Worked Scenario: The Job That Changes Complexity Mid-Stream** — Illustrative synthesis of pausing for documented scope-change approval when a job's actual complexity exceeds its initial assumption.
6. **Practice Exercise** — Five-job continuum-sorting exercise with forward-module mapping.
7. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The four-tier complexity continuum (simple bench repairs, moderate repairs/restoration, redesign/remounting, fully custom design) is this module's own organizing synthesis, built to structure the rest of the course; while not drawn from a single external source using this exact four-tier framework, each tier's description is grounded in the operational and job-type descriptions found across the repair-software and workflow sources reviewed.
- The consistent job-status-tracking sequence (intake, awaiting approval, in progress, quality check, ready for pickup) is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across at least seven independent jewelry-repair-software sources (WJewel, Jooli POS, RepairSuite, PrismaNote's repair-process guide, BusinessMind, Luxare, PIRO Retail, RepairBook.co.uk), all describing highly similar status sequences and consistent core intake data fields (customer details, item description, photos, estimated cost, target date).
- The description of bench jewelers working fluidly across repair and custom-design work, combining traditional hand skills with CAD/3D printing, is sourced from Nobbier's "What is a Bench Jeweler" article and corroborated qualitatively by a Reddit r/jewelers community thread describing the real range of shop types and work-mix variation across bench-jeweler roles.
- The client-communication cadence (intake confirmation, approval, updates on scope/timing changes, completion notification, pickup reminder) is sourced from MJJ Brilliant's wholesale-repair-program guide, which explicitly lists this exact five-step touchpoint sequence, cross-consistent with PrismaNote's eleven-stage repair-process guide (which covers the same touchpoints in more granular stages).
- The chain-of-custody logging practice (recording every handoff — counter to storage, store to outbound shipment/specialist, specialist back to store, store to client — against a single unique job ticket) is sourced directly from MJJ Brilliant's guide, which explicitly describes this exact chain-of-custody workflow for a repair-outsourcing context; the general principle is treated as applicable across the continuum, not just for outsourced work.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
