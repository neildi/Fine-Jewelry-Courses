# C8-M08 Fact-Check: Capstone — Managing the Repair and Custom Design Pipeline

## Verification Status: Pipeline-stage structure and communication triggers confirmed directly from jewelry-industry repair-software sources; prioritization/capacity-buffer/daily-check-in principles adapted from general job-shop and auto-repair-domain scheduling literature (explicitly flagged as domain adaptations)

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Pipeline-stage structure (intake/backlog → scheduled/to-do → in progress with blocked sub-states → QC → ready for pickup).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT and directly sourced from multiple real jewelry-repair-tracking software product descriptions: Shelvdon's explicit 4-stage Kanban pipeline (Backlog, To Do, In Progress, Done, with a Collected archive), PIRO Retail's repair-software workflow (received, at the bench, waiting on parts, polishing, QC, ready), Gem Logic's repair-management workflow (drop-off, photograph/label, workshop progress board visible to the whole team, done/notified, pickup), WJewel's repair-tracking buyer's guide (intake, approval, assign, track status changes, QC/complete, pickup), and Jewelia's production-management feature page (intake, design/CAD, casting/assembly, setting/finishing, QC, delivery). This is a well-corroborated, jewelry-industry-specific, product-market-confirmed structure, not a generic adaptation.

**2. General prioritization principles beyond pure FCFS (bottleneck/blocking visibility, priority criteria beyond arrival order).**
Status: Draws on a formal academic job-shop-scheduling framework (a University of Tennessee dissertation describing bottleneck identification with FCFS applied specifically to non-bottleneck operations) plus applied repair-shop guidance from RedAppy's resource-allocation-optimization and scheduling-optimization articles and Heavy Duty Journal's shop-bay-management guide (both explicitly describing priority criteria beyond simple arrival order, and comeback/priority job categories). IMPORTANT SOURCING NOTE: the RedAppy and Heavy Duty Journal sources are written specifically for the auto-repair-shop industry, not jewelry repair. This module presents the underlying scheduling concepts (bottleneck visibility, multi-factor priority criteria, comeback-job precedence) as reasoned, domain-general adaptations to a jewelry bench context, and this framing should be understood as adapted guidance rather than jewelry-specific research findings.

**3. Capacity buffer guidance (roughly 10-20% below theoretical maximum capacity).**
Status: Sourced from Automotive Business Guide's repair-shop-downtime-reduction article (specific example: planning around ~35 billable hours out of a theoretical 45, i.e., roughly a 22% buffer at 110% productivity) and Mechanics.app's auto-repair-scheduling guide ("build in 10-15% buffer for unexpected issues"). Both sources are from the auto-repair-shop domain, not jewelry repair specifically. This module presents the buffer principle and approximate percentage range as a reasoned adaptation to jewelry bench capacity planning, flagged accordingly rather than presented as jewelry-industry-specific data.

**4. Daily check-in practice comparing the day's plan against emerging reality.**
Status: Sourced from RedAppy's resource-allocation-optimization article's described operational cadence ("night before... morning huddle... live check-ins... rebalance when a job ends early, slips long, or exposes new work"), again from the auto-repair domain and adapted here to a jewelry bench context with the same domain-adaptation caveat as above.

**5. Pipeline-wide proactive client communication at every meaningful status change.**
Status: This claim IS confirmed directly from jewelry-industry-specific sources, distinct from the general scheduling claims above. PIRO Retail's repair-software description explicitly states "send updates when the work is approved, delayed or ready for pickup, instead of leaving your team to field 'is it done yet?' phone calls," and WJewel's buyer's guide independently confirms "every update auto-notifies the customer" as a standard feature across the status-tracking lifecycle. This directly extends and reinforces Module 2's already-established, previously fact-checked status-transparency commitment.

**6. Worked scenario and practice exercise (the twelve-job Monday-morning triage).**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from any external source. It was deliberately designed to integrate elements from prior C8 modules (a rework/QC-failed job from Module 7, a stone-sourcing-blocked job connecting to Module 4/6 content, and a CAD-approval-pending custom job connecting to Module 3's workflow) to serve its stated purpose as this course's capstone integration exercise.
