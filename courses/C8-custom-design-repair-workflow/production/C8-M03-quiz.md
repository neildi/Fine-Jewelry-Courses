# C8-M03 Quiz: The CAD-to-Casting Workflow

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly sequencing the process and tracing defects to their causes, not memorizing every exact temperature figure.

---

**1. What are the two main methods for turning an approved CAD file into a physical pattern?**
A) Hand-carving is the only method ever used
B) 3D-printed wax or resin patterns (now dominant for one-off custom pieces) and traditional hand-carved wax patterns
C) CAD files are cast directly into metal with no intermediate pattern step
D) Only metal 3D printing is used; wax is never involved

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: 3D printing has become the dominant method for one-off custom work because it requires no tooling and captures fine CAD detail precisely, while hand-carving remains used for certain artistic or tactile design processes.

---

**2. What is the key difference between direct wax 3D printing and castable resin 3D printing?**
A) They are identical processes with no meaningful difference
B) Direct wax printers produce a pattern ready for casting with no further processing, while castable resin patterns require UV post-curing and generally carry more burnout risk
C) Resin printing is always faster and more reliable than wax printing
D) Wax printing cannot be used for any custom jewelry work

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This distinction has real practical consequences for burnout success and finished-casting quality, which is why understanding it matters beyond just knowing that "3D printing" is involved.

---

**3. Why does the investment-mixing step (removing air bubbles through timed mixing and vibration) matter for the final casting?**
A) It has no real effect on the finished piece
B) Trapped air bubbles in the investment become defects (like porosity) in the final metal casting, so removing them before the investment sets is a genuine quality step
C) Air bubbles are always harmless and can be ignored
D) This step is purely cosmetic and unrelated to casting quality

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This is a specific, physical mechanism connecting an early-process step (investing) to a later-visible defect (porosity), illustrating why careful process execution matters throughout.

---

**4. What happens during the burnout stage, and why does lost-wax casting get its name from this step?**
A) The metal is melted during burnout; wax is not involved at this stage
B) The filled flask is heated gradually in a kiln (commonly 8-12 hours), which melts and vaporizes the wax pattern entirely, leaving a precise cavity — the wax is genuinely destroyed (lost) in this process
C) Burnout refers to polishing the finished metal piece
D) The wax pattern is preserved and reused after burnout

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The wax pattern's destruction during this controlled heating process is literally what gives lost-wax casting its name.

---

**5. Why are centrifugal or vacuum-assisted casting methods used to introduce molten metal into the mold, rather than simply pouring it in?**
A) These methods have no real advantage over simple pouring
B) They force metal into fine, delicate areas like thin prongs and detailed engraving before the metal solidifies, preventing incomplete fill in those areas
C) These methods are only used for very large, simple pieces
D) Centrifugal and vacuum casting are purely decorative terms with no functional difference

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Without this additional force, molten metal might not fully reach every fine cavity before cooling, leaving thin or missing detail in the finished piece.

---

**6. What two purposes does the water quench serve after the metal solidifies?**
A) It only cools the metal, with no other function
B) It further cools the metal and simultaneously breaks apart the now-brittle investment material, releasing the metal casting from its mold
C) It only breaks apart the investment, with no cooling function
D) The water quench has no functional purpose in the casting process

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This step accomplishes two things at once — cooling and mold removal — which is why it's a standard, efficient part of the process.

---

**7. Which of the following is a common casting defect, and what typically causes it?**
A) "Porosity," caused by using too much metal in the casting
B) "Porosity" (small internal or surface voids), most commonly caused by trapped air in the investment, insufficient vacuum/vibration during investing, or improper metal temperature during the pour
C) Casting defects are extremely rare and essentially never occur in practice
D) All casting defects are immediately visible from the outside before any finishing work

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Understanding the specific mechanical causes of common defects (like porosity) helps explain why careful process execution and post-casting quality control both matter.

---

**8. In the worked scenario, why does the associate explain the physical casting process to a client asking for a faster timeline, rather than simply saying "it takes time"?**
A) Because a vague explanation is always more persuasive than a specific one
B) Because tying the timeline to genuine, physical process steps (like the 8-12 hour kiln burnout) gives the client an honest, credible reason, rather than a placating one that could erode trust if the deadline needs to be pushed later
C) Because the actual casting process has no bearing on realistic timelines
D) Because clients never actually want to understand the reasoning behind a timeline

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Specific, physically-grounded explanations are both more honest and more persuasive than vague appeals, especially when a client is pushing for a faster outcome than the process can safely support.
