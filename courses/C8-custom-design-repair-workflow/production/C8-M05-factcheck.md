# C8-M05 Fact-Check: Pricing Repairs and Custom Work

## Verification Status: Custom deposit/revision structures exceptionally well corroborated across eleven real business policy pages; labor/materials/margin framework flagged as original organizing synthesis

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Labor + materials + margin as the three-part price framework.**
Status: This framework is this module's OWN ORGANIZING SYNTHESIS of standard small-business pricing logic, applied to jewelry repair/custom work, rather than a direct quotation from one jewelry-specific source. The specific examples within each category (bench labor, metal, replacement stones, casting, CAD media, outside-vendor services) are grounded in the repair-workflow and custom-design-policy sources reviewed, but the exact three-part framework itself should be understood as a pedagogical synthesis, not an industry-standard named formula.

**2. Estimate vs. quote distinction and approval-before-exceeding principle.**
Status: Directly supported by Gem Logic's jewelers' deposits-and-quotes guide, which states "A quote without a deposit is a promise that costs nothing to break" and recommends deposits before work begins; PrismaNote's repair-workflow guide independently supports the estimate → client-approval → work sequence. The module's specific distinction (estimate = good-faith projection under stated assumptions; quote = fixed price for defined scope) is a reasoned articulation of this consistent operational logic.

**3. Repair-deposit framing (covering materials and specialist time, applied to final invoice).**
Status: Sourced directly from Gem Logic's guide, which provides nearly identical recommended language ("For this type of repair we ask for a deposit to cover the materials and the specialist time involved. The deposit is applied against the final invoice when you collect."). Cross-consistent with Agiled's jewelry-invoice template guidance noting deposits for significant restorations/materials.

**4. Custom-design deposit structures (design/CAD deposit, production deposit commonly 30-50%+, staged payments for large commissions, design labor becoming non-refundable once performed, defined included revision rounds, new fee for post-approval stone changes).**
Status: EXCEPTIONALLY WELL CROSS-CORROBORATED across ELEVEN independent, real jewelry-business policy pages:
- The Custom Jeweler: $250 non-refundable CAD deposit, applied to final cost; 50% production deposit; $100 third-revision fee.
- AW Jewelry: $250 deposit refundable during concept stage; first project payment non-refundable once CAD begins.
- Elaine B Jewelry: $200+ non-refundable design deposit, $300+ CAD fee with one edit, 50% production deposit.
- Marblehead Custom Jewelry: 50% non-refundable deposit or $300 design fee; explicit scope-change approval requirement.
- Sluder & Co: $200 non-refundable deposit applied to final balance, then 50% after CAD approval.
- Sarah O Jewelry: $200 non-refundable credited deposit, up to three CAD modifications included.
- Diamond Ateliers: non-refundable deposits, explicit written approval required for scope changes.
- Lili Jewels: 50% non-refundable deposit; full payment for lower-value orders.
- Elvie Fine: $1,000 non-refundable initial deposit plus a second deposit for significant stones.
- TL Paul Jewelry Design: three included revisions; new fee for a post-approval stone change requiring a new model.
- Stacy Nolan: non-refundable deposit plus 50% remaining-balance payment; approval timeline requirement.
All sources agree on the CORE PATTERN (deposit before work; design labor becomes non-refundable once performed; meaningful production payment once scope/design is agreed; revision limits prevent unbounded labor) while showing REAL, LEGITIMATE variation in exact dollar amount, refundability timeline, creditability, and number of included revisions. This module correctly presents the pattern and range rather than imposing any one store's policy as universal.
