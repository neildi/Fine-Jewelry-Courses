# C8-M05 Outline: Pricing Repairs and Custom Work: Labor, Materials, and Margin

## Module Metadata
- Course: C8 - Custom Design & Repair Workflow
- Module: 5 of 8
- Persona: P2
- Working title: Pricing Repairs and Custom Work: Labor, Materials, and Margin
- Learning objective: Associates can explain repair/custom pricing as labor + materials + margin, distinguish an estimate from a fixed quote and manage scope changes appropriately, explain deposits as direct coverage for committed work/materials, and set transparent client expectations around custom design deposits, revision limits, staged payments, and post-approval stone changes.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Price repair and custom work accounting for labor, materials, and margin, and explain that pricing transparently to a client."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames a price as a sustainable promise covering real work/materials, not a rough guess.
2. **The Three-Part Price: Labor, Materials, and Margin** — What each component covers; client-facing explanation.
3. **Estimate vs. Quote: A Difference That Prevents Disputes** — Definition of each; appropriate use; approval-before-exceeding discipline.
4. **Repair Deposits: When and Why** — Material/specialist-time protection; applied-to-final-invoice framing; commitment signal.
5. **Custom Design Deposits: Design Work Is Work** — Design/CAD deposit, production deposit, staged payment structures; policy variation and written clarity.
6. **Revision Limits and Scope Changes** — Defined included revision rounds, additional-fee trigger, post-approval stone change requiring a new model/design fee.
7. **A Worked Scenario: The Scope Change After Inspection** — Illustrative synthesis showing estimate-to-revised-quote communication and client approval before expanded work.
8. **Practice Exercise** — Explaining a three-part price and a full custom deposit/revision policy conversation.
9. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The labor + materials + margin framework is this module's own organizing synthesis of standard small-business pricing logic, applied to jewelry repair/custom work. The specific examples of included costs (bench labor, metal, replacement stones, casting, CAD media, outside-vendor services) are grounded in the repair-workflow and custom-design-policy sources reviewed; the framework itself is not quoted from one single jewelry-specific source.
- Estimate-vs-quote distinction and the approval-before-exceeding principle are directly supported by Gem Logic's jewelers' deposits-and-quotes guide, which states: "A quote without a deposit is a promise that costs nothing to break" and recommends deposits before work begins; PrismaNote's repair-workflow guide independently supports the estimate → client-approval → work sequence.
- Repair-deposit framing (covering materials and specialist time, applied to the final invoice) is sourced directly from Gem Logic's guide, which provides nearly identical recommended language; cross-consistent with Agiled's jewelry-invoice template guidance noting deposits for significant restorations/materials.
- Custom-design deposit structures are STRONGLY CROSS-CORROBORATED across eleven independent real jewelry-business policy pages: The Custom Jeweler ($250 non-refundable CAD deposit; 50% production deposit; $100 3rd-revision fee), AW Jewelry ($250 design-calendar deposit refundable during concept stage; non-refundable first project payment once CAD begins), Elaine B Jewelry ($200+ non-refundable design deposit; 50% production deposit; $300+ CAD fee; 1 edit), Marblehead Custom Jewelry (50% non-refundable deposit or $300 design fee), Sluder & Co ($200 non-refundable deposit credited toward final balance; 50% after CAD approval), Sarah O Jewelry ($200 non-refundable credited deposit; up to 3 CAD modifications), Diamond Ateliers (non-refundable production deposits; explicit written approval required for scope changes), Lili Jewels (50% non-refundable), Elvie Fine ($1,000 non-refundable initial deposit plus a second deposit for significant stones), TL Paul Jewelry Design (3 included revisions; new fee for stone change after CAD approval), and Stacy Nolan (non-refundable deposit + 50% remaining-balance payment). All sources agree on the core pattern (deposit before work; design labor becomes non-refundable once performed; 30-50%+ production payment once scope/design is agreed) while showing real variation in dollar amount, refundability timeline, and number of included revisions — this module presents the pattern and ranges rather than imposing a universal policy.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
