# C8-M03 Outline: The CAD-to-Casting Workflow: How a Custom Piece Actually Gets Made

## Module Metadata
- Course: C8 - Custom Design & Repair Workflow
- Module: 3 of 8
- Persona: P2
- Working title: The CAD-to-Casting Workflow: How a Custom Piece Actually Gets Made
- Learning objective: Associates can explain the five-stage lost-wax casting process (pattern creation, investing, burnout, casting, finishing) accurately enough to set credible client expectations, distinguish direct wax vs. castable resin printing, and identify the likely origin point of common casting defects.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Explain the CAD-to-casting workflow well enough to guide a client through a fully custom design project from concept to finished piece."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames physical-process literacy as both a client-confidence tool and an honest-timeline tool, generalizing this course's bridal-specific CAD content into the full manufacturing pipeline.
2. **Stage One: From CAD File to Physical Pattern** — 3D-printed wax/resin vs. hand-carved wax; direct wax vs. castable resin printing distinction and burnout-rate implications.
3. **Stage Two: Building the Investment Mold** — Sprue attachment; investment material composition; the air-bubble-removal step and why it's a real quality step, not cosmetic.
4. **Stage Three: Burnout** — Kiln temperature ramp (~1,350-1,400°F, 8-12 hours); why the wax is genuinely destroyed, giving lost-wax casting its name.
5. **Stage Four: Casting the Metal** — Metal-specific casting temperatures; centrifugal vs. vacuum-assisted casting; why force matters for fine detail like prongs.
6. **Stage Five: Cooling, Breakout, and Finishing** — Water quench (cooling + investment removal); pickling; sprue-cutting; transition into hand-finishing.
7. **Common Casting Defects and Why They Happen** — Porosity, incomplete fill, investment inclusions, each traced to a specific process-stage cause; forward reference to Module 7's quality control.
8. **A Worked Scenario: Explaining the Timeline Honestly** — Illustrative synthesis of using the actual physical process to justify a timeline rather than a vague appeal.
9. **Practice Exercise** — Plain-language process explanation plus defect-origin identification exercise.
10. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The five-stage lost-wax casting process (pattern creation, investing, burnout, casting, cooling/breakout/finishing) is STRONGLY CROSS-CORROBORATED across at least eight independent sources describing highly consistent stage sequences and terminology: Cookson Gold's detailed hands-on guide, HMS Manufacturing's investment-casting guide, 96jewelry's ten-step manufacturing guide, Honho Jewelry's process walkthrough, PepeTools' casting manual, Casting Australia's guide, Jewellers Academy's wax-carving guide, and Guru Diam's blog (a source also used elsewhere in this course's C6 research). Guru Diam's guide was used as the primary reference for the specific stage descriptions and terminology in this module, given its clear, current, and detailed treatment.
- Specific technical figures (investment burnout temperature ~1,350-1,400°F over 8-12 hours; metal casting temperatures ~1,000-1,800°F depending on alloy) are sourced directly from Guru Diam's guide and HMS Manufacturing's guide respectively, both credible, detailed, process-specific sources; Cookson Gold's hands-on guide provides a corroborating, though somewhat different, specific kiln-ramp schedule (150-200°C, then 400°C, then 720°C at specific hold times) for a silver-casting context specifically, illustrating that exact temperatures/times vary by metal and by specific caster's practice — this module presents the general range rather than a single universal figure.
- The direct-wax-vs-castable-resin-printing distinction and the reported burnout-success-rate difference (direct wax printing generally achieving meaningfully higher successful burnout than resin) is sourced from FlashForge Enterprise's WaxJet-vs-resin guide and Originator3D's direct-wax-vs-resin comparison (which cites a specific 99.9% vs. 75-85% burnout-success comparison). IMPORTANT NOTE: both of these specific sources are vendor/manufacturer marketing content for wax-printing equipment, so the specific numeric comparison should be treated as a vendor-reported figure illustrating a real, generally-corroborated qualitative pattern (resin requires more careful post-processing and carries more burnout risk) rather than an independently audited statistic. This module presents the qualitative pattern with appropriate caution about the specific percentage figures' sourcing.
- Common casting defects (porosity from trapped air/insufficient investing; incomplete fill from cooling too fast or insufficient casting force; investment inclusions from a cracked investment shell) are sourced from Guru Diam's guide, which explicitly discusses common defects, cross-consistent with the general quality-control literature reviewed (the AIMnet PDF on jewellery investment casting quality control, a technical/academic source, corroborates porosity and investment-related defect causes at a more technical level).
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
