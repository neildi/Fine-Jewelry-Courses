# C8-M08 Quiz: Capstone: Managing the Repair and Custom Design Pipeline

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This quiz tests whether you can apply pipeline-management principles across a mixed batch of jobs, not just recall a single job's workflow.

---

**1. What is the core problem with treating every job in a repair/custom pipeline as its own isolated task with no shared structure?**
A) There is no real problem; isolated tracking works fine at any volume
B) It makes it impossible for anyone other than the technician who originally took a job to answer a client's status question, and makes it easy for jobs to go stale unnoticed
C) Shared structure is only useful for very large stores, never for smaller operations
D) Isolated tracking is always faster than a shared pipeline structure

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A shared, visible pipeline structure is what allows any team member to track and communicate about any job, which becomes essential as job volume grows.

---

**2. Why should a job that's waiting on a special-order stone or an outside vendor be flagged as a distinct pipeline state rather than simply left in the general queue?**
A) Blocked jobs should be treated exactly the same as jobs that are simply next in line
B) A blocked job can't move forward regardless of bench priority, so repeatedly revisiting it wastes time; flagging it as distinct keeps attention focused on jobs that can actually progress
C) Blocked jobs should always be cancelled rather than flagged
D) There is no meaningful difference between a blocked job and a queued job

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Distinguishing blocked jobs from queued jobs prevents wasted effort and keeps the team's attention on jobs where progress is actually possible.

---

**3. Besides arrival order, what other factors should genuinely inform job prioritization in a repair/custom pipeline?**
A) Prioritization should always be strictly first-come-first-served with no other factors considered
B) Promised due date, what's actually blocking the job, matching complexity to the right technician's skill, and giving real precedence to previously-delayed "comeback" jobs
C) Only the size or dollar value of the job should ever matter
D) Prioritization has no real impact on client outcomes

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A purely first-come-first-served approach ignores real cost-of-delay differences and skill-matching opportunities that a more complete prioritization approach captures.

---

**4. Why might a "comeback" or previously-delayed job deserve real priority precedence over a job just entering the queue?**
A) Comeback jobs should never receive any special priority
B) A client whose job already missed a promised date has a legitimately higher cost of further delay than a job just starting its wait
C) Comeback jobs are always technically simpler and therefore faster to prioritize
D) Priority should be based solely on which job physically arrived first, regardless of any prior delay

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The client on a comeback job has already experienced a broken commitment, making further delay more costly than for a job that hasn't yet missed any promised date.

---

**5. Why does this module recommend planning bench capacity at roughly 10-20% below the theoretical maximum, rather than scheduling every available hour?**
A) Scheduling every available hour is always the most efficient approach with no downside
B) A fully booked schedule has no slack to absorb inspection findings, approval delays, or additional work discovered once a job is on the bench, so a single disruption cascades into every job behind it running late
C) Capacity buffer has no real effect on whether jobs run on time
D) Buffer time should only be used for staff breaks, never for absorbing job delays

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Holding back some capacity as buffer is what allows a schedule to absorb the inevitable disruptions that show up once jobs are actually underway, without cascading delays across the whole pipeline.

---

**6. What is the value of a brief daily check-in comparing the day's plan against emerging reality?**
A) Daily check-ins are unnecessary if the schedule was built correctly the night before
B) It catches schedule drift early — since one late approval or one missing part can reorder a day's priorities — before several jobs have already slipped
C) Daily check-ins should only happen once a week at most
D) Schedules never actually change once they're set, so check-ins serve no purpose

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A quick daily comparison lets the team rebalance priorities in response to real developments, rather than discovering problems only after they've already caused delays.

---

**7. Why does this module argue that proactive client communication becomes MORE important, not less, as pipeline volume grows?**
A) Communication matters less as volume grows, since staff have less time available
B) A larger pipeline makes it easier for any single job's status to go stale without anyone noticing, unless proactive updates are a structural habit rather than something remembered only when time allows
C) Client communication is equally important regardless of pipeline volume, with no relationship between the two
D) Only the largest or most expensive jobs in a pipeline need proactive communication

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: As the number of simultaneous jobs increases, informal or memory-based communication becomes less reliable, making a structural communication habit more essential, not less.

---

**8. In the worked Monday-morning scenario, why does the manager prioritize the two rework jobs from Friday over other work?**
A) Rework jobs should always be handled last since they already received attention once
B) Those jobs represent clients who have already waited past a promised date, giving them a genuinely higher cost of further delay than jobs just entering triage
C) Rework jobs are always the easiest and fastest jobs to complete, which is the only reason for prioritizing them
D) The manager prioritizes them randomly with no specific reasoning

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This reflects the comeback/previously-delayed job precedence principle directly, applied to a specific realistic scenario.
