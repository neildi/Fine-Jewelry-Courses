# C8-M07 Fact-Check: Quality Control and the Client Handoff

## Verification Status: Warranty mechanics confirmed across multiple real primary jewelry-business warranty documents; final-inspection sequence explicitly adapted from wholesale/manufacturing QC sources to a single-piece retail context

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Four-part final inspection sequence (stone security via lateral pressure; surface/finish under 4x-10x magnification; function via cycle testing; specification match against approved reference).**
Status: IMPORTANT SOURCING NOTE — this sequence is adapted from wholesale/manufacturing jewelry QC checklists. Global Jewelry Guide's detailed 30-point pre-shipment checklist, Sourcify's jewelry quality checklist, 96jewelry's pre-shipment QC guide, and Silverbene's final-inspection checklist all independently describe highly consistent inspection categories and specific test methods (lateral pressure stone testing, cycle testing for clasps/hinges, magnified surface inspection, mandrel-verified ring sizing). However, these sources are PRIMARILY WRITTEN FOR WHOLESALE/OEM BULK-ORDER MANUFACTURING QUALITY CONTROL (using AQL batch-sampling methodology), NOT specifically for single-piece retail bench-repair or custom-work handoff. This module explicitly adapts their inspection categories and specific test methods to the single-piece retail context, which is a reasoned, transparent extension rather than a directly retail-sourced practice. The underlying physical inspection techniques (lateral pressure on stones, magnified surface checks, cycle testing moving parts) are equally valid and applicable at the single-piece level, even though the sources' original context is batch manufacturing.

**2. The "do not skip sections because the first few pieces looked fine" discipline.**
Status: Sourced directly and verbatim from Global Jewelry Guide's checklist, originally written in a batch-sampling context ("do not skip sections because the first few pieces looked fine"); this module adapts the same underlying principle to a single-piece systematic-inspection context, where the equivalent risk is skipping inspection categories because early checks on the same piece looked acceptable.

**3. Before/after intake-photo comparison at final inspection.**
Status: This is this module's own direct extension of Module 2's already-established, previously fact-checked photo-pairing discipline (see C8-M02 factcheck) into the final-inspection stage, rather than a new externally-sourced claim requiring independent verification here.

**4. Warranty mechanics (workmanship-only coverage; exclusion of normal wear, pre-existing conditions, and third-party service; capped remedy limited to re-performance or refund of service fee).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across multiple real, primary warranty/terms documents: Marblehead Custom Jewelry's actual repair-warranty terms ("6-month limited warranty on craftsmanship and materials we supply... covers defects in workmanship related to the specific repair we performed... does not cover normal wear and tear... pre-existing damage... issues arising from work not performed by us... liability... limited to the cost of the original repair"), Watch Technicians' terms of service (explicit tiered warranty periods by service type — 90 days/6 months/1 year — with matching exclusions and a re-perform-or-refund remedy, and explicit "Warranty Voidance" if serviced by others), and GemsNY's lifetime warranty policy (explicit exclusion for lost/damaged gems, explicit voidance if maintained by a third party or if not maintained on a timely inspection schedule). This is strong, directly-sourced, multi-document confirmation of a highly consistent industry-standard warranty structure.

**5. Conditional "lifetime" guarantee requiring a documented inspection cadence (commonly six months) to remain valid.**
Status: Sourced directly from SureBright's warranty-explainer article, which specifically and by name cites "Kay's Lifetime Diamond & Gemstone Guarantee" as requiring inspections "performed and documented by an authorized representative" every six months — a specific, named, real-world example. Cross-consistent with GemsNY's own policy language ("Warranty is void if the jewelry is not maintained in a timely manner (i.e. annual gemstone tightening)") and J. Collins' warranty page, which distinguishes a lifetime warranty on non-custom pieces from a separate, shorter 30-day quality guarantee specifically for custom jewelry — illustrating that warranty terms genuinely differ by piece type (custom vs. non-custom), which this module's general framing accounts for.
