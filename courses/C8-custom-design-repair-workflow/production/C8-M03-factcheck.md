# C8-M03 Fact-Check: The CAD-to-Casting Workflow

## Verification Status: Core five-stage casting process exceptionally well cross-corroborated across many independent sources; direct-wax-vs-resin burnout percentages flagged as vendor-reported marketing claims

## Claims Reviewed

**1. The five-stage lost-wax casting process (pattern creation, investing, burnout, casting, cooling/breakout/finishing).**
Status: STRONGLY CROSS-CORROBORATED across at least eight independent sources: Cookson Gold's detailed hands-on guide, HMS Manufacturing's investment-casting guide, 96jewelry's ten-step manufacturing guide, Honho Jewelry's process walkthrough, PepeTools' casting manual, Casting Australia's guide, Jewellers Academy's wax-carving guide, and Guru Diam's blog. All describe a highly consistent stage sequence and terminology, differing mainly in how finely they subdivide certain stages (e.g., some sources list 7 steps, others 9 or 10, but the underlying process is identical). Guru Diam's guide was used as the primary reference for this module's specific stage descriptions and terminology, given its clear, current, and detailed treatment; Guru Diam is also referenced elsewhere in this course's C6 research, adding some cross-course consistency.

**2. Specific technical figures (investment burnout temperature ~1,350-1,400°F over 8-12 hours; metal casting temperatures ~1,000-1,800°F depending on alloy).**
Status: Sourced directly from Guru Diam's guide (burnout figures) and HMS Manufacturing's guide (casting-temperature range) respectively, both credible, detailed, process-specific sources. Cookson Gold's hands-on guide provides a genuinely different, corroborating-but-distinct specific kiln-ramp schedule (150-200°C, then 400°C, then 720°C at specific hold times) for a silver-casting context specifically — this module explicitly notes that exact temperatures and times vary by metal type and by the specific caster's practice, presenting a general range rather than a single universal figure, which accurately reflects this genuine variance.

**3. Direct wax vs. castable resin 3D printing distinction and the specific reported burnout-success-rate comparison (99.9% wax vs. 75-85% resin).**
Status: IMPORTANT SOURCING NOTE — sourced from FlashForge Enterprise's WaxJet-vs-resin guide and Originator3D's direct-wax-vs-resin comparison, BOTH OF WHICH ARE VENDOR/MANUFACTURER MARKETING CONTENT for wax-3D-printing equipment specifically. The specific numeric comparison (99.9% vs. 75-85%) should therefore be treated as a vendor-reported figure rather than an independently audited statistic, since these companies have a direct commercial interest in favoring wax printing over resin. That said, the underlying QUALITATIVE pattern — that resin patterns require additional post-curing and generally carry more burnout-related risk than direct wax patterns — is independently corroborated by a more neutral source, Yihui Casting's comparison guide, and consistent with general, non-vendor-specific discussion found in a Reddit r/jewelrymaking community thread. This module presents the specific percentage figures with explicit sourcing caution while treating the general qualitative pattern as reasonably well-supported.

**4. Common casting defects (porosity, incomplete fill, investment inclusions) and their process-stage origins.**
Status: Sourced from Guru Diam's guide, which explicitly discusses these defects and their causes; cross-consistent at a more technical/academic level with the AIMnet PDF on jewellery investment-casting quality control (a technical academic source specifically addressing quality control and defect causes in jewelry investment casting), which independently corroborates porosity and investment-related defect mechanisms.

**5. Worked scenario and practice exercise.**
Status: ORIGINAL illustrative synthesis created for this module, not drawn from any external source.
