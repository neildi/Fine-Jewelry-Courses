# C8-M04 Outline: Common Bench Repairs: What They Involve and How Long They Take

## Module Metadata
- Course: C8 - Custom Design & Repair Workflow
- Module: 4 of 8
- Persona: P1
- Working title: Common Bench Repairs: What They Involve and How Long They Take
- Learning objective: Associates can explain the specific mechanics and give realistic, factor-based timeline estimates for ring sizing (up/down, by metal and stone configuration), prong retipping/rebuilding, and chain/clasp repair, and can correctly identify when a same-day promise is and isn't honest.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Correctly scope, time-estimate, and explain the most common bench repairs (sizing, retipping, re-shanking, stone replacement, chain/clasp repair)."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames accurate, credible repair timelines as dependent on genuinely understanding the process, not rounding to a guess.
2. **Ring Sizing: Up, Down, and Everything That Changes the Timeline** — Down vs. up sizing mechanics/timelines; metal-type effects (gold/silver fast, platinum slower, hard metals often not resizable); stone-configuration effects (solitaire vs. pavé/halo vs. eternity); design-detail effects (engraving, filigree, two-tone).
3. **Prong Retipping and Rebuilding** — Retipping vs. rebuilding distinction; the five-step process sequence; realistic timeframes for each.
4. **Chain and Clasp Repair** — Single-link re-solder, multi-link replacement, clasp replacement bench-time estimates; the bench-time-vs-total-turnaround distinction.
5. **Why "Same-Day" Isn't Always Honest, Even When It's Technically Possible** — The real risk of rushing heat/metal-manipulation/stone-removal work; when same-day is genuinely appropriate vs. not.
6. **A Worked Scenario: The Client Who Wants It "By Friday"** — Illustrative synthesis of giving an honest, factor-based timeline under real scheduling pressure.
7. **Practice Exercise** — Five-job timeline-estimation exercise with factor-based justification.
8. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- Ring sizing mechanics and timelines (down-sizing 2-4 days for plain gold bands, same-day/24-48hr possible for simple cases; up-sizing 7-14 days for changes ≥1.5 sizes; metal-type effects; stone-configuration effects of 5-14 days for pavé/halo settings; eternity bands often not resizable) are STRONGLY CROSS-CORROBORATED across at least six independent sources: Pawn Shop Greensburg PA's detailed timeline guide, Repair Jewelry Solutions' bench-time table, JewelTrendPro's engagement-ring-resizing guide, Oahlan Jewelry's comprehensive resizing-timeline guide (the most detailed single source, covering all the same factors this module presents), Seven Oaks Jewelers' own in-house process description (7-10 business days), and Wedding Frontier's resizing-timeline guide. This is one of the most heavily cross-corroborated topic areas in this course, with consistent qualitative factors (direction, metal, stones, design detail) even where specific day-ranges vary modestly by source.
- Prong retipping vs. rebuilding mechanics and timelines (retipping 1-5 business days across multiple sources, with some citing same-day to 1-2 days for simple cases; the five-step assess/clean/apply/finish/inspect process sequence) are CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across My Jewelry Repair's three dedicated service pages (prong repair, retipping, replacement), Rick Terry Jewelry's prong-repair guide, Sunshine Jewelry's Philadelphia retipping page, Opulent Opal's prong-inspection guide, iTouch Diamonds' Houston retipping page, and Jewelry Secrets' informal but detailed "why repairs take 2 weeks" blog post. My Jewelry Repair's pages, while commercial service pages, provide the most detailed, consistent process-step breakdown and were used as the primary structural reference.
- Chain and clasp repair bench-time estimates (single-link re-solder 15-25 min; multi-link replacement 45-75 min; clasp replacement 20-40 min) are sourced directly from JewelTrendPro's gold-necklace-chain-repair cost guide, which provides a detailed labor-time table; this is a single specific source for these exact minute-level figures, though consistent with the general pattern (chain/clasp work being faster than sizing or prong work) found across other sources reviewed.
- The risk of rushing heat/metal-manipulation/stone-removal work, and the general principle that same-day service is a "myth" for complex repairs specifically, is sourced directly from Pawn Shop Greensburg PA's guide, which states this explicitly, cross-consistent with the general "don't rush repairs" caution found in Oahlan Jewelry's and Jewelry Secrets' guides.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
