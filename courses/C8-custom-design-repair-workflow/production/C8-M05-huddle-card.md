# C8-M05 Huddle Card: Pricing Repairs and Custom Work

## 5-Minute Pre-Shift Discussion

**The one idea to remember:** An estimate is NOT a quote. An estimate can change when hidden conditions appear; a quote is a fixed price for defined work. Never let a client think one is the other.

## Every Price Has Three Parts
1. **Labor** — skilled bench time, inspection, setting, finishing, QC
2. **Materials** — metal, stones, solder, clasps, casting, vendor work
3. **Margin** — operating cost + sustainable profit
Client-facing language: "This covers the specific work and materials needed to do this safely and correctly."

## Deposit Logic
- **Repair deposit**: new materials, specialist time, or outside-vendor work → applied to final invoice
- **Custom design deposit**: starts concept/CAD labor; typically non-refundable once work starts, often credited toward final piece
- **Production deposit**: commonly 30-50%+ after design approval; covers committed materials and production
- Exact policy varies by store — know and say YOUR store's terms before work begins

## Revision Limits Prevent Disputes
State the included revisions upfront (often 1-3). More changes = disclose fee BEFORE doing them. New stone after CAD approval may mean a new model + new design fee — that's new work, not a penalty.

## Scope Change Script
"The original amount was an estimate based on what we could see. The bench found [specific condition], so the recommended repair is now [new scope]. The updated quote is [amount], and I won't proceed without your approval."

## Today's Discussion Prompt
Practice saying "estimate" and "quote" correctly in a repair conversation. What exact language will you use so a client never confuses the two?
