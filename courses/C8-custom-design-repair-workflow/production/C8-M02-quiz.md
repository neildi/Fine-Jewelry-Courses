# C8-M02 Quiz: Intake

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly applying the inspection, photography, and documentation discipline, not memorizing every checklist item in isolation.

---

**1. Why is documenting a piece's condition before accepting it for work the single most important intake discipline?**
A) It has no real protective value for either the client or the store
B) It protects both the client (from a store misattributing pre-existing damage to the repair) and the store (from a client misattributing unrelated, pre-existing damage to the repair work)
C) It is only useful for high-value pieces
D) It is purely a formality with no practical consequence

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Pre-existing condition documentation protects both parties by establishing an accurate, contemporaneous record before any work begins.

---

**2. What does the "inspect, rotate, confirm, and document" principle address?**
A) The idea that a single viewing angle is always sufficient to assess a piece's condition
B) The risk that a single viewing angle can miss loose settings, hairline cracks, or hidden residue, making multi-angle inspection necessary before drawing any conclusion
C) A requirement that applies only to gemstones, never to metal
D) A marketing slogan with no practical inspection application

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Rotating a piece and checking multiple angles is specifically necessary because certain defects are only visible from particular viewing angles.

---

**3. What is the standard photo sequence recommended at intake?**
A) A single overall photo is always sufficient
B) One clear overall photo, closer photos of the repair area and existing damage, photos of hallmarks/inscriptions, and magnified photos of any stone requiring close inspection
C) Photos should only be taken after the repair is complete, never before
D) Photography is optional and rarely used in practice

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This consistent, multi-shot sequence, taken before work begins and repeated identically afterward, is what actually proves work was done correctly and nothing unrelated changed.

---

**4. Why should the same photos be repeated after work is complete, in the same lighting and angles as the intake photos?**
A) Repeating photos serves no real evidentiary purpose
B) The matched before/after pair is what actually proves the work was done correctly and that nothing unrelated to the repair changed in the process
C) Only the "before" photos matter; "after" photos are unnecessary
D) Photos should be taken in different lighting each time to show variety

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A consistent before/after comparison is the actual mechanism that demonstrates the repair's scope and outcome accurately.

---

**5. Why is a condition note like "some wear" considered inadequate, compared to a note naming a specific location, feature, and neutral observation?**
A) Vague notes are actually preferred because they are faster to write
B) A vague note documents almost nothing useful, while a specific, located, neutral note (e.g., "shank at the 6 o'clock position shows visible thinning") actually holds up if a question arises later
C) Condition notes have no real bearing on any later dispute
D) Specific notes are only required for custom design work, never for repairs

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Specificity is what makes a condition note actually useful as evidence; vague impressions provide little real protection or clarity.

---

**6. What should a complete intake record include, per this module?**
A) Only the customer's name, with no other details
B) Customer/order details, a specific item description, the client's stated concern and requested work, documented condition and photos, the quote/deposit/balance, and the client's confirming signature
C) Intake records are unnecessary for jobs expected to take less than a day
D) Only the estimated price needs to be recorded at intake

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This complete record, confirmed by cross-source agreement across multiple intake-process guides, is what supports both accurate work and later dispute protection.

---

**7. What do typical jewelry-repair liability waivers actually limit store responsibility to, and what do they typically exclude?**
A) Waivers make the store responsible for absolutely any outcome, with no limitations at all
B) Waivers typically limit store liability to damage caused by the store's own negligence (often capped at the lesser of a stated value or repair/replacement cost), and typically exclude responsibility for undisclosed pre-existing conditions or gem treatments
C) Waivers have no real legal content and are purely decorative documents
D) Waivers guarantee full replacement value for any lost or damaged item regardless of cause

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This negligence-based, capped-liability structure with an explicit pre-existing-condition exclusion is confirmed consistently across multiple real repair-agreement documents, and it's exactly why genuine intake documentation is what determines what protection a waiver actually provides.

---

**8. In the worked scenario, why does the bench jeweler point out the pre-existing chip to the client immediately, rather than simply noting it privately and proceeding?**
A) Because pointing it out has no real benefit to either party
B) Because a contemporaneous, disclosed, and documented observation protects both the client (who now has clear information rather than a later surprise) and the store (which now has direct evidence the condition was pre-existing)
C) Because store policy requires refusing any piece with existing damage
D) Because the client would have no way of noticing the chip regardless of when it's mentioned

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Proactive, in-the-moment disclosure and documentation is exactly the practice that prevents a future dispute about whether the chip was caused by the repair.
