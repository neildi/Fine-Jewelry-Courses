# C8-M07 Outline: Quality Control and the Client Handoff

## Module Metadata
- Course: C8 - Custom Design & Repair Workflow
- Module: 7 of 8
- Persona: P1
- Working title: Quality Control and the Client Handoff
- Learning objective: Associates can run a consistent, systematic four-part final inspection (stone security, surface/finish, function, spec match) on any repair or custom piece, compare finished work against intake documentation, accurately explain what a repair/craftsmanship warranty does and doesn't cover (including conditional "lifetime" guarantees), and run a genuine, unhurried client handoff rather than a rushed transaction.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Apply a consistent quality-control standard before any repair or custom piece is handed back to a client."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames final inspection and handoff as the moment every prior module's work is validated and delivered.
2. **The Final Inspection: A Consistent, Systematic Sequence** — Four-part sequence (stone security, surface/finish under magnification, function, spec match); the "don't skip steps" discipline.
3. **Comparing Against the Intake Record** — Direct before/after photo and condition-note comparison, closing the loop from Module 2.
4. **What a Warranty Actually Promises** — Workmanship-only coverage; common exclusions (wear, pre-existing conditions, third-party service); conditional "lifetime" guarantees requiring inspection cadence; capped remedy.
5. **The Handoff Itself: More Than Just Returning the Piece** — Comparison walkthrough, care guidance, warranty explanation, unhurried presentation for custom/redesign pieces.
6. **A Worked Scenario: The Rushed End of a Long Day** — Illustrative synthesis of maintaining full inspection/handoff discipline under time pressure at day's end.
7. **Practice Exercise** — Full inspection-sequence narration plus complete handoff-conversation practice.
8. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The four-part final inspection sequence (stone security via lateral-pressure testing; surface/finish under 4x-10x magnification checking for porosity/tool marks/solder marks/scratches; function via clasp/hinge/moving-part cycle testing and ring-size mandrel verification; specification match against approved reference) is adapted from wholesale/manufacturing jewelry QC checklists — Global Jewelry Guide's detailed 30-point pre-shipment checklist, Sourcify's jewelry quality checklist, 96jewelry's pre-shipment QC guide, and Silverbene's final-inspection checklist all independently describe highly consistent inspection categories and specific test methods (lateral pressure stone testing, cycle testing for clasps/hinges, magnified surface inspection). IMPORTANT NOTE: these sources are primarily written for wholesale/OEM manufacturing quality control (bulk-order, AQL-sampling contexts), not specifically for single-piece retail bench-repair or custom-work handoff — this module adapts their inspection categories and methods to the single-piece retail repair/custom context, which is a reasoned extension rather than a directly retail-sourced practice.
- The "do not skip sections because the first few pieces looked fine" discipline is sourced directly and verbatim from Global Jewelry Guide's checklist, adapted here from a batch-sampling context to a single-piece systematic-inspection context.
- The before/after intake-photo comparison practice at final inspection is this module's own direct extension of Module 2's already-established, previously fact-checked photo-pairing discipline, not a new externally-sourced claim.
- Warranty mechanics (workmanship-only coverage; exclusion of normal wear, pre-existing conditions, and third-party service; capped remedy limited to re-performance or refund of service fee) are CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across several real, primary warranty/terms documents: Marblehead Custom Jewelry's actual repair-warranty terms (6-month limited warranty, explicit exclusion list, liability capped at cost of original repair), Watch Technicians' terms of service (tiered warranty periods by service type: 90 days/6 months/1 year, explicit exclusions, re-perform-or-refund remedy, third-party-service voidance), and GemsNY's lifetime warranty policy (explicit exclusions for lost/damaged gems, voidance if maintained by a third party, voidance if not maintained on a timely inspection schedule). This is strong, directly-sourced, multi-document confirmation of a consistent industry-standard warranty structure.
- The conditional "lifetime" guarantee requiring a documented inspection cadence (commonly six months) to remain valid is sourced directly from SureBright's warranty-explainer article, which specifically cites Kay's Lifetime Diamond & Gemstone Guarantee's six-month documented-inspection requirement as a real, named example; cross-consistent with GemsNY's own policy language requiring "timely" maintenance/tightening to keep the warranty valid, and J. Collins' warranty page (distinguishing a lifetime warranty on non-custom pieces from a 30-day quality guarantee specifically for custom jewelry).
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
