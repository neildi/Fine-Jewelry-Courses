# C8-M04 Quiz: Common Bench Repairs

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly applying the factors that drive repair timelines, not memorizing every exact day figure.

---

**1. Why does sizing a ring down generally take less time than sizing it up?**
A) There is no real timeline difference between the two directions
B) Sizing down involves cutting out and removing a small section of metal and rejoining the ends, while sizing up requires sourcing matching metal, inserting it, and achieving seamless grain alignment — more steps generally means more time
C) Sizing up is always done same-day, while sizing down always takes weeks
D) Metal type has no bearing on sizing direction or timeline

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The additional steps involved in sourcing and integrating new metal for an up-size genuinely add time compared to the simpler removal process for a down-size.

---

**2. How does the presence of side stones, pavé, or a halo setting typically affect a ring-sizing timeline, compared to a solitaire?**
A) Stone configuration has no effect on sizing timelines
B) These settings require careful, gradual heating so stones don't crack or loosen, plus a full prong check and tightening afterward, commonly extending the timeline to 5-14 days
C) Halo and pavé settings are always faster to resize than a plain solitaire
D) Only eternity bands are affected by stone placement; all other settings resize at the same speed

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The need for careful heat management around multiple stones, plus post-resize prong verification, adds real time beyond what a simple solitaire resize requires.

---

**3. What is the difference between prong retipping and prong rebuilding?**
A) They are identical procedures with no meaningful difference
B) Retipping adds new metal to an existing prong worn thin at the tip; rebuilding is needed when a prong has broken off or worn down beyond what retipping can restore, representing a more involved structural repair
C) Rebuilding is always faster than retipping
D) Retipping is only used on platinum, never on gold

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This distinction matters for both cost and complexity — rebuilding addresses more significant damage than a simple tip restoration.

---

**4. What is a realistic bench-time range for a standard prong retipping job at most repair shops?**
A) Always exactly 6 months
B) Commonly 1-5 business days, with some shops quoting as fast as same-day to 1-2 days for straightforward cases
C) Prong retipping is never actually offered by jewelry repair shops
D) Always over a year, regardless of complexity

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: This range reflects genuine, cross-corroborated variation depending on shop workflow and how many prongs need attention, rather than a single fixed figure.

---

**5. Why is it important to distinguish "bench time" from "total turnaround" when discussing a chain or clasp repair with a client?**
A) There is no real difference between bench time and total turnaround
B) A single-link re-solder might take only 15-25 minutes of actual bench work, but total turnaround (including any shop queue or shipping time) can be longer, so clarifying this prevents a client from expecting immediate pickup based on the bench-time figure alone
C) Total turnaround is always identical to bench time for every repair
D) Bench time is irrelevant information that should never be shared with clients

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Being clear about this distinction prevents a client from mishearing "20 minutes of work" as "ready to pick up in 20 minutes."

---

**6. Why can platinum ring sizing take longer than gold or silver ring sizing?**
A) Platinum has no real difference in resizing difficulty compared to gold
B) Platinum is denser and requires higher temperatures and more specialized skill, often extending the timeline and sometimes requiring referral to a specialist bench jeweler
C) Platinum is always faster to resize than any other metal
D) Platinum cannot be resized under any circumstances

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Platinum's specific physical properties genuinely require different, often more specialized handling than gold or silver, which is why its resizing timeline is typically longer.

---

**7. Why does this module caution against promising same-day service for a repair involving stones, structural rebuilding, or platinum, even if a client requests it?**
A) Because same-day service should never be offered under any circumstances, even for simple jobs
B) Because any work involving heat, metal manipulation, or stone removal needs careful, deliberate handling, and rushing it is a documented cause of damaged or lost stones
C) Because clients never actually want same-day service
D) Because same-day service is illegal for jewelry repairs

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Same-day service remains genuinely appropriate for certain simple, stone-free jobs, but promising it for more complex work risks exactly the kind of rushed handling that causes real damage.

---

**8. In the worked scenario, why does the associate decline to simply promise the halo-set ring will be ready by Friday?**
A) Because the associate doesn't want to help the client at all
B) Because the stone-adjacent work this ring requires (careful heating, prong re-tightening) realistically takes 5-14 days, and promising Friday risks either overpromising or rushing the work in a way that could damage a stone
C) Because halo settings can never be resized under any circumstances
D) Because the associate has no idea how long any repair takes

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Giving an honest, factor-based timeline rather than agreeing to an unrealistic deadline protects both the client's expectations and the actual safety of the repair work.
