# C8-M06 Outline: Redesign Consultations: Turning Heirloom Stones Into New Pieces

## Module Metadata
- Course: C8 - Custom Design & Repair Workflow
- Module: 6 of 8
- Persona: P2
- Working title: Redesign Consultations: Turning Heirloom Stones Into New Pieces
- Learning objective: Associates can run a complete, documented heirloom redesign discovery consultation (history, preservation priorities, decision-making authority, combination priorities), assess stone/metal condition honestly, explain the concrete risks and value implications of recutting before any irreversible work, and present melting vs. hand-assembly as genuine alternatives for combining multiple pieces.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Run a redesign/remounting consultation that respects a client's heirloom materials while setting honest expectations about what's achievable."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Frames redesign consultations as combining every technical skill in the course with the highest emotional stakes.
2. **Document Before You Touch Anything** — Complete "before" record (photos, marks, condition, provenance) and decision-making-authority confirmation, connecting to Module 2's intake discipline and emphasizing irreversibility.
3. **The Discovery Conversation: What to Actually Ask** — History, what must actually be preserved (often not the client's first assumption), lifestyle/wearability, combination priorities, timeline/budget.
4. **Assessing What You're Actually Working With** — Stone condition inspection under magnification; metal purity/alloy assessment; the reset-as-is default.
5. **Recutting: A High-Risk Option, Not a Routine Upgrade** — Concrete weight-loss figures (15% round-to-round, up to 40% cushion-to-round); breakage risk; value implications; independent-second-opinion recommendation.
6. **Melting Gold vs. "The Art of Assembly"** — Melting as one option; hand-disassembly/mechanical reassembly as a genuine alternative preserving physical continuity.
7. **A Worked Scenario: The Client Who Wants to Modernize Grandmother's Diamond** — Illustrative synthesis of presenting recutting trade-offs honestly and concretely before any irreversible commitment.
8. **Practice Exercise** — Full discovery-conversation roleplay plus recutting-trade-off explanation practice.
9. **Key Takeaways** — Five-point recap.

## Production Notes
- The document-before-touching, decision-making-authority-first, preservation-vs-redesign-comparison discovery process is sourced directly and in detail from Leyloon's "Redesigning Heirloom Jewelry" guide, a preservation-first, detailed guide covering provenance, condition, family authority, and documentation without promising alteration — this is the most rigorous single source used in this module and was used as the primary structural reference for sections 2 and 3.
- The specific discovery-question set (history/meaning, what to preserve, style/lifestyle, design concepts, timeline/pricing, combining heirlooms) is CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across Leon Diamond's "Incorporating Family Heirlooms into New Designs" guide, Legacy Jewellery's "Ultimate Guide to Redesigning Inherited Jewellery," D&H Jewelers' heirloom-redesign service description, and Hingham Jewelers' heirloom-redesign service page — all four independently describe a highly similar consultation-first, history-and-preservation-focused discovery process.
- Stone condition inspection practices (magnified inspection for chips, girdle wear, fractures, prior repair/repolishing evidence) are sourced from Imperial Cushions' "Antique Diamond Condition Guide" and "Recut or Preserve an Old Mine Diamond?" guide (both detailed, gemologically rigorous sources), cross-consistent with Your Diamond Guys' vintage-diamond guide and Peter Indorf's appraiser-evaluation guide.
- Recutting weight-loss figures (approximately 15% for an old round recut to modern round; up to 40% for an old cushion recut to round) and the specific risk factors (expense of weight loss plus possible breakage during faceting) are sourced directly from Michael Goldstein's "Antique Diamonds" article (a specialist antique-cut-diamond dealer's own published price-guide commentary, a credible primary trade source specifically on this topic), cross-consistent with Imperial Cushions' recut-vs-preserve framework, Gem Society's "Recutting Diamonds and Colored Gemstones" guide, and JCK's "Appraising 'Old-Cut' Gemstones" article (a trade-press source specifically discussing recutting formulas for appraisal purposes).
- The claim that recutting typically reduces rather than increases market value, since antique-cut-focused buyers pay a premium for original character, is sourced from Imperial Cushions' guide and Uniglo Diamonds' diamond-resale-value guide, both independently making this point; consistent with the general antique-jewelry-market logic established in this course's C7 sibling content.
- Melting vs. hand-disassembly/mechanical-reassembly ("the art of assembly") as a genuine alternative for combining multiple heirloom pieces is sourced directly from Gewels by Mona's "The Art of Assembly" guide (a specialist practitioner's detailed description of this exact technique) and independently corroborated by a LinkedIn Pulse article specifically titled "Should You Melt Down Old Jewellery for a Redesign?" which makes the same point about combining pieces without melting.
- The worked scenario and practice exercise are ORIGINAL illustrative syntheses, not externally sourced cases.
