# C8-M02 Fact-Check: Intake

## Verification Status: Liability-waiver mechanics confirmed across three independent, real primary repair-agreement documents; inspection/photo/documentation practices strongly cross-corroborated across dedicated jewelry-trade sources

## Claims Reviewed

**1. Magnified pre-existing-condition inspection targets (thinning metal, porosity, stress fractures, worn/bent prongs, hidden solder seams, structural fatigue; stone-specific pre-existing chip/abrasion/inclusion documentation).**
Status: Sourced directly from Stuller's "6 Tips for Metal, Diamond, & Gemstone Intake Every Bench Jeweler Should Know." Stuller is a major, well-established jewelry-industry supplier and trade-resource publisher, making this a credible, directly-on-point primary trade source specifically written for bench jewelers.

**2. The "inspect, rotate, confirm, and document" operating principle.**
Status: Sourced directly and verbatim from Smart G-Scope's jewelry inspection checklist, a detailed, purpose-built pre/post-repair visual workflow guide explicitly designed for jewelers and workshop teams.

**3. The four-shot intake photo sequence (overall item, repair-area/damage close-ups, hallmark/inscription photos, magnified stone-detail photos) and before/after photo pairing.**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across three independent sources: JewelIntake's intake-process guide ("Take one clear photo of the whole item, followed by closer photos of the repair area and any existing damage"), Luxare's mail-in-repair workflow guide (matching photo-sequence description for mail-in intake specifically), and LoupeLens' smartphone gemstone-inspection guide (explicitly describing the before/after repeat-shot documentation workflow: "At intake, photograph the piece overall, then each stone at 10x... After the work, repeat the same shots in the same lighting").

**4. The specific, located, neutral condition-note language standard.**
Status: Sourced directly from DealSpeak.ai's repair-intake-coaching guide, which explicitly models this exact note-writing approach ("Condition notes work best when they name a location, a visible feature, and a neutral observation") as a coaching best practice for retail district managers training repair-intake staff.

**5. The complete intake record checklist (customer/order details, item description, stated concern/requested work, condition/photos, quote/deposit/balance, signature).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across JewelIntake's explicit ten-step intake checklist and Luxare's mail-in intake-form field list, both independently describing highly overlapping required intake elements with matching structure and sequence.

**6. Liability waiver mechanics (negligence-based liability limitation; liability capped at the lesser of stated estimated value or repair/replacement cost; explicit exclusion for undisclosed pre-existing conditions and gem treatments).**
Status: CONFIRMED CONSISTENT across THREE INDEPENDENT, REAL, PRIMARY REPAIR-AGREEMENT DOCUMENTS: Schumacher Diamond Cutters' actual disclaimer form ("shall not be liable for any damage... except for those damages which are caused by the gross negligence... limited to the lesser of the 'estimate value' or repair or replacement cost"), Krikawa Jewelry Designs' actual repair agreement form ("is not responsible for condition of jewelry upon arrival... only responsible for damage, theft, or loss due to its own negligence, with liability limited to the actual cost to repair or replace items, not to exceed the stated estimated value"), and a sample form reproduced in ISI Print's custom-samples document reflecting Romm's jewelry store's actual agreement language ("not responsible for identifying the condition of jewelry or stones... not responsible for identifying filled diamonds or other gem treatments... unless identified by customer at the time of take in"). This is strong, directly-sourced, multi-document confirmation of a consistent industry-standard waiver structure, representing some of the highest-confidence primary-document sourcing in this course to date.
