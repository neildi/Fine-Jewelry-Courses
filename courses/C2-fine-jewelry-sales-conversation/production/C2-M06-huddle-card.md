# Huddle Card: "I'll Think About It" and the Follow-Up That Wins

**Headline:** "I'll think about it" is a pause, not a no. What you do in the next 30 seconds, and the next 7 days, decides which one it becomes.

**3 key points:**
1. Before a client reaches the door, ask one specific question: "Just so I know how to follow up, is there something specific you're still weighing?" This often surfaces the real objection.
2. Offer to hold the piece for 24-48 hours, framed as a service. It gives the client a reason to think about that exact piece, and gives you a reason to follow up.
3. Run a three-step follow-up ladder: same-day thank you, a 48-hour reason-driven touch, and a 7-day low-pressure invitation. One attempt is not a follow-up plan.

**Script to practice today:**
"Take all the time you need. Can I hold this one for you until tomorrow, so it's here if you decide?"

**Question to ask the team:**
Think of the last "I'll think about it" you didn't follow up on. What would a same-day, reason-driven message have said?
