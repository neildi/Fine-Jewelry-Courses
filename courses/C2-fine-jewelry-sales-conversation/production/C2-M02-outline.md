# C2 Module 2 — Production Outline

## Module purpose
Teach associates to replace assumption-led selling with a permission-based discovery conversation that produces a relevant, small product edit and accurate clienteling notes.

## Learning outcomes
By the end, learners can:

1. Explain why discovery precedes product presentation.
2. Use neutral questions across seven discovery areas.
3. Ask about investment range respectfully.
4. Connect lifestyle answers to product-care considerations.
5. Summarize a brief and curate two or three purposeful options.
6. Create useful, privacy-respecting clienteling notes.

## Article architecture

| Section | Learner job | Core content |
|---|---|---|
| Scenario hook | Recognize the cost of assumption | Emerald-pendant client; premature engagement assumption |
| Why discovery matters | Adopt the method | Trust, relevance, care, curation, continuity |
| Seven areas | Conduct the conversation | Occasion, wearer, style, lifestyle, collection, process, range |
| Edit | Turn answers into action | Confirm brief; anchor, contrast, stretch/simplify |
| Notes | Preserve service continuity | Consent, preferences, care needs, next step; privacy boundaries |
| Repairs | Avoid predictable failures | Rapid questioning, assumptions, early budget, over-showing |
| Practice and self-check | Build habit | Three-minute drill and closing checklist |

## Evidence plan

- FTC Jewelry Guides: treatment and special-care disclosures.
- GIA emerald and pearl care pages: care examples used only to show why lifestyle discovery matters.
- No sales-performance statistics or unsupported conversion claims.

## Accessibility and style controls

- Use plain-language questions associates can say aloud.
- Do not assume gender, relationship, wealth, or occasion.
- Make budget wording permission-based and offer an alternative.
- Frame care as an informed choice, not a fear tactic.
