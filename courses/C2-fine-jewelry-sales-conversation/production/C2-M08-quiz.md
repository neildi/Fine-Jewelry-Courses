# Module 8 Self-Assessment Quiz: Add-Ons, Protection Plans and Care Products Done Ethically

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: presenting add-ons as genuine service with clear disclosure and no pressure.

**1. What is a key difference between jewelry insurance and a typical store warranty?**
A. They are exactly the same product
B. Insurance typically covers loss, theft, and mysterious disappearance; a store warranty typically doesn't
C. Store warranties always cost more than insurance
D. Insurance only covers manufacturing defects
**Answer: B** - Conflating the two misleads the client about what they're actually protected against.

**2. What is the conflict of interest with an in-house appraisal?**
A. There is no conflict of interest; in-house appraisals are always neutral
B. The store has a financial interest in the appraised value, since a higher number supports the sale it just made
C. In-house appraisals are illegal
D. Only third-party appraisers have conflicts of interest
**Answer: B** - A seller cannot remain fully impartial evaluating merchandise it sold.

**3. What makes an appraisal truly independent?**
A. Any appraisal performed inside a jewelry store
B. An appraisal commissioned directly by the consumer, from an appraiser with no relationship to the sale
C. An appraisal the store pays a third party to perform
D. Independence doesn't matter for appraisals
**Answer: B** - Even a store-hired third party still works for the seller, not the consumer.

**4. What should you disclose when offering an in-house appraisal?**
A. Nothing; disclosure isn't necessary
B. That it's prepared by the store that sold the piece, and that an independent appraisal is available for significant purchases
C. That it is completely neutral and unbiased
D. That it replaces the need for insurance entirely
**Answer: B** - Clear disclosure builds trust rather than undermining the sale.

**5. What are the four steps of the ethical upsell framework described in this module?**
A. Pitch, pressure, discount, close
B. Observe, flag, offer options (including one with no cost), accept the answer
C. Assume, insist, repeat, close
D. Discount, bundle, upsell, ignore objections
**Answer: B** - This framework centers genuine service rather than attachment-rate pressure.

**6. Why should an add-on pitch typically come after the client commits to the main purchase?**
A. Timing doesn't matter
B. Raising add-ons before the main decision reads as distraction or pressure rather than service
C. It's required by law
D. Add-ons should never be mentioned at all
**Answer: B** - Sequencing the pitch this way keeps the focus on the client's primary decision first.

**7. What should you do if a client mentions they already have insurance covering a piece?**
A. Pitch a duplicate protection plan anyway
B. Ask what their current coverage includes before recommending anything additional
C. Insist they need your store's plan regardless
D. Ignore what they said and proceed with the standard script
**Answer: B** - Selling duplicate, unnecessary coverage isn't genuine service.

**8. What should you do if a client declines an add-on?**
A. Offer it again immediately, using a different angle
B. Accept the answer gracefully and move on
C. Lower the price to change their mind
D. Ask a manager to pressure the client further
**Answer: B** - Repeated pressure after a clear "no" erodes the trust built earlier in the visit.

**9. How should care products be recommended?**
A. As a blanket add-on sold identically to every client
B. Based on the specific metal and stones in the piece the client is buying
C. Only to clients who ask for them unprompted
D. Bundled automatically into every ticket without discussion
**Answer: B** - A generic, one-size-fits-all pitch isn't genuine service.

**10. What consumer-protection principle applies to bundling a protection plan into a total?**
A. Silence counts as consent
B. An explicit yes should always be obtained before adding any optional protection plan or insurance referral to the ticket
C. Bundling without disclosure is always acceptable in retail
D. Only large purchases require this kind of disclosure
**Answer: B** - Clear consent, not assumed consent, is the ethical standard for optional add-ons.
