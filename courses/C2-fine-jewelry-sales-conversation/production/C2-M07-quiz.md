# Module 7 Self-Assessment Quiz: Online Comparison and the Showrooming Client

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: verifying an online price comparison and winning the sale through demonstrated value.

**1. Why does Kyle Bullock say showrooming "doesn't have to be our enemy"?**
A. Online prices are always fake
B. A client using their phone in your store is still shopping there, not already gone, which is an opportunity
C. Showrooming never actually happens in jewelry stores
D. Store policy requires ignoring it
**Answer: B** - The client is still physically present and engaged, which gives you a real chance to win the sale.

**2. What should you do first when a client shows you a lower online price?**
A. Immediately match the price to avoid losing the sale
B. Verify the comparison is genuinely apples-to-apples (carat, cut, clarity, certifying lab, included services)
C. Tell them the online price is definitely fake
D. Ignore the comparison entirely
**Answer: B** - Many "same" listings differ meaningfully once you check the actual specifications.

**3. What is the side-by-side selection technique described in this module?**
A. Showing the client the cheapest option first
B. Letting the client choose between a few pieces by eye, before revealing specs or prices
C. Refusing to show more than one piece at a time
D. Always showing the most expensive piece first
**Answer: B** - Letting the client's own eyes make the choice is more persuasive than arguing specifications verbally.

**4. What does Devon Bond's collection of internet-purchased rings demonstrate?**
A. That all online jewelry is fraudulent
B. A tangible, physical way to show quality differences (thin prongs, porosity, missing stones) rather than just describing them
C. That customers should never buy jewelry online
D. A required store display for regulatory reasons
**Answer: B** - Physical evidence is more convincing than a verbal claim about quality.

**5. What does Tim Lewis mean when he says a grading report "doesn't tell you whether or not the diamond is pretty"?**
A. Grading reports are inaccurate
B. Two stones with identical color and clarity grades can still look very different in person
C. Grading reports are unnecessary
D. Beauty is graded on the report itself
**Answer: B** - This is why seeing stones in person, side by side, resolves comparisons that specs alone cannot.

**6. What did Van Hildebrand do when he genuinely could not match a client's price on a 3-carat oval?**
A. Refused to let the client leave
B. Told the client to buy it, then offered to verify the diamond and its grading report once it arrived
C. Matched the price anyway at a loss
D. Discouraged the client from buying at all
**Answer: B** - Honesty in that moment built long-term trust, and the client became a repeat customer.

**7. According to Adam Foster, what happens when a jeweler routinely matches or beats internet prices?**
A. Nothing changes for the business
B. It can be "the beginning of the end" of the business
C. It always increases profit
D. It guarantees customer loyalty
**Answer: B** - Reflexive price-matching undermines the value of a store's own product and pricing integrity.

**8. What are the key criteria for a disciplined price-match policy?**
A. Match any price a customer mentions, no questions asked
B. Match only identical items from reputable, verifiable sellers, in stock, without going below your margin floor
C. Never discuss price matching under any circumstances
D. Match only if the customer complains loudly enough
**Answer: B** - Applying clear criteria consistently protects margin and avoids teaching every client to negotiate.

**9. What is a calm way to hold your price if a client keeps pushing after a fair, verified comparison?**
A. Immediately give in to end the conversation
B. Repeat your price calmly and consistently, and if truly at an impasse, say you're unable to help them today
C. Argue forcefully about who is right
D. Call another employee to take over immediately
**Answer: B** - Consistent, calm persistence often works better than either caving or arguing.

**10. Why might an online price list from an unfamiliar source not be a fair comparison?**
A. All online prices are automatically fair comparisons
B. It may come from a wholesaler site or unverified marketplace seller not meant for retail comparison
C. Online prices are always higher than in-store prices
D. Price lists are illegal to bring into a store
**Answer: B** - Checking the source of a price list matters before treating it as a valid, comparable reference point.
