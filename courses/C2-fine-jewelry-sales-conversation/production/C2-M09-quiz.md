# Module 9 Self-Assessment Quiz: Three Ways to Ask for the Sale

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: recognizing a buying signal and closing with a matched technique, delivered confidently.

**1. According to Brad Huisken's estimate, how often is no attempt made to close a jewelry sale?**
A. Almost never
B. As high as 60% of the time
C. Exactly 10% of the time
D. Only when the customer asks for a discount
**Answer: B** - This means the majority of missed sales may come from never asking, not from a "no."

**2. What is the key language shift in the assumptive close?**
A. Using "ours" instead of "yours" or "his/hers"
B. Describing the piece as "yours," "his," or "hers" instead of "ours," throughout the presentation
C. Avoiding any pronouns entirely
D. Only using the client's first name repeatedly
**Answer: B** - This ownership language sets the stage before the actual closing question is even asked.

**3. Which of these is an example of an assumptive close?**
A. "Do you want to buy this ring?"
B. "I can have the ring sized by tomorrow morning. What time would you like to pick it up?"
C. "Is this too expensive for you?"
D. "Would you like to think about it?"
**Answer: B** - This question assumes the purchase is decided and moves straight to a logistics detail.

**4. What is the alternate choice close?**
A. Giving the customer many options to choose from
B. Offering two specific choices, structured so that either answer results in a completed sale
C. Asking the customer to choose between buying and not buying
D. Offering a discount as one of the choices
**Answer: B** - Example: "Would you like to pay with a check or a credit card?"

**5. What is the reflexive close?**
A. Ignoring a client's question and changing the subject
B. Answering a client's logistics question while simultaneously asking for the sale
C. Repeating the same close multiple times
D. Asking the client to reflect on their decision overnight
**Answer: B** - Example: client asks "Can I get this sized by Friday?" and you respond "Absolutely, what time would you like to pick it up?"

**6. What is the Ask For It Close?**
A. A close that requires a discount
B. Directly and simply asking the customer to buy
C. A close only used with returning customers
D. A close that avoids mentioning price
**Answer: B** - Example: "Let me write the earrings up for you. How did you want to pay for them?"

**7. Why does confidence matter as much as the words used in a close?**
A. It doesn't matter; the exact words are all that counts
B. Customers sense and react to uncertainty, which can undercut even a well-worded close
C. Confidence only matters for expensive items
D. Confidence should be avoided to seem less pushy
**Answer: B** - A hesitant, apologetic tone can undermine a technically correct closing script.

**8. What should you do if a close produces an objection instead of a "yes"?**
A. Give up and move to a different client
B. Treat it like any other objection, resolve it, and then try closing again
C. Immediately offer a large discount
D. Repeat the exact same close word-for-word
**Answer: B** - An objection after a close attempt is useful information, not a failed sale.

**9. When is the alternate choice close best used?**
A. At the very start of a presentation, before showing any merchandise
B. When a client has narrowed their choice to two items, or a decision point has clearly arrived
C. Only when a client explicitly asks for options
D. Only for the least expensive items in the store
**Answer: B** - This technique works well specifically when the client is close to a decision already.

**10. What is the underlying principle behind all three closing techniques in this module?**
A. Manipulating the customer into a purchase they don't want
B. Recognizing a buying signal and confidently asking for the sale, matched to the specific moment
C. Always using the exact same script regardless of context
D. Avoiding ever directly asking for the sale
**Answer: B** - None of these techniques involve deception; they are different ways of asking a question once trust and value are already established.
