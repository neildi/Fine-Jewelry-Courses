# C2 Module 2 — Knowledge Check

## Questions

1. What is the primary purpose of discovery before showing product?
   - A. To collect as much personal information as possible
   - B. To understand the client’s criteria and make a relevant edit
   - C. To establish the highest possible budget
   - D. To explain every feature in the case

2. Which is the most neutral occasion question?
   - A. “Is this an engagement ring?”
   - B. “Who is the lucky lady?”
   - C. “What would you like this piece to mark?”
   - D. “Why do you need jewelry today?”

3. A client says, “I don’t want anything flashy.” What is the best next step?
   - A. Assume they want the least expensive item
   - B. Ask what “not flashy” means to them—size, sparkle, profile, or something else
   - C. Immediately show only plain gold bands
   - D. Tell them all diamonds are flashy

4. Which question best reveals wear and care needs?
   - A. “Do you want a discount?”
   - B. “Will this be an everyday piece, or for particular occasions?”
   - C. “What is your income?”
   - D. “Do you know the Four Cs?”

5. When should an associate raise investment range?
   - A. Before greeting the client
   - B. Immediately after learning their name
   - C. After establishing the purchase direction, with permission or an alternative comparison
   - D. Never

6. Which statement handles budget ethically?
   - A. “What is the maximum you can spend?”
   - B. “To keep this relevant, may I ask what investment range feels comfortable?”
   - C. “This is the only range worth considering.”
   - D. “You should increase your budget before we continue.”

7. Why might lifestyle discovery matter for emerald or pearl jewelry?
   - A. It has no bearing on the recommendation
   - B. It helps the associate explain suitable care and choose an appropriate design
   - C. It means the associate should refuse to show the item
   - D. It replaces treatment disclosure

8. After hearing the client’s answers, what should the associate do before selecting pieces?
   - A. Summarize the brief and confirm it
   - B. Open every case
   - C. Ask the same questions again
   - D. Begin the checkout process

9. What is the purpose of a contrast piece in a two- or three-piece edit?
   - A. To confuse the client with more choices
   - B. To reveal preferences by comparing a deliberately different interpretation
   - C. To guarantee a higher sale
   - D. To show the most expensive piece first

10. Which clienteling note is appropriate?
   - A. “Client seemed unable to afford preferred ring.”
   - B. “Client may be hiding relationship problems.”
   - C. “Client requested email follow-up; prefers yellow gold, low-profile everyday styling; compare two pendant sizes.”
   - D. “Client’s appearance suggested a limited budget.”

## Answer key

1. **B** — Discovery identifies criteria so the associate can curate rather than guess.
2. **C** — It invites meaning without assuming a relationship or event.
3. **B** — The same phrase can refer to different visual preferences; clarify it.
4. **B** — Wear pattern informs setting, durability, and care discussion.
5. **C** — The question is most useful after a relevant direction exists and should be permission-based.
6. **B** — It explains why the question is useful and leaves the client agency.
7. **B** — Lifestyle can affect the design and care conversation; it does not replace required disclosure.
8. **A** — A recap lets the client correct misunderstandings before the edit begins.
9. **B** — A purposeful contrast helps the client articulate taste and priorities.
10. **C** — It records consent, useful preferences, and the next action without judgment or speculation.
