# C2 Module 3 — Production Outline

## Purpose
Teach associates to present a selected piece as a response to the client’s confirmed brief, while separating visible facts from claims that require product documentation.

## Learning outcomes

1. Use the six-part presentation loop.
2. Translate features into conditional, client-relevant benefits.
3. Use approved storytelling and craftsmanship language only.
4. Avoid unsupported durability, origin, investment, or resale claims.
5. Invite client participation through try-on and comparison.
6. Integrate disclosure and care information into the presentation.

## Article architecture

| Section | Job | Core content |
|---|---|---|
| Scenario | Identify a generic recital | Yellow-gold pendant example |
| Presentation loop | Create a repeatable method | Connect, show, translate, invite, check, disclose/next step |
| Translation table | Improve floor language | Feature versus client meaning |
| Story/craft/value | Control claims | Observable facts, approved records, no resale promises |
| Participation | Center client response | Try-on and comparison prompts |
| Disclosure | Make facts useful | Product records, treatment, care, material facts |
| Practice | Build fluency | 60-second presentation drill |

## Evidence boundaries

- FTC Jewelry Guides support truthful material representations, including treatment, durability, origin, and value.
- GIA setting guidance supports the need for precise, non-diagnostic setting language.
- The six-part loop and 60-second drill are course methods, not claimed industry performance data.

## Style controls

- No claim of guaranteed durability, appreciation, resale, origin, handwork, or treatment status without approved documentation.
- Use plain language and client choice rather than persuasive pressure.
