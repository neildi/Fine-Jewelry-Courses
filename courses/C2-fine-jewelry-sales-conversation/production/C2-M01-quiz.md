# Module 1 Self-Assessment Quiz: The First 90 Seconds

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: opening a client interaction without "Can I help you?" and converting the first 90 seconds into a real conversation.

**1. Why does "Can I help you?" underperform as an opener?**
A. It is grammatically incorrect
B. It is a closed-ended question that gives the client an easy one-word dismissal
C. Clients find it offensive
D. It is too long to say quickly
**Answer: B** - As a closed-ended question, it can be answered with a simple "no" or "just looking," ending the interaction before it starts.

**2. What documented sales uplift is associated with asking "Have you been here before?" instead of "Can I help you?"**
A. No measurable difference
B. Approximately 16% higher sales, per a study cited in JCK
C. 50% higher sales
D. It actually decreases sales
**Answer: B** - A study cited in JCK found this phrasing produced roughly 16% higher sales than "Can I help you?"

**3. Why are statement-based openers like "There must be an occasion coming up" recommended?**
A. They sound more formal
B. They cannot be answered with a simple "no," unlike a direct question
C. They are required by company policy everywhere
D. They work only with repeat customers
**Answer: B** - Because a statement isn't a yes/no question, it naturally invites elaboration rather than a one-word dismissal.

**4. What is the recommended response when a client says "I'm just looking"?**
A. "Okay, let me know if you need anything."
B. Validate fully, then ask an open question: "That's great, please look all you want. So what brought you in to look?"
C. Walk away immediately
D. Repeat "Can I help you?" more insistently
**Answer: B** - This response validates the client's stated position while still asking an open-ended question that requires more than a one-word answer.

**5. How long should you let a client decompress after entering before approaching?**
A. Approach immediately upon entry
B. Roughly 10-20 seconds
C. At least 10 minutes
D. There is no recommended waiting period
**Answer: B** - Giving a client roughly 10-20 seconds to adjust to the environment before approaching avoids the interaction feeling like immediate pressure.

**6. What phrase does this module identify as a second common mistake, alongside "Can I help you?"**
A. "Welcome to our store"
B. "How are you today?"
C. "What's your budget?"
D. "Thank you for coming in"
**Answer: B** - "How are you today?" is flagged as similarly scripted-sounding, typically eliciting a reflexive "fine" that doesn't advance the conversation.

**7. What is the jewelry-cleaning technique, and why is it effective?**
A. A way to inspect merchandise for theft
B. Offering to clean a client's worn jewelry as a genuine service, which naturally provides 10-15 minutes of low-pressure engagement time
C. A mandatory upsell technique
D. A way to avoid talking to difficult clients
**Answer: B** - Offering a genuine cleaning service extends the interaction naturally and should be delivered as a real courtesy, not a pretext for a sales pitch.

**8. Why did Shane Decker warn against using the same opening line for every client?**
A. It violates store policy
B. It becomes a habit and stops sounding genuine or attentive to the specific person
C. Clients prefer variety for its own sake
D. It is illegal in some states
**Answer: B** - A repeated, unvaried opener risks sounding like a scripted announcement rather than genuine engagement with the specific client in front of you.

**9. When should you introduce yourself by name to a client?**
A. Only if they ask
B. Early in the interaction, ideally within the first exchange
C. Only after a sale is close to closing
D. Never; it's considered too informal
**Answer: B** - Introducing yourself early personalizes the interaction and gives the client your name to reference later, supporting future clienteling.

**10. If a client does not respond to your opener and seems uninterested, what should you do?**
A. Repeat the same line more insistently
B. Give them space, remain visibly available, and try a different, situational opener later
C. Follow them closely around the store
D. Ignore them for the rest of their visit
**Answer: B** - Respecting the client's space while remaining available, then trying a fresh opener later, is more effective than pressuring an unreceptive client.
