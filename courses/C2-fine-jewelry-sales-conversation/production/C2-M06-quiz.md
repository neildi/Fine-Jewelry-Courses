# Module 6 Self-Assessment Quiz: "I'll Think About It" and the Follow-Up That Wins

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: responding to "I'll think about it" in the moment and running a structured follow-up sequence.

**1. What does "I'll think about it" usually represent?**
A. Always a firm no
B. A pause that could go either way, depending on what happens next
C. Always a genuine yes that just needs time
D. A request for a discount
**Answer: B** - Treating every instance identically is a mistake; the outcome often depends on the follow-up that happens afterward.

**2. What should you do before a client who says "I'll think about it" reaches the door?**
A. Say nothing further and let them leave
B. Ask one specific, non-pushy diagnostic question about what they're still weighing
C. Immediately offer a discount
D. Ask five separate questions to find the objection
**Answer: B** - One well-placed question is more effective than several, and it often surfaces the real objection.

**3. Why does offering to hold a piece matter more than simply handing over a business card?**
A. It doesn't matter; both are equally effective
B. It gives the client a reason to think about a specific piece, and gives you a legitimate reason to follow up
C. It obligates the client to buy
D. It's required by store policy everywhere
**Answer: B** - A hold converts a vague goodbye into a scheduled next contact.

**4. What is the first step in the follow-up ladder described in this module?**
A. A hard-close call after 30 days
B. A same-day thank-you that offers something new, not a repeated pitch
C. Ignoring the client until they reach out
D. An immediate discount offer
**Answer: B** - The same-day message keeps the visit fresh without repeating the sales pitch.

**5. What is the purpose of the 48-hour follow-up step?**
A. To repeat the exact same message as the same-day touch
B. To give a concrete, reason-driven touch, such as an update on the held piece or a new arrival
C. To pressure the client into a same-day decision
D. To apologize for the price
**Answer: B** - A reason-driven message works better than a generic "just checking in."

**6. What is the purpose of the 7-day follow-up step?**
A. A final, aggressive closing attempt
B. A direct but low-pressure invitation to make a decision with confidence
C. Offering a large discount to force a decision
D. Ending all further contact
**Answer: B** - This step respects that a real decision can take time while still keeping the door open.

**7. What makes a follow-up touch feel like a "gift" rather than a "chase"?**
A. Repeating the exact same pitch each time
B. Offering something of value, like a photo, an update, or genuine appreciation, rather than only asking if they've decided
C. Sending as many messages as possible
D. Only following up once and then giving up
**Answer: B** - Generous, specific touches build the relationship regardless of whether this particular sale closes.

**8. What is the "2-2-2" outreach rhythm mentioned in this module?**
A. Touchpoints roughly 2 days, 2 weeks, and 2 months apart
B. Exactly 2 phone calls per client
C. A 2% discount offered twice
D. A rule limiting follow-up to 2 attempts total
**Answer: A** - This spacing balances staying present with not overwhelming the client.

**9. Why does a handwritten or clearly personal thank-you note matter, even if the client hasn't decided yet?**
A. It has no measurable effect on retention
B. It builds a relationship that outlasts this specific sale and supports referrals and return visits regardless of outcome
C. It is only useful after a completed purchase
D. It replaces the need for any other follow-up
**Answer: B** - Being treated like a person, not a transaction, is remembered even if the client buys elsewhere this time.

**10. What should you do if a client doesn't respond after the full three-step follow-up ladder?**
A. Continue messaging daily indefinitely
B. Space further contact out further, maintaining a slower, respectful cadence
C. Give up on the client entirely and remove all records
D. Send an aggressive final offer demanding a decision
**Answer: B** - A patient, respectful cadence protects the relationship even if this specific sale doesn't close immediately.
