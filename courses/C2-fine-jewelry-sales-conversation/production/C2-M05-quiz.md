# Module 5 Self-Assessment Quiz: Price Objections Without Discounting

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: diagnosing a price objection and responding without defaulting to a discount.

**1. According to trade sources cited in this module, what does "that's too expensive" usually really mean?**
A. The customer has definitely decided not to buy
B. Something else entirely — insufficient perceived value, an unplanned budget, or a test of whether the price is firm
C. The customer wants a manager
D. The item is defective
**Answer: B** - Price objections are frequently a stand-in for a different, more specific concern.

**2. What are Shane Decker's two diagnostic questions for a price objection?**
A. "Do you want a discount?" and "How much can you spend?"
B. "Compared to what?" and "Is the price too high, or is it just more than you want to spend?"
C. "Would you like to speak to my manager?" and "Is there anything else?"
D. "Are you sure?" and "Why not?"
**Answer: B** - These two questions separate a value problem from a budget problem before you respond.

**3. What is the difference between a value objection and a budget objection?**
A. There is no meaningful difference
B. A value objection means the customer doesn't yet see enough value; a budget objection means they see the value but can't stretch that far right now
C. A value objection always requires a discount
D. A budget objection means the customer is lying
**Answer: B** - Each requires a different response: more value-building for one, option negotiation or payment flexibility for the other.

**4. Per Zig Ziglar's framing cited in this module, what is the difference between price and cost?**
A. They mean the same thing
B. Price is what you pay once; cost is what you live with afterward
C. Cost only applies to online purchases
D. Price includes tax, cost does not
**Answer: B** - A cheaper alternative often carries its own hidden costs, like future repairs or the urge to upgrade later.

**5. What is "option negotiation"?**
A. Offering a discount if the customer negotiates hard enough
B. Changing the item (carat, metal, or stone) to match the customer's budget, instead of changing the price
C. Giving the customer two prices to choose from
D. Asking the customer to negotiate with a manager
**Answer: B** - This protects the price integrity of the original piece while still working within the customer's stated budget.

**6. What is Eve Alfillé's recommended response to "Can you do better on this?"**
A. "Let me check with my manager."
B. "No, this is the price," delivered calmly and with a smile
C. Immediately offering 10% off
D. Ignoring the question and changing the subject
**Answer: B** - A firm, polite, final answer confirms the price was real the first time.

**7. Why does blaming "store policy" for not discounting hurt a salesperson, according to Kate Peterson?**
A. It's against company rules to mention policy
B. It demotes the salesperson from a professional to a clerk in the customer's eyes
C. Customers don't understand what policy means
D. It has no effect either way
**Answer: B** - A better response is "I can't do that, but here's what I can do," offering a real alternative.

**8. What should you do if a client cites a lower price from a competitor?**
A. Point out everything wrong with the competitor's store
B. Be glad they researched it, and talk only about what your store offers, not what the competitor lacks
C. Immediately match the competitor's price
D. Tell them the competitor is lying
**Answer: B** - Bashing a competitor reads as fear of losing the sale; a client citing a price still wants the piece.

**9. What discount ladder discipline is recommended if a concession is genuinely necessary?**
A. Discount as much as needed to close the sale immediately
B. Keep the first concession small (around 10% or less), make any second concession about half the first, and avoid discounting more than about 15% total
C. Always offer 50% off on the second ask
D. Never offer any concession under any circumstance
**Answer: B** - Treating discounts as rare, bounded exceptions protects margin and preserves trust in your prices.

**10. What should you do immediately after stating a price or a value-based reframe?**
A. Immediately offer a discount in case they're unhappy
B. Stop talking and let the client respond first
C. Repeat the price a second time for clarity
D. Change the subject to a different piece
**Answer: B** - David Geller's "power of shut up" principle: after quoting a price, whoever speaks next usually loses ground.
