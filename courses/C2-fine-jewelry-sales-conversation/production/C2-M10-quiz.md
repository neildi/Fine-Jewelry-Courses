# Module 10 Self-Assessment Quiz: Role-Play Scripts and a Weekly Practice Cadence

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: running structured, spaced role-play practice that makes Modules 1-9 stick.

**1. What does the forgetting curve describe?**
A. Sales skills never fade once learned
B. People forget a large share of new information within a short time unless it's deliberately revisited
C. Only new associates forget training; experienced staff never do
D. Training is only forgotten if delivered poorly
**Answer: B** - This is why reading a module once is not enough to build a durable skill.

**2. According to Bob Phibbs's guidance, where should role-play practice happen?**
A. In front of real customers, so it feels authentic
B. Off the floor or during a genuinely quiet moment, never in front of customers
C. Only during official annual training events
D. It doesn't matter where it happens
**Answer: B** - Practicing in front of customers can confuse them and make the exercise feel like a performance.

**3. What is "kill the leader," and how should it be handled during role-play?**
A. A required part of every role-play session
B. When a group starts arguing about exact wording instead of practicing; stop it and start a fresh round
C. A technique for choosing who leads the next session
D. A punishment for associates who make mistakes
**Answer: B** - Debating wording derails practice; simply restart the exercise instead.

**4. How should the person playing the "client" in a role-play behave?**
A. Try to deliberately trip up the associate as much as possible
B. Challenge realistically, similar to a real client, without sabotaging or reciting a fixed script
C. Simply agree with everything the associate says
D. Read directly from a script with no improvisation at all
**Answer: B** - This forces the associate to actually use the sales process, not memorize one fixed exchange.

**5. Why is running role-play only once, during onboarding, a problem?**
A. It isn't a problem; one session is always enough
B. Without ongoing repetition, the practiced skills fade just like any other unreinforced training
C. Role-play should never be repeated more than once
D. It only matters for experienced staff, not new hires
**Answer: B** - Role-play needs to be an ongoing habit to keep skills from decaying.

**6. What are the three parts of the short practice-huddle structure described in this module?**
A. Warm-up, core drill, reflection
B. Lecture, quiz, discount
C. Sales pitch, objection, close
D. Introduction, break, dismissal
**Answer: A** - A brief warm-up, a focused role-play drill, and specific feedback.

**7. What makes feedback after a role-play useful rather than vague?**
A. Saying "good job" or "needs work" without detail
B. Tying feedback to specific, observable behaviors, like whether a diagnostic question was asked
C. Only giving feedback once a year
D. Avoiding feedback entirely to keep morale high
**Answer: B** - Specific, behavior-based feedback gives the next attempt something concrete to improve.

**8. What does spaced repetition mean in the context of this module's weekly cadence?**
A. Practicing only the newest module, every single day
B. Revisiting skills at increasing intervals, including older material, rather than only practicing new content once
C. Cramming all nine modules into one long session
D. Avoiding repetition entirely to keep training fresh
**Answer: B** - Rotating older scenarios back in keeps earlier skills from fading while new ones are being built.

**9. Which scenario in this module's script bank practices recognizing a buying signal and diagnosing a price objection?**
A. The opener and the brush-off
B. Buying signal into price objection
C. The phone on the counter
D. The close
**Answer: B** - This scenario draws directly from Modules 4 and 5.

**10. What is the overall purpose of this capstone module?**
A. To introduce entirely new sales techniques not covered in Modules 1-9
B. To convert the content of Modules 1-9 into durable floor skill through structured, spaced practice
C. To replace the need for any of the previous nine modules
D. To test associates once and then conclude the course permanently
**Answer: B** - This module is about practice and retention, not new content.
