# Module 4 Self-Assessment Quiz: Buying Signals and When to Stop Talking

10 multiple-choice questions testing the terminal outcome: recognizing buying signals and shifting into a trial close or close instead of talking past the sale.

**1. What is a buying signal?**
A. Any complaint a customer makes about price
B. Any question or statement a client makes, once trust and value are established, that shows readiness to buy
C. A discount request
D. A request to speak with a manager
**Answer: B** - A buying signal reveals the client is mentally moving from "if I buy" to "when I buy."

**2. A client asks, "Can you size this down to a 6?" What should you do?**
A. Re-explain the diamond's clarity grade to reinforce value
B. Answer directly, then move toward closing (confirm sizing, ask if you should write it up)
C. Ask if she wants to see other rings first
D. Wait for her to ask for the price before responding
**Answer: B** - This is a clear buying signal; answering and moving to close protects the sale you already made.

**3. Why do many salespeople lose a sale they had already won?**
A. They quote the wrong price
B. They keep presenting and explaining features after the client already signaled readiness
C. They close too quickly
D. They ask too many discovery questions
**Answer: B** - Continuing to sell after a buying signal risks reopening a decision the client had already made.

**4. Which of these is a non-verbal (behavioral) buying signal?**
A. Crossed arms and toe-tapping
B. Leaning forward and relaxed posture while examining the piece
C. Checking a phone repeatedly
D. Standing near the exit
**Answer: B** - Leaning in and relaxed posture reflect engagement and comfort, both positive readiness signals.

**5. What does crossed arms or fidgeting during a presentation usually indicate?**
A. The client is ready to buy immediately
B. The client is still guarded or has an unresolved concern
C. The client wants a faster presentation
D. Nothing; body language should be ignored
**Answer: B** - Defensive or closed body language suggests you should return to discovery rather than push for a close.

**6. What is a trial close?**
A. A final, binding request for payment
B. A minor closing attempt used to reveal whether a client is ready to buy, without demanding a decision
C. A discount offered to hesitant clients
D. A follow-up call made after the client leaves
**Answer: B** - A trial close tests readiness at low risk; if the client isn't ready, the conversation simply continues.

**7. According to research cited by JCK, roughly how many times do many consumers say "no" before deciding to buy?**
A. Never; they decide on the first try
B. More than 60% say no multiple times, up to four, before buying
C. Exactly once
D. Only after receiving a discount
**Answer: B** - This means an early objection or hesitation is not a lost sale; it's expected and should be worked through.

**8. What should you do immediately after asking a closing or trial-close question?**
A. Immediately clarify your question with more detail
B. Stop talking and let the client respond
C. Offer a discount to speed up their decision
D. Ask a second question right away
**Answer: B** - The first person to speak after a closing question usually loses the moment; silence lets the client's real answer surface.

**9. A client says, "I never thought about it that way." What does this usually signal?**
A. Confusion that requires you to restart your presentation
B. A resolved objection or hesitation — a green light to move toward closing
C. Disinterest
D. A request for a different piece
**Answer: B** - This kind of comment typically means a concern just resolved in the client's mind, making it a good moment for a trial close.

**10. A client hesitates after your trial close. What is the best interpretation?**
A. The sale is lost; move on to another client
B. This is normal and gives you useful information about a specific, addressable concern
C. You should immediately offer a discount
D. You should stop selling to this client for the rest of the visit
**Answer: B** - Hesitation after a trial close is common and simply surfaces the objection you need to address next, rather than signaling a lost sale.
