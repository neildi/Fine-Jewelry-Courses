# C2 Module 3 — Knowledge Check

## Questions

1. What should start a product presentation?
- A. A full list of specifications
- B. The client’s confirmed preference or need
- C. A prediction of resale value
- D. The most expensive option

2. Which line best translates a feature into client meaning?
- A. “This is better quality.”
- B. “This bezel is guaranteed never to catch.”
- C. “Its smooth outline may suit the low-profile feel you described.”
- D. “Every client prefers this setting.”

3. Which statement needs approved documentation rather than visual inference?
- A. “This pendant has a textured surface.”
- B. “This chain has adjustable length.”
- C. “This gemstone is untreated.”
- D. “This setting frames the stone with metal.”

4. What is the purpose of the invite step?
- A. Keep speaking while the client decides
- B. Let the client experience and react to the piece
- C. Avoid showing a comparison
- D. Close the sale immediately

5. A client asks whether a ring will hold its value. What is the correct response?
- A. Promise appreciation
- B. Predict an insurance value
- C. Explain current documented attributes and avoid predicting future value
- D. Say all fine jewelry is an investment

6. Which is an observable feature?
- A. “Made by a master artisan” without documentation
- B. “A hinged bangle construction” when visibly confirmed
- C. “The rarest stone in the world”
- D. “The strongest setting available”

7. Why should relevant disclosure occur during the presentation rather than only at checkout?
- A. It helps the client make an informed comparison
- B. It creates urgency
- C. It eliminates the need for product records
- D. It guarantees the sale

8. What should you do if you do not know a treatment, origin, or service detail?
- A. Infer it from appearance
- B. Give the answer another client gave you
- C. Check the approved product record
- D. Change the subject

9. Which question best checks the client’s response?
- A. “You love it, right?”
- B. “What are you noticing: the scale, sparkle, color, or profile?”
- C. “Can I ring this up?”
- D. “Why are you hesitating?”

10. The course’s six-part loop ends with:
- A. A pressure close
- B. A promise about value
- C. Disclosure using approved information and an agreed next step
- D. A longer product speech

## Answer key

1. **B** — The presentation demonstrates that the product choice followed discovery.
2. **C** — It ties a visible feature to the client’s stated need without a guarantee.
3. **C** — Treatment must come from approved documentation, not appearance.
4. **B** — Client participation makes the presentation a conversation.
5. **C** — Explain what is documented, but do not predict market performance.
6. **B** — Visible or confirmed construction can be described accurately.
7. **A** — Material information helps the client evaluate fit, care, and purchase.
8. **C** — Accurate verification is better service than improvisation.
9. **B** — It invites specific feedback without pressure.
10. **C** — Disclosure and a purposeful next step complete the loop.
