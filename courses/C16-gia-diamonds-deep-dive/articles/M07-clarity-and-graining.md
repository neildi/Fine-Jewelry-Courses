## The rule — then the exception that is the real skill

The 11-grade scale (FL, IF, VVS1-2, VS1-2, SI1-2, I1-3) and the **10× rule** — "Flawless indicates that there are no inclusions or blemishes visible at 10x magnification; I3 is for diamonds with inclusions obvious to the naked eye" (GIA's own report-reading narration, video GIA-04, verified live) — are vocabulary every associate has. The research literature (A11's pair, King et al. 2006 being the operative one) exists because the vocabulary leaks at both ends:

1. **Grading is *relative to a size/position/relief framework*, not counting.** Number, size, relief, nature and position — GIA's five stated factors — plus, per King et al., for some features "analysis that goes beyond visibility at 10× magnification." That phrase is the thesis of the whole A11 assignment, so unpack it concretely below.
2. **Plotting is identification, not accusation.** The plot diagram exists so "this unique plot helps identify a particular stone" (GIA-04/-03 narration) — a registry, not a rap sheet. Your clarity conversations should inherit that framing: the plot is *why the report matches the stone*, and grade is *how visible it is*.

## Graining: the whitish and the mirror

King, Moses & Wang's 2006 study is GIA's definitive account of the two graining types that move grades and minds — "whitish" and "reflective" graining — in D-to-Z diamonds at the lab. What the abstract establishes (verbatim, safe to quote):

- Graining "often is the **only characteristic present in large, high-clarity, high-color diamonds**" — which is exactly why the top of the market is where graining literacy pays: a 5-carat stone graded VVS2 with *no visible inclusions* is often not a mystery but a graining story.
- Determining its impact "requires analysis beyond 10× visibility" — graining is judged by its *effect on transparency/luster*, and the article's stated method is the lab's documented methodology of critical assessment. [VERIFY before quoting specific cap rules (e.g., "graining alone can cap a stone at VS") — the study's case logic is in the PDF, summarized here at abstract level: findings on how whitish/reflective graining lowered clarity grades in study samples are the reason trade folklore says 'VS cap'; read the PDF before repeating.]

Mechanism (textbook, consistent with the M02 formation module): graining is **misoriented crystal structure along growth boundaries** — slip planes and interrupted growth planes from the stresses of mantle growth, i.e., *the scar of the story, not a foreign body*. Reflective graining reads as thin mirror lines (light bouncing along the plane); whitish as faint cloudy lines/patches. The formation link is why graining correlates with type: it lives most in Ia goods; and it's why some of the most expensive stones on earth (remember Cullinan I: "approximately D, potentially flawless" **with faint internal graining** — Scarratt & Shor) carry it.

For a valuation context (your IIA/GAVA audience): graining is also a **cut-plan tell** — it forces the cutter to angle the table against polishing direction; that interplay between graining plane and final proportions sometimes leaves the faint surface relief finish graders see (M06's polish/symmetry section).

## Clarity at the lab: the verified workflow (no invented drama)

From GIA's own narrated tour (videos GIA-03/-04, both verified today), the sequence is: stone enters in cassette → natural/synthetic screen first → (for rounds) cut, polish, symmetry inputs → **clarity at 10× loupe/microscope; inclusions noted; plot marked; treatment evidence checked — "any treatment will be noted on the report"** → and, per the transcript: *"an asterisk next to the color or clarity grade indicates the diamond has been treated."* Two implications for your floor:

## The SI/VS conversation, rebuilt from the literature's logic

The A11 pair + video narrative support a cleaner professional script than the usual "eye clean" pitch. The lab's factors, translated to counter language:

| Grade band | What is actually being reported | The honest sentence |
|---|---|---|
| FL/IF | Nothing at 10× (IF: no inclusions, surface blemishes allowed) | "The 10x test found nothing to plot." |
| VVS | "Extremely difficult for graders" | "There is something; finding it is the skill." |
| VS | "Difficult to somewhat easy" | "There for an experienced grader, invisible to the eye." |
| SI | "Somewhat easy to easy" | "A grader sees it instantly — our job is whether *you* do, face-up." |
| I | Visible to the naked eye; transparency/durability implications considered | "It is part of the stone's character; grade reflects that." |

The literature's addendum: "eye clean SI1" is a **grade-plus-a-claim** — the claim is real and checkable (look face-up, your eyes, no loupe), which is exactly the demonstration discipline GIA's method was built on. When SI1 is *not* eye clean, you don't argue; you reframe ("this is a grader-visible pin at the girdle, under a prong — here's the plot, here's what the grade says"). The plot diagram makes that a five-second, physical conversation instead of a ten-minute trust negotiation.

## What the research does *not* say

- Clarity ≥ beauty: the modeling module (M06) proved appearance lives in light behavior; 2016 research says CLIPPIR's *lack* of inclusions contributes to why big pure stones grade high — but top-clarity is a scarcity story, not a sparkle story.
- "More inclusions = weaker": only *durability-relevant* features (feathers to surface, large fractures at girdle) are structural — and the lab's durability component of cut, plus setting practice, already flags them. [VERIFY before any durability claim about a specific stone — that's jeweler judgment, not report data.]
- "Laser drill holes = damage": they're a *history* (fracture-filled/drilled stones, Module 12) that reports note; the grader's job is disclosure, the jeweler's is maintenance.
