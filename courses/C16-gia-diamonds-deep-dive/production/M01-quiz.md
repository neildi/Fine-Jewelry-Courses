# M01 Quiz — Before the Report (10 questions)

**1.** Who standardized the four diamond-value terms into the "4Cs," and in what capacity?
a) Ernest Oppenheimer, as De Beers chairman · b) **Robert M. Shipley, founding GIA to professionalize jewelry retail** · c) Lancelot Hardcastle, as Trade magazine editor · d) F. John Henry, as Gem-A president

**2.** Before standardization, the clarity factor was typically described as:
a) "loupe clean" or "oily" · b) "good water" · c) **"without flaws" / "with imperfections"** · d) "first / second / third water"
*(GIA's source lists the merchant phrasing; "water" is period terminology but not the cited usage — answer per Gilbertson.)*

**3.** Which of the four Cs was the only one with a consistent, internationally meaningful term since the 1500s?
**Carat weight.** It survived because a weight can be measured objectively; color, clarity and cut terms were opinion words until methods standardized them.

**4.** True or false: De Beers coined the term "4Cs."
**False.** Shipley/GIA coined and taught it (in advertising and course use from the early 1940s; earliest surviving GIA course mention 1949). De Beers adopted the term in national magazine advertising in **August 1962** — swapping "purity" for "clarity" to fit the mnemonic.

**5.** The Vallerano ring matters to a diamond professional because:
a) it proves diamonds were cut in Roman times · b) **it shows value attached to an uncut natural crystal — the brilliant-factory paradigm is recent** · c) it is the oldest known synthetic · d) its diamond grades I1
*(Bonus: what did FTIR find? — type Ia with aggregated nitrogen (B centers), A centers inferred.)*

**6.** Janse's 98% figure refers to:
a) African share of jewelry sales in the 1990s · b) **southern Africa's share of world diamond production, 1889–1959** · c) De Beers' share of rough sorting · d) kimberlite share of all pipes

**7.** Order correctly (oldest → newest):
**Eureka (21.25 ct, 1867) → Star of South Africa (83.5 ct, 1869) → Bultfontein dry diggings (1870) → kimberlite pipes recognized as diamond hosts (1872) → Cullinan (3,106 ct, 1905).**

**8.** What did the Transvaal legislature do in July 1903, and what lesson does a mine-level analyst draw from it alongside the £52,000 Elandsfontein purchase price?
**60% tax on Premier mine profits.** Lesson: the same deposit produced vastly different private returns depending on fiscal and ownership terms — mine "value" is geology × policy. (Cullinan case study, Scarratt & Shor 2006.)

**9.** The 1990s examination of Cullinans I and II recorded them as:
a) D/IF with minor graining affecting grade · b) **approximately D color, potentially flawless** · c) E/VVS1 · d) Fancy pale blue
*(Note "potentially": historic cuts prevented definitive IF/FL certification — good discussion item.)*

**10.** The Premier/Cullinan mine is unusual because it yielded:
a) most industrial diamond · b) **>25% of all diamonds over 400 ct ever discovered** · c) the first Canadian gem diamond · d) the largest alluvial parcel on record
