# M01 Huddle Card — Where the 4Cs Came From (one page)

**The one-liner:** The grading language you use is a 1940s teaching invention, not an ancient standard — and Africa's 1867 supply shock, not a slogan, created the market the language describes.

**Three facts to have ready:**
- Shipley (GIA, 1931) standardized Color/Clarity/Cutting/Carat as a mnemonic; first recorded in GIA course material 1949. De Beers adopted "4Cs" nationally only in Aug 1962 (and took the word "clarity" from GIA's system, dropping "purity").
- 1867: a kid's river pebble near Hopetown → Atherstone IDs 21.25 ct → 1870 dry diggings → 1872 kimberlite. Southern Africa supplied >98% of world diamonds 1889–1959.
- 1905: Premier mine finds the 3,106 ct Cullinan; Cullinan I (530.4 ct) + II (317.4 ct) sat in the Crown Jewels; a 1990s GIA-lab era examination found ~D color, potentially flawless. Same pipe produced >25% of all 400+ ct diamonds ever found.

**One myth to retire on the floor:** "De Beers invented the 4Cs / the grading system." No: the educators did; the advertisers industrialized them second.

**One script:** Client asks why color starts at D → "Before GIA, every shop had its own words. Starting at D was a deliberate fresh line — no old 'A' or 'first quality' could be confused with it. The scale is a reset, and the reset is the point." [Confirm the 'deliberate D' framing against Module 9 sources before using as absolute; safe as commonly-GIA-attributed.]
