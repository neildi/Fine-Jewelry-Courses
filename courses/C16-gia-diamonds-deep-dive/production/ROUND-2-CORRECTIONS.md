## Part 1: The M08/M09 fluorescence correction (ready to splice in)

**Replace the existing Moses 1997 paragraph in M08 ("Why Diamonds Have Color") with:**

> Fluorescence, the commercial asterisk: the N3 center, the A-center pair's optical cousin, causes the familiar blue fluorescence. The handout's Moses 1997 study, "A Contribution to Understanding the Effect of Blue Fluorescence on the Appearance of Diamonds" (Moses, Reinitz, King, et al., *Gems & Gemology*, Winter 1997), is the empirical answer to the market myth, and its actual finding is more specific, and more favorable to fluorescent stones, than trade shorthand usually credits it for. Four sets of very similar round brilliants, spanning colorless to faint yellow, were shown to trained graders, trade professionals, and average observers under controlled viewing. For average observers, the group that matters most at retail, no systematic negative effect of fluorescence on appearance was detected. Strongly blue fluorescent diamonds were perceived to have *better* color appearance when viewed table-up, with the benefit most pronounced at lower color grades (the I and K color sets showed a stronger effect than the E and G sets). Most observers saw no relationship between fluorescence and transparency in this study's sample, meaning no general support for a "hazy" penalty. Cite the paper directly rather than the trade's flattened "fluorescence doesn't hurt" summary; the real finding is that it can measurably help, at the exact color range where clients are most price-sensitive.

**Remove the trailing "VERIFY any numeric claim..." flag** on this passage; the numeric claims (I/K vs. E/G differential benefit) are now sourced to the primary paper directly.

**Replace the Moses 1997 cross-reference line in M09 ("Grading Color") with:**

> Conditions are part of the grade. Face-up, through the pavilion, daylight-equivalent source, neutral surround: change the environment and you change what "absence of color" means. That is why a stone can look whiter in daylight than its report suggests and the report is not wrong: GIA's own Winter 1997 research (Moses, Reinitz, King, et al.) found that strongly blue-fluorescent stones are perceived as having better color face-up, with the largest benefit at lower color grades (I/K sets more than E/G sets), for average observers specifically. Fluorescence is not cosmetic trivia in a color conversation; it is a documented, favorable interaction at exactly the price point where clients scrutinize color hardest.

**Update the M09 self-check answer key** (question referencing M08's Moses 1997 link) to: "Blue fluorescence's documented effect is a perceived color *improvement* face-up, strongest at lower color grades (I/K), for average observers; it is not a neutral 'no measurable harm' finding, it is a positive one."

## Part 2: Corrected factual error found during resolution (not merely unverified, but wrong as stated)

**Module 4 ("The Market") and the related M09 huddle card both state:** "De Beers HQ to Gaborone 2001 [VERIFY date]"

**This date is incorrect as stated.** Cross-checked against three independent sources (Council on Foreign Relations, De Beers Group's own newsroom, National Jeweler trade press), the actual timeline is:
- **2011:** De Beers and the Government of Botswana signed the 10-year sales agreement underpinning the move.
- **November 2013:** De Beers physically began diamond sales operations in Gaborone, completing the transfer of its rough-diamond sales operation (Global Sightholder Sales) from London to Botswana.

**Corrected text to splice in, wherever "2001" appears in this context:** "De Beers signed a 10-year sales agreement with the Government of Botswana in 2011, and completed the physical move of its rough-diamond sales operation from London to Gaborone in November 2013, the centerpiece of Botswana's beneficiation policy."

This is a genuine correction, not just a verification, since the original date was wrong by roughly 12 years. It is the single most important fix in this round.
