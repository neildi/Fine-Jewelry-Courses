# Annotated Bibliography — GIA "Diamonds & Diamond Grading" Further Reading

This is the complete reading list from GIA's official *Further Reading for Diamonds and Diamond Grading* handout (the 8-page PDF distributed with the GIA D&DG course), re-set here with annotations. It is the spine of this course: every module cites from it, and every learner can follow the original sources — all of which GIA makes available free on gia.edu.

**How to use this file**
- **★ = digest-grade source.** Full text (or a substantial extract) was fetched during course production and is used in the corresponding module. Its annotation states what the article actually says.
- ⚠ = article content recovered from an indirect copy (cache/library) because GIA's current URLs for it are broken; see notes at the end.
- Unmarked entries: annotation is based on the citation's title, journal volume/issue, and assignment context. The article remains free to download from GIA; the annotation tells you what it covers and which module pairs with it. Verify before quoting specifics.
- Legacy PDF links of the form `gia.edu/doc/<season>_<year>.pdf` (old volume-wide PDFs cited in the GIA handout) **frequently 404 today** — this was tested during production. For those articles, use GIA's library search (gia.edu/library) or the G&G archive; annotations for them are necessarily title-level.
- The handout itself notes: *"This reading is optional. You will not be tested on the content of these articles."* This course changes that — it treats the list as the core curriculum.

G&G = *Gems & Gemology*, GIA's quarterly professional journal. All G&G articles are free to download at gia.edu.

## Assignment 1: Beyond the Essentials → Course Module 1

- ★ **Gilbertson A. (2016) Diamond Quality: A Short History of the 4Cs.** GIA Research & News — https://www.gia.edu/gia-news-research/diamond-quality-short-history-4Cs — The foundational provenance piece: before 1940 there was no standard vocabulary; "tincture/tint," "without flaws," "made well" were used inconsistently since the 1500s (only "carat" was stable). Robert M. Shipley, who founded GIA in 1931, standardized the four factors and named them the 4Cs as a teaching mnemonic; GIA/AGS advertising spread it from 1940–1941; De Beers did not adopt "4Cs" (or "clarity" in place of "purity") in its national ads until August 1962.

## Assignment 2: Birth of the Modern Diamond Industry → Course Module 1

- ⚠ **Bedini A. et al. (2012) The Vallerano Diamond from Ancient Rome: A Scientific Study.** G&G 48:1 — cited in the GIA handout as gia.edu/gems-gemology/spring-2012-vallerano-diamond-**bedino** (typo) — both that slug and the corrected one now 404, and the article PDF path (gia.edu/doc/SP12A4.pdf) redirects to a deleted archive location. A 1993 excavation of a Roman necropolis at Vallerano (Rome) produced a gold ring set with a rough octahedral diamond, the only ancient diamond ring with known chronological/social context (a young woman of probable Syrian origin, c. 1st c. AD); portable FTIR classified the stone as type Ia with nitrogen aggregates. Evidence that rough diamonds were valued, not cut, in antiquity. *Text recovered 2026-09-09 via search-engine caches of the GIA PDF; retrieve the original from GIA's library (gia.edu/library) before quoting page numbers.*
- ★ **Scarratt K., Shor R. (2006) The Cullinan Diamond Centennial: A History and Gemological Analysis of Cullinans I and II.** G&G 42:2 — https://www.gia.edu/gems-gemology/summer-2006-cullinan-diamond-scarratt (PDF: gia.edu/dam/migrated-assets/docs/doc1/SU06A2.pdf) — The single best history of the Premier/Cullinan mine: the 3,106 ct find (26 Jan 1905), the land purchase from the Prinsloo heirs, the 1908 presentation to Edward VII, and the first modern examination of Cullinan I (530 ct) and II (317 ct) — "approximately D color, potentially flawless." Also documents why this mine has yielded >25% of all 400+ ct diamonds ever found.
- ★ **Janse A.J.A. (1995) A History of Diamond Sources in Africa: Part I.** G&G 31:4 — https://www.gia.edu/gems-gemology/winter-1995-diamond-history-africa-janse (full PDF on same page) — Country-by-country history of African discoveries from original sources. Key number: Africa held >98% of world output 1867–1959; Kimberley's "dry diggings" (1870) created the kimberlite pipe model; the author's method includes what he could *not* substantiate — a model of source discipline worth teaching.
- **Janse A.J.A. (1996) A History of Diamond Sources in Africa: Part II.** G&G 32:1 — https://www.gia.edu/gems-gemology/spring-1996-diamond-history-africa-janse — Completes Part I: eastern/west Africa, plus the evolution of prospecting, mining and recovery methods.
- **Boyajian W.E. (1988) An Economic Review of the Past Decade in Diamonds.** G&G 24:3 — https://www.gia.edu/gems-gemology/fall-1988-economic-review-diamonds-boyajian — Late-1980s market cycle analysis; Boyajian became *the* G&G economics columnist.
- **Shipley R.M. (1949) Diamond Syndicates and Their Successors.** G&G 6:7 — legacy link gia.edu/doc/fall_1949.pdf (404 at test time; use GIA library) — Shipley's own account of how consolidated control of supply (from the 1888 De Beers merger onward) shaped prices and distribution. Primary source for the pre-De Beers syndicate era.
- **Swindler K. (1949) The Diamond as an Engagement Ring.** G&G 6:7 — same legacy issue — Traces how the diamond engagement ring became the industry's anchor product; pairs with the 1940s GIA/De Beers advertising history in Module 1.
- **Gemological Digests (1949) Jagersfontein, Dutoitspan, Premier, Oppenheimer, Congo, Diamond Clubs.** G&G 6:7 — Trade briefs from the same 1949 issue: the other Kimberley-area pipes (Jagersfontein/Dutoitspan), early Oppenheimer-era commerce, Congo concessions, and the retail "diamond clubs" that popularized the 4Cs language.
- ★ **Shigley J.E. Historical Reading: The Diamond Fields of South Africa: Part 1 (1868–1893).** GIA Research & News — https://www.gia.edu/gia-news-research/historical-reading-diamond-fields-south-africa-1868-1893 — Annotated 19th-century bibliography (Royal Geographical Society, Journal of the Arts letters) with Shigley's own summaries: the 1867 Jacobs/Star-of-South-Africa finds, "blink klippe," garnet as an alluvial indicator, and the contemporary scientific skepticism (Gregory) vs. confirmation (Chalmers, Muskett).
- **Shigley J.E. Historical Reading: The Diamond Fields of South Africa: Part 2 (1893–2014).** — https://www.gia.edu/gia-news-research/historical-reading-diamond-fields-south-africa-1893-2014 — The modern half: kimberlite pipe mining, the Consolidated Mines era, into the 21st century.
