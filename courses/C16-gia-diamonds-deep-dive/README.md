# C16 The GIA Diamonds Deep Dive

**Diamonds & Diamond Grading, Mastered Through GIA's Own Research**

**Track:** Advanced / Specialist
**Audience:** GIA students and graduates, senior sales associates, gemologists, appraisers, and diamond buyers who want the research-grade depth behind the grading vocabulary
**Study load:** ~40–60 hours (13 long-form modules + the annotated bibliography + video track)

> **Independence and trademark disclaimer.** This is an independent study companion, not an official GIA product and not endorsed by or affiliated with the Gemological Institute of America. It is built entirely from GIA's own published materials — principally the ~250 *Gems & Gemology* and GIA Research & News articles listed in GIA's official "For Further Reading" handout for the *Diamonds & Diamond Grading* course, fetched and digested from gia.edu. GIA and Gems & Gemology are the property of their respective owners; all articles are cited and linked, never copied or re-hosted.

## Why this course exists

GIA's Diamonds & Diamond Grading course ships with a bibliography, not a textbook: a per-assignment list of *Gems & Gemology* articles ("optional; you will not be tested on it") that represents 85+ years of gemological research, from Shipley's 1941 color-grading paper to 2017 studies of treated red HPHT synthetics. Most students never read it. This course reads it for you — it fetches the flagship articles of each assignment, digests them, and teaches diamond grading the way the research itself does it: mechanism first, vocabulary second.

## Terminal outcomes

The learner can:
- Narrate the origin of the 4Cs and the grading vocabulary, and why standardization changed diamond commerce
- Explain diamond formation, exploration, and mining at the level of a GIA laboratory's own published science
- Trace how each grade on a GIA report (cut, clarity, color, carat) was researched into existence — proportion ranges, graining, masterstones, the geometry of brilliance
- Identify and explain simulants, lab-grown diamonds, and treatments using the detection features the G&G literature established (graining, fluorescence patterns, spectroscopy, conductivity, magnetism)
- Read any future *Gems & Gemology* article with the vocabulary to understand it, using the course bibliography as the entry point
