# C4-M08 Quiz: Internal Controls and Building a Security Culture

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes recognizing the shared underlying principle across the course and applying it at the organizational level.

---

**1. What is the single underlying principle this module identifies as running through Modules 1, 2, 3, 5, and 7?**
A) Every module has a completely unrelated, standalone rule
B) No single person, key, control, or moment should represent a single point of failure for the store's security
C) Only management needs to worry about security
D) Cost reduction is the primary goal of every procedure

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: From key segregation (Module 1) to never leaving a customer alone with merchandise (Module 3) to separating who receives stock from who counts it (Module 7), every specific rule in this course is an application of the same "no single point of failure" principle.

---

**2. According to trade guidance cited in this module, who should have sole authority for any single function in a store?**
A) The most senior sales associate
B) No one except the owner
C) Every employee equally, with no restrictions
D) Whoever has worked there longest

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The sourced principle states that no one except the owner should have sole authority for any function in a store — this is the organizational-level expression of segregation of duties.

---

**3. Why does this module treat the JSA weekly crime bulletin habit as a "structural habit," not just a nice-to-have?**
A) It has no real value beyond checking a compliance box
B) It keeps staff current on evolving crime patterns AND the signed training log serves as documented evidence of an active security culture
C) It replaces the need for any other security training
D) It is only relevant to store managers, not associates

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The bulletin habit serves two purposes: keeping staff genuinely current as crime tactics evolve, and creating a documented record (the signed training log) that the store maintains an ongoing security-awareness practice.

---

**4. What distinguishes a Suspicious Incidents Book that functions as a real control from one that merely exists?**
A) The book's physical binding or format
B) Whether entries are specific, reviewed periodically by a manager, and actually used — not just logged and forgotten
C) How many entries it contains, regardless of quality
D) Whether it's kept in digital or paper form

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A Suspicious Incidents Book only functions as a genuine control if entries are specific and someone actually reviews them periodically, rather than existing as an unreviewed record referenced only reactively.

---

**5. Per this module's capstone synthesis, which modules primarily protect "the accuracy of your own records" so that loss investigations are based on real discrepancies?**
A) Modules 1-2
B) Module 3
C) Module 7
D) Module 6

**Correct answer: C**
Explanation: Module 7 (Perpetual Inventory, Cycle Counts, and Shrink Control) is specifically about record accuracy and the discrepancy protocol, distinguishing it from the physical-perimeter focus of Modules 1-2 or the transaction focus of Modules 5-6.

---

**6. If a store's shrinkage rate rises but the Suspicious Incidents Book shows no corresponding increase in documented, investigated discrepancies, what does this module suggest that pattern might indicate?**
A) It definitely means theft has increased with no other explanation
B) It may suggest the discrepancy protocol from Module 7 isn't actually being followed, not necessarily that theft itself has increased
C) It has no meaningful interpretation
D) It always means the KPI metric itself is being calculated incorrectly

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The module explicitly cautions against assuming a metric change directly proves theft — a shrinkage increase without corresponding documented investigation may instead indicate the discrepancy protocol isn't being consistently applied.

---

**7. What does this module recommend as the correct way to act on a full course gap-mapping exercise, rather than trying to fix every identified gap at once?**
A) Ignore all gaps since perfection isn't achievable
B) Bring the single most significant gap to a manager, framed around which control is missing and why it matters
C) Fix every gap independently without discussing any of them with management
D) Wait until the next annual review to mention any gaps

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The practice exercise specifically recommends prioritizing the most significant identified gap and raising it with a manager in a specific, actionable way, rather than attempting to address everything simultaneously.

---

**8. According to this module's synthesis, what distinguishes "strong individual procedures" from a "genuine security culture"?**
A) There is no real difference between the two
B) A genuine security culture requires procedures that reinforce each other through organizational structure (segregation of duties, ongoing training, periodic review), not just isolated rules followed individually
C) Genuine security culture only requires following Module 1's procedures perfectly
D) Security culture is entirely unrelated to the procedures taught in this course

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The module explicitly distinguishes a store that follows individual procedures well but lacks organizational reinforcement (segregation of duties, training habits, review) from one with a genuine, mutually-reinforcing security culture — the latter is this course's actual goal.
