# C4-M02 Quiz: Safes, Vaults, and Nightly Put-Away Discipline

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes understanding why safe specification, in-safe warranties, and special-handling categories matter, not memorizing exact figures.

---

**1. Why does JSA consider a TRTL-30x6 rated safe stronger protection than a TL-15 or TL-30 rated safe?**
A) It is simply more expensive
B) It is tested to resist both tools and torch attacks on all six sides for a longer duration, giving burglars less working time
C) It has a different color finish that deters burglars
D) There is no meaningful difference between the ratings

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: TR ratings add torch resistance to tool resistance, and the "x6" indicates all-side testing; a higher-rated, longer-duration-tested safe gives a burglar crew meaningfully less working time to breach it.

---

**2. Where should a safe NOT be placed, according to this module?**
A) In the center of the store floor
B) Against an outside wall or a wall shared with a neighboring business
C) Near the point-of-sale counter
D) On a raised platform

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Safes placed against outside or shared walls give a burglary crew concealment and extra time to work through the wall itself rather than the safe door.

---

**3. What is the nature of an insurance policy's "in-safe warranty"?**
A) A voluntary best practice with no real consequences if ignored
B) A contractual condition of coverage — non-compliance can affect claim outcomes
C) A marketing term with no legal weight
D) A one-time requirement that only applies during the first year of a policy

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The in-safe warranty is a contractual requirement specifying how much merchandise must be secured overnight; if a loss occurs while the store isn't compliant, the insurer can have grounds to deny or reduce the claim.

---

**4. Why should inventory documentation (invoices, cost records, itemized inventory) be stored off-premises, not just in the store's safe?**
A) Off-premises storage is required by law in every jurisdiction
B) If a fire, flood, or burglary damages the store, on-site-only documentation could be destroyed at the exact moment it's needed to prove ownership and value
C) It has no real benefit and is simply traditional practice
D) Off-premises storage is cheaper than on-site storage

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Storing documentation off-premises protects your ability to prove what you owned and what it cost even if the store itself is damaged or destroyed.

---

**5. What distinguishes a "memo" item from a "consignment" item in vault inventory?**
A) There is no real difference between the two terms
B) Memo items are owned by a vendor; consignment items are owned by a customer
C) Memo items are always more valuable than consignment items
D) Consignment items never need to be tracked separately from store stock

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Memo goods are owned by a vendor (the store owes the vendor the item or payment), while consignment goods are owned by a customer (the store owes the customer proceeds or the item back) — different ownership means different liability.

---

**6. Why is a "non-invoiced" item sitting in the vault considered a red flag?**
A) It's not actually a concern as long as the item is locked up
B) It has no matching purchase invoice, which needs to be resolved rather than left ambiguous
C) Non-invoiced items are always evidence of theft
D) It means the item is definitely counterfeit

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: An item without a matching purchase invoice is a documentation gap that needs to be investigated and resolved, not simply treated as a normal inventory category.

---

**7. What perpetual-inventory element allows a store to later investigate a specific missing item (e.g., checking if it's in a repair box or was sold under a different item number)?**
A) A rough visual estimate of stock levels
B) A unique inventory number assigned to each piece
C) An annual-only count with no interim tracking
D) Verbal knowledge held by senior staff

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Unique inventory numbers are what make later discrepancy investigation possible — without them, there's no reliable way to trace a specific item's movement history.

---

**8. According to JSA, why should a store update its inventory list with its insurance company regularly, beyond just the annual export?**
A) Insurance companies require weekly updates for all data points
B) To reflect the current price of gold, since a stale valuation could understate what's needed to be made whole after a loss
C) It has no real financial impact
D) Only to comply with tax filing deadlines

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: JSA specifically notes that jewelers should update their inventory list with their insurance company to reflect the current price of gold, since gold price movement can significantly affect true replacement value.
