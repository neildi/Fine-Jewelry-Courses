# C4-M04 Outline: Recognizing Distraction Theft and Robbery Patterns

## Module Metadata
- Course: C4 - Store Security & Loss Prevention
- Module: 4 of 8
- Persona: P1
- Working title: Recognizing Distraction Theft and Robbery Patterns
- Learning objective: Associates can recognize casing behavior, distraction-theft team tactics, and grab-and-run patterns before or as they happen, apply "if in doubt, keep them out" identity verification during business hours, and know the general safety-first principle for actual incident response.
- Terminal outcome tie-in: "Recognize the transaction and behavioral red flags associated with distraction theft, grab-and-run, and armed robbery patterns."

## Section Map

1. **Why This Module Matters** — Shift from fixed procedure (Modules 1-3) to pattern recognition; recap JSA timing data.
2. **The "Meet and Greet" as a Deterrent — and a Diagnostic** — Greeting as dual-purpose service/security tactic; behavioral signals worth noticing (not proving).
3. **Distraction Theft: Built on Team Coordination** — Team mechanic explanation; links back to Module 3's cash-flash red flag and full selling protocol as the defense.
4. **Grab-and-Run: Speed Is the Entire Strategy** — Contrasting mechanic (speed vs. stealth); timing/foot-traffic correlation; showcase-discipline defense.
5. **Casing Behavior: What to Watch For Before an Incident** — Repeat low-interest visits, staff/camera/closing-pattern interest, parked-vehicle indicator; Suspicious Incidents Book tie-back to Module 1.
6. **Identity Verification: A Habit That Extends Beyond Opening and Closing** — JCK's "if in doubt, keep them out" applied during business hours, not just at the door before/after.
7. **What to Do During and Immediately After an Incident** — General safety-first principle (comply, don't resist, preserve scene); explicit deferral to store's own written incident plan for specifics.
8. **Practice Exercise** — Naming a past "off-feeling" interaction in concrete, reportable terms.
9. **Why This Module Comes Before the Fraud and Compliance Modules** — Explicit bridge explaining the pattern-recognition skill transfers to Modules 5-6.
10. **Key Takeaways** — Seven-point recap.

## Ties to Other Modules
- Recaps and extends Module 1's JSA timing data and Suspicious Incidents Book habit.
- Directly builds on Module 3's cash-flash distraction-theft cue and full selling protocol, framing them as the primary defense against both distraction theft and grab-and-run.
- Explicitly bridges to Modules 5-6 (fraud and Form 8300), noting the shared underlying skill of noticing "engineered urgency" or "attention-dividing" patterns.
