# C4-M04 Huddle Card: Recognizing Distraction Theft and Robbery Patterns

## 5-Minute Pre-Shift Discussion

**The one idea to remember:** When something feels rushed, distracting, or unusually charming, that's the cue to slow down and tighten protocol — not relax it. That's exactly the moment a distraction crew is counting on.

## Two Different Threats, Two Different Tells
- **Distraction theft:** Team-based. One person occupies you (cash flash, urgent question, dropped item) while a partner works. Defense = Module 3's full protocol, especially "never leave a customer alone with merchandise."
- **Grab-and-run:** Speed-based. Relies on an open case or unattended item and a fast exit. Defense = lock the case immediately, one item at a time, watch peak hours (1-6 p.m. per JSA data).

## Casing Signs Worth Logging (not accusing)
- Repeat visits, little buying interest each time
- More interest in staff/cameras/locks than merchandise
- Odd questions about closing time, staffing, or when the manager isn't around
- A parked, occupied vehicle lingering near the entrance

Log it in the Suspicious Incidents Book. One instance isn't proof — a pattern is worth a manager's attention.

## Identity Checks Aren't Just for Opening/Closing
"If in doubt, keep them out" applies all day — to a surprise "vendor rep" or "inspector" asking for back-room access too.

## If Something Actually Happens
Safety first. Comply. Don't resist. Don't tidy the scene. Call police once safe. Know your store's specific written plan.
