# C4-M08 Huddle Card: Internal Controls and Building a Security Culture

## 5-Minute Pre-Shift Discussion

**The one idea to remember:** Every rule in this entire course is the same principle applied differently: no single person, key, or moment should ever represent your whole security posture.

## The Pattern, Across the Course
- Key segregation (M1) → no one person opens both the building and the safe
- Special-handling categories (M2) → no one count conflates different ownership
- Never leave a customer alone with merchandise (M3) → no unobserved moment for a switch
- Layered card verification (M5) → no single control is the "whole" defense
- Separate receiving from counting (M7) → no one person controls both the goods and the record

## Two Habits That Make It a Culture, Not Just Rules
1. **JSA weekly crime bulletin + signed training log** — keeps everyone current, and proves an active security culture exists.
2. **Suspicious Incidents Book, actually reviewed** — a log nobody reads isn't a control, it's just paperwork.

## The Real Test
A rising shrinkage number with an empty, unreviewed incident log doesn't necessarily mean more theft — it might mean the discrepancy protocol (Module 7) isn't actually being followed.
