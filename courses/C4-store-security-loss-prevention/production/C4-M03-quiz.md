# C4-M03 Quiz: Showcase and Selling-Floor Security

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correct sequencing of the selling protocol and correctly interpreting red-flag behaviors, not memorizing exact wording.

---

**1. What should you do immediately after removing an item from a showcase to show a customer?**
A) Leave the case unlocked since you'll be right there
B) Lock the case immediately
C) Remove several similar items at the same time to save trips
D) Set your keys down on the counter for easy access

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Showcases should be locked immediately after removing merchandise — never left unlocked "for just a minute," regardless of how close you'll be standing.

---

**2. Why do you loupe an item both before AND after showing it to a customer, according to this module?**
A) It's purely a product-knowledge demonstration for the customer
B) It creates a structured, timed moment to catch a possible switch or substitution before the customer leaves the counter
C) It's only necessary for items under a certain value
D) It has no real security function

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The loupe-before-and-after step is specifically an anti-switch control — comparing the item before and after gives you a structured chance to notice a substitution while there's still something you can do about it.

---

**3. Why should only one item be shown at a time, per this module?**
A) It slows down the sales conversation intentionally
B) Multiple items out simultaneously multiply both switch risk and grab-and-run risk, and divide your attention
C) It's required only when a manager is present
D) There is no real reason; it's simply tradition

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Showing one item at a time limits what's exposed and vulnerable at any single moment, keeping your full attention on one piece and one customer's hands rather than splitting it across multiple items.

---

**4. Why does this module recommend distributing high-end watches and loose diamonds across several cases rather than concentrating them in one?**
A) It makes the display look more visually appealing
B) It limits the maximum possible loss from any single compromised case
C) It has no security purpose, only a merchandising one
D) It's required only in stores with fewer than three showcases

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Concentrating the highest-value inventory in one case creates a single point of maximum loss if that case is ever compromised; distributing it limits the damage from any one incident.

---

**5. A customer flashes $10,000 in cash while shopping. What should this cue you to do, per this module?**
A) Relax normal protocol since the sale is clearly secure with cash in hand
B) Treat it as a possible distraction-theft cue and tighten normal selling-floor discipline
C) Immediately refuse to serve the customer
D) Ask the customer to leave the cash at the register before continuing

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Trade guidance specifically frames a large unprompted cash display as a possible distraction-theft tactic, not a reassuring sign — the correct response is to tighten protocol (locking cases, one item at a time, staying present), not loosen it.

---

**6. When should a photo ID be required and a private showroom be used, per the escalating-control principle in this module?**
A) Never, regardless of item value
B) For all items regardless of value, applied identically every time
C) With increasing intensity as the value of the item being shown increases
D) Only on weekends

**Correct answer: C**
Explanation: The level of control (ID verification, private showroom) should scale with what's actually at risk in that specific interaction — proportionate to value, not flat regardless of value.

---

**7. Why does this module recommend a wrist key holder specifically, rather than keeping showcase keys in a pocket or on the counter?**
A) It's required by law in most jurisdictions
B) It prevents keys from being set down "for a second" and forgotten or lost during a busy or distracting moment
C) It makes the associate's uniform look more professional
D) It has no real functional benefit over a pocket

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A wrist key holder solves an ordinary-distraction failure mode — keys attached to your body can't silently disappear the way keys set down on a counter or dropped in a pocket can during a hectic shift.

---

**8. What underlying principle connects this module's showcase protocol to Module 1's opening/closing procedures?**
A) Both modules cover completely unrelated topics with no shared theme
B) Security failures often come from ordinary, routine moments where protocol felt unnecessary, not just from rare dramatic events
C) Both modules require the exact same two-person staffing rule
D) Neither module has any connection to insurance requirements

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Module 1 covers danger from predictable, high-stakes moments (opening/closing); Module 3 covers danger from routine moments that feel too ordinary to warrant care — both point to the same principle that discipline in "unremarkable" moments matters as much as discipline in obviously risky ones.
