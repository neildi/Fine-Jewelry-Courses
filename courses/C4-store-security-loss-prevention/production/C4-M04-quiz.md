# C4-M04 Quiz: Recognizing Distraction Theft and Robbery Patterns

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly distinguishing theft patterns and applying the right response, not memorizing exact statistics.

---

**1. Why is greeting every person who walks into the store considered a security tactic, not just customer service?**
A) It has no real security value, only a sales purpose
B) It signals to a legitimate customer they're attended to, and signals to someone casing the store that they've been noticed
C) It is required only during the last hour before closing
D) It replaces the need for any other security procedure

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A prompt greeting serves a dual purpose — it's good service, and it signals that whoever entered has been noticed, which can deter someone casing the store from continuing unobserved.

---

**2. What is the core mechanic behind distraction theft, as described in this module?**
A) A single person quickly grabbing merchandise and running
B) Team coordination — one person occupies your attention while a partner exploits the resulting lapse in protocol
C) A customer simply asking too many questions about pricing
D) An associate accidentally leaving a case unlocked with no external involvement

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Distraction theft typically involves at least two coordinated people — one creating a distraction (large cash display, urgent question, dropped item) while a partner accesses unattended merchandise.

---

**3. What is the correct response when you feel unusually rushed, charmed, or distracted by a customer?**
A) Relax your normal protocol slightly to keep the interaction moving smoothly
B) Slow down and tighten your normal protocol rather than loosening it
C) Immediately end the interaction without explanation
D) Ignore the feeling since it's probably nothing

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A distraction crew is specifically trying to create a moment where bending protocol feels reasonable — the correct response is to deliberately slow down and tighten discipline, not relax it.

---

**4. What is the core mechanic behind grab-and-run theft, as distinguished from distraction theft in this module?**
A) It relies on stealth and avoiding all detection over an extended period
B) It relies on speed — grabbing accessible merchandise and leaving quickly, whether or not it's noticed immediately
C) It always involves a large cash display as the primary tactic
D) It only occurs during store opening and closing procedures

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Grab-and-run works on speed rather than stealth — the thief grabs visible, accessible merchandise and leaves before anyone can react, unlike the team-coordination approach of distraction theft.

---

**5. Which of the following is a casing indicator worth documenting, even though no single instance proves intent?**
A) A customer trying on a ring and asking the price
B) Repeat visits from the same person with little or no purchase interest each time, combined with unusual attention to staff movement or camera locations
C) A customer asking about return policy
D) A customer requesting a specific metal type

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Repeat low-interest visits combined with unusual attention to staff/camera/security details is a pattern worth documenting in the Suspicious Incidents Book, even though any single visit alone isn't proof of anything.

---

**6. According to this module, does the "if in doubt, keep them out" identity-verification principle apply only at opening and closing?**
A) Yes, it applies exclusively before opening and after closing
B) No, it extends to business hours as well — for example, an unscheduled "vendor representative" or unexpected "inspector" asking for back-room access
C) It applies only to delivery personnel, never to other visitors
D) It applies only if a manager is not present

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: While Module 1 introduced this principle in the opening/closing context, this module extends it to business hours — anyone requesting unusual access during the day deserves the same scrutiny.

---

**7. If an actual robbery or theft-in-progress incident occurs, what is the overriding general safety principle described in this module?**
A) Physically intervene to protect merchandise at all costs
B) Personal safety comes first, ahead of merchandise — comply, don't resist, avoid sudden movements
C) Immediately begin tidying the scene to minimize disruption
D) Confront the individual verbally to delay them until police arrive

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The overriding principle across retail security guidance is personal safety first — comply with instructions, don't physically resist, and avoid actions that could be misread as resistance.

---

**8. Why does this module explicitly avoid giving a detailed, universal incident-response procedure?**
A) Because no such procedures exist anywhere in the industry
B) Because exact incident-response steps (who calls police, who handles customers, etc.) need to be store-specific, and each store should have its own written plan
C) Because incident response is not an important topic
D) Because it's covered in a completely unrelated course

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The module presents only the general safety-first principle and explicitly defers to each store's specific written active-incident plan, since exact procedural details vary by store.
