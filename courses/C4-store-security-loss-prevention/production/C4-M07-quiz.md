# C4-M07 Quiz: Perpetual Inventory, Cycle Counts, and Shrink Control

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correct sequencing of the discrepancy protocol and the rationale behind cycle-count design, not memorizing exact figures.

---

**1. Why is annual-only inventory counting considered inadequate, per this module?**
A) It's actually the recommended best practice
B) Regular weekly/monthly/bimonthly counts catch errors as they happen rather than letting them accumulate silently for a year
C) Annual counts are more accurate than frequent counts
D) It has no real drawback compared to frequent counting

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Trade guidance is explicit that regular cycle counts (weekly, monthly, or bimonthly) catch discrepancies early, rather than letting a full year of undetected errors or losses accumulate before a single annual count.

---

**2. Why does this module recommend unannounced or surprise counts on specific categories?**
A) It's simply more convenient for management scheduling
B) An employee who knows the counting schedule in advance can prepare for it, undermining the count as an internal control
C) Announced counts are always more accurate
D) There is no real reason; it's arbitrary

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: If staff know exactly when and what will be counted, that predictability can be exploited, defeating much of the internal-control purpose of counting.

---

**3. What is the correct FIRST step when a cycle count shows an item missing?**
A) Immediately report it as a suspected theft
B) Recount the location first
C) Question the associate who most recently worked that case
D) Write off the loss and adjust inventory records immediately

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The discrepancy protocol starts with a recount, since a large share of apparent discrepancies are simple counting errors, not actual losses.

---

**4. In the discrepancy protocol, what should be checked AFTER a recount confirms an item is genuinely not in its expected location?**
A) Immediately declare it a loss
B) Whether the item is in a repair box or out on approval with a customer, then check recent sales for a possible scanning error
C) Skip directly to filing an insurance claim
D) Assume it was a data entry error with no further investigation

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: After a recount, the protocol checks repair boxes and approval-out records, then checks recent sales for a scanning error — only after all of these come up empty is a loss declared.

---

**5. What is the purpose of the four-step high-value count model (two independent counts, comparison, third-person recount of differences, then system adjustment)?**
A) It has no real purpose beyond tradition
B) It builds in a check against both simple counting errors and one counter's number being influenced by seeing the other's number first
C) It is required only for items under a certain value
D) It replaces the need for any other inventory controls

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Having two people count independently (without comparing first), then bringing in a third person to resolve any differences, protects against both honest mistakes and the risk of one count being subtly influenced by seeing the other.

---

**6. Why should the person who signs for incoming shipments NOT also be the person who conducts inventory counts on that same stock?**
A) It has no real security purpose, only an efficiency one
B) Combining both roles removes a check on records and increases the risk of undetected errors or collusion
C) It is required only in stores with more than ten employees
D) It makes receiving shipments slower with no other benefit

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Separating who receives stock from who counts it is a segregation-of-duties control that prevents one person from having unchecked control over both the physical stock and its recorded count.

---

**7. Which of the three physical-inventory modes described in this module would be most appropriate for a store's weekly cycle count of a single category?**
A) Full physical inventory
B) Case count
C) Partial scan only, with no location tracking
D) No formal mode is needed for weekly counts

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Case count mode allows scanning specific locations as needed, which matches the one-category-per-week cycle-count model described in this module, as opposed to full physical inventory (whole-store, typically annual).

---

**8. Why does this module emphasize investigating clusters of disappearances quickly, rather than treating each missing item as an isolated event?**
A) Clusters are always coincidental and don't need investigation
B) A pattern across multiple missing items (same time, category, or associate) may indicate a real, ongoing problem that a single-item view would miss
C) Investigating clusters is legally required in all cases
D) It has no real analytical value

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Treating each disappearance as isolated can mask a genuine pattern (e.g., a recurring issue with a specific category, time period, or process) that only becomes visible when discrepancies are looked at collectively.
