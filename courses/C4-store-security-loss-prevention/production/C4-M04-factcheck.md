## Verification Status: Mixed — directly sourced claims separated from module-synthesized guidance; general safety framing intentionally kept non-prescriptive

## Claims Reviewed

**1. JSA 2025 crime count and 2024 robbery/grab-and-run timing data.**
Status: Sourced from the research file (same JSA data cited in Module 1), attributed to JSA's Annual Crime Reports via National Jeweler and JCK. This module reuses, rather than independently re-derives, that data — same inheritance caveat as Module 1: confirm directly against JSA's published reports before external publication.

**2. "Meet and greet as a deterrent" framing.**
Status: Sourced from the research file's citation of a retailer security guide (Jackson Dieken, distributed guide) which explicitly names "meet-and-greet as a deterrent" and "casing detection" as retail security behaviors. The module's elaboration of WHY this works (dual service/security signal) is a reasonable interpretive expansion of the sourced concept.

**3. JCK's "if in doubt, keep them out" quote.**
Status: Sourced directly from the research file (JCK, "Security: A Life and Death Issue for Jewelers"), same source used in Module 1. This module's application of the principle to business-hours scenarios (unscheduled vendor reps, surprise inspectors) is the module's own extension of the sourced principle to a new context, not itself a directly sourced claim — flagged here for transparency.

**4. Casing-behavior indicator list (repeat low-interest visits, staff/camera/closing-pattern interest, parked-vehicle indicator).**
Status: This is a SYNTHESIZED list drawing on general themes referenced in the research file's citation of security guidance (JIS Show's "8 practical vigilance tactics," Jackson Dieken's casing-detection reference), rather than a verbatim reproduction of a single named source's exact list. MUST VERIFY: if this module is published externally, consider sourcing a specific named list (e.g., directly from JSA's Manual of Jewelry Security or the JIS Show article) rather than presenting a synthesized list without a single clear citation.

**5. Distraction theft and grab-and-run mechanic descriptions (team coordination vs. speed-based).**
Status: The distraction-theft mechanic is a reasonable elaboration of the cash-flash red flag already sourced in Module 3. The grab-and-run description is consistent with the general, widely-understood definition of the crime type and the JSA timing correlation sourced in Module 1, but the specific mechanic description itself is the module's own synthesis, not a direct quotation.

**6. "What to Do During and Immediately After an Incident" section.**
Status: INTENTIONALLY GENERAL. This reflects widely-shared general retail-security safety principles (personal safety first, don't resist, preserve the scene) rather than any single named source in the research file. The module explicitly and correctly defers to each store's own written incident-response plan for specifics rather than presenting this as a complete procedure. No correction needed, but this section should NOT be treated as a substitute for actual active-threat training, which the Free-Learning-Resources research file notes is available free through Jewelers Mutual's Jeweler Safety & Security Academy (aligned to the JSA Manual of Jewelry Security).
