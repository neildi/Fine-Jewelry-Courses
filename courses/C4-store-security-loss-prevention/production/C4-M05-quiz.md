# C4-M05 Quiz: Card, Check, and Remote-Order Fraud Prevention

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correctly applying liability rules and recognizing clustered risk factors, not memorizing exact wording.

---

**1. For a stolen card used in a card-present (in-person) transaction, what typically determines whether the card network or the jeweler absorbs the loss?**
A) Whether the terminal displayed an "approved" message
B) Whether the jeweler obtained an imprint of the card and the customer's signature, following proper procedure
C) Whether the transaction amount was under $500
D) Whether the customer provided a phone number

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Card networks generally cover the jeweler's loss on a stolen card used in person only if the jeweler obtained authorization, an imprint, and the customer's signature — terminal approval alone does not guarantee protection.

---

**2. Which of the following is the single most useful diagnostic question for a remote (phone/email) order, per this module?**
A) What is the customer's favorite jewelry style?
B) Why would a person in a distant city be buying from this specific store rather than a dozen jewelers locally?
C) Does the customer have a good credit score?
D) Is the customer polite on the phone?

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: A legitimate long-distance purchase usually has an obvious reason (referral, prior relationship, a specific unavailable piece); a fraudulent one usually has pressure to move quickly instead of a good answer to this question.

---

**3. Why doesn't calling a customer's bank to "confirm funds" protect against check fraud?**
A) Banks never answer these calls
B) A confederate can remove the money from the account right after the verification call, before the check actually clears
C) It's illegal to call a bank for this purpose
D) It actually does fully protect against check fraud

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Verbal fund confirmation reflects the account balance at that moment, but a confederate can withdraw the funds immediately afterward — the only real protection is waiting until money has actually transferred into the jeweler's own account.

---

**4. Which of the following is listed as a check-fraud red flag in this module?**
A) A check with a pre-printed bank return address
B) An out-of-state personal check with no bank-printed return address and a very low check number
C) A check from a well-established local customer
D) A check for a small, routine purchase amount

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Out-of-state checks, missing pre-printed return addresses, and unusually low sequential check numbers (suggesting a new or rarely-used account) are specifically flagged as red flags.

---

**5. What does EMV (chip) technology protect against, and what does it NOT protect against, per this module?**
A) It protects both in-person and online transactions equally
B) It protects card-present (in-person) transactions but does nothing to prevent remote-order/online fraud
C) It only protects online transactions
D) It has no real fraud-prevention function

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Chip technology is specifically designed for card-present transactions; it does not address the remote-order fraud patterns (phone/email orders) covered elsewhere in this module.

---

**6. If a bank issues a chip-enabled (EMV) card and a fraudulent transaction is processed through a non-EMV-capable terminal, what happens to liability?**
A) The card network always absorbs the loss regardless of terminal type
B) Liability shifts to the business, and this loss type is generally not covered by standard jewelers' insurance
C) Liability is always split 50/50 between the bank and the business
D) There is no liability consequence either way

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: If a bank-issued EMV card is fraudulently used through a terminal that isn't EMV-capable, liability shifts to the business processing the transaction, and this is generally not a covered loss type under standard policies.

---

**7. Why does this module recommend a documented sales-order process rather than relying on individual associate judgment for fraud prevention?**
A) Documentation has no real benefit over judgment
B) A consistent process (defined required fields, an internal record of past fraud patterns, prompt chargeback response) reduces reliance on inconsistent case-by-case decisions
C) It's only required for online sales, never in-person ones
D) It replaces the need for any card-verification tools

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Trade guidance frames fraud prevention as a documented, repeatable process — consistent required fields, an internal fraud database, and prompt chargeback handling — rather than leaving each decision to individual judgment in the moment.

**8. In the module's worked remote-order scenario, which single factor alone should have triggered a refusal to process the sale?**
A) The customer being polite and articulate
B) No single factor alone was necessarily disqualifying — it was the cluster of five risk factors together that warranted slowing down and adding verification
C) The item being a tennis bracelet specifically
D) The sale amount being under $10,000

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The module explicitly notes that any one risk factor alone might be innocent; it's the cluster of multiple factors appearing together in a single transaction that warrants additional verification, not necessarily an outright refusal.
