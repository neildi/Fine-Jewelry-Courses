# C4-M06 Huddle Card: Large Cash Transactions and Form 8300 Compliance

## 5-Minute Pre-Shift Discussion

**The one idea to remember:** Never send a customer a copy of the actual Form 8300. And if the "suspicious" box was checked, never let them know a filing happened — no exceptions.

## The Threshold
More than $10,000 in cash, in one transaction OR related transactions. Layaway deposits, custom-order payments, and split payments toward the same purchase all get added together — splitting cash across visits doesn't avoid the requirement.

## The Two Filing Steps
1. File Form 8300 with the IRS.
2. Furnish a written statement to the customer by January 31 of the following year — an invoice works if it has all the required info (business name/address, contact, total cash received, IRS-disclosure language).

## What NEVER to Do
- Never send the actual filed form as the customer's statement.
- Never tell a customer their transaction was flagged as suspicious.

## If a Customer Won't Give Full Information
Don't just skip the filing — document your attempts to get the information. That protects you if the filing is ever questioned.

## Today's Discussion Prompt
Does our layaway/deposit tracking add up cash payments per customer across visits, or only look at one payment at a time? If we're not sure, that's worth asking a manager.
