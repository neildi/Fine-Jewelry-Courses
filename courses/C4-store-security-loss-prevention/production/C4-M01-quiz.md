# C4-M01 Quiz: Opening and Closing: The Two-Person Rule and Alarm Response

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. This module's quiz emphasizes correct sequencing of the opening/closing procedure and alarm response, not memorizing statistics.

---

**1. According to JSA's 2024 Annual Crime Report, when do jewelry-store robberies peak?**
A) Only during late-night hours after closing
B) 5:00-6:00 p.m., with a secondary peak at 12:00-1:00 p.m., with no safe hour during a normal workday
C) Exclusively on weekends when the store is closed
D) Only during the first hour after opening

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: JSA's data shows robberies peaked between 5:00-6:00 p.m. (26 events) with a secondary peak at 12:00-1:00 p.m. (21 events), and there was no 60-minute period during a normal workday when robberies did not occur.

---

**2. What is the correct first step when opening the store, per Jewelers Mutual's guidance?**
A) Unlock the doors immediately so the first customers aren't kept waiting
B) One person enters while a colleague watches from a safe distance, after inspecting the surrounding area for signs of a break-in
C) Both employees enter together immediately without inspecting anything
D) Call the alarm company before anyone approaches the building

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: The two-person sequence requires inspecting the surrounding area first, then having one person enter while the other watches from a safe distance, giving an all-clear signal before anyone else follows.

---

**3. Why should the same person never hold both premises keys and safe keys, per this module?**
A) It's simply a workplace tradition with no functional reason
B) It splits custody so a single compromised individual can't access both the building and the vault
C) It's required only for stores with fewer than two employees
D) It has no real security purpose and is mostly about convenience

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Key segregation is a structural control — a bad actor would need to compromise two separate people rather than one to gain full access to both the building and the safe.

---

**4. What is wrong with covering a fully-loaded showcase with a cloth overnight instead of putting merchandise away?**
A) Nothing — it's an acceptable alternative to put-away as long as the case is locked
B) It actually signals to burglars that something valuable is hidden underneath
C) It's only a cosmetic issue, not a security issue
D) It's fine as long as the store has an alarm system

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: As JCK summarizes the burglar's logic: "Why's that covered? Because something is in it." Covering a case doesn't hide it — it draws attention. All goods belong in the safe overnight, not covered on display.

---

**5. An alarm signal comes in reporting a communication error, not a full alarm trigger. What should staff do?**
A) Ignore it since it's just a technical error, not a real alarm
B) Treat it as a real alarm signal requiring the same response procedure
C) Wait until the next business day to investigate
D) Enter the premises alone quickly to check since it's probably nothing

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Notices of communication errors or power interruption should never be dismissed as technical noise — they should be treated with the same alarm-response procedure as any other signal.

---

**6. When responding to an alarm, what should staff do regarding entering the premises?**
A) Enter immediately to assess the situation before police arrive
B) Never enter alone; wait to be accompanied by police
C) Enter only if it's daytime and the parking lot looks empty
D) Send the newest employee in first to check

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Staff should never enter the premises alone in response to an alarm signal, and should wait to be accompanied by police before investigating.

---

**7. A caller claims to be from the alarm company and provides a phone number to call back and confirm. What should staff do?**
A) Call back on the number the caller provided, since they identified themselves
B) Call back using the alarm company's own published number, not the number given by the caller
C) Ignore the call entirely
D) Ask a coworker to call the provided number instead

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: Inbound alarm calls should be verified by calling back on the alarm company's published number — not necessarily the number given by the caller, since a fraudulent caller could provide a number that routes back to them.

---

**8. Why are TL-15 and TL-30 rated safes described as inadequate by JSA, according to this module?**
A) They are too heavy for most store floors
B) They are not adequate protection against today's more sophisticated burglary methods, unlike a TRTL-30x6 rated safe
C) They are no longer manufactured
D) They require too much floor space compared to newer models

**Correct answer: B**
Explanation: JSA specifically states that TL-15 and TL-30 rated safes are not adequate protection from today's burglars, and that a TRTL-30x6 rated safe is significantly harder for a professional crew to penetrate.
